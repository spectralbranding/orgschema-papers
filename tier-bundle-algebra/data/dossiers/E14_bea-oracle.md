## 1. Deal identification
- Acquirer / parties: Oracle Corporation (NASDAQ: ORCL), described as the world's largest enterprise software company, acquired BEA Systems, Inc. via merger. `[SRC: SEC DEFM14A accession 0001193125-08-044381, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1031798/000119312508044381/ddefm14a.htm]`
- Target: BEA Systems, Inc. (NASDAQ: BEAS), a Delaware corporation headquartered at 2315 North First Street, San Jose, California — a middleware software vendor. `[SRC: SEC DEFM14A accession 0001193125-08-044381, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1031798/000119312508044381/ddefm14a.htm]`
- Announced / closed dates: Merger agreement entered January 16, 2008; special meeting of BEA stockholders scheduled April 4, 2008; acquisition completed April 29, 2008 (after European regulatory approval). `[SRC: SEC DEFM14A accession 0001193125-08-044381, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1031798/000119312508044381/ddefm14a.htm]` `[SRC: CBC News, https://www.cbc.ca/news/science/oracle-completes-8-5b-takeover-of-bea-systems-1.724924]`
- Disclosed deal value: $19.375 in cash, without interest, per share of BEA common stock; the offer was valued at approximately $8.5 billion, or $7.2 billion net of BEA's cash on hand of $1.3 billion. `[SRC: SEC DEFM14A accession 0001193125-08-044381, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1031798/000119312508044381/ddefm14a.htm]` `[SRC: SEC 8-K (BEA), Oracle/BEA press release, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1031798/000089882208000103/pressrelease.htm]`
- Legal form (share deal / asset deal / merger / etc.): All-cash statutory merger of a public C-corporation; BEA became a wholly owned subsidiary of Oracle. `[SRC: SEC DEFM14A accession 0001193125-08-044381, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1031798/000119312508044381/ddefm14a.htm]`

## 2. Tier-level transfer content (evidence for the bundle signature)
For each tier, what crossed the ownership boundary and under what direction.
- **T1 Owner Intent** — BEA's prior controlling-principal imprint (a widely held public company whose board had faced an activist challenge from Carl Icahn) exited; the business came under Oracle's stated strategic intent to extend and enhance its Fusion Middleware suite. `[SRC: SEC DEFM14A accession 0001193125-08-044381, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1031798/000119312508044381/ddefm14a.htm]`
- **T2 Business Model** — BEA's enterprise-middleware licensing/support model transferred and was folded into Oracle's middleware business; Oracle framed the deal as accretive within the first full year after closing. `[SRC: SEC 8-K (BEA), Oracle/BEA press release, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1031798/000089882208000103/pressrelease.htm]`
- **T3 Business Entity** — BEA Systems, Inc. survived the merger as a wholly owned subsidiary of Oracle (share/entity acquisition, not an asset purchase). `[SRC: SEC DEFM14A accession 0001193125-08-044381, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1031798/000119312508044381/ddefm14a.htm]`
- **T4 Product** — BEA's product franchise (notably the WebLogic Java application server) transferred; post-close Oracle sorted BEA products into "strategic," "continue and converge," and "maintenance" categories, with WebLogic Server designated Oracle's strategic Java (J2EE) container. `[SRC: web-search-surfaced trade press, InfoWorld/ADTmag, https://adtmag.com/articles/2008/01/24/a-closer-look-oracle-acquires-bea.aspx]`
- **T5 Process** — Product-development and support operations transferred into Oracle's Fusion Middleware organization; specific operating-infrastructure detail not located in the effort budget. `[UNVERIFIED]`
- **T6 Organization** — BEA's workforce transferred into Oracle; precise headcount-transfer figures not located in the effort budget. `[UNVERIFIED]`

## 3. Post-deal performance at the 3-5 year horizon
- CAR / share-price reaction, OR write-down magnitude as fraction of deal value, OR divestiture timing (months from close): No divestiture; BEA's products were retained and integrated into Oracle Fusion Middleware, with WebLogic Server adopted as Oracle's strategic Java application server. `[SRC: web-search-surfaced trade press, InfoWorld/ADTmag, https://adtmag.com/articles/2008/01/24/a-closer-look-oracle-acquires-bea.aspx]`
- Narrative outcome (integration succeeded / failed / mixed): Succeeded from Oracle's standpoint — the acquisition (its largest since PeopleSoft in 2005) materially extended the Fusion Middleware platform, and WebLogic became a flagship Oracle middleware product; "continue and converge" products were committed to at least nine years of maintenance and "maintenance" products to five. `[SRC: web-search-surfaced trade press, InfoWorld/ADTmag, https://adtmag.com/articles/2008/01/24/a-closer-look-oracle-acquires-bea.aspx]`
