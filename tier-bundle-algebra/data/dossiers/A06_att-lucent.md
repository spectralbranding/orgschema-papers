## 1. Deal identification
- Parties: AT&T Corporation (parent) spun out its equipment/systems business as Lucent Technologies Inc., which comprised the former AT&T Technologies unit — Western Electric manufacturing + Bell Labs research. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucent_Technologies]`
- Context: part of AT&T's 1995-announced "trivestiture" into three companies — a services company (AT&T), an equipment/R&D company (Lucent), and a computer company (NCR). `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucent_Technologies]` `[SRC: https://www.spokesman.com/stories/1996/oct/01/att-performs-21-billion-spinoff-lucent-creation/]`
- Announced / closed dates: separation completed September 30, 1996; initial public offering earlier in 1996 (Carly Fiorina central to IPO planning), with AT&T subsequently distributing its remaining Lucent shares to AT&T shareholders. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucent_Technologies]`
- Disclosed deal value: the 1996 IPO raised approximately USD 3 billion; press reported the resulting Lucent as a ~USD 21.4 billion high-technology company; AT&T distributed 525 million Lucent shares to ~3.3 million AT&T holders. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucent_Technologies]` `[SRC: https://www.spokesman.com/stories/1996/oct/01/att-performs-21-billion-spinoff-lucent-creation/]`
- Legal form: equity carve-out (IPO of a minority) followed by full pro-rata stock distribution (spin-out) to AT&T shareholders, creating an independent listed company. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucent_Technologies]`

## 2. Tier-level transfer content (evidence for the bundle signature)
- **T1 Owner Intent** — New/replaced. Lucent received its own independent board and strategic mandate; the strategic rationale was to free the equipment business from AT&T's ownership so it could sell to AT&T's telecom competitors, who had been reluctant to buy from a direct competitor. `[SRC: https://www.spokesman.com/stories/1996/oct/01/att-performs-21-billion-spinoff-lucent-creation/]`
- **T2 Business Model** — Changed/liberated. The equipment-and-systems value model was reoriented from an in-house AT&T supplier to a merchant supplier able to serve the whole carrier market. `[SRC: https://www.spokesman.com/stories/1996/oct/01/att-performs-21-billion-spinoff-lucent-creation/]`
- **T3 Business Entity** — New vehicle. A fresh, separately incorporated and NYSE-listed legal entity (Lucent Technologies Inc., Murray Hill, NJ) was created to hold the carved-out business. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucent_Technologies]`
- **T4 Product** — Transferred whole. The network-equipment product portfolio plus the Bell Labs R&D franchise (name and ~three-quarters of staff) moved to Lucent. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucent_Technologies]` `[SRC: https://www.spokesman.com/stories/1996/oct/01/att-performs-21-billion-spinoff-lucent-creation/]`
- **T5 Process** — Transferred whole. Western Electric's manufacturing operations transferred intact to Lucent as its production base. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucent_Technologies]`
- **T6 Organization** — Transferred. Lucent launched in 1996 with roughly 120,000–130,000 employees inherited from AT&T Technologies; three-quarters of Bell Labs staff went to Lucent (the remainder became AT&T Labs). `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucent_Technologies]` `[SRC: https://www.spokesman.com/stories/1996/oct/01/att-performs-21-billion-spinoff-lucent-creation/]`

## 3. Post-deal performance at the 3-5 year horizon
- Share-price reaction: Lucent became a "darling" stock of the late-1990s market, reaching a high near USD 84/share and a peak market capitalization of ~USD 258 billion, the most widely held U.S. stock with ~5.3 million shareholders. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucent_Technologies]`
- Subsequent collapse: after the telecom/dot-com bust the company contracted sharply — headcount fell from ~120,000+ (1996) to ~30,500 by 2006 amid layoffs and restructuring. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucent_Technologies]`
- Terminal outcome: Lucent was acquired by France's Alcatel on December 1, 2006, forming Alcatel-Lucent — ending ~ten years of independent existence. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucent_Technologies]`
- Narrative outcome: mixed → negative long-run. The separation itself executed cleanly and the entity boomed near-term, but the standalone equipment business could not sustain its peak and lost independence within a decade. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucent_Technologies]`

## 4. Failure-pathway evidence (parent-paper P1-P7)
- P4 Product-Process disruption (T4<->T5): none at separation — product and manufacturing transferred together as a self-contained unit. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucent_Technologies]`
- P5 Process-Organization fracture (T5<->T6): none documented at the split; workforce and operations moved together. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucent_Technologies]`
- T1-archetype incoherence (non-cascade channel): none documented at spin-out. `[UNVERIFIED]`
- T2-model incompatibility (non-cascade channel): the later decline is attributed to market/industry collapse (telecom-equipment downturn) rather than to a tier-transfer defect at separation. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucent_Technologies]`
- Other Pn manifested: post-2000 industry-wide equipment glut and demand collapse drove the contraction; not a structural transfer failure of the 1996 bundle. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucent_Technologies]`

## 6. Collapse-state evidence
- No Tier-1=Tier-4 founder-identity fusion (a large corporate carve-out, not a founder-embodied firm). The Bell Labs *name* carried identity value and was deliberately assigned to Lucent along with most of its staff — brand/identity was transferred with the asset rather than fused to a retained principal. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucent_Technologies]`
