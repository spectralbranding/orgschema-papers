# Preparing to Sell and Preparing to Hand Over Are Rival Investments: A Persistence Column for the Six-Tier Ontology

Dmitry Zharnikov

ORCID: 0009-0000-6893-9231

DOI: [10.5281/zenodo.21868658](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21868658)

Working Paper v1.0.0 – August 2026

---

## Abstract

An owner preparing to leave a firm faces two futures usually treated as one: sale, or handover to a successor. This paper derives why the preparation each rewards differs. A six-tier ontology of organizational transferability already states what arrives in a buyer's possession, but that column is indexed to a counterparty, which a succession lacks. Decomposing it into three mechanisms of persistence — legal, artifact, and person — yields a second column indexed instead to two independent facts: whether the going concern is conveyed, and whether the incoming principal must acquire what a person carries. Crossed, a sale to an acquirer supplying its own principal disturbs legal and prices artifact continuity, a succession disturbs only person continuity, and a fourth case inherits both. Among the tiers a succession disturbs, one is drawn at operating frequency rather than at discretion, so its loss cannot be deferred. In an owner-operated firm the holder of that residual is the only one who could document it and the only one who could teach it, making the two preparations rivals for one budget. The paper states the crossover this predicts, supplies a pre-registered design with its power, and reports an audit finding neither construct measured.

**Keywords**: succession, business transfer, owner-managed firms, knowledge codification, tacit knowledge, organizational tiers, pre-registered design, measurement.

---

An owner who intends to leave a firm is told to prepare it. The advice is usually undifferentiated: document the processes, reduce owner dependence, build a management layer, get the accounts in order. Underneath it sits an assumption that preparation is one activity with one direction of improvement — that a firm made ready to sell is thereby made ready to hand over, and vice versa. This paper argues the assumption is false, derives why, and specifies the study that would test it.

The setting is deliberately narrow. The firm is owner-*operated*: the outgoing principal both holds the controlling stake and is the person who runs the business. The event is *replacement* rather than removal — the occupant of the top tier is replaced, the going concern is conveyed to nobody, and the firm carries on trading in the same hands. Neither event is primitive; a later section decomposes both into the two independent facts that generate them — whether the going concern is conveyed to a counterparty, and whether the incoming operating principal must acquire what a person carries — and the derivation runs on the two cells named there. Where the owner does not operate, the constraint this paper identifies is simply absent, because the operating residual sits with managers whom the event does not replace. That boundary is not a caveat added at the end; it is what makes the argument go through, and it belongs in the first paragraph.

The vehicle is an existing decomposition of the firm into six specification tiers — owner intent, business model, business entity, product, process, organization — each governed by a distinct question and each transferring differently on sale [@zharnikov-2026-dual-hierarchies-organizational-transferability-six]. A companion result models the migration of signal from a founder-bound tier into an institutionally separable one as a continuous curve with a discontinuity in acquisition value, and explicitly defers succession as "a geometrically distinct event" [@zharnikov-2026ai-tier-rotation-curve]. This paper takes up that deferral. It does not inherit evidentiary weight from either predecessor; both supply structure, and every empirical claim below is either derived, cited to an independently verified external record, or declared unmeasured.

The premise that leaving is not one event is not new, and the paper should say whose it is. Entrepreneurial exit is established as a constitutive part of the entrepreneurial process rather than its termination [@detienne-2010-entrepreneurial-exit-critical], and exit is established as a set of *divergent routes* with different drivers rather than a single act [@wennberg-2010-reconceptualizing-entrepreneurial-exit]. That literature asks which route an owner takes and what predicts the choice. This paper asks a question it leaves open: given that the routes diverge, what does *preparing* for one do to readiness for the other? The routes literature treats preparation as largely undifferentiated antecedent; the claim here is that preparation is where the divergence originates, and that the two routes make competing claims on the same input before the owner has chosen between them.

Two contributions follow, and there are two rather than three. The first is a second column for the existing ontology. The second is a resource-allocation trade-off under a budget constraint. A third candidate — that the transfer-rate bound differs in kind from a codification-rate bound — turns out to be a corollary of the second rather than an independent claim, and is presented as one.

Propositions are numbered in the order they entered the specification, not the order they are read. The chain of derivation runs P1, P2, P3, P9, P4, P5, P6, P10, P8, P7, and that is the order in which they appear below.

## The Persistence Column

### *Two columns, not one.*

The six-tier ontology carries a column headed *what transfers*. Read carefully, it is a **transaction** column: every entry answers the question *if this firm changed hands, what would arrive in the buyer's possession?* Nothing directly transfers from owner intent, only its imprint on lower tiers; the business entity transfers by document; the product transfers if specified and not if tacit; process transfers if codified; organization transfers partially at best.

That is the right column for an event whose defining feature is a counterparty. A succession asks a different question of the same stack: *if the occupant of the top tier is replaced and nothing is conveyed, what stops working?* Call the answer the **persistence column**.

**P1.** The published transfer column is indexed to a counterparty, so an event with no counterparty requires its own column — one that is derivable from the same primitives rather than posited alongside them.

This is a claim about the ontology before it is a claim about succession, and that is what makes the first contribution a result rather than a redescription. The move is not "succession is different because tacit knowledge is hard." It is that the existing apparatus has one column where it needs two, and the second is recoverable from the first.

P1 is falsifiable in a way worth stating. If, for every tier, what a buyer would receive coincides with what survives a replacement, the second column is redundant and this contribution collapses. The remainder of this section shows the two columns disagree.

### *Three modes of persistence.*

Whatever survives a control event survives by one of three mechanisms. They are mutually exclusive, and they are exhaustive over this stack in a sense worth stating precisely rather than asserting: something persists either because an institution outlives the event, because an inspectable object does, or because a person does. The classification is by *carrier*, and those three carriers are the only ones the six-tier ontology admits. It applies to *components* rather than to whole tiers, and the distinction matters immediately: the product splits into a specified part and a tacit part carried differently, and process splits the same way. A tier is a bundle of components; a carrier attaches to a component.

One boundary of that claim should be conceded now. A residual distributed across several people, held by none of them individually, is not cleanly person-carried in the sense used here — it is closer to the organization tier, and the account treats it as such. If a substantial part of what a firm needs to keep operating is group-carried in that way, the analysis below applies to a smaller share of the firm than it appears to. That share is not measured or bounded anywhere in this paper, and the Limitations return to the concession rather than leaving it as a gesture.

**Legal continuity (L).** The component is carried by the registered entity in which the business sits, and survives any event in which the going concern is not conveyed to a counterparty: the same entity keeps its own contracts, permissions and standing. Conveyance disturbs it whether effected by transferring the shares or by transferring the assets into a new vehicle — in the second case the seller's registered entity survives the transaction, but the business it carried does not survive inside it. The business entity by construction; the contracted part of the business model.

**Artifact continuity (A).** The component is carried by an inspectable object existing independent of any particular person — a specification, a document, a codebase, a standard operating procedure. The specified part of the product; the codified part of process.

**Person continuity (P).** The component is carried in the state of a particular human being and does not survive that person's departure. Owner intent by definition; the tacit part of the product; the uncodified part of process; organization in the strong sense of the *particular* people.

Nothing here is new. The published transfer column is exactly the statement that L and A arrive in a buyer's possession and P does not. It is decomposed this way so that the second column can be derived from the same primitives rather than asserted beside them.

## Which Tier Binds

### *What replacement leaves intact.*

**P2.** A tier whose persistence mechanism is undisturbed by an event cannot be the binding constraint on value retained through that event.

This is close to definitional and it does most of the work: a constraint binds only where the event removes something. Applied to replacement with the entity persisting, it eliminates almost everything. The business entity is not disturbed — no assignment occurs, the contracts are the same contracts, registrations do not lapse. The business model is largely undisturbed, being instantiated in live contracts, prices and channels. The product is not disturbed at the event: the delivered specification is the same specification, and its tacit part constrains *changing* the product rather than continuing to ship it. Organization is not disturbed at the event either — the other people are not replaced, and their departure in response is a second-order effect and a scope condition rather than the constraint. Owner intent is not a constraint at all here; it is the treatment.

Run the same test against removal — the going concern conveyed to an acquirer that supplies its own operating principal — and a different set lights up. The entity's legal mechanism is precisely what is disturbed: assignment occurs, change-of-control provisions fire, standing has to be re-established. That happens whether the buyer takes the shares or takes the assets into a new vehicle, which is why the fact that matters is the conveyance of the going concern rather than the fate of a registration. The product's artifact component is what the buyer is paying for, so its presence or absence sets the price. And every person-carried component is written off identically, because in this case the buyer does not need the occupant and no preparation programme delivers one.

**P3.** In the two cases just tested, removal — the going concern conveyed to an acquirer supplying its own operating principal — disturbs legal continuity and prices artifact continuity, while replacement disturbs only person continuity. Those two events therefore bind on different tiers. The claim is stated over the two cases and not over the event names: where the going concern is conveyed to a buyer who must acquire the person-carried residual instead, both disturbances occur together and neither clause of P3 holds alone.

Under removal so understood, the person-carried column is not a constraint to be relieved but a quantity to be minimized. Under replacement it is the only thing at risk. The divergence result thus arrives as a corollary of P2 rather than as a second claim, which is strictly stronger than asserting it.

### *Two facts, not two events.*

Removal and replacement have been used so far as though they were primitive, and they are not. Each is a bundle of two independent facts: whether the going concern is conveyed to a counterparty, and whether the incoming operating principal must acquire the person-carried residual. Legal continuity turns on the first and person continuity on the second, which is why the two facts separate rather than travel together. Table 1 crosses them.

The first fact is economic rather than registrar-level, and the difference decides how this population's most common transaction sorts. In owner-operated firms a third-party sale is frequently an *asset* purchase: the buyer takes the assets, contracts and goodwill into a new vehicle, and the seller's registered entity persists and is later wound up. A fact stated as "is the entity conveyed" files that transaction with the successions, which is backwards — assignment occurs, consents are needed, registrations and licences do not travel, and the legal mechanism is disturbed exactly as it would be in a share sale. Conveyance of the going concern is satisfied by both forms and by neither succession, which is what makes it the fact legal continuity turns on.

The second fact is not the bare fact of replacement, and the table says so rather than burying it. What person continuity turns on is whether the incoming principal must *acquire* what the incumbent carried, which is a judgment about the replacement's relation to the residual and not something a register records. An acquirer that installs its own operating principal has replaced the principal while writing the residual off; a buyer who steps into the role personally has not. Bare replacement is a separate and record-verifiable third fact, and the design carries it as one.

**Table 1.** Clean Control Transfers Crossed by the Two Facts, With the Paper's Two Arms Located.

| | Incoming principal need not acquire the residual — the incumbent stays, or the buyer supplies a principal who does not need it | Incoming principal must acquire the residual |
|---|---|---|
| **Going concern not conveyed** | No event, or a handover to someone who already holds the residual | Succession — the replacement arm above |
| **Going concern conveyed** | Acquisition into an operating platform — the removal arm above | Arm's-length sale to a buyer supplying no principal — both disturbances at once |

*Notes*: The two facts are independent, not two readings of one fact. Legal continuity is disturbed by conveyance of the going concern, whether effected by transferring the shares or by transferring the assets into a new vehicle; person continuity is disturbed by the incoming principal having to acquire the residual, whether or not a counterparty exists. The second fact encodes a judgment about the residual and not a registrar fact, and the column labels are written to show it. The table crosses the two facts over *clean control transfers* and is not the whole space of exits: liquidation and dissolution, partial or staged conveyance, and management buyouts are not binary on both facts and lie outside it. The frame below excludes buyouts explicitly and retains dissolution as an outcome within an arm rather than as a cell. The paper's derivation runs on the two named cells.

**P9.** Over clean control transfers the event space is generated by two independent facts rather than by an event name, so the persistence column is indexed to those facts. The derivation of the propositions above runs on two of the four cells; the design's exit arm, as sampled, spans two of them, because the frame excludes buyouts but not the fourth cell. The fourth cell inherits both disturbances at once.

Those last two statements are about different objects and the paper states both, because conflating them is how a refinement quietly changes what a design measures. The derivation is a claim about which cells the reasoning covers. The exit arm is a claim about which cells a realized sample will contain, and it will contain the third and the fourth together unless something separates them.

The fourth cell is not exotic. It is a straight third-party sale of an owner-operated firm to a buyer who brings no operating principal: search-fund and individual acquirers, standalone-hold private equity, a strategic buyer acquiring a specialist practice it intends to keep running as it is. There the going concern is conveyed *and* the incoming principal must acquire the residual personally — a succession with a counterparty attached. Whether it is the *modal* exit in the population this paper is scoped to, an owner-operated firm of ten to two hundred and fifty employees, is a conjecture: nothing in this paper's evidence base measures the share, and the claim is carried as a conjecture throughout rather than used as a premise. What the refinement needs is weaker and is not in doubt — that the cell is populated rather than a corner case.

What this changes is the column's *indexing*, not the carriers it classifies by. The persistence column is a decomposition by carrier, and refining the partition does not add a carrier; what gains cells is the map from event to disturbance. That map is what P3 encodes, and P8 below, which is why those two are the propositions that had to be restricted. P1, P2 and P4 through P7 stand as written. Indexing by two facts rather than by an event name makes the first contribution sharper, because a column indexed to a partition reaches cases a column indexed to an event name does not. Two consequences are carried in the sections below — a correction to how this paper reads the earn-out, and a moderator the design would otherwise have omitted. What the column says in the fourth cell tier by tier, and whether the two disturbances there simply superpose or interact, is not worked through here and is left open.

### *Draw frequency, not tacitness.*

P2 narrows the field to the person-carried residuals but does not single one out: the product has a tacit part, the business model a relational part, and organization is person-carried throughout. The discriminating property is not tacitness — which several tiers share — but **draw frequency**: how often the residual must actually be exercised for the firm to keep operating.

The product's tacit residual is drawn at discretion. Continuation does not require re-deriving the specification; it requires not changing it. A successor who knows nothing about why the product is as it is can continue shipping it. The residual binds on the firm's ability to *evolve* the specification, which is a real constraint and a deferrable one.

The business model's relational residual is drawn at intervals and is re-formable by introduction. Renewals, negotiations and referrals arrive on a contract calendar, and the standard instrument — introducing the successor before the handover — addresses it directly.

Organization's residual is not replaced by this event at all. It becomes live only if the surviving people leave.

Process is different. Its uncodified residual is drawn continuously, at the rate the firm operates. Production happens every day, and every deviation, exception, escalation or judgment call the codified process does not cover must be resolved by a person, now, or output degrades now. This residual cannot be deferred past the transition, because the transition does not pause the business.

**P4.** Process is the unique tier whose person-carried residual is both destroyed by replacement in the owner-operated population and exercised at operating frequency, so that its loss cannot be absorbed by deferring anything.

What P4 does not claim is worth stating plainly, because a reader who knows the knowledge-transfer literature will expect the tacitness argument and should be told immediately that this is not it. P4 does not claim process knowledge is more tacit than product knowledge, or harder to articulate, or more valuable. Tacitness is shared. Draw frequency is not.

That distinction is what separates this account from its nearest prior claimant. There is an established succession-specific stream reading the predecessor-to-successor transfer of tacit, firm-specific knowledge as the constraint on family-firm succession, from a resource- and knowledge-based view of the firm [@cabrerasuarez-2001-succession-process-resource]. The premise is shared and is not contested here: what a handover puts at risk is something a person carries. Two things separate the accounts. The first is that tacitness cannot select a tier, because the business model, the product, process and organization all have tacit residuals; the elimination above runs on how often the residual has to be exercised for the firm to keep operating, which is a different property and picks out one tier. The second is that a knowledge-transfer account has no budget term, so it does not predict that preparing for a handover and preparing for a sale are *rivals*. That prediction arrives with P6, and it is where the two accounts part company.

A reader in organization theory will also want to know how the process residual relates to routines, which is the field's standing unit for how work actually gets done [@pentland-2005-organizational-routines-as]. The relation is close and the difference is the point. Routines are internally variable: the performative side of a routine is where participants improvise against the ostensive pattern, and that variation is a source of change rather than noise [@feldman-2003-reconceptualizing-organizational-routines]. The residual named here is precisely the part of that performance the ostensive pattern does not fix — what the documented procedure leaves to judgment. So the claim is not that routines are person-carried, which would be too strong, but that the *unfixed* share of them is, and that in an owner-operated firm the person supplying it is the one being replaced.

### *The rate limit.*

The same property delivers what might otherwise be listed as a third contribution.

Capacity to resolve novel exceptions is built by exposure to exceptions. Exceptions arrive at a rate the business generates, not at a rate the incumbent can choose. The successor's acquisition of the process residual is therefore rate-limited by **exception arrival**, which is a property of the business rather than of the preparation effort.

**P5.** Because exception exposure cannot be purchased, the transfer-rate bound differs in kind from a codification-rate bound rather than only in parameter: money buys codification, and it does not buy elapsed operating time.

Codification responds to effort — hire more people to write more documents and the artifact set grows faster. Exception exposure does not: no quantity of managerial attention makes a rare case arrive sooner. A multi-decade preparation window is therefore a structural feature and not a planning failure. Supporting evidence for the mechanism, though not for any parameter, comes from work showing that transfer difficulty depends on the timing of methods relative to the occurrence of difficulties, so that investment is necessary but does not substitute for elapsed exposure to unpredictable moments of difficulty [@szulanski-2016-overcoming-stickiness-how]. The foundational treatment of transfer stickiness and its predictors sits underneath both claims [@szulanski-1996-exploring-internal-stickiness], as does the canonical statement that the knowledge at issue is held in a form its holder cannot fully articulate [@polanyi-1966-the-tacit-dimension].

P5 is not a new species of constraint, and saying which established one it belongs to is what places it in the conversation rather than beside it. An asset that can only be accumulated through elapsed time, so that a given stock cannot be bought more quickly by spending more, is the time-compression diseconomy of the resource-based tradition [@dierickx-1989-asset-stock-accumulation]. P5 says the successor's capacity to resolve exceptions is such an asset, and it identifies the specific clock: not calendar time, but the firm's own rate of throwing uncovered cases. The same structure appears one level up in the classic constraint on firm growth, where expansion is bounded by existing management's capacity to absorb and train new management, and capital does not relax the bound [@penrose-1959-theory-growth-firm]. That is a non-self ancestor for the claim, and it answers directly the objection that this is tacitness under another name.

Two nearer relatives should be named for the same reason. The mechanism P5 describes — capability moving from one person to another through shared experience rather than through telling — is the socialization mode of the standard model of organizational knowledge creation, which is where tacit-to-tacit transfer is theorized as requiring joint activity over time rather than transmission [@nonaka-1994-dynamic-theory-organizational]. And the empirical instance of the same bound is firm-specific human capital accumulated through learning by doing, which is valuable precisely because it is built rather than bought [@hatch-2004-human-capital-learning]. Neither supplies a parameter for the exception-arrival clock, which remains unmeasured; both establish that the shape of the constraint is not novel here.

P5 is the most exposed link in this chain, and the exposure is declared rather than managed. If transfer duration responds strongly to investment intensity — more contact hours, structured rotation, simulated exception exposure — the bound is soft and "differs in kind" weakens to "differs in parameter."

## The Budget Constraint

### *One person's time.*

The second contribution is a resource-allocation claim, not a claim about knowledge types.

**P6.** Under a finite budget of managerial attention and capital, an owner allocates between converting person-carried residual into artifact — which raises value under removal — and moving person-carried residual into a specific successor, which raises value under replacement. The two draw on the same scarce input, and the second is additionally rate-limited by exception arrival, which the budget cannot relax.

The shared input is the incumbent's own time, and its being shared is a consequence of the population rather than an assumption imposed on it: in an owner-operated firm the person who holds the residual is simultaneously the only person who could document it and the only person who could teach it. That identity does not hold in a large firm, which is precisely why this paper's population is not the executive-succession population.

Codification and personal transfer are therefore **complements as knowledge and rivals as investments**. Both statements are needed. Dropping the first makes the claim false; dropping the second makes it uninteresting.

### *What the vocabulary does and does not supply.*

The distinction between codifying knowledge and moving it through people is named in a well-known practitioner treatment, and this paper adopts that vocabulary [@hansen-1999-whats-your-strategy]. It adopts nothing else from it. That treatment is built on qualitative observation of professional-service firms; it reports no sample, no measure and no quantitative test, and the ratio it is best remembered for is a *recommendation about how a firm should allocate*, not a finding about how firms do. Measured directly in product-development units, firms do not hold that ratio [@kumar-2011-balancing-knowledge-strategy]. That alone demotes it from an observed equilibrium to a recommendation, which is all this paper needs; the stronger companion claim sometimes attached to it — that departing from the ratio carries no performance cost — is a different statement from a null on a moderator and is not relied on here.

This matters more than a citation-hygiene note, because the same empirical literature appears at first glance to refute P6: a style combining high levels of both approaches is reported to yield the highest performance [@choi-2003-empirical-investigation-km-styles]. Those studies sample firms in which the documenter and the teacher are different people — corporate product-development units and large professional practices. The identity that generates the rivalry is absent there by construction. The complementarity findings are therefore not counterexamples to P6; they are evidence for its scope condition, which is stated next. Saying so here, rather than leaving it for a reader to raise, is the difference between a scope condition and a hole.

### *When the trade-off vanishes.*

The trade-off disappears — not weakens — under either of two conditions, and both are observable before the transition.

The first is that the process residual is empty or near-empty, because the codified process already covers ordinary operation. Both events then bind on the same near-empty set. The second is that the budget does not bind: sufficient managerial slack, a deputy who can run operations while the incumbent documents, or a successor whose prior related knowledge lets them absorb more per unit of exposure — absorptive capacity in the standard sense [@cohen-1990-absorptive-capacity-new].

For the first condition the composite that matters is the *product* of documentation coverage and exception rate, not either alone. A heavily documented firm in a high-variance environment still has a live residual; a lightly documented firm in a placid one may not.

## The Rival Account

### *Three observable differences.*

The structural rival is information asymmetry. On that account, succession differs from sale because an internal successor and the incumbent know more about each other than a stranger and the incumbent do. The rival is well developed, and its own results run in the direction that sharpens the disagreement: internal handovers carry *lower* information asymmetry, so on that account succession is the easy case [@dehlen-2014-role-information-asymmetry].

This paper does not rule the rival out. It has no data with which to do so, and a theory paper claiming to have defeated its structural rival without data would deserve the scrutiny it received. What it does instead is state three differences that are observable in principle, so the accounts are distinguishable rather than merely different.

First, zero information asymmetry does not imply zero transfer cost. Information asymmetry is a property of a transaction: it is indexed to a counterparty and to what that counterparty cannot verify. An internal successor who has been told everything tellable still has not *acquired* the capacity to resolve exceptions, because that capacity is built by exposure rather than by disclosure. Communication closes an information gap; it does not close a capacity gap, and the rival account has no term for the difference.

Second, the two accounts predict different remedies, and remedies are documented. Information asymmetry is closable by instruments that move risk without moving capability — disclosure, warranty, escrow, contingent consideration. A capacity constraint is closable by none of them: a contingent payment does not teach anyone anything.

The earn-out is the instructive case rather than another item on that list, and reading it as one item was an error the two-fact partition exposes. An earn-out is two instruments bundled. The contingent payment is a risk-shifting device and belongs above, unchanged: it moves consideration onto a later observable and teaches no one anything. The retention period it enforces is a different thing entirely — it is elapsed operating time with the incumbent present, which is the input that produces exception exposure.

The established reading of the instrument has to be stated before the addition to it, because that reading gets there first and it belongs to the rival. The finance literature treats the earn-out as a response to valuation disagreement under asymmetric information, and carries retention within the same contract rather than as a separate device [@kohers-2000-earnouts-mergers-agreeing]; later work treats contingent earn-outs specifically as a remedy for the information problems that attend acquisitions of privately held targets [@ragozzino-2009-contingent-earnouts-acquisitions]. Nothing below displaces that. What follows adds a second function to one half of the instrument.

**P10.** In the fourth cell of Table 1, where the buyer must acquire the residual personally, the retention half of an earn-out carries a second function alongside risk-shifting: it is a priced purchase of *access* to exposure.

The word *access* is doing necessary work, because P5 says exposure cannot be purchased and P10 says a retention window purchases it, and without the distinction the two are contradictory. They are not. Money cannot compress elapsed exposure time, because the exception-arrival rate is set by the business and not by the contract. Money can buy access to the flow that produces it. A buyer can pay for the incumbent to be present for twelve months; no buyer can pay to make those twelve months into six. P5 denies the second and P10 asserts the first, and the pair is consistent.

Placing that reading in the vocabulary already used for P5 is what keeps it from being an ad hoc rescue. An asset subject to time-compression diseconomies cannot be bought as a stock, because the stock is produced only by elapsed exposure and spending more does not shorten the elapsing [@dierickx-1989-asset-stock-accumulation]. A buyer who needs such an asset and cannot purchase it therefore has one remaining instrument: purchase access to the *flow* that produces it. A retention clause is exactly that purchase — it conveys a fixed quantity of operating time with the incumbent present, which is the input the successor's capacity is accumulated from.

What P10 must not do is treat the prevalence of retention as corroboration, and an earlier statement of this argument did. The inference from "buyers impose retention" to "the capacity constraint is real" affirms the consequent, because at least three other accounts predict the same observation. Retention makes the contingent payment enforceable in the first place, since a seller who leaves cannot be held to an earn-out target, which interlocks the two halves rather than separating them. Customer and relationship continuity predicts it, and that channel is conceded to the rival below. And hold-up over relationship-specific investment predicts retention covenants and staged consideration for reasons independent of both accounts [@williamson-1985-economic-institutions-capitalism]. Observing a retention window therefore discriminates between none of them.

What would discriminate is a contrast between cells rather than the existence of the instrument. Holding the verifiability of the contingent metric fixed, retention windows should be longer where the buyer supplies no operating principal than where the buyer supplies one, because only in the first case must the incoming principal absorb the residual personally; on the risk-shifting reading alone the buyer's operating posture is irrelevant to how long the seller must stay. Both terms of that contrast — window length, and whether the acquirer installed its own principal — are carried by transaction records. An earlier version of this argument proposed instead that window length should scale with the size of the uncodified residual. That test is withdrawn: the size of the uncodified residual is precisely the construct the audit below finds no instrument measures, so a transaction record cannot carry it, and proposing it applied a looser evidentiary standard to this paper's own test than the audit applies to the management-practice surveys.

Third, the two have different cross-sectional gradients. Information asymmetry scales with the counterparty's distance from the firm, so its discount should fall as the counterparty becomes better informed. Transfer capacity scales with the size of the uncodified residual and is invariant to who the counterparty is: the same firm is exactly as hard to hand over to an insider as to a stranger, once the insider has been told everything tellable. The gradients are separable in principle, though not cleanly in practice — an insider is typically also more exposed, so distance and exposure move together in most samples, and separating them is a demand on the design rather than a property of the world.

One correction belongs here, because the adjacent evidence is often stretched. Work on buyouts and the family management dyad establishes that a mutually agreed price is significantly less likely when incumbent management controls the relevant information [@howorth-2004-buyouts-information-asymmetry]. It does not establish that owner-bound knowledge *forces* handover or liquidation regardless of preference. That stronger reading is an interpretive extension and is not relied on here.

### *What this paper concedes.*

The relational residual is genuinely closer to the rival's territory, and it is conceded rather than contested. Revenue continuity through founder relationships is a real second channel, it is well covered by the information and relational-capital literatures, and the design below must *control* for it rather than claim it.

The claim of this paper is about retained productive capability, not about revenue continuity. Stating that in the introduction costs nothing and removes a reader's easiest objection.

## Boundary Objects

### *A gradient in the operating environment.*

The theory as stated predicts a documented phenomenon it was not built to explain. In a study of Italian small and medium-sized firms, the succession penalty is not uniform: it is borne by previously good performers in highly competitive sectors [@cucculelli-2008-family-succession-firm]. The finding is what is relied on here; the specification that produced it is not described, because this paper has not read that specification at the level required to describe it.

The conditionality is what matters, not the average effect. A highly competitive sector generates exceptions at a higher rate, so the person-carried process residual is drawn on more often and its loss bites harder and sooner; and a previously good performer is, on this reading, precisely the firm whose performance depended on that residual being present. The information-asymmetry account has no natural reason to predict a gradient in competitive intensity, because counterparty verifiability is not a function of how competitive a sector is.

### *One magnitude, one direction, and their limits.*

Two further results bear on magnitude and identification without being tests of this mechanism. Only the first supplies a magnitude; the second is directional and is reported as such rather than counted toward the anchor.

Using the gender of the departing chief executive's firstborn child as an instrument for family succession, family successions cause a decline in operating profitability on assets of at least four percentage points around the transition [@bennedsen-2007-inside-family-firm]. This anchors what a succession costs when it goes badly, and it supplies the identification precedent the design borrows. It does not discriminate between the accounts: the rival predicts a decline too.

Under adult adoption, non-consanguineous successors outperform blood heirs [@mehrotra-2013-adoptive-expectations-rising]. This is consistent with selection on the successor's capacity to absorb the residual rather than on family membership — but it holds in one institutional setting, no replication outside it exists, and the features that make the natural experiment work do not travel. It is cited as evidence from a single institutional setting and must never be reported as a general regularity.

A related correction: the canonical result on inherited control concentrates its underperformance in firms whose incoming family chief executive did not attend a selective institution [@perezgonzalez-2006-inherited-control-firm]. The effect is conditional on successor characteristics rather than on family succession as such, which is the part most often mis-cited and the part that makes the foil more interesting than the flat version.

## The Testable Signature

### *The crossover.*

**P8.** Comparing the platform-acquisition cell with the succession cell, the account predicts that documentation coverage predicts realized sale price but not handover disruption, and that successor exposure predicts handover disruption but not sale price. In the fourth cell the second clause fails — there successor exposure plausibly raises the price too, because the buyer is pricing handover risk they are about to bear personally — so P8 is stated over the two cells the derivation runs on, and the confirmatory estimate is the platform-acquisition stratum rather than a pooled exit arm.

The null this must beat is not "no association." It is the single-portfolio account under which one preparation programme raises value under both events — that well-run firms are simply better prepared for everything, and the apparent divergence is an artifact of measuring two outcomes. The estimand is therefore a crossover rather than two main effects. With standardized outcomes within each arm, the quantity of interest is a difference-in-differences of standardized regression coefficients:

$$\delta = (b_{\text{cod},A} - b_{\text{exp},A}) - (b_{\text{cod},B} - b_{\text{exp},B})$$

where $A$ is the exit arm, $B$ the succession arm, $b_{\text{cod}}$ the coefficient on documentation coverage and $b_{\text{exp}}$ the coefficient on successor exposure.

The factor of two is worth showing rather than asserting, because it is the whole reason the contrast is stated this way. Write $\beta$ for the standardized effect of the matching investment within its own arm. P3 says the two events bind on different tiers, so under the account the non-matching investment has no effect within an arm: documentation coverage raises the realized price and successor exposure does not, while successor exposure reduces handover disruption and documentation coverage does not. Then

$$b_{\text{cod},A} = \beta, \quad b_{\text{exp},A} = 0, \quad b_{\text{cod},B} = 0, \quad b_{\text{exp},B} = \beta$$

so the within-arm differences are $\beta$ and $-\beta$, and $\delta = \beta - (-\beta) = 2\beta$. The single-portfolio account makes the opposite structural claim: whatever raises value raises it under both events, so within each arm the two coefficients are equal — $b_{\text{cod},A} = b_{\text{exp},A}$ and $b_{\text{cod},B} = b_{\text{exp},B}$ — each within-arm difference is zero, and $\delta = 0$. Note what that null does *not* require. The common value may be large, it may differ between the arms, and both investments may predict both outcomes strongly; $\delta$ is zero all the same. The contrast is built to be blind to how well-prepared a firm is and sensitive only to whether preparation is route-specific, which is the claim in dispute.

Three assumptions are carried by the shorthand $\delta = 2\beta$ and should be stated where the estimand is defined rather than discovered later. It requires the same standardized $\beta$ in both arms; it requires the non-matching investment's true coefficient to be exactly zero within each arm, which the fourth cell already violates; and reading the power table's per-arm entries as half of a total requires equal per-arm $n$, which no real frame will supply, since sales and successions do not arrive in equal numbers. The estimand itself — the difference of within-arm differences — is defined without the first and third. It is only the factor of two and the doubling rule that depend on them.

### *Companion computation script.*

Power for this contrast was computed by Monte Carlo. The script is published at <https://github.com/spectralbranding/orgschema-papers/tree/main/succession-rotation/code> as `power_divergence_signature.py`, and is run with `uv run python code/power_divergence_signature.py`; the seed is fixed at 20260810 and the output is deterministic, so every figure in Table 2 and every number quoted below reproduces exactly. Each cell is 20,000 replications at a two-sided $\alpha$ of .05. The correlation $\rho$ between the two investments is varied because its sign is not known in advance: the budget constraint pushes it negative, since the investments compete for one person's time, while owner conscientiousness and managerial slack push it positive.

**Table 2.** Smallest Per-Arm Sample Size Reaching Power of .80 for the Crossover Contrast.

| Correlation between investments | $\beta$ = .25 | $\beta$ = .35 | $\beta$ = .45 |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\rho$ = −.30 (investments compete) | 100 | 50 | 40 |
| $\rho$ = .00 | 125 | 75 | 40 |
| $\rho$ = +.30 (common cause) | 200 | 100 | 50 |

*Notes*: Monte Carlo, 20,000 replications per cell, two-sided $\alpha$ = .05. $\beta$ is the standardized effect of the matching investment within its own arm. Entries are per arm; total sample is twice the entry. Entries are the smallest value on the searched grid of 40, 50, 60, 75, 100, 125, 150, 200, 300 and 400 per arm, not the smallest value in principle.

Three readings follow. First, the design is best powered exactly where the theory says the effect exists: the budget constraint predicts $\rho < 0$, which is the favourable row. Where the budget does not bind — the scope condition of the preceding section — $\rho$ is plausibly positive and power falls sharply. Second, at $\rho$ = .00 the crossover test has essentially the power of a single standardized slope at the same per-arm $n$. In the $\beta$ = .25 column the pairs are .352 against .349 at $n$ = 40 and .719 against .720 at $n$ = 100. The equality is asymptotic rather than exact: at $\beta$ = .45 and $n$ = 40 the same comparison is .854 against .865. The contrast is twice the effect and carries four coefficient variances, and the two very nearly cancel. Third, and consequently, the factor of two falls on the *total* sample rather than on the contrast. A target stated as a total is half the design it appears to be.

## The Pre-Registered Design

### *The frame and the window.*

The design is described here as pre-registered, and the sense is worth fixing before the section runs. It is pre-registered *by publication*: the specification below is a public, timestamped, archived record with its own identifier, made before any data exist and unalterable afterwards except by a new version that carries its own date. That is what the commitment rests on, not an entry in a registry, and a reader should hold it to the standard the published record supports.

An event enters the frame when five conditions hold, four of them verifiable from a record rather than from an informant's recollection: the firm was owner-operated before the event; the change in operating principal is dated and both persons are identifiable; the arm assignment is unambiguous, with mixed leveraged handovers that transfer control and ownership together excluded rather than controlled for; and the firm falls in a band where documented process is a meaningful variable and the residual has not already devolved to a management layer the event does not replace, operationalized as ten to two hundred and fifty employees. The fifth condition — that exception resolution is not already fully delegated downward — is not record-verifiable, and saying so matters, because the locus of exception resolution is one of the two quantities the audit below reports no dataset collects. It is carried as a record-verifiable *proxy*, the absence of a separately titled operating manager on the register, with the proxy labelled as one; establishing the condition itself requires an informant, and any frame that does so forfeits the record-verifiability claim for that condition and should say which route it took. The employee band is likewise an operationalization of the construct and not the construct itself, and it should be re-set if a setting makes a different band the one where the owner still resolves exceptions personally.

Both investments are to be measured at $t-1$, a fixed reference date one year before the transition — by an instrument the following section shows does not yet exist, which is the reason this remains a specification. Anything measured at $t$ is contaminated by the transition and anything after it is an outcome. The exit arm's outcome is the realized price at $t$, where event and outcome coincide. The succession arm's outcome runs from $t+1$ to $t+3$, with $t+3$ primary.

Pre-trend controls from $t-3$ to $t-1$ are mandatory rather than a robustness check. The conditionality that makes the competitive-sector result attractive — that the penalty falls on *previously good* performers — is exactly the shape mean reversion produces, and a design omitting the pre-trend will recover mean reversion and report it as a succession effect. Firms that dissolve inside the window are retained, not dropped: dissolution is the maximal-disruption outcome, and dropping it would select on the dependent variable.

### *Identification and its limit.*

No instrument for the succession-versus-exit choice is known to exist, so the design targets the pattern rather than an average causal effect. The surviving threat is selection into preparing for one path rather than the other.

The frame therefore carries a pre-registered stratum of transitions triggered by death or sudden incapacity of the operating owner. There the owner did not choose the timing, so selection into preparation cannot operate: whatever documentation coverage and successor exposure existed, existed for other reasons. Two limits are stated rather than discovered. The stratum is thin and will not reach the sample size of Table 2 by itself, so it bounds rather than replaces the planned stratum. And it is a within-arm instrument: it cleans selection into preparation on the succession side, and largely forecloses the exit arm, so the exit side continues to rest on the matched design.

One moderator is pre-registered rather than left to analysis, and it is owed to the partition above. The frame excludes mixed leveraged handovers, which are management-buyout-shaped events; it does not exclude the fourth cell of Table 1, which is an ordinary arm's-length sale. The exit arm will therefore contain fourth-cell sales in unknown proportion, and in those sales successor exposure plausibly raises the price too, because the buyer is pricing handover risk they are about to bear personally. **Acquirer operating posture** — does the buyer supply an operating principal? — is accordingly pre-registered as a moderator on the exit arm, verifiable from the transaction record rather than from an informant.

Which estimate is confirmatory is fixed here rather than left open, because a moderator added without that designation is a moderator that can change the answer after the fact. The **crossover within the platform-acquisition stratum is the confirmatory estimate**, since that is the comparison the derivation predicts; the pooled crossover across the whole exit arm will be reported as secondary and read as a contaminated version of it. That designation has a sample-size consequence Table 2 does not cover. The platform stratum is a fraction of the exit arm, so the per-arm entries there apply to the stratum and the raw frame must be inflated by the reciprocal of the platform share — if the fourth cell is anything like as common as the preceding section conjectures, the raw exit frame needed is at least twice the table entry.

Three further checks are specified. Externally mandated codification regimes — franchise systems, certified or regulated process regimes — should show the trade-off attenuated or absent, since documentation coverage there is set outside the owner's budget. The divergence should not depend on which operationalization of documentation coverage is used, and an externally recorded proxy such as dated certification scope should recover the pattern the survey measure recovers. And the relational channel conceded above must be measured and controlled, not assumed away.

## Why the Design Cannot Yet Be Run

### *What the instruments measure instead.*

The design above is specified and has not been executed. The reason is not cost or access. It is that neither construct it requires is measured by any instrument the following audit could locate.

The scope of that audit bounds the claim, so it is stated before the result. Candidate frames were enumerated in five families — national company registers, management-practice surveys, transaction and valuation databases, government loan registers, and practitioner survey programmes — and each was assessed at instrument level, by reading the questionnaire or data dictionary rather than a description of it, with the sources that could not be obtained recorded as unobtained rather than assumed empty. What follows is therefore a strong negative over a bounded search, not a proof of non-existence. Two findings emerged, and they are reported as findings rather than as limitations.

The large management-practice instruments descend from a common questionnaire grid, re-fielded across several national statistical programmes. They should be named, because the audit's central negative is only checkable if a reader knows which instruments it covers: the interview grid of the World Management Survey [@bloom-2012-organization-firms-across] and the Management and Organizational Practices Survey re-fielded from it through a national statistical agency [@bloom-2019-what-drives-differences], together with the national management surveys built on the same instrument. Each contains exactly one item touching operational problems, and in every case that item scores the *response mode* to a problem on a short ordinal scale — whether problems are fixed, whether learnings are captured, whether improvement is continuous. None measures what share of ordinary operation is written down. The item closest to the construct is titled for process documentation and scores improvement maturity; reading it as documentation coverage would be a proxy stretch, and it is declined here.

Exception rate is in a worse position. It was located in no firm-level dataset examined, administrative or survey. It is a shop-floor quantity that no statistical agency in the audited set collects, and the absence is uniform enough across families to suggest it is a property of what statistical agencies measure rather than an accident of which ones were reached.

### *Why no pairing works.*

A second obstacle is structural rather than a matter of gaps. The best available exit-side and succession-side sources differ simultaneously in informant, unit of observation, country, selection rule and outcome type. Estimating the exit coefficient from a broker-reported transaction database and the handover coefficient from an administrative establishment panel would compare coefficients whose instruments differ more than the arms do, and the resulting $\delta$ would be confounded with the instrument rather than identified by the design. What the construct wants is a single assessment scored before the owner knows which path they will take, on firms subsequently observed to sell or to hand over.

**P7.** The trade-off has gone untested because neither of its terms is measured by the instruments the field has built. The absence of prior evidence is therefore predicted by the account rather than evidence against it.

This is offered as part of the contribution and not as an apology. A theory whose two terms are invisible to every instrument the field has built is a theory the field could not have tested, and the audit converts "this has not been shown" from an objection into a prediction that the audit confirms. It also names what would have to be built: one instrument, administered before the path is known, measuring documented coverage of ordinary operation and the within-firm rate at which operation throws uncovered cases. The second of those has no precedent to adopt, and this paper does not design it.

## Discussion

### *What follows if the signature holds.*

If the crossover is observed, the practical implication is uncomfortable and specific: an owner cannot defer the choice between the two futures until the exit is in view. The preparation that raises a sale price and the preparation that produces a low-disruption handover are competing claims on one person's calendar, and the second is rate-limited by something no budget relaxes. The advice to "prepare the business" is then not merely vague but wrong in a decidable way, because it implies a single direction of improvement where there are two.

The scope condition is equally practical. Where the process residual is near-empty or the budget does not bind, the trade-off dissolves and undifferentiated preparation advice is correct. Those conditions are observable in advance, which means the theory says which firms need to choose.

### *What follows if it does not.*

If documentation coverage and successor exposure predict both outcomes with the same sign, the single-portfolio account survives and this paper's second contribution fails. The first would not: the persistence column is derived from the ontology rather than from the data, and a null on the crossover would leave it standing while removing the allocation claim built on top of it.

That asymmetry is deliberate. The two contributions are separable, and the empirical risk is concentrated in the second.

## Limitations

The design is specified and not run. Every proposition above is derived; none is tested here. The paper is presented as a specification with its power stated, and it should be read as one.

The exception-arrival bound is the most exposed link. Two parameters underneath it are declared unmeasured rather than estimated: no study measures how contact hours with a successor reduce current firm performance, which is the other half of the budget constraint, and no defensible estimate exists of transfer duration as a function of investment intensity. There is no equation here to calibrate, and none is implied.

Selection into succession versus exit is unbounded outside the sudden-transition stratum, and that stratum bounds only the succession arm.

The exit arm is not homogeneous, and the power table assumes it is. Every cell of Table 2 is computed for an arm in which the matching investment is documentation coverage alone; the fourth cell of Table 1 puts sales in that arm where successor exposure also predicts price, and the crossover attenuates toward zero as their share rises. The direction of *this* bias is the redeeming feature: it works against this paper's own hypothesis, so a positive finding survives it. What it costs is power, and the cost is not quantified, because the attenuation factor is the fourth-cell share and that share is itself unmeasured. The moderator specified in the design measures it rather than assuming it, and the confirmatory estimate is the platform-only stratum for that reason.

Not every known bias here runs that way, and a paper that trades on declaring its biases cannot declare only the flattering one. The two arms carry outcomes of very unequal reliability. The exit arm's outcome is a realized transaction price, measured with near-zero error. The succession arm's outcome is operating disruption over $t+1$ to $t+3$, measured with substantial error. Differential attenuation shrinks the succession arm's coefficients relative to the exit arm's, and since the estimand subtracts the succession-arm contrast from the exit-arm contrast, shrinking the second *inflates* $\delta$. That bias runs toward the hypothesis, not against it, and no reliability estimate exists in advance from which to bound it. Any realized study should estimate outcome reliability within each arm and report the contrast corrected for attenuation alongside the raw one.

The power table is an upper bound for further reasons besides the exit arm's heterogeneity, and they should be named rather than left to be found. The simulation assumes no measurement error in either construct, in a paper whose audit concludes that neither construct is measured at all, so attenuation in any real instrument is certain rather than possible. It assumes no covariates, while the design makes pre-trend controls and a relational-channel control mandatory. It assumes balanced arms, identical standardized effects in the two arms, and a standard-normal rather than a $t$ reference for the test statistic, the last of which is mildly anti-conservative at the smallest sample sizes shown. Each of these pushes the required sample up. The entries in Table 2 are floors.

The group-carried residual conceded at the outset has no consequence anywhere in the design, and that is a limitation rather than an oversight now corrected. Nothing here measures or bounds the share of a firm's operating residual that is held jointly rather than by the departing principal, and no instrument in the audited set separates the two. The concession therefore narrows the claim by an unknown amount, and the honest position is to say so rather than to assume the share is small.

The evidence the argument leans on and the setting it proposes to test in are not the same places. The three documented phenomena come from Italy, Denmark and Japan, chosen because those are where the natural experiments are; the frame most likely to supply dated, identified transitions with an unambiguous sold-versus-retained test is a common-law company register elsewhere. Nothing in the derivation is jurisdiction-specific — draw frequency and a single owner's time do not depend on company law — but the succession-penalty magnitudes quoted above are not transportable to a different institutional setting without argument, and they are used here to anchor scale rather than to predict a coefficient. A design fielded in one country should be powered and interpreted on its own.

Two boundary objects that an earlier version of this argument claimed are absent. The stage-dependence of transfer stickiness is not established for the stage of the recipient *relationship*; the established result concerns stages of the transfer *process*, which is a different variable [@szulanski-2000-process-knowledge-transfer]. And the well-known allocation ratio is a prescription rather than an observed equilibrium, so it is not a phenomenon this theory can claim to explain.

Finally, a measurement risk that applies to any documentary operationalization of documentation coverage: its behaviour on low-activity units is not known in advance, and the error plausibly runs toward scoring an inactive firm as prepared. The composite of coverage and exception rate is the antidote and is fixed in the pre-registration rather than left as an analysis choice. A related test in adjacent work did not confirm the analogous premise it set out to check [@zharnikov-2026an-specification-readiness-empirical], which is the reason this is carried as a design risk rather than as an established effect.
