# Preparing to Sell and Preparing to Hand Over Are Rival Investments: A Persistence Column for the Six-Tier Ontology

Dmitry Zharnikov

ORCID: 0009-0000-6893-9231

DOI: [10.5281/zenodo.21868658](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21868658)

Working Paper v1.0.0 – August 2026

---

## Abstract

An owner preparing to leave a firm faces two futures that are usually treated as one: the firm is sold, or the firm is handed to a successor. This paper derives why the preparation each rewards is different. A six-tier ontology of organizational transferability already states what arrives in a buyer's possession, but that column is indexed to a counterparty, and a succession has none. Decomposing it into three mechanisms of persistence — legal, artifact, and person — yields a second column indexed to the event, under which a sale disturbs legal continuity and prices artifact continuity while a succession disturbs only what a person carries. Among the tiers a succession disturbs, exactly one is drawn at the firm's operating frequency rather than at discretion, so its loss cannot be absorbed by deferral. In an owner-operated firm the holder of that residual is the only person who could document it and the only person who could teach it, making the two preparations rivals for one budget. The paper states the crossover this predicts, supplies a pre-registered design with its power, and reports an instrument-level audit finding neither construct measured in the survey and administrative families examined.

**Keywords**: succession, business transfer, owner-managed firms, knowledge codification, tacit knowledge, organizational tiers, pre-registered design, measurement.

---

An owner who intends to leave a firm is told to prepare it. The advice is usually undifferentiated: document the processes, reduce owner dependence, build a management layer, get the accounts in order. Underneath it sits an assumption that preparation is one activity with one direction of improvement — that a firm made ready to sell is thereby made ready to hand over, and vice versa. This paper argues the assumption is false, derives why, and specifies the study that would test it.

The setting is deliberately narrow. The firm is owner-*operated*: the outgoing principal both holds the controlling stake and is the person who runs the business. The event is *replacement* rather than removal — the occupant of the top tier is replaced, the registered entity persists, and nothing is conveyed to any counterparty. Where the owner does not operate, the constraint this paper identifies is simply absent, because the operating residual sits with managers whom the event does not replace. That boundary is not a caveat added at the end; it is what makes the argument go through, and it belongs in the first paragraph.

The vehicle is an existing decomposition of the firm into six specification tiers — owner intent, business model, business entity, product, process, organization — each governed by a distinct question and each transferring differently on sale [@zharnikov-2026-dual-hierarchies-organizational-transferability-six]. A companion result models the migration of signal from a founder-bound tier into an institutionally separable one as a continuous curve with a discontinuity in acquisition value, and explicitly defers succession as "a geometrically distinct event" [@zharnikov-2026ai-tier-rotation-curve]. This paper takes up that deferral. It does not inherit evidentiary weight from either predecessor; both supply structure, and every empirical claim below is either derived, cited to an independently verified external record, or declared unmeasured.

The premise that leaving is not one event is not new, and the paper should say whose it is. Entrepreneurial exit is established as a constitutive part of the entrepreneurial process rather than its termination [@detienne-2010-entrepreneurial-exit-critical], and exit is established as a set of *divergent routes* with different drivers rather than a single act [@wennberg-2010-reconceptualizing-entrepreneurial-exit]. That literature asks which route an owner takes and what predicts the choice. This paper asks a question it leaves open: given that the routes diverge, what does *preparing* for one do to readiness for the other? The routes literature treats preparation as largely undifferentiated antecedent; the claim here is that preparation is where the divergence originates, and that the two routes make competing claims on the same input before the owner has chosen between them.

Two contributions follow, and there are two rather than three. The first is a second column for the existing ontology. The second is a resource-allocation trade-off under a budget constraint. A third candidate — that the transfer-rate bound differs in kind from a codification-rate bound — turns out to be a corollary of the second rather than an independent claim, and is presented as one.

## The Persistence Column

### *Two columns, not one.*

The six-tier ontology carries a column headed *what transfers*. Read carefully, it is a **transaction** column: every entry answers the question *if this firm changed hands, what would arrive in the buyer's possession?* Nothing directly transfers from owner intent, only its imprint on lower tiers; the business entity transfers by document; the product transfers if specified and not if tacit; process transfers if codified; organization transfers partially at best.

That is the right column for an event whose defining feature is a counterparty. A succession asks a different question of the same stack: *if the occupant of the top tier is replaced and nothing is conveyed, what stops working?* Call the answer the **persistence column**.

**P1.** The published transfer column is indexed to a counterparty, so an event with no counterparty requires its own column — one that is derivable from the same primitives rather than posited alongside them.

This is a claim about the ontology before it is a claim about succession, and that is what makes the first contribution a result rather than a redescription. The move is not "succession is different because tacit knowledge is hard." It is that the existing apparatus has one column where it needs two, and the second is recoverable from the first.

P1 is falsifiable in a way worth stating. If, for every tier, what a buyer would receive coincides with what survives a replacement, the second column is redundant and this contribution collapses. The remainder of this section shows the two columns disagree.

### *Three modes of persistence.*

Every tier survives a control event by one of three mechanisms. They are mutually exclusive, and they are exhaustive over this stack in a sense worth stating precisely rather than asserting: a tier persists either because an institution outlives the event, because an inspectable object does, or because a person does. The classification is by *carrier*, and those three carriers are the only ones the six-tier ontology admits.

One boundary of that claim should be conceded now. A residual distributed across several people, held by none of them individually, is not cleanly person-carried in the sense used here — it is closer to the organization tier, and the account treats it as such. If a substantial part of what a firm needs to keep operating is group-carried in that way, the analysis below applies to a smaller share of the firm than it appears to.

**Legal continuity (L).** The tier is carried by the registered entity and survives any event in which the entity is not conveyed. The business entity by construction; the contracted part of the business model.

**Artifact continuity (A).** The tier is carried by an inspectable object existing independent of any particular person — a specification, a document, a codebase, a standard operating procedure. The specified part of the product; the codified part of process.

**Person continuity (P).** The tier is carried in the state of a particular human being and does not survive that person's departure. Owner intent by definition; the tacit part of the product; the uncodified part of process; organization in the strong sense of the *particular* people.

Nothing here is new. The published transfer column is exactly the statement that L and A arrive in a buyer's possession and P does not. It is decomposed this way so that the second column can be derived from the same primitives rather than asserted beside them.

## Which Tier Binds

### *What replacement leaves intact.*

**P2.** A tier whose persistence mechanism is undisturbed by an event cannot be the binding constraint on value retained through that event.

This is close to definitional and it does most of the work: a constraint binds only where the event removes something. Applied to replacement with the entity persisting, it eliminates almost everything. The business entity is not disturbed — no assignment occurs, the contracts are the same contracts, registrations do not lapse. The business model is largely undisturbed, being instantiated in live contracts, prices and channels. The product is not disturbed at the event: the delivered specification is the same specification, and its tacit part constrains *changing* the product rather than continuing to ship it. Organization is not disturbed at the event either — the other people are not replaced, and their departure in response is a second-order effect and a scope condition rather than the constraint. Owner intent is not a constraint at all here; it is the treatment.

Run the same test against removal, where the entity is conveyed, and a different set lights up. The entity's legal mechanism is precisely what is disturbed: assignment occurs, change-of-control provisions fire, standing has to be re-established. The product's artifact component is what the buyer is paying for, so its presence or absence sets the price. And every person-carried component is written off identically, because no buyer receives the occupant under any preparation programme.

**P3.** Removal disturbs legal continuity and prices artifact continuity; replacement disturbs only person continuity. The two events therefore bind on different tiers.

Under removal the person-carried column is not a constraint to be relieved but a quantity to be minimized. Under replacement it is the only thing at risk. The divergence result thus arrives as a corollary of P2 rather than as a second claim, which is strictly stronger than asserting it.

### *Draw frequency, not tacitness.*

P2 narrows the field to the person-carried residuals but does not single one out: the product has a tacit part, the business model a relational part, and organization is person-carried throughout. The discriminating property is not tacitness — which several tiers share — but **draw frequency**: how often the residual must actually be exercised for the firm to keep operating.

The product's tacit residual is drawn at discretion. Continuation does not require re-deriving the specification; it requires not changing it. A successor who knows nothing about why the product is as it is can continue shipping it. The residual binds on the firm's ability to *evolve* the specification, which is a real constraint and a deferrable one.

The business model's relational residual is drawn at intervals and is re-formable by introduction. Renewals, negotiations and referrals arrive on a contract calendar, and the standard instrument — introducing the successor before the handover — addresses it directly.

Organization's residual is not replaced by this event at all. It becomes live only if the surviving people leave.

Process is different. Its uncodified residual is drawn continuously, at the rate the firm operates. Production happens every day, and every deviation, exception, escalation or judgment call the codified process does not cover must be resolved by a person, now, or output degrades now. This residual cannot be deferred past the transition, because the transition does not pause the business.

**P4.** Process is the unique tier whose person-carried residual is both destroyed by replacement in the owner-operated population and exercised at operating frequency, so that its loss cannot be absorbed by deferring anything.

What P4 does not claim is worth stating plainly, because a reader who knows the knowledge-transfer literature will expect the tacitness argument and should be told immediately that this is not it. P4 does not claim process knowledge is more tacit than product knowledge, or harder to articulate, or more valuable. Tacitness is shared. Draw frequency is not.

A reader in organization theory will also want to know how the process residual relates to routines, which is the field's standing unit for how work actually gets done [@pentland-2005-organizational-routines-as]. The relation is close and the difference is the point. Routines are internally variable: the performative side of a routine is where participants improvise against the ostensive pattern, and that variation is a source of change rather than noise [@feldman-2003-reconceptualizing-organizational-routines]. The residual named here is precisely the part of that performance the ostensive pattern does not fix — what the documented procedure leaves to judgment. So the claim is not that routines are person-carried, which would be too strong, but that the *unfixed* share of them is, and that in an owner-operated firm the person supplying it is the one being replaced.

### *The rate limit.*

The same property delivers what might otherwise be listed as a third contribution.

Capacity to resolve novel exceptions is built by exposure to exceptions. Exceptions arrive at a rate the business generates, not at a rate the incumbent can choose. The successor's acquisition of the process residual is therefore rate-limited by **exception arrival**, which is a property of the business rather than of the preparation effort.

**P5.** Because exception exposure cannot be purchased, the transfer-rate bound differs in kind from a codification-rate bound rather than only in parameter: money buys codification, and it does not buy elapsed operating time.

Codification responds to effort — hire more people to write more documents and the artifact set grows faster. Exception exposure does not: no quantity of managerial attention makes a rare case arrive sooner. A multi-decade preparation window is therefore a structural feature and not a planning failure. Supporting evidence for the mechanism, though not for any parameter, comes from work showing that transfer difficulty depends on the timing of methods relative to the occurrence of difficulties, so that investment is necessary but does not substitute for elapsed exposure to unpredictable moments of difficulty [@szulanski-2016-overcoming-stickiness-how]. The foundational treatment of transfer stickiness and its predictors sits underneath both claims [@szulanski-1996-exploring-internal-stickiness], as does the canonical statement that the knowledge at issue is held in a form its holder cannot fully articulate [@polanyi-1966-the-tacit-dimension].

P5 is not a new species of constraint, and saying which established one it belongs to is what places it in the conversation rather than beside it. An asset that can only be accumulated through elapsed time, so that a given stock cannot be bought more quickly by spending more, is the time-compression diseconomy of the resource-based tradition [@dierickx-1989-asset-stock-accumulation]. P5 says the successor's capacity to resolve exceptions is such an asset, and it identifies the specific clock: not calendar time, but the firm's own rate of throwing uncovered cases. The same structure appears one level up in the classic constraint on firm growth, where expansion is bounded by existing management's capacity to absorb and train new management, and capital does not relax the bound [@penrose-1959-theory-growth-firm]. That is a non-self ancestor for the claim, and it answers directly the objection that this is tacitness under another name.

P5 is the most exposed link in this chain, and the exposure is declared rather than managed. If transfer duration responds strongly to investment intensity — more contact hours, structured rotation, simulated exception exposure — the bound is soft and "differs in kind" weakens to "differs in parameter."

## The Budget Constraint

### *One person's time.*

The second contribution is a resource-allocation claim, not a claim about knowledge types.

**P6.** Under a finite budget of managerial attention and capital, an owner allocates between converting person-carried residual into artifact — which raises value under removal — and moving person-carried residual into a specific successor, which raises value under replacement. The two draw on the same scarce input, and the second is additionally rate-limited by exception arrival, which the budget cannot relax.

The shared input is the incumbent's own time, and its being shared is a consequence of the population rather than an assumption imposed on it: in an owner-operated firm the person who holds the residual is simultaneously the only person who could document it and the only person who could teach it. That identity does not hold in a large firm, which is precisely why this paper's population is not the executive-succession population.

Codification and personal transfer are therefore **complements as knowledge and rivals as investments**. Both statements are needed. Dropping the first makes the claim false; dropping the second makes it uninteresting.

### *What the vocabulary does and does not supply.*

The distinction between codifying knowledge and moving it through people is named in a well-known practitioner treatment, and this paper adopts that vocabulary [@hansen-1999-whats-your-strategy]. It adopts nothing else from it. That treatment is built on qualitative observation of professional-service firms; it reports no sample, no measure and no quantitative test, and the ratio it is best remembered for is a *recommendation about how a firm should allocate*, not a finding about how firms do. Measured directly in product-development units, firms do not hold that ratio, and departing from it does not degrade performance [@kumar-2011-balancing-knowledge-strategy].

This matters more than a citation-hygiene note, because the same empirical literature appears at first glance to refute P6: a style combining high levels of both approaches is reported to yield the highest performance [@choi-2003-empirical-investigation-km-styles]. Those studies sample firms in which the documenter and the teacher are different people — corporate product-development units and large professional practices. The identity that generates the rivalry is absent there by construction. The complementarity findings are therefore not counterexamples to P6; they are evidence for its scope condition, which is stated next. Saying so here, rather than leaving it for a reader to raise, is the difference between a scope condition and a hole.

### *When the trade-off vanishes.*

The trade-off disappears — not weakens — under either of two conditions, and both are observable before the transition.

The first is that the process residual is empty or near-empty, because the codified process already covers ordinary operation. Both events then bind on the same near-empty set. The second is that the budget does not bind: sufficient managerial slack, a deputy who can run operations while the incumbent documents, or a successor whose prior related knowledge lets them absorb more per unit of exposure — absorptive capacity in the standard sense [@cohen-1990-absorptive-capacity-new].

For the first condition the composite that matters is the *product* of documentation coverage and exception rate, not either alone. A heavily documented firm in a high-variance environment still has a live residual; a lightly documented firm in a placid one may not.

## The Rival Account

### *Three observable differences.*

The structural rival is information asymmetry. On that account, succession differs from sale because an internal successor and the incumbent know more about each other than a stranger and the incumbent do. The rival is well developed, and its own results run in the direction that sharpens the disagreement: internal handovers carry *lower* information asymmetry, so on that account succession is the easy case [@dehlen-2014-role-information-asymmetry].

This paper does not rule the rival out. It has no data with which to do so, and a theory paper claiming to have defeated its structural rival without data would deserve the scrutiny it received. What it does instead is state three differences that are observable in principle, so the accounts are distinguishable rather than merely different.

First, zero information asymmetry does not imply zero transfer cost. Information asymmetry is a property of a transaction: it is indexed to a counterparty and to what that counterparty cannot verify. An internal successor who has been told everything tellable still has not *acquired* the capacity to resolve exceptions, because that capacity is built by exposure rather than by disclosure. Communication closes an information gap; it does not close a capacity gap, and the rival account has no term for the difference.

Second, the two accounts predict different remedies, and remedies are documented. Information asymmetry is closable by instruments that move risk without moving capability — disclosure, warranty, escrow, earn-out, contingent consideration. A capacity constraint is closable by none of them: an earn-out does not teach anyone anything.

Third, the two have different cross-sectional gradients. Information asymmetry scales with the counterparty's distance from the firm, so its discount should fall as the counterparty becomes better informed. Transfer capacity scales with the size of the uncodified residual and is invariant to who the counterparty is: the same firm is exactly as hard to hand over to an insider as to a stranger, once the insider has been told everything tellable. The gradients are separable in principle, though not cleanly in practice — an insider is typically also more exposed, so distance and exposure move together in most samples, and separating them is a demand on the design rather than a property of the world.

One correction belongs here, because the adjacent evidence is often stretched. Work on buyouts and the family management dyad establishes that a mutually agreed price is significantly less likely when incumbent management controls the relevant information [@howorth-2004-buyouts-information-asymmetry]. It does not establish that owner-bound knowledge *forces* handover or liquidation regardless of preference. That stronger reading is an interpretive extension and is not relied on here.

### *What this paper concedes.*

The relational residual is genuinely closer to the rival's territory, and it is conceded rather than contested. Revenue continuity through founder relationships is a real second channel, it is well covered by the information and relational-capital literatures, and the design below must *control* for it rather than claim it.

The claim of this paper is about retained productive capability, not about revenue continuity. Stating that in the introduction costs nothing and removes a reader's easiest objection.

## Boundary Objects

### *A gradient in the operating environment.*

The theory as stated predicts a documented phenomenon it was not built to explain. In a study of Italian small and medium-sized firms with mean-reversion controls and family-size instruments, the succession penalty is not uniform: it is borne by previously good performers in highly competitive sectors [@cucculelli-2008-family-succession-firm].

The conditionality is what matters, not the average effect. A highly competitive sector generates exceptions at a higher rate, so the person-carried process residual is drawn on more often and its loss bites harder and sooner; and a previously good performer is, on this reading, precisely the firm whose performance depended on that residual being present. The information-asymmetry account has no natural reason to predict a gradient in competitive intensity, because counterparty verifiability is not a function of how competitive a sector is.

### *Two anchors and their limits.*

Two further results anchor magnitude and identification without being tests of this mechanism.

Using the gender of the departing chief executive's firstborn child as an instrument for family succession, family successions cause a decline in operating profitability on assets of at least four percentage points around the transition [@bennedsen-2007-inside-family-firm]. This anchors what a succession costs when it goes badly, and it supplies the identification precedent the design borrows. It does not discriminate between the accounts: the rival predicts a decline too.

Under adult adoption, non-consanguineous successors outperform blood heirs [@mehrotra-2013-adoptive-expectations-rising]. This is consistent with selection on the successor's capacity to absorb the residual rather than on family membership — but it holds in one institutional setting, no replication outside it exists, and the features that make the natural experiment work do not travel. It is cited as evidence from a single institutional setting and must never be reported as a general regularity.

A related correction: the canonical result on inherited control concentrates its underperformance in firms whose incoming family chief executive did not attend a selective institution [@perezgonzalez-2006-inherited-control-firm]. The effect is conditional on successor characteristics rather than on family succession as such, which is the part most often mis-cited and the part that makes the foil more interesting than the flat version.

## The Testable Signature

### *The crossover.*

**P8.** The account predicts that documentation coverage predicts realized sale price but not handover disruption, and that successor exposure predicts handover disruption but not sale price.

The null this must beat is not "no association." It is the single-portfolio account under which one preparation programme raises value under both events — that well-run firms are simply better prepared for everything, and the apparent divergence is an artifact of measuring two outcomes. The estimand is therefore a crossover rather than two main effects. With standardized outcomes within each arm, the quantity of interest is a difference-in-differences of standardized regression coefficients:

$$\delta = (b_{\text{cod},A} - b_{\text{exp},A}) - (b_{\text{cod},B} - b_{\text{exp},B})$$

where $A$ is the exit arm, $B$ the succession arm, $b_{\text{cod}}$ the coefficient on documentation coverage and $b_{\text{exp}}$ the coefficient on successor exposure. Under the derivation $\delta = 2\beta$; under the single-portfolio account $\delta = 0$.

### *Companion computation script.*

Power for this contrast was computed by Monte Carlo. The script is published at <https://github.com/spectralbranding/orgschema-papers/tree/main/succession-rotation/code> as `power_divergence_signature.py`, and is run with `uv run python code/power_divergence_signature.py`; the seed is fixed at 20260810 and the output is deterministic, so every figure in Table 1 and every number quoted below reproduces exactly. Each cell is 20,000 replications at a two-sided $\alpha$ of .05. The correlation $\rho$ between the two investments is varied because its sign is not known in advance: the budget constraint pushes it negative, since the investments compete for one person's time, while owner conscientiousness and managerial slack push it positive.

Three readings follow. First, the design is best powered exactly where the theory says the effect exists: the budget constraint predicts $\rho < 0$, which is the favourable row. Where the budget does not bind — the scope condition of the preceding section — $\rho$ is plausibly positive and power falls sharply. Second, at $\rho$ = .00 the crossover test has exactly the power of a single standardized slope at the same per-arm $n$: .352 against .349 at $n$ = 40, and .719 against .720 at $n$ = 100. The contrast is twice the effect and carries four coefficient variances, and the two cancel. Third, and consequently, the factor of two falls on the *total* sample rather than on the contrast. A target stated as a total is half the design it appears to be.

## The Pre-Registered Design

### *The frame and the window.*

An event enters the frame when five conditions hold, each verifiable from a record rather than from an informant's recollection: the firm was owner-operated before the event; the change in operating principal is dated and both persons are identifiable; the arm assignment is unambiguous, with mixed leveraged handovers that transfer control and ownership together excluded rather than controlled for; the firm falls in a band where documented process is a meaningful variable and the residual has not already devolved to a management layer the event does not replace, operationalized as ten to two hundred and fifty employees; and exception resolution is not already fully delegated downward. The employee band is an operationalization of the construct and not the construct itself, and it should be re-set if a setting makes a different band the one where the owner still resolves exceptions personally.

Both investments are measured at $t-1$, a fixed reference date one year before the transition. Anything measured at $t$ is contaminated by the transition and anything after it is an outcome. The exit arm's outcome is the realized price at $t$, where event and outcome coincide. The succession arm's outcome runs from $t+1$ to $t+3$, with $t+3$ primary.

Pre-trend controls from $t-3$ to $t-1$ are mandatory rather than a robustness check. The conditionality that makes the competitive-sector result attractive — that the penalty falls on *previously good* performers — is exactly the shape mean reversion produces, and a design omitting the pre-trend will recover mean reversion and report it as a succession effect. Firms that dissolve inside the window are retained, not dropped: dissolution is the maximal-disruption outcome, and dropping it would select on the dependent variable.

### *Identification and its limit.*

No instrument for the succession-versus-exit choice is known to exist, so the design targets the pattern rather than an average causal effect. The surviving threat is selection into preparing for one path rather than the other.

The frame therefore carries a pre-registered stratum of transitions triggered by death or sudden incapacity of the operating owner. There the owner did not choose the timing, so selection into preparation cannot operate: whatever documentation coverage and successor exposure existed, existed for other reasons. Two limits are stated rather than discovered. The stratum is thin and will not reach the sample size of Table 1 by itself, so it bounds rather than replaces the planned stratum. And it is a within-arm instrument: it cleans selection into preparation on the succession side, and largely forecloses the exit arm, so the exit side continues to rest on the matched design.

Three further checks are specified. Externally mandated codification regimes — franchise systems, certified or regulated process regimes — should show the trade-off attenuated or absent, since documentation coverage there is set outside the owner's budget. The divergence should not depend on which operationalization of documentation coverage is used, and an externally recorded proxy such as dated certification scope should recover the pattern the survey measure recovers. And the relational channel conceded above must be measured and controlled, not assumed away.

## Why the Design Cannot Yet Be Run

### *What the instruments measure instead.*

The design above is specified and has not been executed. The reason is not cost or access. It is that neither construct it requires is measured by any instrument the following audit could locate.

The scope of that audit bounds the claim, so it is stated before the result. Candidate frames were enumerated in five families — national company registers, management-practice surveys, transaction and valuation databases, government loan registers, and practitioner survey programmes — and each was assessed at instrument level, by reading the questionnaire or data dictionary rather than a description of it, with the sources that could not be obtained recorded as unobtained rather than assumed empty. What follows is therefore a strong negative over a bounded search, not a proof of non-existence. Two findings emerged, and they are reported as findings rather than as limitations.

The large management-practice instruments descend from a common questionnaire grid, re-fielded across several national statistical programmes. Each contains exactly one item touching operational problems, and in every case that item scores the *response mode* to a problem on a short ordinal scale — whether problems are fixed, whether learnings are captured, whether improvement is continuous. None measures what share of ordinary operation is written down. The item closest to the construct is titled for process documentation and scores improvement maturity; reading it as documentation coverage would be a proxy stretch, and it is declined here.

Exception rate is in a worse position. It was located in no firm-level dataset examined, administrative or survey. It is a shop-floor quantity that no statistical agency in the audited set collects, and the absence is uniform enough across families to suggest it is a property of what statistical agencies measure rather than an accident of which ones were reached.

### *Why no pairing works.*

A second obstacle is structural rather than a matter of gaps. The best available exit-side and succession-side sources differ simultaneously in informant, unit of observation, country, selection rule and outcome type. Estimating the exit coefficient from a broker-reported transaction database and the handover coefficient from an administrative establishment panel would compare coefficients whose instruments differ more than the arms do, and the resulting $\delta$ would be confounded with the instrument rather than identified by the design. What the construct wants is a single assessment scored before the owner knows which path they will take, on firms subsequently observed to sell or to hand over.

**P7.** The trade-off has gone untested because neither of its terms is measured by the instruments the field has built. The absence of prior evidence is therefore predicted by the account rather than evidence against it.

This is offered as part of the contribution and not as an apology. A theory whose two terms are invisible to every instrument the field has built is a theory the field could not have tested, and the audit converts "this has not been shown" from an objection into a prediction that the audit confirms. It also names what would have to be built: one instrument, administered before the path is known, measuring documented coverage of ordinary operation and the within-firm rate at which operation throws uncovered cases. The second of those has no precedent to adopt, and this paper does not design it.

## Discussion

### *What follows if the signature holds.*

If the crossover is observed, the practical implication is uncomfortable and specific: an owner cannot defer the choice between the two futures until the exit is in view. The preparation that raises a sale price and the preparation that produces a low-disruption handover are competing claims on one person's calendar, and the second is rate-limited by something no budget relaxes. The advice to "prepare the business" is then not merely vague but wrong in a decidable way, because it implies a single direction of improvement where there are two.

The scope condition is equally practical. Where the process residual is near-empty or the budget does not bind, the trade-off dissolves and undifferentiated preparation advice is correct. Those conditions are observable in advance, which means the theory says which firms need to choose.

### *What follows if it does not.*

If documentation coverage and successor exposure predict both outcomes with the same sign, the single-portfolio account survives and this paper's second contribution fails. The first would not: the persistence column is derived from the ontology rather than from the data, and a null on the crossover would leave it standing while removing the allocation claim built on top of it.

That asymmetry is deliberate. The two contributions are separable, and the empirical risk is concentrated in the second.

## Limitations

The design is specified and not run. Every proposition above is derived; none is tested here. The paper is presented as a specification with its power stated, and it should be read as one.

The exception-arrival bound is the most exposed link. Two parameters underneath it are declared unmeasured rather than estimated: no study measures how contact hours with a successor reduce current firm performance, which is the other half of the budget constraint, and no defensible estimate exists of transfer duration as a function of investment intensity. There is no equation here to calibrate, and none is implied.

Selection into succession versus exit is unbounded outside the sudden-transition stratum, and that stratum bounds only the succession arm.

The evidence the argument leans on and the setting it proposes to test in are not the same places. The three documented phenomena come from Italy, Denmark and Japan, chosen because those are where the natural experiments are; the frame most likely to supply dated, identified transitions with an unambiguous sold-versus-retained test is a common-law company register elsewhere. Nothing in the derivation is jurisdiction-specific — draw frequency and a single owner's time do not depend on company law — but the succession-penalty magnitudes quoted above are not transportable to a different institutional setting without argument, and they are used here to anchor scale rather than to predict a coefficient. A design fielded in one country should be powered and interpreted on its own.

Two boundary objects that an earlier version of this argument claimed are absent. The stage-dependence of transfer stickiness is not established for the stage of the recipient *relationship*; the established result concerns stages of the transfer *process*, which is a different variable [@szulanski-2000-process-knowledge-transfer]. And the well-known allocation ratio is a prescription rather than an observed equilibrium, so it is not a phenomenon this theory can claim to explain.

Finally, a measurement risk that applies to any documentary operationalization of documentation coverage: its behaviour on low-activity units is not known in advance, and the error plausibly runs toward scoring an inactive firm as prepared. The composite of coverage and exception rate is the antidote and is fixed in the pre-registration rather than left as an analysis choice. A related test in adjacent work did not confirm the analogous premise it set out to check [@zharnikov-2026an-specification-readiness-empirical], which is the reason this is carried as a design risk rather than as an established effect.

It has a fixed seed (20260810), no network or credential dependency, and no input beyond its own parameter grid, so the reported figures reproduce exactly from a clean checkout.

**Data.** There is none, and the absence is the paper's own subject rather than an omission.
