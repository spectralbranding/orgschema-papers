# Structural sub-dossier — CH2M HILL Companies, Ltd. acquisition by Jacobs Engineering Group Inc. (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Transaction and parties
- On August 1, 2017, Jacobs Engineering Group Inc. ("Jacobs") entered into an Agreement
  and Plan of Merger with CH2M HILL Companies, Ltd. ("CH2M") and Basketball Merger Sub
  Inc., a Delaware corporation and wholly-owned direct subsidiary of Jacobs ("Merger
  Sub"), pursuant to which Merger Sub would merge with and into CH2M, with CH2M
  continuing as the surviving corporation and becoming a wholly-owned direct subsidiary
  of Jacobs. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-338957, DEFM14A cover letter]
- CH2M is described as "a large employee-controlled professional engineering services
  firm, founded in 1946, providing engineering, construction, consulting, design,
  design-build, procurement, engineering-procurement-construction, operations and
  maintenance, program management and technical services" to U.S. federal, state,
  municipal and local government agencies, national governments, private industry and
  utilities; a substantial portion of its professional fees were derived from projects
  funded directly or indirectly by government entities; CH2M was a private company and
  neither its common stock nor its Series A Preferred Stock was publicly traded. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-338957, DEFM14A "Summary"]
- At the effective time, each outstanding share of CH2M common stock would convert, at
  the holder's election, into one of: (i) $52.85 in cash plus 0.6677 shares of Jacobs
  common stock; (ii) $88.08 in cash; or (iii) 1.6693 shares of Jacobs common stock,
  without interest; holders who did not elect would receive the mixed consideration. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-338957, DEFM14A "Summary"]
- It was anticipated that, upon closing, CH2M's stockholders would own approximately 15%
  of Jacobs common stock on a fully-diluted basis. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-338957, DEFM14A "Summary"]
- Apollo (owner of 100% of CH2M's Series A Preferred Stock, convertible into
  approximately 17.94% of CH2M common stock as of the record date) entered into a Voting
  Agreement with Jacobs agreeing to vote in favor of the merger proposal and against
  competing proposals. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-338957, DEFM14A "Summary"]

## Jacobs' stated rationale (controlling intent, as disclosed at signing)
- Jacobs' board cited, among other factors, the complementary nature of CH2M's business
  (expertise in water, transportation, environmental and nuclear sectors) with Jacobs'
  business; the expectation the merger would expand Jacobs' capabilities in higher-growth,
  higher-margin areas; anticipated cost savings and cross-selling opportunities between
  the two companies' offerings; and management's integration plan for combining Jacobs
  and CH2M. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-338957, DEFM14A "Jacobs' Reasons for the Merger"]
- The filing separately disclosed, as a closing-era risk factor, that realizing the
  anticipated benefits would depend on Jacobs' ability to integrate CH2M's business and
  operations, and listed "difficulties assimilating the operations and personnel of
  CH2M into Jacobs' operations" among the risks to that integration. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-338957, DEFM14A "Risk Factors"]

## What crossed at closing, per tier
- Legal vehicle: the entire corporate entity — not a carved-out business unit — was the
  transaction's subject; Merger Sub merged into CH2M, and CH2M continued as the surviving
  corporation and a wholly-owned direct subsidiary of Jacobs. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-338957, DEFM14A cover letter]
- Product/customer-facing asset: CH2M's engineering, construction, consulting,
  design-build, procurement, operations-and-maintenance and program-management services
  to government and private-industry clients were the acquired asset, described as
  complementary to and cross-sellable with Jacobs' existing service lines. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-338957, DEFM14A "Summary" / "Jacobs' Reasons for the Merger"]
- People/organization: the Merger Agreement's employee provisions required Jacobs to
  provide, to non-union "Continuing Employees," cash compensation (base salary,
  commission, target bonus opportunity) at least as favorable as immediately prior to
  the effective time, and either continuation of CH2M's benefit plans or participation
  in Jacobs' benefit plans with service credit and paid-time-off balances carried over;
  employees covered by a collective bargaining agreement remained governed by that CBA.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-338957, DEFM14A "The Merger Agreement — Employee Benefits"]
- Directors/officers: all members of CH2M's board of directors tendered resignations
  effective as of the effective time, and CH2M's then-CEO (Jacqueline Hinman), CFO (Gary
  L. McArthur) and General Counsel (Thomas M. McCoy) ceased to be officers of the
  company; the directors of Merger Sub (Steven Demetriou, Robert Pragada, Vinayak Pai,
  Gary Mandel and Terry Hagen) became the directors of CH2M, and new officers were
  appointed. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-17-073715, Form 8-K Item 5.02]
- Board composition (Jacobs level): prior to closing, Jacobs increased its ten-member
  board by one seat to be filled by a CH2M director who qualified as an "independent
  director" under NYSE rules; Jacobs' then-current executive officers were "currently
  expected to remain unchanged" after the merger. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-338957, DEFM14A "Summary"]
- Charter/bylaws: on December 15, 2017, CH2M amended and restated its certificate of
  incorporation pursuant to the Merger Agreement, and the bylaws of Merger Sub in effect
  immediately before the effective time became CH2M's bylaws. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-17-073715, Form 8-K Item 5.03]
- No transition-services, shared-services, or supply agreement bridging a carved-out
  shared substrate is described in the DEFM14A; unlike a spin-off/carve-out, this was a
  whole-company merger in which CH2M's outstanding indebtedness was expected to be
  repaid or otherwise satisfied and discharged at the effective time rather than
  continued as a standalone obligation. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-338957, DEFM14A "Financing of the Merger"]

## Financing structure
- Jacobs entered a commitment letter for a three-year senior unsecured delayed-draw term
  loan facility, later increased from $1.2 billion to $1.5 billion, to help fund the
  cash component of the merger consideration and the repayment of CH2M's outstanding
  indebtedness. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-338957, DEFM14A "Financing of the Merger"]
- Jacobs estimated the aggregate principal amount of indebtedness to be incurred in
  connection with the merger at approximately $1.9 billion. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-338957, DEFM14A "Financing of the Merger"]

## Regulatory process and termination-fee terms
- HSR Act notifications were filed on August 15, 2017; Jacobs voluntarily withdrew and
  refiled its notification (effective September 14 and September 18, 2017 respectively)
  to extend the review period; early termination of the HSR waiting period was granted
  October 18, 2017. Canadian Competition Bureau clearance (no-action letter) issued
  September 25, 2017; the European Commission's Phase I review period ended October 25,
  2017. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-338957, DEFM14A "Regulatory Approvals Required for the Merger"]
- CH2M would owe Jacobs a termination fee of $85,444,783.80 (less any previously paid
  Expense Fee) under specified circumstances, including termination to accept a superior
  proposal; CH2M's reimbursement of Jacobs' documented expenses was capped at $15.0
  million if CH2M stockholders did not approve the merger. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-338957, DEFM14A "Transaction Expenses; Termination Fee and Expense Fee"]

## Closing (consummation)
- CH2M's Special Meeting was held December 13, 2017 at CH2M's Englewood, Colorado
  headquarters to vote on the merger proposal. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-338957, DEFM14A cover letter]
- On December 15, 2017, pursuant to the Merger Agreement, the merger was consummated:
  CH2M became a direct, wholly-owned subsidiary of Jacobs. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-17-073715, Form 8-K Item 2.01]
- Aggregate merger consideration paid was approximately $1,644,214,180 in cash and
  20,767,577 shares of Jacobs common stock; because the stock-consideration election was
  oversubscribed, stockholders who elected all-stock consideration instead received
  approximately $41.52 in cash plus 0.8824 Jacobs shares per CH2M share, subject to
  proration mechanics in the Merger Agreement. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-17-073715, Form 8-K Item 2.01]
- CH2M filed a Form 15 with the SEC on December 18, 2017 certifying termination of its
  registration under Section 12(g) of the Exchange Act (Rule 12g-4(a)(1)), ending its
  independent reporting obligations. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-17-073717, Form 15-12G]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- CH2M, a private, employee-controlled engineering-services firm serving government and
  private-industry clients, merged into a Jacobs merger subsidiary and survived as a
  direct wholly-owned Jacobs subsidiary in a whole-company, mixed cash-and-stock
  transaction (CH2M stockholders receiving approximately 15% of pro forma Jacobs common
  stock); CH2M's entire legal vehicle, its board, and its named executive officers were
  replaced at the effective time, while non-union rank-and-file employees were carried
  forward under contractual pay/benefit-continuity terms and union-covered employees
  remained under their existing collective bargaining agreements; CH2M's pre-existing
  debt was slated for repayment at closing rather than continuing as a standalone
  obligation, and CH2M ended its own SEC reporting obligations three days after closing.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-338957, DEFM14A] [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-17-073715, Form 8-K] [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-17-073717, Form 15-12G]
