# Structural sub-dossier — Nortel Networks Corporation, court-supervised asset sales (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transactions were structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## The drawn filing (described for what it establishes)
- The drawn Form 8-K reports that on June 30, 2009 Nortel Networks Inc. (a Delaware
  corporation and indirect subsidiary of the Registrant) and several other U.S.
  subsidiaries (the "U.S. Debtors") filed their unaudited condensed combined
  debtors-in-possession financial statements included in a Monthly Operating Report for
  the period May 3, 2009 to May 30, 2009 with the United States Bankruptcy Court for the
  District of Delaware (In re Nortel Networks Inc., et al., Chapter 11, Case No. 09-10138).
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-141833, Form 8-K Item 8.01]
- This drawn filing is a court-reporting event, not a deal-terms document; it establishes
  the Chapter 11 court-supervision context under which the asset sales below were
  structured, and is filed under Item 8.01 with the report attached as Exhibit 99.1.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-141833, Form 8-K Items 8.01 and 9.01]

## Transaction form (how the deals were structured at closing)
- The registrant sold its operating businesses as separate asset packages through
  court-approved auctions rather than as a whole company; each sale required approval of
  the U.S. Bankruptcy Court and the Canadian court, and, in some regions, EMEA employee
  information-and-consultation processes. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-191355, Form 8-K Item 1.01]
- The controlling parent retained no residual equity value: the registrant stated it did
  not expect common shareholders or NNL preferred shareholders to receive any value and
  expected the proceedings to result in cancellation of these equity interests. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-155788, Form 8-K Item 8.01]

## CDMA / LTE Access assets — sale to Ericsson
- On July 24, 2009 Nortel concluded the auction of substantially all of its Code Division
  Multiple Access (CDMA) business and Long Term Evolution (LTE) Access assets; Ericsson
  emerged as winning bidder at a purchase price of US$1.13 billion, to be purchased under
  an asset sale agreement (ASA). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-155788, Form 8-K Item 1.01]
- The customer-facing product crossed the boundary while the running organization crossed
  only through fresh acquirer offers: at least 2,500 Nortel employees supporting the CDMA
  and LTE Access business were expected to receive offers of employment from Ericsson
  (not an automatic transfer of the workforce). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-155788, Form 8-K Item 1.01]
- The operating processes did not move intact with the product: at closing NNC and certain
  subsidiaries would enter a transition services agreement (TSA) with Ericsson under which
  Nortel would provide certain transition services for up to 24 months after closing. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-155788, Form 8-K Item 1.01]

## Enterprise Solutions business — sale to Avaya
- On September 14, 2009 Nortel concluded the auction of substantially all of its global
  Enterprise Solutions business, together with the shares of Nortel Government Solutions
  Incorporated and DiamondWare, Ltd.; Avaya Inc. emerged as winning bidder agreeing to pay
  US$900 million in cash, with an additional pool of US$15 million reserved for an employee
  retention program. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-191355, Form 8-K Item 1.01]
- The transaction combined an asset sale (the business) with a share sale (the two named
  subsidiaries), and at closing Nortel and Avaya would enter a transition services
  agreement under which Nortel would provide certain transition services for up to 12 to 18
  months (depending on jurisdiction) after closing. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-191355, Form 8-K Item 1.01]

## Optical Networking and Carrier Ethernet businesses — sale to Ciena
- On November 23, 2009 Nortel concluded the auction of substantially all of the assets of
  its global Optical Networking and Carrier Ethernet businesses and, on November 24, 2009,
  entered agreements with Ciena Corporation to pay US$530,000,000 in cash plus
  US$239,000,000 aggregate principal amount of a new class of Ciena senior convertible
  notes due 2017; these agreements amended and restated asset sale agreements originally
  entered on October 7, 2009. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-244173, Form 8-K Item 1.01]
- At closing Nortel and Ciena would enter a transition services agreement under which
  Nortel would provide certain transition services for up to 24 months (up to 12 months in
  certain EMEA jurisdictions) after closing. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-244173, Form 8-K Item 1.01]

## Packet Core (Carrier Networks) assets — sale procedure
- On September 21, 2009 Nortel announced a plan to sell, by auction, the Packet Core Assets
  of its Carrier Networks business (software supporting data transfer over existing and
  next-generation wireless networks, including relevant non-patent intellectual property,
  equipment and related tangible assets), and filed a motion to establish a Section 363
  sale procedure with the U.S. Bankruptcy Court, with a parallel motion for the Ontario
  Superior Court of Justice. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-195707, Form 8-K Item 8.01]
- The structure separated the transferred product/asset from a portion of the underlying
  intellectual property: NNL expected to grant the purchaser a non-exclusive license of
  relevant patent intellectual property rather than convey those patents outright. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-195707, Form 8-K Item 8.01]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- Across the sales, customer-facing product lines and their tangible assets crossed the
  ownership boundary as court-approved asset (and, for Avaya, partial share) packages, the
  running workforce crossed only through discretionary acquirer employment offers and
  retention pools rather than automatic transfer, and the operating/support processes were
  not conveyed intact but bridged by transition services agreements (up to 12–24 months) in
  which the seller continued to run services for the buyers after closing. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-155788, Form 8-K Item 1.01] [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-191355, Form 8-K Item 1.01] [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-244173, Form 8-K Item 1.01]
