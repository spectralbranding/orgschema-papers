# Structural sub-dossier — AbbVie Inc. separation from Abbott Laboratories (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Transaction and parties at closing
- Abbott Laboratories announced in October 2011 a plan to separate into two publicly
  traded health care companies; AbbVie Inc. was the newly formed entity holding Abbott's
  research-based proprietary pharmaceuticals and biologics business, while the Abbott
  name remained with the diversified medical products business. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-12-010903, Form 10-12B EX-99.1 information statement]
- The separation was structured as a pro rata distribution of all outstanding AbbVie
  common stock to Abbott shareholders — one AbbVie share for each Abbott common share
  held on the record date of December 12, 2012, with the distribution date on
  January 1, 2013. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-12-010903, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Abbott received a private letter ruling that the distribution would be tax-free to
  Abbott shareholders. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-12-010903, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## What crossed the boundary at the tier level
- Customer-facing PRODUCT: the proprietary pharmaceuticals and biologics portfolio moved
  into AbbVie, including HUMIRA, Lupron, Synagis, Kaletra, Creon, and Synthroid, plus a
  development pipeline of more than 20 compounds or indications in Phase II/III. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-12-010903, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Business/value-capture MODEL: the information statement states AbbVie's business model
  "is distinctly different from Abbott's diversified model," driven by development and
  commercialization of new pharmaceuticals and biologics. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-12-010903, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Operational PROCESSES / assets: principal manufacturing plants transferred to AbbVie
  ownership at North Chicago, Illinois; Barceloneta and Jayuya, Puerto Rico; Worcester,
  Massachusetts; Campoverde di Aprilia, Italy; Cork and Sligo, Ireland; and Ludwigshafen,
  Germany, along with four U.S. R&D facilities and R&D sites in Shanghai and Ludwigshafen. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-12-010903, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- PEOPLE / organization: AbbVie expected to employ approximately 30,000 persons as of the
  distribution date. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-12-010903, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Management continuity: the management team was led by Richard A. Gonzalez, becoming
  AbbVie Chairman and CEO, who had served as Executive Vice President of Abbott's
  Pharmaceutical Products Group since 2010; expected AbbVie executive officers who were
  then Abbott employees would cease to be Abbott employees at separation. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-12-010903, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## What did NOT cross intact (shared substrate retained/replicated)
- Corporate functions historically performed for the business by Abbott — accounting,
  information technology, and finance — did not transfer as an intact operating substrate;
  AbbVie stated it "will need to make significant investments to replicate or outsource
  from other providers certain facilities, systems, infrastructure, and personnel to
  which AbbVie will no longer have access" after separation. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-12-010903, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Working-capital and general corporate-purpose funding had "historically been satisfied
  as part of the corporate-wide cash management policies of Abbott"; after separation
  AbbVie would need to obtain independent financing. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-12-010903, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- AbbVie was required to pay a $10.2 billion distribution to Abbott, financed through
  senior notes and other debt, as a term of the separation agreement. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-12-010903, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Contractual arrangements bridging the shared substrate (closing-time)
- The parties entered into a separation and distribution agreement and, in connection with
  it, agreed to a U.S. and an ex-U.S. transition services agreement, a tax sharing
  agreement, an employee matters agreement, a special products master agreement, an
  information technology agreement, international commercial operations agreements,
  finished goods supply agreements, contract manufacturing agreements, and a transitional
  trademark license agreement. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-12-010903, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Under the ex-U.S. transition services agreement, Abbott would provide AbbVie with back
  office functions and other services in certain markets outside the United States "until
  AbbVie has established sufficient back office infrastructure to conduct operations in
  such markets." [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-12-010903, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Lease agreements cross-allocated real estate: AbbVie would lease from Abbott a portion
  of Abbott Park (Abbott's then-headquarters) under an initial 20-year term, plus space in
  Germany, Chile, Spain, and Mexico; Abbott would lease manufacturing, office, and
  warehouse facilities from AbbVie in Puerto Rico, Germany, Ireland, and Italy. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-12-010903, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction moved an intact customer-facing product portfolio, its owned
  manufacturing/R&D processes, and an incumbent management team into a new standalone
  entity, while the shared corporate-service substrate (accounting, IT, finance, cash
  management, back-office in ex-U.S. markets) was not transferred whole but replicated by
  AbbVie and bridged at closing by transition services, supply, tax, employee, IT, and
  lease agreements. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-12-010903, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
