# Structural sub-dossier — Marathon Oil Corporation / Marathon Petroleum Corporation separation (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Note on the drawn source document
- The drawn filing (Form 8-K, accession 0001193125-11-151791, filed 2011-05-26) is
  itself an Item 1.01 "Entry into a Material Definitive Agreement" report: it announces
  and summarizes the definitive separation agreements entered into by Marathon Oil
  Corporation on May 25, 2011, and attaches them as exhibits. It is therefore a genuine
  deal-terms document for the transaction it documents. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-11-151791, Form 8-K Item 1.01]
- The transaction it documents is a spin-off: the Board of Directors of Marathon Oil
  Corporation approved separating its downstream (refining, marketing, and
  transportation) operations from its upstream operations by distributing all shares
  of a wholly owned subsidiary, Marathon Petroleum Corporation ("MPC"), to Marathon Oil's
  stockholders (one MPC share for every two Marathon Oil shares held), with a distribution
  date of June 30, 2011. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-11-151791, Form 8-K Item 1.01]
- Because the drawn 8-K's own narrative is thin on business/operational detail, this
  dossier also draws on MPC's contemporaneous Form 10-12B/A registration (the closing-era
  information statement for the same transaction, filed the same week), each fact tagged
  with its own source per the multi-primary-source sourcing protocol for cases where the
  drawn accession is not itself the full deal-terms record. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-11-151775, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1 information statement]

## Transaction and parties at closing
- Marathon Petroleum Corporation was incorporated in Delaware on November 9, 2009 as a
  wholly owned subsidiary of Marathon Oil, in connection with an internal restructuring
  undertaken to prepare for the eventual separation. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-11-151775, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]
- To effect the spin-off, on May 25, 2011 Marathon Oil and MPC entered into a Separation
  and Distribution Agreement, a Tax Sharing Agreement, an Employee Matters Agreement, and
  a Transition Services Agreement. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-11-151791, Form 8-K Item 1.01]
- Following the distribution, MPC became an independent, publicly traded company and
  Marathon Oil retained no ownership interest in MPC. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-11-151791, Form 8-K Item 1.01]
- Completion of the spin-off was conditioned on, among other things, an effective Form 10
  registration statement, continued validity of an IRS private letter ruling that the
  distribution qualifies as tax-free under Sections 355 and 368(a) of the Internal
  Revenue Code, and NYSE listing approval for MPC common stock. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-11-151791, Form 8-K Item 1.01]

## What separated at the tier level
- Customer-facing product/asset: the separated business supplied refined petroleum
  products to approximately 5,100 Marathon-branded retail outlets operated by
  independent dealers and jobbers, and sold directly to consumers through approximately
  1,350 Speedway-branded convenience stores owned and operated by a wholly owned
  subsidiary. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-11-151775, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]
- Operational processes/legal vehicle: the separated business comprised six refineries
  in the Gulf Coast and Midwest with an aggregate crude oil refining capacity exceeding
  1.1 million barrels per day, ownership interests in approximately 9,600 miles of crude
  and refined-product pipelines, and a majority interest in LOOP LLC, owner/operator of
  the only U.S. deepwater oil port — organized as three segments (Refining & Marketing,
  Speedway, Pipeline Transportation) under the new MPC legal vehicle. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-11-151775, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]
- People/management: Gary R. Heminger, who had over 35 years of experience at Marathon
  Oil and served as its Executive Vice President, Downstream, resigned that Marathon Oil
  officer position in connection with the spin-off and was slated to serve as MPC's
  President and Chief Executive Officer. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-11-151791, Form 8-K Item 5.02; SEC accession 0001193125-11-151775, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]
- The same six individuals who resigned from the Marathon Oil board of directors in
  connection with the spin-off (David A. Daberko, William L. Davis, Charles R. Lee,
  Seth E. Schofield, John W. Snow, and Thomas J. Usher) are the individuals identified in
  MPC's information statement as its incoming directors, with Usher expected to serve as
  MPC's Chairman. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-11-151791, Form 8-K Item 5.02; SEC accession 0001193125-11-151775, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]
- What did NOT separate as an intact package at closing: the retained upstream company
  (Marathon Oil) kept its exploration and production, oil sands mining, and integrated
  gas operations, while indemnity allocation was split along that same product-line
  boundary — Marathon Oil agreed to indemnify MPC for liabilities relating to historical
  E&P, oil sands, and integrated gas operations, and MPC agreed to indemnify Marathon Oil
  for liabilities relating to the historical downstream operations. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-11-151791, Form 8-K Item 1.01]

## Contractual arrangements bridging the shared substrate at closing
- Under the Transition Services Agreement, Marathon Oil agreed to provide MPC —
  and MPC agreed to provide Marathon Oil — administrative, accounting, audit, health/
  environmental/safety, information technology, legal, tax, and treasury services (plus
  natural gas administration from Marathon Oil, and human resources from MPC), for a
  one-year period beginning on the distribution date, at fees generally set to recover
  the providing party's direct and indirect costs. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-11-151791, Form 8-K Item 1.01]
- The Employee Matters Agreement provided for continued benefit protection for Marathon
  Oil employees who transferred to MPC prior to December 31, 2011, and set out how
  outstanding equity awards (options, stock appreciation rights, restricted stock/units,
  performance units) held by employees on each side of the separation would be adjusted
  or replaced to preserve intrinsic value as of the distribution date. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-11-151791, Form 8-K Item 1.01]
- The Tax Sharing Agreement allocated responsibility for taxes attributable to each
  company's pre- and post-distribution periods and contained covenants intended to
  protect the tax-free status of the distribution. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-11-151791, Form 8-K Item 1.01]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction separated a downstream, asset-heavy refining/pipeline/retail-fuel
  business (product: refined fuels sold under the Marathon and Speedway brands;
  processes: six refineries, ~9,600 miles of pipeline, a deepwater port interest) into a
  newly incorporated standalone legal vehicle (MPC), carrying with it — as a bloc — the
  senior executive (Heminger) and a cohort of outside directors who had overseen that
  business inside Marathon Oil, while shared corporate-function processes (accounting,
  tax, treasury, IT, legal, HR) were bridged for a fixed one-year transition period by a
  Transition Services Agreement rather than transferred outright to either side. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-11-151791, Form 8-K Item 1.01]
