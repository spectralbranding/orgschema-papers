## Realized fate of the transaction
- The proposed acquisition was not consummated. On December 23, 2009, Tronox delivered a notice of termination of the Tronox Purchase Agreement to Huntsman, after Tronox received an order from the bankruptcy court authorizing it to replace its existing senior secured financing and an interim order authorizing it to enter into certain agreements as part of a plan to pursue an alternative transaction. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-10-001019, Form 10-K for fiscal year ended December 31, 2009 ("Termination of Tronox Purchase Agreement")]
- The alternative transaction Tronox pursued instead was sponsored by an ad hoc group of Tronox's unsecured bondholders. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-10-001019, Form 10-K for fiscal year ended December 31, 2009]
- Because the Buyers had entered the Agreement as a "stalking horse" bidder subject to Tronox's solicitation of higher or better offers and a court-supervised auction, no titanium dioxide, electrolytics, working-capital, subsidiary-equity, or joint-venture asset of Tronox crossed the ownership boundary to Huntsman under this Agreement. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-10-001019, Form 10-K for fiscal year ended December 31, 2009]

## Realized financial record of the transaction's resolution
- Under the Agreement, Huntsman had made a $12 million refundable deposit toward the purchase price and incurred $13 million in costs in connection with the proposed transaction. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-10-001019, Form 10-K for fiscal year ended December 31, 2009]
- Prior to December 31, 2009, the $12 million deposit was refunded to Huntsman, Huntsman received an additional $12 million as a break-up fee, and Huntsman received $3 million for partial reimbursement of its costs. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-10-001019, Form 10-K for fiscal year ended December 31, 2009]
- The break-up fee and the partial cost reimbursement, net of the costs incurred, were recorded in other operating income in Huntsman's consolidated statements of operations for the year ended December 31, 2009. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-10-001019, Form 10-K for fiscal year ended December 31, 2009]

## Realized record over the fixed outcome window
- Huntsman's Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2013 (within the fixed window) references Tronox only as one of the major global competing titanium dioxide producers ("the major global producers against whom we compete are DuPont, Tronox, Kronos and Cristal"), not as an acquired business, segment, or source of goodwill/impairment — consistent with a transaction that never closed. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-14-000771, Form 10-K for fiscal year ended December 31, 2013]
- Huntsman's Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2010 likewise references Tronox only as a competing global titanium dioxide producer, with no acquired-Tronox business in Huntsman's consolidated operations. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-11-001007, Form 10-K for fiscal year ended December 31, 2010]

## Outcome summary (realized record only)
- The proposed acquisition of Tronox's titanium dioxide and electrolytics businesses by Huntsman did not close: after Huntsman's August 28, 2009 stalking-horse agreement, Tronox terminated the agreement on December 23, 2009 to pursue an alternative reorganization sponsored by its unsecured bondholders; Huntsman's $12 million deposit was refunded and Huntsman received a $12 million break-up fee plus $3 million of expense reimbursement (recorded net in other operating income for FY2009), and Huntsman's in-window FY2010 and FY2013 annual reports show no Tronox business within its consolidated operations (Tronox appears only as a competitor), consistent with a transaction that was never consummated. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-10-001019, Form 10-K for fiscal year ended December 31, 2009] [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-11-001007, Form 10-K for fiscal year ended December 31, 2010] [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-14-000771, Form 10-K for fiscal year ended December 31, 2013]
