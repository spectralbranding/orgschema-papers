## Transaction and parties at closing
- On November 30, 2011, the board of directors of The Williams Companies, Inc. ("Williams") approved the spin-off of its natural gas and oil exploration and production business as a separate, publicly traded company named WPX Energy, Inc. ("WPX"); upon completion Williams stockholders would own 100% of WPX's outstanding common stock. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-11-101646, Form 10-12B EX-99.1 information statement]
- The spin-off was structured as a pro rata distribution on December 31, 2011, of WPX common stock to Williams stockholders of record as of the close of business on December 14, 2011 (the spin-off record date). [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-11-101646, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The distribution ratio was one share of WPX common stock for every three shares of Williams common stock held on the record date; no stockholder approval or consideration was required, and the spin-off was intended to be tax-free to Williams stockholders (except cash in lieu of fractional shares) subject to a private letter ruling and opinion of counsel. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-11-101646, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- WPX common stock was expected to be listed on the New York Stock Exchange under the symbol "WPX"; following the spin-off Williams would not retain any ownership interest in WPX. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-11-101646, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## The business separated (customer-facing asset / operations)
- WPX was formed in April 2011 to hold Williams's natural gas and oil exploration and production business and to effect the spin-off; it described itself as an independent natural gas and oil exploration and production company engaged in the exploitation and development of long-life unconventional properties. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-11-101646, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Its principal areas of operation were the Piceance Basin (Rocky Mountain region), the Bakken Shale oil play (Williston Basin, North Dakota), the Marcellus Shale natural gas play (Pennsylvania), the Powder River Basin (Wyoming) and the San Juan Basin (southwestern United States); the Piceance Basin operations formed the majority of its proved reserves and current production. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-11-101646, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- WPX also held a 69 percent controlling ownership interest in Apco Oil and Gas International Inc. ("Apco"), which held oil and gas concessions in Argentina and Colombia and traded on the NASDAQ Capital Market under "APAGF." [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-11-101646, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- As of December 31, 2010, WPX's proved reserves were 4,473 Bcfe (59 percent proved developed); average daily production for the month of October 2011 was 1,308 MMcfe/d. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-11-101646, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Restructuring transactions (what crossed the ownership boundary)
- As part of the spin-off, Williams contributed to WPX's capital all intercompany debt associated with the business, and would contribute and transfer to WPX the assets and liabilities associated with the business; WPX would amend and restate its certificate of incorporation and bylaws (collectively, the "restructuring transactions"). [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-11-101646, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## People / management crossing the boundary
- At September 30, 2011, Williams had 1,073 full-time employees dedicated to WPX's business (this figure excluded Williams employees who provided services to both WPX's business and other Williams businesses); WPX itself had no employees as of the date of the information statement, and effective as of January 1, 2012 Williams would transfer to WPX the employees who provide services to its business. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-11-101646, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Ralph A. Hill was identified as Chief Executive Officer of WPX Energy, Inc. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-11-101646, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Shared substrate retained at the parent (processes not transferred whole)
- WPX stated it currently depended on Williams for a number of administrative functions, including cash management and treasury administration, finance and accounting, tax, internal audit, investor relations, payroll and human resources administration, information technology, legal, regulatory and government affairs, insurance and claims administration, records management, real estate and facilities management, sourcing and procurement, and mail/print/office services. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-11-101646, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Contractual arrangements bridging the shared substrate at closing
- Separation and distribution agreement: entered into with Williams to effect the spin-off and provide for the allocation between WPX and Williams of various assets, liabilities and obligations; under it WPX would indemnify Williams against various claims and liabilities relating to the past operation of the business. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-11-101646, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Transition services agreement: WPX intended to enter into a transition services agreement under which certain services would be provided on an interim basis; the services would commence on the distribution date and terminate upon the earlier of (i) one year after the distribution date or (ii) sixty days' notice by either party, and Williams could immediately terminate any service involving a conflict of interest or that would cause Williams to violate applicable law. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-11-101646, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Employee matters agreement: would set forth agreements concerning certain employee compensation and benefit matters between Williams and WPX. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-11-101646, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Tax sharing agreement: would govern the respective rights, responsibilities and obligations for payment of taxes, filing of returns, control of audits, and liability for taxes triggered by the spin-off; WPX would indemnify Williams for taxes resulting from a failure of the spin-off to qualify as tax-free under Sections 355 and 368(a)(1)(D) of the Code to the extent caused by WPX's breach. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-11-101646, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- WPX noted that, after the expiration or termination of the transition services agreement, it might not be able to replace these services or negotiate third-party terms comparable to those received from Williams. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-11-101646, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Stated rationale for the separation
- Williams stated the two businesses had different financial and operating characteristics and operating strategies; the separation would let each focus managerial attention on its respective business, better align management resources, and eliminate the dilution of attention from managing businesses with competing goals; separate management teams would better prioritize resource allocation. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-11-101646, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Williams stated the separation would give each company direct and differentiated access to the debt and equity capital markets — noting the exploration and production business is typically capital intensive throughout the business cycle — and would enhance market recognition, WPX's simpler single-business-segment structure fitting the exploration and production investor sector. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-11-101646, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction separated Williams's natural gas and oil exploration and production business into a newly formed, wholly owned subsidiary (WPX Energy, Inc., formed April 2011) and distributed 100% of its shares pro rata to Williams stockholders (one WPX share per three Williams shares) on December 31, 2011 in a tax-free spin-off after which Williams held no interest; WPX received the E&P asset base (Piceance, Bakken/Williston, Marcellus, Powder River, San Juan, plus a 69% interest in Apco), had no employees of its own at the date of the statement (1,073 Williams employees dedicated to the business were to transfer effective January 1, 2012), and depended on Williams for a broad set of administrative functions bridged at closing by a separation and distribution agreement, a transition services agreement (commencing on the distribution date, terminating at the earlier of one year or sixty days' notice), an employee matters agreement and a tax sharing agreement. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-11-101646, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
