# Structural sub-dossier — WashingtonFirst Bankshares, Inc. combination with Sandy Spring Bancorp, Inc. (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Transaction and parties at closing
- On May 15, 2017, Sandy Spring Bancorp, Inc. (Maryland), WashingtonFirst Bankshares,
  Inc. (Virginia; $5.3B-asset acquirer vs. a 19-office target with wealth-management and
  mortgage-banking subsidiaries), and Touchdown Acquisition, Inc. (a Virginia merger sub
  formed solely to effect the merger) entered an Agreement and Plan of Merger. Structure:
  (i) Merger Sub merged into WashingtonFirst, WashingtonFirst surviving as a Sandy Spring
  subsidiary; (ii) WashingtonFirst then merged into Sandy Spring, Sandy Spring surviving;
  (iii) WashingtonFirst Bank then merged into Sandy Spring Bank, Sandy Spring Bank
  surviving. Both boards unanimously approved. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-281975,
  Form DEFM14A]
- Consideration: each WashingtonFirst common/non-voting share converted into 0.8713
  shares of Sandy Spring common stock (subject to a collar outside $37.07-$50.15), plus
  cash for fractional shares. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-281975, Form DEFM14A]
- The transaction completed on January 1, 2018; the Integrated Mergers and the bank-level
  merger of WashingtonFirst Bank into Sandy Spring Bank closed as structured, with
  outstanding WashingtonFirst options cashed out at $34.42 less exercise price. [SRC: SEC
  accession 0001144204-18-000242, Form 8-K Item 2.01]

## What crossed the ownership boundary and what did not
- Neither legal vehicle survived independently: WashingtonFirst common stock ceased Nasdaq
  Capital Market listing and was to be deregistered under the Exchange Act at the
  first-step merger; WashingtonFirst Bank's charter did not continue, being merged into
  Sandy Spring Bank as the surviving bank. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-281975, Form
  DEFM14A; SEC accession 0001144204-18-000242, Form 8-K Item 2.01]
- The acquirer's stated rationale described the two banks' branch footprints as "unusually
  complementary" and their cultures as complementary, anticipating "smooth integration of
  key personnel and systems" — i.e., the branch network and personnel were expected to
  combine into one operating structure rather than remain parallel. [SRC: SEC accession
  0001193125-17-281975, Form DEFM14A]
- Controlling-intent/people: WashingtonFirst's CEO (Shaza L. Andersen) and Chairman
  (Joseph S. Bracewell), plus two other WashingtonFirst directors, joined the Sandy Spring
  and Sandy Spring Bank boards (expanded to fifteen members); Andersen and Bracewell also
  joined the Sandy Spring board's executive committee. However, Andersen's operating
  employment with WashingtonFirst and WashingtonFirst Bank terminated at the effective
  time, replaced by a 12-month consulting/separation agreement capped at 20% of her prior
  service level — board presence continued but her executive operating role did not. [SRC:
  SEC accession 0001193125-17-281975, Form DEFM14A; SEC accession 0001144204-18-000242,
  Form 8-K Item 5.02 (agreement effective December 29, 2017)]
- By contrast, George W. Connors, IV (WashingtonFirst Bank's President/Chief Credit
  Officer) was retained in an operating capacity via a two-year Sandy Spring EVP
  employment agreement; other named WashingtonFirst executives (General Counsel Richard D.
  Horn, CFO Matthew R. Johnson) were covered only by change-in-control severance payment
  agreements rather than continuing employment arrangements disclosed in this filing.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-281975, Form DEFM14A]

## Bridging mechanisms at closing (people/process substrate)
- Sandy Spring agreed to establish a bonus retention pool for key WashingtonFirst Bank
  employees, and separately allocated Mr. Connors a $615,000 transaction bonus plus
  $100,000 in retention payments (two installments), contingent on non-competition/
  non-solicitation covenants — mechanisms to bridge the workforce through the transition
  rather than an automatic transfer of personnel. [SRC: SEC accession
  0001193125-17-281975, Form DEFM14A]
- The merger agreement required Sandy Spring to maintain WashingtonFirst's existing
  directors'-and-officers' liability insurance (or a comparable tail policy) for six years
  after closing, capped at 300% of the pre-signing annual premium — bridging legacy
  liability exposure rather than transferring an operating function. [SRC: SEC accession
  0001193125-17-281975, Form DEFM14A]
- The filing's own risk-factor language states the companies "have operated and, until the
  completion of the Transactions, will continue to operate, independently," and flags that
  integration could produce "the loss of key employees, the disruption of either company's
  ongoing businesses or inconsistencies in standards, controls, procedures and policies" —
  a forward-looking risk the filing itself identifies, not a resolved fact. [SRC: SEC
  accession 0001193125-17-281975, Form DEFM14A]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction was a whole-company, two-step statutory merger in which the acquired
  holding company and its bank subsidiary were both absorbed into the acquirer's legal
  vehicles at the effective time (January 1, 2018), the target's stock ceased separate
  public listing, and its branch network, deposits, and loan book combined into the
  acquirer's structure. A subset of the target's board and one senior executive carried
  into ongoing roles; the CEO's operating role ended (replaced by a time-limited
  consulting arrangement) and other named executives were covered by severance rather than
  continuing employment; a retention bonus pool and named-executive retention payments
  bridged the employee base through the transition. [SRC: SEC accession
  0001193125-17-281975, Form DEFM14A; SEC accession 0001144204-18-000242, Form 8-K]
