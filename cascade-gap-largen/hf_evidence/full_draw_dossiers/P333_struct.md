# Structural sub-dossier — FCB Financial Holdings, Inc. acquisition by Synovus Financial Corp. (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Transaction and parties
- On July 23, 2018, Synovus Financial Corp. ("Synovus"), FCB Financial Holdings, Inc.
  ("FCB"), and Azalea Merger Sub Corp., a wholly-owned subsidiary of Synovus ("Merger
  Sub"), entered into an Agreement and Plan of Merger under which Merger Sub would merge
  with and into FCB, with FCB as the surviving corporation; immediately following that
  merger, FCB would merge with and into Synovus ("upstream merger"), with Synovus
  continuing as the surviving entity. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-041495, DEFM14A p.1]
- Immediately following the upstream merger (or at such later time as Synovus may
  determine), FCB's wholly-owned subsidiary, Florida Community Bank, National
  Association ("FCB Bank"), would merge with and into Synovus's wholly-owned
  subsidiary, Synovus Bank, a Georgia state-chartered bank, with Synovus Bank as the
  surviving bank; the merger agreement states this bank-level merger "will be
  implemented pursuant to an agreement and plan of merger, each in a form to be
  mutually agreed upon by Synovus and FCB" — i.e., left to a separate, later-executed
  instrument rather than fixed at signing. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-041495, DEFM14A "Agreement and Plan of Merger" §1.13]
- Each outstanding share of FCB Class A common stock (with specified exceptions) would
  convert into the right to receive 1.055 shares of Synovus common stock (the "Exchange
  Ratio"); consideration was 100% stock, not cash. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-041495, DEFM14A "Questions and Answers"]
- Based on shares outstanding as of October 24, 2018, FCB stockholders as a group were
  expected to hold approximately 31.7% of Synovus common stock outstanding immediately
  after closing. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-041495, DEFM14A "Comparative Per Share Market Price and Dividend Information"]
- FCB's financial advisors calculated an aggregate implied transaction value of
  approximately $2,846 million (inclusive of consideration for FCB options, warrants,
  and restricted stock) based on the 1.055x exchange ratio and Synovus's closing price
  on July 20, 2018. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-041495, DEFM14A "Opinions of FCB's Financial Advisors"]

## What crossed at closing, per tier
- Legal vehicle: FCB's separate corporate existence would cease entirely upon the
  upstream merger — not a subsidiary-preserving acquisition but a full absorption of
  the legal entity into Synovus. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-041495, DEFM14A "Agreement and Plan of Merger" §1.13]
- People/organization: for the one-year period after closing, Synovus agreed to provide
  continuing FCB employees base pay no less favorable than immediately pre-closing,
  incentive-compensation targets no less favorable than similarly-situated Synovus
  employees, and comparable other benefits, with severance protection for terminations
  within twelve months of closing. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-041495, DEFM14A "Employee Benefit Matters"]
- Named executives (Kent Ellert, Vincent Tese, Les Lieberman, James Baiter) had their
  existing FCB Bank employment agreements terminated at the effective time in exchange
  for lump-sum change-in-control payments (Ellert $11,900,000; Tese and Lieberman
  $4,050,000 each; Baiter $1,400,000); Ellert and Baiter separately entered new
  employment agreements directly with Synovus governing post-closing employment. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-041495, DEFM14A "Interests of FCB Directors and Executive Officers in the Merger"]
- The merger agreement and DEFM14A repeatedly flag "the costs and risks of ... successfully
  integrating the differing business models of the two companies" and note Synovus
  "will incur integration costs following the completion of the merger ... including
  facilities and systems consolidation costs and employment-related costs" as a
  forward-looking risk factor, rather than describing integration as already complete
  at signing. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-041495, DEFM14A "Risk Factors"]
- Cost-savings assumptions used by both companies' financial advisors modeled
  approximately 25%-26% of FCB's estimated noninterest expense as achievable synergies
  from the combination, an estimate rather than a contracted or realized figure. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-041495, DEFM14A "Opinion of Synovus' Financial Advisor" / "Opinions of FCB's Financial Advisors"]

## Deal-protection and financial terms
- A termination fee of $93.5 million would be payable by either FCB or Synovus to the
  other party upon termination of the merger agreement under certain circumstances,
  including if FCB's board changed its recommendation. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-041495, DEFM14A "The Merger Agreement--Termination; Termination Fee"]
- The merger agreement restricted FCB's ability to solicit competing acquisition
  proposals prior to closing and required both parties to operate their businesses in
  the ordinary course pending completion. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-041495, DEFM14A "Risk Factors" / "The Merger Agreement--Covenants and Agreements"]

## Regulatory process and closing timing
- Completion required approval from the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System
  and the Georgia Department of Banking and Finance ("Georgia DBF"), among other
  approvals; applications were filed August 22, 2018 and Georgia DBF approval was
  received September 25, 2018. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-041495, DEFM14A "Regulatory Approvals Required for the Merger"]
- The merger was expected to be completed by the first quarter of 2019, subject to
  shareholder approvals of both companies and regulatory approvals, with neither party
  able to guarantee timing. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-041495, DEFM14A "Regulatory Approvals Required for the Merger"]
- Closing was conditioned on, among other things, authorization for NYSE listing of the
  Synovus shares to be issued, effectiveness of the Form S-4 registration statement of
  which the joint proxy statement/prospectus is a part, and receipt by each party of a
  tax opinion that the merger would qualify as a reorganization under Section 368(a) of
  the Internal Revenue Code. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-041495, DEFM14A "The Merger Agreement--Conditions to Complete the Merger"]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- A whole-company, all-stock combination in which Merger Sub would merge into FCB and
  FCB would then immediately merge into and cease to exist within Synovus (FCB
  stockholders receiving 1.055 Synovus shares per FCB share, ~31.7% pro forma Synovus
  ownership), with the separate bank-level merger of FCB Bank into Synovus Bank left to
  a later-executed agreement rather than fixed at signing, one-year compensation/benefit
  continuity commitments for continuing employees, negotiated executive exit and
  retention packages, a $93.5 million mutual termination fee, and closing contingent on
  Federal Reserve Board and Georgia DBF approval, NYSE listing authorization, and
  effectiveness of the Form S-4 registration statement. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-041495, DEFM14A]
