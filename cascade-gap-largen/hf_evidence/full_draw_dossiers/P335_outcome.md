## Realized record: bankruptcy proceeding naming the acquired entity (2023)
- On May 14, 2023, MLCJR LLC and six affiliated entities filed voluntary chapter 11
  bankruptcy petitions in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas;
  the affiliated debtors were identified in a related federal court opinion as including
  "Energy XXI Gulf Coast, LLC" (case No. 23-90329) and "Energy XXI GOM, LLC" (case No.
  23-90330), alongside Cox Operating, LLC, Cox Oil Offshore, L.L.C., EPL Oil & Gas, LLC,
  and M21K, LLC, jointly administered with MLCJR LLC's case. [SRC: U.S. Bankruptcy Court, Eastern District of Louisiana, Case No. 23-32223, Document 73, Memorandum Opinion and Order filed 2023-05-23, p.1, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-txsb-4_23-bk-32223/pdf/USCOURTS-txsb-4_23-bk-32223-0.pdf]
- Two days earlier, on May 12, 2023, a separate group of five Louisiana trade creditors
  (Keystone Chemical LLC, R&R Boats Inc., Specialty Offshore Inc., Total Production
  Supply LLC, and DLS LLC) had filed an involuntary chapter 7 petition against Cox
  Operating, LLC in the Eastern District of Louisiana bankruptcy court; that court later
  found venue proper in Texas and ordered the case to proceed there alongside the
  voluntary cases. [SRC: U.S. Bankruptcy Court, Eastern District of Louisiana, Case No. 23-32223, Document 73, pp.1-2, 6, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-txsb-4_23-bk-32223/pdf/USCOURTS-txsb-4_23-bk-32223-0.pdf]
- The court's findings of fact recorded that, per testimony from the debtors' own
  chief restructuring officer, the debtor group had "continued to experience adverse
  events that have curtailed production and, therefore, strained the debtors' liquidity"
  since 2019: a 2020 OPEC price war and COVID-19-related shut-ins reduced production;
  Gulf assets sustained damage from five named storms/hurricanes over 2020-2021; and a
  2020 collision by a foreign-flagged vessel into a debtor-owned offshore platform caused
  major damage, leaving the platform still offline as of the May 2023 hearing, with
  litigation pending to recover damages. [SRC: U.S. Bankruptcy Court, Eastern District of Louisiana, Case No. 23-32223, Document 73, pp.3-4, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-txsb-4_23-bk-32223/pdf/USCOURTS-txsb-4_23-bk-32223-0.pdf]
- The same findings state the debtor group's production volume, at the time of filing,
  was "half what it was in 2019," and that volumetric production payment agreements
  running through December 2023 had "become onerous ... and have contributed to the
  debtors' liquidity problems." [SRC: U.S. Bankruptcy Court, Eastern District of Louisiana, Case No. 23-32223, Document 73, p.4, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-txsb-4_23-bk-32223/pdf/USCOURTS-txsb-4_23-bk-32223-0.pdf]
- The debtor group retained restructuring advisor Alvarez & Marsal in October 2022,
  investment bank Moelis & Company in December 2022, and counsel Latham & Watkins in
  January 2023, and after failing to secure out-of-court refinancing or third-party
  debtor-in-possession financing, negotiated a term sheet with prepetition secured
  lenders Amarillo National Bank (estimated ~$80 million secured claim) and BP Energy
  Company (estimated ~$200 million secured claim); the debtors' estimated prepetition
  trade debt totaled approximately $211 million, with roughly $7.8 million further owed
  on plugging-and-abandonment bond premiums. [SRC: U.S. Bankruptcy Court, Eastern District of Louisiana, Case No. 23-32223, Document 73, pp.4-5, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-txsb-4_23-bk-32223/pdf/USCOURTS-txsb-4_23-bk-32223-0.pdf]
- On May 16, 2023, the debtors obtained interim court approval to draw $20 million of a
  proposed $75 million first-lien senior super-priority debtor-in-possession term loan
  facility for operations, and the voluntary chapter 11 filings proposed "a quick
  timetable to pursue an orderly liquidation of substantially all of their assets as a
  going concern with the ability to toggle to a stand-alone plan of reorganization."
  [SRC: U.S. Bankruptcy Court, Eastern District of Louisiana, Case No. 23-32223, Document 73, pp.4-5, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-txsb-4_23-bk-32223/pdf/USCOURTS-txsb-4_23-bk-32223-0.pdf]
- Reputable press reported the debtor group's total estimated liabilities at approximately
  $500 million, more than $200 million of which was owed to small businesses in the
  Houma-Thibodeaux and Acadiana regions of Louisiana, and that the bankruptcy followed a
  pattern of "using debt to acquire large fields of aging wells in shallow Gulf waters."
  [SRC: https://budsoffshoreenergy.com/2023/06/12/cox-bankruptcy-rocks-gulf-of-mexico-offshore-program/]

## Outcome summary (realized record only)
- The acquired company deregistered from SEC reporting within two weeks of the October
  2018 closing and generated no further periodic public filings. Within the outcome
  window, a federal bankruptcy court record shows an entity named "Energy XXI Gulf
  Coast, LLC" among seven affiliated debtors (led by the 2018 acquirer, MLCJR LLC, and
  including Cox Operating, LLC) that filed voluntary chapter 11 petitions on May 14,
  2023 — two days after a group of Louisiana trade creditors filed an involuntary
  chapter 7 petition against Cox Operating, LLC — with the court's findings describing
  liquidity strain traced to 2019-2021 pricing, pandemic, storm, and platform-collision
  disruptions, production roughly half of 2019 levels, estimated aggregate liabilities
  near $500 million, and a proposed rapid asset-liquidation timetable funded by new
  debtor-in-possession financing. [SRC: U.S. Bankruptcy Court, Eastern District of Louisiana, Case No. 23-32223, Document 73, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-txsb-4_23-bk-32223/pdf/USCOURTS-txsb-4_23-bk-32223-0.pdf] [SRC: SEC accession 0001558370-18-007955, Form 15-12G]
