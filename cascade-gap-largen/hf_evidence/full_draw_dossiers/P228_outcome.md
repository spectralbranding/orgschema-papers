## Public-reporting status of the reorganized entity (feasibility-relevant)
- On June 18, 2019 (roughly nine months after the September 20, 2018 closing), Legacy
  Reserves Inc. ("Legacy") and certain subsidiaries commenced voluntary Chapter 11 cases
  in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-19-011356, Form 8-K]
- The Bankruptcy Court entered an order confirming Legacy's Joint Chapter 11 Plan of
  Reorganization on November 15, 2019; under the Plan, "all existing equity interests in
  Legacy will receive no recovery under the Plan and will be extinguished," Legacy's
  114,810,671 outstanding shares of existing common stock (the shares issued in the 2018
  reorganization) were to be cancelled, and Reorganized Legacy was to issue new common
  stock to certain claimholders, including through a $189.8 million equity purchase
  backstopped by GSO Capital Partners LP and a $66.5 million rights offering. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-19-020949, Form 8-K]
- The Plan became effective December 11, 2019, and the Debtors emerged from Chapter 11;
  the terms of incumbent board members expired and a new seven-member board was seated,
  which was expected to include James Daniel Westcott (a former Legacy officer) along
  with other new and GSO-affiliated directors. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-19-022759, Form 8-K]
- Legacy Reserves Inc. (CIK 0001735828) filed a Form 15-12B on January 2, 2020,
  certifying deregistration; its last periodic report on EDGAR is a Form 10-K for fiscal
  year 2019, filed April 30, 2020. A search of its EDGAR filing history finds no
  subsequent periodic report (10-K, 10-Q, or 8-K) after that date. [SRC: SEC accession 0001735828-20-000006, Form 10-K FY2019] [SRC: https://data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK0001735828.json]

## Documented financial trajectory through fiscal year 2019 (primary filing)
- For the predecessor period January 1 through December 10, 2019 (i.e., up to the day
  before the Plan's Effective Date), Legacy recorded a net loss of $173.9 million,
  which included $447.9 million of reorganization expense associated with the Chapter 11
  cases (versus reorganization expense of zero for fiscal year 2018 and fiscal year
  2017). [SRC: SEC accession 0001735828-20-000006, Form 10-K FY2019]
- For the predecessor period covering fiscal year 2018 (the year of the corporate
  reorganization), Legacy recorded net income of $43.8 million; for the successor
  period December 11-31, 2019 (after fresh-start accounting was adopted under ASC 852
  following emergence), Legacy recorded net income of $3.1 million. [SRC: SEC accession 0001735828-20-000006, Form 10-K FY2019]
- As part of the Plan's implementation, "Cancellation of Predecessor equity" was
  recorded in the FY2019 10-K's statement of changes in equity, consistent with the
  extinguishment of the common stock issued in the 2018 reorganization. [SRC: SEC accession 0001735828-20-000006, Form 10-K FY2019]
- Separately, on April 26, 2019 (before the Chapter 11 filing), Legacy received a Nasdaq
  staff notice that its common stock had closed below the $1.00 minimum bid price for
  30 consecutive business days, triggering a 180-day compliance period; the notice
  stated it had no immediate effect on the stock's continued trading. [SRC: SEC accession 0001735828-19-000016, Form 8-K]

## Realized record under private ownership within the outcome window (press-sourced)
- Reuters reporting carried by Hart Energy (dated January 19, 2022, ~3.3 years after the
  September 2018 closing) states that the by-then-private Legacy Reserves had hired an
  investment bank to run a sale process for its Permian Basin and Haynesville Shale
  assets, valuing them at approximately $800 million. [SRC: https://www.hartenergy.com/exclusives/legacy-reserves-markets-permian-basin-haynesville-shale-assets-sources-say-198359]
- On March 2, 2023 (~4.5 years after closing), Legacy Reserves Inc. announced completion
  of its rebranding as Revenir Energy Inc., stating that "after several transformative
  years, Revenir has transitioned into a private company led by a new executive team";
  the announced senior leadership (CEO Richard Betz, President & CFO Michael
  Stefanoudakis, and others) were described as "all former members of the senior
  management team of Resolute Energy Corp.," a separate, unrelated Permian Basin
  operator sold to Cimarex Energy in 2019 — i.e., an executive team with no stated
  continuity to the Legacy Reserves LP/New Legacy leadership named at the 2018 closing.
  [SRC: https://oilandgaspress.com/legacy-reserves-to-become-revenir-energy/ (reproducing a March 2, 2023 Business Wire release)]

## Outcome summary (realized record only)
- Within roughly nine months of the September 2018 corporate reorganization's closing,
  the reorganized entity filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection (June 2019),
  extinguished the common stock issued to the 2018 reorganization's unitholders without
  recovery, recorded a $173.9 million net loss (including $447.9 million of
  reorganization expense) for the pre-emergence period of fiscal year 2019, emerged
  under new equity ownership in December 2019, and deregistered with the SEC in January
  2020, filing no further periodic reports after its FY2019 Form 10-K. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-19-011356, Form 8-K] [SRC: SEC accession 0001735828-20-000006, Form 10-K FY2019] [SRC: SEC accession 0001735828-20-000006, Form 10-K FY2019]
- Within the fixed ~2021-2023 outcome window, press reporting documents the private
  company marketing roughly $800 million of Permian Basin and Haynesville Shale assets
  for sale (January 2022) and then rebranding as Revenir Energy Inc. under an entirely
  new executive team with no stated tie to the leadership represented at the 2018
  closing (March 2023). [SRC: https://www.hartenergy.com/exclusives/legacy-reserves-markets-permian-basin-haynesville-shale-assets-sources-say-198359] [SRC: https://oilandgaspress.com/legacy-reserves-to-become-revenir-energy/]
