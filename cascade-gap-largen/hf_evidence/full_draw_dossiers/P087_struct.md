## Transaction and parties

- Corning Incorporated and The Dow Chemical Company each owned half of Dow Corning
  Corporation, described by Corning as "an equity company headquartered in Michigan that
  manufactures silicone products worldwide," offering more than 7,000 products and
  services. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-12-056163, Form 10-K for fiscal year 2011, Item 1 Business]
- Dow Corning was the majority owner (63%) of Hemlock Semiconductor Group, a producer of
  high-purity polycrystalline silicon for the semiconductor and solar-energy industries,
  which was consolidated into Dow Corning's own financial statements as its subsidiary.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-12-056163, Form 10-K for fiscal year 2011, Legal Proceedings / MD&A]

## What crossed the ownership boundary and what did not

- The legal vehicle was a separately incorporated, non-consolidated entity: Corning
  accounted for its 50% interest as an equity-method investment reported in Note 7
  (Investments) to Corning's consolidated financial statements, rather than consolidating
  Dow Corning's operations into Corning's own. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-12-056163, Form 10-K for fiscal year 2011, Note 7 Investments]
- Governance overlap without operational consolidation: three of Corning's own executive
  officers held board seats at Dow Corning — Vice Chairman and CFO James B. Flaws,
  Executive Vice President Lawrence D. McRae, and Chief Technology Officer Joseph A.
  Miller each served as a director of Dow Corning Corporation; McRae additionally sat on
  the board of Samsung Corning Precision Materials Co., Ltd. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-12-056163, Form 10-K for fiscal year 2011, Executive Officers of the Registrant]
- The customer-facing product (silicone products) and the processes that produce and
  deliver it remained inside Dow Corning's own operating entity; Corning did not operate
  Dow Corning's manufacturing directly and instead recorded a commercial, arm's-length
  purchase relationship (Corning's purchases from Dow Corning totaled $22 million in
  2011) alongside cash distributions received from the venture ($310 million in
  dividends in 2011) — consistent with an equity-earnings relationship rather than an
  operationally integrated one. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-12-056163, Form 10-K for fiscal year 2011, Note 7 Investments]

## Bridging / support arrangement at the shared substrate

- Corning had agreed to provide a credit facility related to Dow Corning, but funding of
  that facility would be required only if Dow Corning were unable to meet its scheduled
  funding obligations under its confirmed Bankruptcy Plan — a contingent financial
  backstop rather than an operating integration. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-12-056163, Form 10-K for fiscal year 2011, Commitments and Contingencies]
- The legal vehicle carried a pre-existing encumbrance from its own history: Dow Corning
  had filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in May 1995 over product-liability
  claims and emerged in June 2004 under a confirmed Plan of Reorganization funding a
  Settlement Trust and Litigation Facility for tort claimants; as of December 31, 2011
  Dow Corning had recorded a reserve for this litigation of $1.6 billion. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-12-056163, Form 10-K for fiscal year 2011, Note 17 Commitments, Contingencies and Guarantees]

## Structural summary (closing-era only)

- At this closing-era snapshot, Dow Corning existed as a separately incorporated,
  non-consolidated 50/50 equity joint venture between Corning and Dow Chemical: the
  customer-facing product and the processes producing it stayed inside the venture's own
  entity (with its own consolidated majority-owned subsidiary, Hemlock Semiconductor
  Group), governance was bridged by Corning placing three of its own executives on the
  venture's board without operational consolidation, and the shared financial substrate
  was bridged only by a contingent credit-facility backstop tied to the venture's
  bankruptcy-plan obligations rather than an integrated shared-services arrangement.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-12-056163, Form 10-K for fiscal year 2011, Item 1 Business; Note 7 Investments; Note 17 Commitments, Contingencies and Guarantees]
