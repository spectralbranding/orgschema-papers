# Structural sub-dossier — DuPont Fabros Technology, Inc. combination with Digital Realty Trust, Inc. (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Transaction and parties at closing
- Digital Realty Trust, Inc. ("DLR") and DuPont Fabros Technology, Inc. ("DFT") each
  unanimously approved an Agreement and Plan of Merger dated June 8, 2017, structured as
  (i) a merger of DFT into a wholly owned DLR subsidiary ("REIT Merger Sub," DFT's
  Surviving Entity), and (ii) a parallel merger of a DLR OP subsidiary into DFT's
  operating partnership, with the DFT operating partnership surviving. [SRC: SEC
  accession 0001193125-17-257795, Form DEFM14A]
- Each DFT common share converts into 0.545 DLR common shares (the "exchange ratio"), a
  stock-for-stock consideration not adjusted for market-price changes before closing;
  the parties expected to close "as soon as reasonably practicable" after stockholder
  approvals. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-257795, Form DEFM14A]

## Business model / customer-facing asset at each party
- DFT is described as designing and operating "innovative, multi-tenant, wholesale data
  centers" (12 data centers, 3.5 million gross square feet, 301.5 megawatts of customer
  power). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-257795, Form DEFM14A]
- DLR is described as owning, acquiring, developing and operating data centers, and
  providing data center, colocation, and interconnection solutions — a broader service
  scope than DFT's wholesale-only description. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-257795,
  Form DEFM14A]

## Controlling intent / legal vehicle / governance at closing
- The Combined Company was structured to retain the name "Digital Realty Trust, Inc.,"
  continue trading as NYSE:DLR, retain DLR's pre-merger executive officers (with A.
  William Stein continuing as CEO), and expand its board to 12 seats: the 10 then-current
  DLR directors plus only two DFT-designated directors. [SRC: SEC accession
  0001193125-17-257795, Form DEFM14A]
- Continuing DLR stockholders were estimated to hold approximately 77% of the Combined
  Company's stock and former DFT holders approximately 23%. Both companies stated they
  had operated, and intended to continue operating, so as to qualify as REITs. [SRC: SEC
  accession 0001193125-17-257795, Form DEFM14A]

## People / organization provisions at closing
- The DFT Board approved a severance program for DFT's non-executive employees in
  connection with the transaction. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-257795, Form DEFM14A]
- DFT's named executive officers held "double-trigger" employment/severance agreements
  paying cash severance and continued health benefits if terminated without cause (or
  resigning for "good reason," including reassignment away from DFT's headquarters)
  within three months before or 12 months after closing. [SRC: SEC accession
  0001193125-17-257795, Form DEFM14A]
- The joint proxy statement disclosed that Combined Company success would depend in part
  on retaining key DLR and DFT employees, that such employees "may depart either before
  or after the mergers because of issues relating to the uncertainty and difficulty of
  integration," and that no assurance of retention "to the same extent as in the past"
  could be given. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-257795, Form DEFM14A]

## Stated integration difficulties disclosed at signing
- Disclosed risks to the anticipated cost savings (attributed to "the elimination of
  duplicative corporate functions") included "the complexities associated with managing
  the combined businesses out of several different locations and integrating personnel
  from the two companies," and "the additional complexities of combining two companies
  with different histories, cultures, regulatory restrictions, markets and customer
  bases," which could cause "distraction of the Combined Company's management" and
  "disruption of the Combined Company's ongoing business." [SRC: SEC accession
  0001193125-17-257795, Form DEFM14A]
- No transition-services agreement, shared-services agreement, or supply arrangement
  bridging a shared operating substrate between DLR and DFT was located in the reviewed
  sections of the joint proxy statement/prospectus. [UNVERIFIED]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction was an all-stock, whole-company combination in which DFT's
  customer-facing wholesale-data-center portfolio and its corporate legal entity crossed
  into the Combined Company via merger, while pre-existing DLR management (CEO and other
  executive officers) and a DLR board majority (10 of 12 seats) were structured to retain
  control of governance and operations, with DFT contributing two board designees and a
  non-executive-employee severance program rather than a stated retention mandate. [SRC:
  SEC accession 0001193125-17-257795, Form DEFM14A]
