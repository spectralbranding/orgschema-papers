# Structural sub-dossier — Exterran Corporation separation from Exterran Holdings (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Transaction and parties at closing
- Exterran Holdings, Inc. (the parent) effected a pro rata stock distribution spinning
  off Exterran Corporation, a previously wholly owned subsidiary; in connection with the
  spin-off Exterran Holdings changed its name to "Archrock, Inc." (NYSE: AROC) and the
  spun-off registrant traded on the NYSE under the symbol "EXTN." [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-15-006019, Form 10-12B EX-99.1 information statement]
- The distribution ratio was one share of Exterran Corporation common stock for every two
  shares of Exterran Holdings common stock; upon completion the former parent's
  shareholders owned 100% of the new company and Archrock retained no ownership interest. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-15-006019, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The separation was intended to qualify as a tax-free reorganization under Sections 355
  and 368(a)(1)(D) of the Internal Revenue Code; shareholder approval was not required. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-15-006019, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## What crossed the ownership boundary at the tier level
- The new company received the assets and liabilities of the former parent's international
  contract operations, international aftermarket services, and global fabrication (product
  sales) businesses (the "second contribution"); the former parent's U.S. contract
  operations and U.S. aftermarket services businesses were transferred to a separate entity
  that remained with Exterran Holdings/Archrock. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-15-006019, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The customer-facing operations that crossed comprised three reporting segments — contract
  operations, aftermarket services, and product sales — spanning design, engineering,
  manufacture, installation and sale of natural gas compression packages and oil/gas
  production, treating and processing equipment, plus operation and maintenance services,
  in approximately 30 countries worldwide. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-15-006019, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The new company employed approximately 7,000 people worldwide at separation. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-15-006019, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Pursuant to the separation and distribution agreement, the new company acquired all
  rights to the "Exterran" name and trademarks; Archrock was required to discontinue use of
  that name within 180 days after completion. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-15-006019, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Shared substrate that did NOT cross intact (bridged, not transferred whole)
- The business had not been operated as a separate, stand-alone company; corporate
  functions were historically provided centrally by the parent, including finance, legal,
  risk management, tax, treasury, information technology and systems, human resources,
  internal audit, and provision of real property, with their costs allocated to the
  business rather than existing as a free-standing operating substrate. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-15-006019, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Some benefit-plan infrastructure ran in the opposite direction: the former parent's
  employees historically participated in health and welfare plans sponsored by the entity
  being separated, so Archrock had to establish substantially similar replacement plans for
  its own employees at separation. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-15-006019, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Contractual arrangements bridging the shared substrate (closing-time)
- Separation and Distribution Agreement — identifies the assets, liabilities and contracts
  to be transferred, assumed or retained; transfers occur on an "as is, where is" basis;
  imposes a three-year mutual noncompete (U.S. vs. outside-U.S. services and worldwide
  product sales) and a two-year mutual no-solicit/no-hire of the other company's employees. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-15-006019, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Transition Services Agreement — each party provides the other, on an interim basis
  (generally up to one year per service), accounting, administrative, payroll, human
  resources, environmental health and safety, real estate, fleet, financial audit support,
  legal, tax, treasury and other corporate services; the personnel performing them are the
  new company's own employees and/or independent contractors. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-15-006019, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Tax Matters Agreement — allocates tax rights, responsibilities and pre-spin consolidated
  group tax liabilities and restricts actions that could disqualify the tax-free treatment. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-15-006019, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Employee Matters Agreement — allocates compensation and benefit-plan liabilities; splits
  outstanding equity awards between the two companies; transfers 401(k) and non-qualified
  deferred-compensation account balances to each employee's post-distribution employer;
  transfers employment-related liabilities to post-distribution employers, with liabilities
  for employees who served both businesses split 50/50. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-15-006019, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Supply Agreement (with related storage agreements) — the new company would supply Archrock
  and Archrock Partners with newly fabricated compression equipment on an exclusive basis
  under an initial two-year term; separate services agreements covered installation, start-up
  and commissioning. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-15-006019, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction separated an integrated customer-facing product-and-services operation
  (fabrication/product sales, contract operations, aftermarket services, and the "Exterran"
  marks) into a new standalone company, while the corporate service substrate (finance,
  legal, tax, treasury, IT, HR, internal audit, real property) — historically centralized in
  the parent rather than owned by the business — was allocated at closing and bridged by a
  transition services agreement plus tax, employee-matters, supply and services agreements
  rather than transferred as an intact operating whole. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-15-006019, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
