# Outcome sub-dossier — American Airlines Group Inc. realized record, fixed ~4-year post-closing window (FY2015-FY2017)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. The registrant continued to file periodic reports
> under the same SEC filer (the pre-transaction registrant was renamed rather than
> deregistered), so the realized record below is drawn from its own subsequent Form
> 10-K. The outcome window is fixed at the FY2017 Form 10-K (filed 2018-02-21), roughly
> four years after the transaction's Effective Date, with 2015-2016 comparatives drawn
> from the same filing's selected financial data. This sub-dossier contains no
> information about how the transaction was structured at closing.

## Realized financial trajectory
- The FY2017 Form 10-K reports net income of $1,919 million for 2017, $2,676 million for
  2016, and $7,610 million for 2015, and total operating revenues of $42,207 million
  (2017), $40,180 million (2016), and $40,990 million (2015). [SRC: SEC accession 0000006201-18-000009, Form 10-K, Item 6 Selected Financial Data]
- The filing does not report a goodwill-impairment charge for the 2015-2017 period; the
  goodwill disclosure in this filing is limited to the standing annual impairment-testing
  policy and a forward-looking risk-factor caution about possible future impairment. [SRC: SEC accession 0000006201-18-000009, Form 10-K, Item 1A / Item 8 Notes]

## Documented cost of continuing integration through the window
- The filing separately reports "Merger integration costs" as a component of special
  items in each of the three years shown: $826 million (2015), $514 million (2016), and
  $273 million (2017) — a declining but still material charge four years after the
  transaction closed. [SRC: SEC accession 0000006201-18-000009, Form 10-K, Item 7 Special Items]
- The filing attributes these costs to information-technology work, professional fees,
  re-branding of aircraft and airport facilities, training, and (in 2015 and 2016) the
  alignment of labor-union contracts. [SRC: SEC accession 0000006201-18-000009, Form 10-K, Item 7 Special Items]

## Documented status of the operational/process interface
- The filing states that the company had, by the report date, "successfully integrated"
  several systems, "including our customer reservations system and our pilot and fleet
  scheduling system," while stating that "several additional important system
  integration projects" remained to be completed. [SRC: SEC accession 0000006201-18-000009, Form 10-K, Item 1A Risk Factors]
- As a risk factor covering the remaining integration work, the filing states: "The
  integration of these systems in a number of prior airline mergers has taken longer,
  been more disruptive and cost more than originally forecast." [SRC: SEC accession 0000006201-18-000009, Form 10-K, Item 1A Risk Factors]

## Documented status of the labor/organization interface
- The filing states that joint collective bargaining agreements had been reached with
  post-transaction employee groups, "except the maintenance, fleet service, stock
  clerks, maintenance control technicians and maintenance training instructors"
  represented by a named labor association, who remained covered by separate,
  legacy collective bargaining agreements that were not scheduled to become amendable
  until the third quarter of 2018 — more than four years after the transaction's
  closing. [SRC: SEC accession 0000006201-18-000009, Form 10-K, Item 1 Employees]
- The filing states that, until those legacy agreements become amendable, negotiations
  toward a joint contract for that workgroup proceed outside the ordinary bargaining
  process, and no work stoppage or other form of self-help is permitted in the interim.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0000006201-18-000009, Form 10-K, Item 1 Employees]

## Outcome summary (realized record only)
- Over the FY2015-FY2017 window, the surviving public registrant reported sustained
  profitability and revenue in the low-$40-billion range each year, alongside a
  declining-but-still-material annual charge for merger-integration costs ($826M to
  $273M) and two specifically documented interfaces still not fully aligned four years
  after closing: an operational-systems integration effort the company itself flagged
  as incomplete and historically disruptive in prior industry mergers, and a labor
  workgroup still under separate legacy contracts pending a later amendability date.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0000006201-18-000009, Form 10-K, Item 6 Selected Financial Data; Item 7 Special Items; Item 1A Risk Factors; Item 1 Employees]
