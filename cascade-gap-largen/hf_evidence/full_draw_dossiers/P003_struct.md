# Structural sub-dossier — CareFusion Corporation separation from Cardinal Health (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Transaction and parties at closing
- Cardinal Health, Inc. (the Parent) announced plans to separate its clinical and medical
  products businesses into a separate, publicly traded company named CareFusion
  Corporation. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-09-008871, Form 10-12B EX-99.1 information statement]
- The separation was structured as a spin-off: a pro rata distribution of at least 80% of
  CareFusion common stock to Cardinal Health shareholders, at a distribution ratio of one
  CareFusion share for each Cardinal Health common share held on the record date. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-09-008871, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Immediately following the distribution, Cardinal Health was to retain no more than 19.9%
  of CareFusion's outstanding common stock. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-09-008871, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- CareFusion Corporation was incorporated in Delaware on January 14, 2009 and, at the time
  of the information statement, was a wholly owned subsidiary of Cardinal Health. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-09-008871, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The separation was expected to be tax-free to Cardinal Health shareholders, subject to a
  requested IRS ruling; no shareholder approval of the separation was required. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-09-008871, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Customer-facing product/asset transferred
- The business separated was a global medical technology company; its primary product
  brands included Alaris IV infusion systems (with proprietary Guardrails software), Pyxis
  automated medication and supply dispensing systems, AVEA/Pulmonetic/Jaeger/SensorMedics
  respiratory and pulmonary products, ChloraPrep and MedMined infection-prevention
  products, and V. Mueller surgical instruments. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-09-008871, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- For fiscal year ended June 30, 2008 the transferred business generated revenue of
  $4.5 billion and net earnings of $663 million; ~74% of fiscal 2008 revenue was from U.S.
  customers. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-09-008871, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Operational processes and people/organization transferred
- At December 31, 2008 the business had approximately 16,700 employees (about 7,600 in the
  United States), and owned or leased 32 operating facilities worldwide handling
  manufacturing production, assembly, research, quality-assurance testing, distribution,
  packaging and sterilization. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-09-008871, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The management team moved with the business: David L. Schlotterbeck was to be Chairman
  and CEO after the separation, and while some executive officers were then officers/
  employees of Cardinal Health, upon separation none would continue as employees or
  officers of Cardinal Health. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-09-008871, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Shared corporate substrate NOT transferred whole
- General corporate functions had been provided by the Parent: for fiscal 2008 Cardinal
  Health allocated to the business ~$407 million of general corporate expenses (finance,
  legal, information technology, human resources, communications, ethics and compliance,
  shared services, employee benefits, insurance, stock-based compensation); effective with
  the separation the new company would assume responsibility for all of these functions. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-09-008871, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Working-capital and general-corporate cash needs had historically been satisfied through
  Cardinal Health's corporate-wide, centralized cash-management policies (most domestic
  cash swept to Cardinal Health daily, which then funded operating and investing
  activities); after separation the company would need to obtain its own financing. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-09-008871, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Contractual arrangements bridging the shared substrate (closing-time)
- The parties were to enter into a separation agreement plus a transition services
  agreement, a tax matters agreement, an employee matters agreement, intellectual property
  agreements and certain other commercial agreements. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-09-008871, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Under the reciprocal transition services agreement, Cardinal Health would provide the new
  company services including payroll, human resources, information systems, and procurement
  and sourcing support, while the new company would provide Cardinal Health administrative,
  HR, enterprise IT and other support; charges were generally set at cost recovery without
  profit. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-09-008871, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Transition services would terminate at various times generally ranging from 6 to 24
  months after the separation (except certain information-technology services), with the
  company anticipating completing the transition of Cardinal-provided services (except
  certain IT services) within about two years of the distribution. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-09-008871, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction detached an intact customer-facing product operation (medical-technology
  brands), together with its manufacturing/quality/distribution facilities, workforce and
  management team, from the Parent's shared centralized corporate substrate (finance/IT/HR/
  treasury/cash management), with that substrate bridged at closing by a transition services
  agreement and related tax, employee, IP and commercial agreements rather than transferred
  whole. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950123-09-008871, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
