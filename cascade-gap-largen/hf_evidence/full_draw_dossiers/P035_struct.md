# Structural sub-dossier — PNK Entertainment, Inc. ("OpCo") separation from Pinnacle Entertainment (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Transaction and parties at closing
- Pinnacle Entertainment, Inc.'s Board of Directors approved a plan to separate Pinnacle's
  real estate (except the Belterra Park property and excess land at certain locations) from
  its operations. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-410962, Form 10-12B EX-99.1 information statement]
- To effect the separation, Pinnacle's operating business was transferred to PNK
  Entertainment, Inc. ("OpCo"), a newly formed wholly owned subsidiary of Pinnacle holding
  all of Pinnacle's operating assets; Pinnacle then distributed all OpCo shares pro rata to
  Pinnacle stockholders (one OpCo share per Pinnacle share), spinning OpCo off as a
  stand-alone publicly traded company. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-410962, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Immediately after the distribution, OpCo would change its corporate name to "Pinnacle
  Entertainment, Inc."; the retained real-estate entity ("PropCo") would then merge with a
  wholly owned subsidiary of Gaming and Leisure Properties, Inc. ("GLPI"), pursuant to an
  Agreement and Plan of Merger dated July 20, 2015. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-410962, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- OpCo's common stock was to be listed on the NASDAQ Global Select Market under the symbol
  "PNK." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-410962, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## What crossed the ownership boundary at the tier level
- The customer-facing operating business crossed into OpCo: OpCo would own and operate
  fifteen gaming facilities across Colorado, Indiana, Iowa, Louisiana, Mississippi, Missouri,
  Nevada, and Ohio; hold a majority interest in the racing license owner and a management
  contract for Retama Park Racetrack; and operate the Heartland Poker Tour. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-410962, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The underlying real property did NOT stay with the operating business: fourteen of the
  fifteen gaming facilities' real estate was separated to PropCo and, upon the merger, owned
  by GLPI, then leased back to an OpCo subsidiary — i.e., the operations crossed into OpCo
  while the real estate they occupy moved the opposite direction. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-410962, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Management and people were structured to stay with the operating business: "It is
  anticipated that Pinnacle's Chairman of the Board and its Chief Executive Officer will
  serve as OpCo's Chairman of the Board and its Chief Executive Officer, respectively,"
  Pinnacle's independent directors would serve as OpCo directors, and such individuals would
  "not continue as officers or directors of PropCo following the merger." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-410962, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- "While we expect that our employees and executive officers will remain the same as under
  Pinnacle, we cannot assure you that this will be the case following the separation." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-410962, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Contractual arrangements bridging the separated substrate (closing-time)
- The parties entered a Separation and Distribution Agreement (OpCo, Pinnacle, and, as to
  Article VIII, GLPI), an Employee Matters Agreement, a Tax Matters Agreement dated July 20,
  2015 (Pinnacle, GLPI, and PNK Entertainment), and a Master Lease. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-410962, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The Master Lease is a "triple-net" lease under which Pinnacle MLS, LLC (an OpCo subsidiary,
  "Tenant") leases from the Landlord (PropCo, succeeded by a GLPI subsidiary) the real
  property associated with fourteen gaming facilities; the initial aggregate annual rent is
  $377 million, payable monthly, with an initial ten-year term extendable for up to five
  five-year renewal terms. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-410962, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Under the triple-net structure the Tenant is additionally responsible for facility
  maintenance, required insurance, and taxes/assessments on the leased properties — i.e., the
  operating substrate for the separated real estate is re-attached to OpCo contractually
  rather than transferred whole. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-410962, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction split a single going gaming operator into an operating company (OpCo,
  taking the business, management, employees, and gaming operations) and a real-estate
  company (PropCo, taking the properties and merging into a REIT, GLPI), reattaching the
  separated real estate to the operations through a triple-net Master Lease and a set of
  separation, employee-matters, and tax-matters agreements. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-410962, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
