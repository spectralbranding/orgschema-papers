# Structural sub-dossier — Nationstar Mortgage Holdings Inc. / WMIH Corp. merger (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Transaction and parties at closing
- On February 12, 2018, WMIH Corp. ("WMIH") and its wholly owned merger subsidiary,
  Wand Merger Corporation, entered into an Agreement and Plan of Merger with Nationstar
  Mortgage Holdings Inc. ("Nationstar"), providing for Nationstar to merge with and into
  Wand Merger Corporation, with Nationstar surviving as a direct, wholly owned subsidiary
  of WMIH. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-180666, Form DEFM14A cover letter to stockholders]
- Nationstar stockholders could elect, per share, either $18.00 in cash or 12.7793
  shares of WMIH common stock, subject to proration so total cash paid equaled exactly
  $1,225,885,248.00; roughly 416 million WMIH shares were expected to be issued, leaving
  former Nationstar stockholders with approximately 35% of WMIH's outstanding shares
  immediately after closing. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-180666, Form DEFM14A cover letter to stockholders]
- Nationstar, formed in Delaware in 2011, described itself as one of the largest
  residential loan servicers in the U.S., earning fees through servicing, origination and
  transaction-based services for single-family residences. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-180666, Form DEFM14A "The Parties" p.22]
- Among the WMIH board's stated reasons for the merger was that Nationstar's flagship
  brand, Mr. Cooper, was the largest non-bank mortgage servicer in the U.S., and that
  Nationstar served over 3.2 million customers as a leading mortgage servicing platform.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-180666, Form DEFM14A "WMIH's Reasons for the Merger" p.113]
- WMIH, formerly Washington Mutual, Inc. / WMI Holdings Corp., had operated since its
  2012 bankruptcy emergence solely as a legacy reinsurance run-off business (through its
  subsidiary WM Mortgage Reinsurance Company) with four full-time employees for 2017 and
  approximately $614.1 million in consolidated total assets as of December 31, 2017; it
  was actively seeking a "Qualified Acquisition" (as defined in its charter) in the
  financial-services sector to deploy against its net operating loss carryforwards. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-180666, Form DEFM14A "The Parties" p.21]
- The merger closed July 31, 2018, when Wand Merger Corporation merged with and into
  Nationstar, effecting a change in control of Nationstar, which continued as a direct,
  wholly owned subsidiary of WMIH. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-233669, Form 8-K Item 5.01]

## What crossed the ownership boundary and what did not, by tier
- Controlling intent / imprint: pursuant to the merger agreement, WMIH's post-closing
  board was fixed to consist of three incumbent WMIH directors (Steve Scheiwe, Tagar
  Olson, Chris Harrington), three current Nationstar directors selected by Nationstar,
  and the CEO of Nationstar (Jay Bray); WMIH's other four incumbent directors were
  expected to resign. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-180666, Form DEFM14A "Management and Board of Directors of WMIH After the Merger" p.145]
- Business/value-capture model: WMIH and Nationstar stated that, following the merger,
  the combined company would be managed by current members of Nationstar's management
  team, i.e. Nationstar's operating business became the substantive business of the
  post-merger public company rather than WMIH's prior run-off/shell activity. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-180666, Form DEFM14A "Management and Board of Directors of WMIH After the Merger" p.145]
- Legal vehicle: the public reporting entity remained WMIH's pre-existing Delaware
  corporate shell (Commission registrant), while Nationstar's own certificate of
  incorporation was amended and restated and it continued to exist as a subsidiary
  entity rather than being dissolved. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-233669, Form 8-K Item 5.03]
- Customer-facing product/asset: the residential mortgage servicing and origination
  business operating under the "Mr. Cooper" brand, and its customer base, transferred
  intact into the surviving subsidiary; no product or brand divestiture is described in
  the merger agreement. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-180666, Form DEFM14A "WMIH's Reasons for the Merger" p.113]
- People/management: at the Effective Time, all of Nationstar's pre-merger directors
  (Jay Bray, Robert Gidel, Roy Guthrie, Brett Hawkins, Michael D. Malone) resigned;
  Merger Sub's two directors (Charles E. Smith, Thomas L. Fairfield) briefly became
  Nationstar's directors and then also resigned, after which WMIH, as sole stockholder,
  appointed Jay Bray and Amar Patel as Nationstar's new directors — Jay Bray being
  Nationstar's own pre-merger Chairman, President and CEO. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-233669, Form 8-K Item 5.02]
- Processes/organization: the joint proxy statement/prospectus discloses no transition
  services agreement or shared-services arrangement between WMIH and Nationstar bridging
  operational processes; WMIH's four-employee legacy operation and Nationstar's
  operating processes are described as separate, with WMIH's pre-existing operations
  (reinsurance run-off) continuing in parallel rather than being integrated into
  Nationstar's mortgage-servicing processes. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-180666, Form DEFM14A "The Parties" p.21]

## Governance and financing terms fixed at closing
- Nationstar was required to pay WMIH a termination fee of $65 million upon termination
  of the merger agreement in certain circumstances involving competing acquisition
  proposals. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-180666, Form DEFM14A "Summary" p.20]
- If WMIH's stockholders failed to approve the stock issuance and the merger agreement
  was terminated on that basis, WMIH owed Nationstar $29.4 million in expense
  compensation; if WMIH then entered into or consummated an alternative business
  combination within twelve months, WMIH owed Nationstar a further $35.6 million (if
  the transaction related to a proposal made before the termination) or an additional
  $18.6 million (if it did not). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-180666, Form DEFM14A "Risk Factors" p.38]
- Fortress Investment Group entered a voting agreement to vote approximately 69% of
  Nationstar's voting power in favor of the merger; KKR Entities holding approximately
  24% of WMIH's voting power entered a separate voting agreement covering their WMIH
  shares. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-180666, Form DEFM14A "Summary" pp.14-15]
- Concurrently with signing, Wand Merger Corporation entered a debt commitment letter for
  a one-year senior unsecured term loan bridge facility of up to $2.75 billion (or, in
  lieu of the bridge facility, up to $2.75 billion of senior unsecured notes); Nationstar,
  as surviving corporation, assumed Wand Merger Corporation's obligations under this debt
  financing upon consummation of the merger. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-180666, Form DEFM14A "Description of Debt Financing" p.186]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction placed Nationstar's intact mortgage-servicing/origination business,
  brand, customer base and (via reappointment of its own CEO and a director) much of its
  leadership into a wholly owned subsidiary of a public shell corporation (WMIH) that
  itself had no comparable operating business, contributing instead its public listing,
  balance sheet and net operating loss carryforwards; the reconstituted parent board
  mixed incumbent WMIH directors with Nationstar-designated directors, and WMIH's
  pre-existing legacy operations continued in parallel rather than merging into
  Nationstar's operating processes. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-180666, Form DEFM14A "The Parties" p.21-22, "WMIH's Reasons for the Merger" p.113, "Management and Board of Directors of WMIH After the Merger" p.145]
