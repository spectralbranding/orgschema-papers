# Structural sub-dossier — Phillips Edison Grocery Center REIT I acquisition of its external manager's assets (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Transaction and parties at closing
- Phillips Edison Grocery Center REIT I, Inc. ("PECO I"), a Maryland corporation, and
  Phillips Edison Grocery Center Operating Partnership I, L.P. ("PECO I OP") entered a
  Contribution Agreement dated May 18, 2017 with Phillips Edison Limited Partnership
  ("PELP") — the parent company of PECO I's external advisor and property manager — and
  other named contributors, under which PECO I would acquire certain real estate assets,
  PELP's third-party asset management business, and certain other assets (the "PELP
  Transaction"). [SRC: SEC accession 0001626129-17-000444, DEFM14A Notice of 2017 Annual Meeting]
- In exchange for the Contributors' equity interests in the "Contributed Companies," PECO
  I OP agreed to issue 40,360,504 OP Units and pay $50.0 million in cash at closing (the
  "Closing Consideration"), with the Contributors eligible for up to 12,490,196 further OP
  Units as "Earn-out Consideration" contingent on stated milestones, including a
  stockholder liquidity event and a separate capital-raise target for an affiliated entity,
  Phillips Edison Grocery Center REIT III, Inc. ("PECO III"). [SRC: SEC accession 0001626129-17-000444, DEFM14A Q&A "What is the effect of the PELP Transaction?"]
- Upon completion, the Contributed Companies were to become subsidiaries of PECO I OP, and
  PECO I stated it would thereby become "self-managed and self-advised," ending the
  advisory and property-management fee arrangements it paid to its then-external advisor
  and property manager, Phillips Edison & Company, Ltd. [SRC: SEC accession 0001626129-17-000444, DEFM14A Q&A "What is the effect of the PELP Transaction?"]
- The transaction was to be accounted for as a business combination under ASC 805, with
  PECO I treated as the accounting acquirer. [SRC: SEC accession 0001626129-17-000444, DEFM14A "Interests of Certain Persons in the PELP Transaction" section]
- PECO I's Special Committee of independent directors recommended the transaction after a
  multi-year evaluation involving "dozens of telephonic and in-person meetings," and the
  uninterested directors present unanimously approved its terms. [SRC: SEC accession 0001626129-17-000444, DEFM14A letter to stockholders]

## Controlling intent and its fusion with the acquired tier
- Jeffrey S. Edison, PECO I's Chair of the Board and Chief Executive Officer, together
  with other members of PECO I's management team, were disclosed as investors in PELP who
  "form the management team of" PELP; these same individuals were to receive consideration
  and OP Unit grants under the Contribution Agreement and to become participants in a
  newly adopted executive severance plan effective at closing. [SRC: SEC accession 0001626129-17-000444, DEFM14A "Interests of Certain Persons in the PELP Transaction"]
- After the closing, the same named executive officers were disclosed as continuing to
  hold equity interests in PELP and to keep their existing roles at PECO I, while PELP
  would retain and continue to operate certain real properties and "legacy funds" not
  contributed to PECO I OP, with the Property Manager (by then indirectly reached through
  PECO I) continuing to provide asset-management services to those retained PELP assets —
  a structure the proxy statement itself identified as a potential conflict of interest.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001626129-17-000444, DEFM14A "Continued Interests in Legacy PELP"]

## What crossed the boundary at closing: product, process, people
- The customer-facing assets to cross into PECO I OP were a portfolio of 76
  grocery-anchored shopping centers (approximately 8.7 million square feet), together with
  PELP's third-party asset management business — which earns acquisition, disposition, and
  asset-management fees from affiliated non-traded REITs including Phillips Edison Grocery
  Center REIT II, Inc. and PECO III — and a captive insurance business. [SRC: SEC accession 0001626129-17-000444, DEFM14A Q&A "What is the effect of the PELP Transaction?"]
- PECO I OP agreed to form a taxable REIT subsidiary (the "Manager") to which the Property
  Manager entity, certain employees, and certain third-party investment-management
  agreements would be transferred — the operational staff and management contracts that
  had produced and delivered the asset-management product were structured to move together
  with the product itself. [SRC: SEC accession 0001626129-17-000444, DEFM14A "The PELP Transaction"]
- Before the transaction, the proxy statement disclosed that PECO I "currently has no
  employees of its own" and would have employees, and bear employer liabilities and
  benefit-plan costs, only after the PELP Transaction closed. [SRC: SEC accession 0001626129-17-000444, DEFM14A Risk Factors "PECO I may be exposed to risks to which PECO I has not historically been exposed"]
- The Contribution Agreement obligated the Contributors to use "reasonable efforts to keep
  available the services of its employees" through closing, and the proxy disclosed a risk
  that key employees of the Contributed Companies "may depart either before or after the
  Closing," with no assurance PECO I would be able to retain them. [SRC: SEC accession 0001626129-17-000444, DEFM14A Risk Factors "PECO I may be unable to retain key employees"]

## Contractual bridge for the substrate that did not fully transfer
- At closing, PELP and the newly formed Manager (a PECO I OP subsidiary) were to enter
  into a Services Agreement under which the Manager would provide finance/accounting/tax,
  insurance-procurement, corporate-recordkeeping, and wind-down/disposition services to the
  PELP entities and assets that were NOT contributed, in exchange for a $200,000 annual
  fee; the Services Agreement was to remain in effect for five years, terminable earlier by
  PELP at will or by either party for material breach. [SRC: SEC accession 0001626129-17-000444, DEFM14A "The Services Agreement"]
- The Services Agreement also provided for PECO I OP to license the "Phillips Edison" and
  "PECO" names back to the retained PELP entities on a non-exclusive, royalty-free basis
  for the earlier of 15 years or the wind-down of the licensed entity. [SRC: SEC accession 0001626129-17-000444, DEFM14A "The Services Agreement"]
- Other transaction documents contemplated at closing included an Escrow Agreement
  (holding back 7.5% of the OP Unit closing consideration for indemnification claims), a
  Tax Protection Agreement, an Equityholder Agreement (imposing lock-up periods on certain
  OP Unit and share recipients), an Executive Severance Plan, a Third Amended and Restated
  Agreement of Limited Partnership for PECO I OP, and Third Amended and Restated Bylaws for
  PECO I. [SRC: SEC accession 0001626129-17-000444, DEFM14A "The PELP Transaction Documents" table of contents; Q&A "What is the effect of the PELP Transaction?"]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- As structured, PECO I's acquisition of PELP's real estate portfolio and fee-earning
  asset-management business converted PECO I from an externally advised, employee-less
  REIT into a self-managed, self-advised one: the acquired product (real estate plus
  management contracts) was structured to cross together with its producing processes and
  staff via a newly formed taxable REIT subsidiary, while a residual layer of assets and
  legacy funds remained with the seller (PELP) under PELP's own continuing operation,
  bridged to the surviving public REIT by a services agreement, a name-license, a tax
  protection agreement, and lock-up/severance arrangements — with senior PECO I management
  holding continuing equity interests on both sides of the transaction. [SRC: SEC accession 0001626129-17-000444, DEFM14A "The PELP Transaction"; "Continued Interests in Legacy PELP"; "The Services Agreement"]
