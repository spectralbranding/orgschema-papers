## The drawn closing-era filing (what the accession actually reports)
- The drawn closing-era filing is the registrant's Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2009 — the first annual report of AOL Inc. as a newly independent, publicly traded company; it describes the just-completed separation and the standalone platform, not a single deal-terms agreement. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-045310, 10-K FY2009]
- In the fourth quarter of 2009 the Board of Directors of Time Warner Inc. approved the complete legal and structural separation of AOL Inc. from Time Warner; the spin-off occurred on December 9, 2009, when Time Warner distributed all of its AOL shares to Time Warner shareholders at a ratio of one AOL share per 11 Time Warner shares held as of the November 27, 2009 record date. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-045310, 10-K FY2009]
- Prior to the spin-off Time Warner caused a reorganization of AOL's corporate structure: on July 8, 2009 AOL Holdings LLC was converted into a Delaware corporation named AOL Inc., and substantially all of the assets and liabilities of AOL LLC (excluding guarantees of Time Warner and other non-AOL affiliate indebtedness) were transferred to and assumed by AOL Inc. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-045310, 10-K FY2009]
- On December 10, 2009, after completion of the spin-off, the common stock began trading "regular way" on the New York Stock Exchange under the symbol "AOL." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-045310, 10-K FY2009]

## Business focus and value-capture model at the closing era
- The registrant describes itself as "a leading global web services company with an extensive suite of brands and offerings and a substantial worldwide audience," whose business "spans online content, products and services that we offer to consumers, publishers and advertisers." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-045310, 10-K FY2009]
- In August 2006 AOL "fundamentally shifted the primary strategic focus" of the business from generating subscription access revenues to attracting and engaging Internet consumers and monetizing that audience through advertising; it describes this as a shift to "an online advertising-supported business model." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-045310, 10-K FY2009]
- Advertising is marketed to advertisers on both AOL Properties and third-party sites under the brand "AOL Advertising," and to publishers on the "Third Party Network" under the brand "Advertising.com"; the legacy subscription access service is retained as "a valuable distribution channel" for AOL Properties. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-045310, 10-K FY2009]

## Platform-assembly / acquisition program (customer-facing products acquired)
- The registrant states it has "developed and acquired a number of platforms" to aggregate, distribute and consume local content, and that its local offerings include Patch, "a community-specific news and information platform dedicated to providing comprehensive and trusted local coverage for individual towns and communities." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-045310, 10-K FY2009]
- The company discloses that "[s]ince January 1, 2008, we have acquired 12 businesses and we are likely to make additional acquisitions and strategic investments in the future," and that integrating acquired companies "involve[s] a substantial commitment of resources." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-045310, 10-K FY2009]
- Named ad-technology/property acquisitions carried into the standalone platform include: Bebo, Inc. (global social-media network), completed May 14, 2008 for $859.8 million net of cash, recognizing $765.8 million of goodwill and $86.5 million of intangibles; Perfiliate Limited (buy.at), completed February 5, 2008 for $125.2 million net of cash ($99.3 million goodwill). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-045310, 10-K FY2009]
- Earlier ad-technology acquisitions: Quigo Technologies, Inc. (site-/content-targeted advertising), completed December 19, 2007 for $346.4 million net of cash ($239.6 million goodwill); TACODA, Inc. (behavioral-targeting advertising), completed September 6, 2007 for $273.9 million net of cash ($219.1 million goodwill); ADTECH AG (online ad-serving technology), majority stake acquired May 15, 2007 for $88.1 million cash. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-045310, 10-K FY2009]
- Two closing-era additions: Patch Media Corporation, purchased June 10, 2009 for $7.0 million (with an anticipated further investment of up to $50.0 million in Patch during 2010); and StudioNow, Inc. (a video content-management platform), completed January 22, 2010. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-045310, 10-K FY2009]
- Proprietary in-house-branded search/serving subsidiaries and services carried in the platform include Truveo (video search), Relegence (news search) and ADTECH AG (ad serving). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-045310, 10-K FY2009]

## Operational processes / people carried at separation, and the bridging agreements
- The global web services and subscription access businesses "share infrastructure such as data centers and network operations centers," and AOL states it "currently operate[s] as a single integrated business rather than as two separate businesses." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-045310, 10-K FY2009]
- The company acknowledges it is now responsible for "additional costs associated with being an independent, publicly-traded company," and that its historical financial statements "may not be indicative of our future performance as an independent company." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-045310, 10-K FY2009]
- The separation was bridged by a set of Time Warner agreements: a Separation and Distribution Agreement (entered November 16, 2009), a Transition Services Agreement, a Second Tax Matters Agreement, an Employee Matters Agreement, an Intellectual Property Cross-License Agreement, an IT Applications and Database Agreement, and a Master Services Agreement for ATDN and Hosting Services. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-045310, 10-K FY2009]
- The company notes these separation agreements "were negotiated in the context of our separation from Time Warner while we were still part of Time Warner" and "may not reflect terms that would have resulted from arm's-length negotiations among unaffiliated third parties." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-045310, 10-K FY2009]
- On December 9, 2009 AOL entered into a 364-day $250 million senior secured revolving credit facility; Time Warner guaranteed all of AOL's obligations under that facility. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-045310, 10-K FY2009]
- As of the disclosed period the country outside the United States with AOL's largest employee population was India, with over 1,000 employees, and the company states it was "in the midst of a significant" restructuring. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-045310, 10-K FY2009]

## Structural summary (closing era only)
- The registrant became a standalone Delaware corporation on December 9, 2009 via a Time Warner spin-off, carrying an advertising-supported "global web services" model that had been assembled through a program of ad-technology and content-platform acquisitions (Bebo, buy.at, Quigo, TACODA, ADTECH, Truveo, Relegence, Patch, StudioNow), with "12 businesses" acquired since January 1, 2008. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-045310, 10-K FY2009]
- At separation, shared operating substrate (data centers, IT applications/databases, hosting, tax, IP, employee matters) was contractually bridged to the former parent through a Transition Services Agreement and a suite of related separation agreements rather than being fully self-standing, and the revolving credit facility remained Time-Warner-guaranteed. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-045310, 10-K FY2009]
