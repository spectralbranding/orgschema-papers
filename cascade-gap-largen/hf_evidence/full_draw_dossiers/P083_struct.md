# Structural sub-dossier — Dow / Saudi Aramco joint venture (Sadara Chemical Company) at closing

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Transaction and parties at closing
- The drawn filing, The Dow Chemical Company's Form 10-K for fiscal year 2010 (filed
  2011-02-18), is the Company's annual report; it does not itself set out the terms of
  a specific new joint venture, but does disclose as a company-strategy risk factor that
  "the Company is evaluating the formation of new joint ventures in emerging
  geographies to build and operate integrated, world-scale facilities." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-11-040023, Form 10-K, Risk Factors]
- The actual joint-venture agreement was executed later the same year: on October 8,
  2011, Dow Saudi Arabia Holding B.V. ("Dow HoldCo"), an indirect wholly-owned
  subsidiary of The Dow Chemical Company, entered into a Shareholders' Agreement with
  Performance Chemicals Holding Company ("Saudi Aramco HoldCo"), a wholly-owned
  subsidiary of Saudi Arabian Oil Company ("Saudi Aramco"). [SRC: SEC accession 0000947871-11-000837, Form 8-K, Item 1.01]
- The Shareholders' Agreement provided for a new limited liability company organized
  under the laws of the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, Sadara Chemical Company, to construct
  and operate a petrochemical complex at Jubail Industrial City II comprising 26
  manufacturing units, with total project investment of approximately $20 billion and
  an expectation of full operation in 2016. [SRC: SEC accession 0000947871-11-000837, Form 8-K, Item 1.01]
- Both companies' boards authorized formation of the venture on July 25, 2011, and the
  Shareholders' Agreement was signed October 8, 2011 by Saudi Aramco President and CEO
  Khalid A. Al-Falih and Dow Chairman and CEO Andrew N. Liveris at a ceremony in
  Dhahran, Saudi Arabia. [SRC: SEC accession 0000947871-11-000837, Form 8-K, Exhibit 99.1 press release]
- Initial ownership was 35 percent Dow HoldCo and 65 percent Saudi Aramco HoldCo; it was
  contemplated that, before completion of the complex, Saudi Aramco HoldCo would
  transfer a 30 percent stake to a special-purpose vehicle to be offered to the Saudi
  public via listing on the Tadawul exchange. Dow's expected direct-and-indirect equity
  contribution was no greater than $2.1 billion, including $900 million already
  committed to development expenses. The joint venture's initial term was 99 years. [SRC: SEC accession 0000947871-11-000837, Form 8-K, Item 1.01]

## What crossed the boundary at the tier level, and what did not
- Legal vehicle: Sadara is a newly formed, separately organized Saudi limited liability
  company, distinct from both parent corporate entities, rather than an existing Dow or
  Aramco operating unit re-labeled. [SRC: SEC accession 0000947871-11-000837, Form 8-K, Item 1.01]
- Business/value-capture model: the venture was described as "utilizing Dow's
  cutting-edge product technologies and Saudi Aramco's world-class project management
  and execution capabilities" — i.e., the two parents contributed different functional
  tiers (Dow: technology; Aramco: project execution) rather than either parent
  transferring a complete, pre-existing business model intact. [SRC: SEC accession 0000947871-11-000837, Form 8-K, Exhibit 99.1 press release]
- Customer-facing product/marketing: Sadara itself was to market its output only within
  a defined regional zone of eight countries including the Kingdom; Dow subsidiaries
  would market and sell on Sadara's behalf to the rest of the world under separate
  marketing and lifting agreements, for which Sadara pays marketing fees — the
  customer-facing marketing function for most of the JV's output was retained by Dow's
  own existing sales organization rather than built inside the new entity. [SRC: SEC accession 0000947871-11-000837, Form 8-K, Item 1.01 and Exhibit 99.1]
- Operational processes: Sadara itself "will build and operate" the manufacturing
  complex; Dow subsidiaries would license certain proprietary technologies and provide
  related technical support and proprietary catalysts to Sadara, with Sadara paying
  license fees and royalties — the production technology was transferred by license,
  not by moving an existing Dow production process or plant into the new entity.
  Saudi Aramco separately agreed to supply Sadara's feedstock requirements under supply
  agreements. [SRC: SEC accession 0000947871-11-000837, Form 8-K, Item 1.01]
- People/organization: the venture's workforce was to be newly recruited rather than
  transferred from either parent's existing operating staff — "by the end of 2011, Dow
  and Saudi Aramco are aiming to recruit hundreds of Saudi nationals as the first batch
  of technical trainees for competitive and unique manufacturing and engineering
  training programs." [SRC: SEC accession 0000947871-11-000837, Form 8-K, Exhibit 99.1 press release]
- Standards/culture: Sadara was to "select and employ standards and operational
  procedures ... consistent with the most stringent environmental, health and safety
  principles of the Company and Saudi Aramco," i.e. governance norms imported by
  agreement from both parents into the new entity rather than one parent's existing
  organizational culture transferring wholesale. [SRC: SEC accession 0000947871-11-000837, Form 8-K, Item 1.01]

## Contractual arrangements bridging the shared substrate at closing
- The Shareholders' Agreement contemplated that the parties would enter into further
  agreements to allocate the shared substrate among the new entity and its two parents:
  guarantees, supply agreements, marketing and lifting agreements, license agreements,
  technical service agreements, and cost-sharing agreements. [SRC: SEC accession 0000947871-11-000837, Form 8-K, Item 1.01]
- Dow undertook to (directly or indirectly) provide, until completion of the complex, a
  corporate guarantee of Sadara's project financing proportionate to Dow HoldCo's
  shareholding in Sadara. [SRC: SEC accession 0000947871-11-000837, Form 8-K, Item 1.01]
- A predecessor Omnibus Confidentiality Agreement between the Company and Saudi Aramco,
  dated April 27, 2007, governed protection of confidential business and technical
  information exchanged in connection with the project. [SRC: SEC accession 0000947871-11-000837, Form 8-K, Item 1.01]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction structured a new, separately organized 35/65 (Dow/Aramco, initially)
  joint-venture entity in which the two parents contributed different tiers rather than
  either transferring a complete existing business: Dow contributed technology
  licenses, proprietary catalysts, minority equity, a proportionate financing
  guarantee, and retained the customer-facing marketing function for most world
  markets; Saudi Aramco contributed majority equity, feedstock supply, and project
  execution capability and retained regional marketing rights; the new entity itself
  was to build its own manufacturing operations and recruit its own workforce, with the
  shared substrate bridged by a set of license, technical-service, marketing/lifting,
  supply, guarantee, and cost-sharing agreements rather than by transferring an
  intact operating business into the venture. [SRC: SEC accession 0000947871-11-000837, Form 8-K, Item 1.01 and Exhibit 99.1]
