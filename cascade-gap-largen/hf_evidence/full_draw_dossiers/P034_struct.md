# Structural sub-dossier — California Resources Corporation separation from Occidental Petroleum (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Transaction and parties at closing
- California Resources Corporation ("CRC") was a wholly owned subsidiary of Occidental
  Petroleum Corporation ("Occidental") formed to hold Occidental's California oil and gas
  operations and related assets; Occidental's board authorized management to pursue the
  separation on February 14, 2014, and the separation was effected as a spin-off. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-14-007128, Form 10-12B EX-99.1 information statement]
- The separation was structured as a pro rata distribution of at least 80.1% of CRC's
  outstanding common stock to Occidental stockholders; Occidental would continue to hold
  up to 19.9% of CRC's outstanding shares (the "Retained Securities") for up to 18 months
  following the spin-off. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-14-007128, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The separation was registered on Form 10-12B; stockholder approval of the distribution
  was not required, and Occidental determined the principal terms and the allocation of
  assets, liabilities, and obligations between the two companies. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-14-007128, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## What crossed the ownership boundary at the tier level
- The customer-facing asset separated into the standalone company was an independent oil
  and natural gas exploration and production business focused exclusively on California,
  described as the largest producer in California on a gross-operated basis, holding
  approximately 2.3 million net mineral acres spanning the state's four major oil and gas
  basins. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-14-007128, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The operational processes that produce and deliver that asset moved with it: CRC ran its
  own drilling and production activity, with approximately 26 drilling rigs employed in
  California across the San Joaquin, Los Angeles, and Ventura basins. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-14-007128, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The people and management that run those operations crossed as well: the CRC leadership
  team was drawn from Occidental — William E. Albrecht (Executive Chairman, previously
  Occidental Vice President and President of Oxy Oil & Gas, Americas, with managerial
  oversight of the upstream assets), Todd A. Stevens (President and Chief Executive
  Officer), Marshall D. "Mark" Smith (Senior EVP and CFO), and EVPs of Northern and
  Southern Operations. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-14-007128, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- As of December 31, 2013 the California business had approximately 1,600 California
  employees, approximately 86 of whom were represented by labor unions. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-14-007128, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## What did NOT cross as an intact substrate
- Centrally located corporate functions historically performed by Occidental did not
  transfer as a whole operating substrate; the combined financial statements reflected
  expense allocations for executive oversight, accounting, procurement, engineering,
  drilling, exploration, marketing, internal audit, legal, risk management, finance, tax,
  treasury, information technology, government relations, investor relations, public
  relations, financial reporting, human resources, and ethics and compliance — functions
  the standalone company would have to establish, procure, or duplicate. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-14-007128, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The information statement disclosed that costs to "separate and/or duplicate information
  technology systems" and similar separation costs would be incurred, and that
  transitional services Occidental would provide "may not be sufficient to meet our
  needs." [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-14-007128, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Contractual arrangements bridging the shared substrate (closing-time)
- CRC and Occidental agreed to enter a Separation and Distribution Agreement plus several
  ancillary agreements to allocate assets, liabilities, and obligations relating to the
  California business between the two companies. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-14-007128, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- A Transition Services Agreement provided for Occidental to furnish specified transitional
  assistance to CRC (and vice versa) to facilitate operation of the business as it became
  standalone, with services available only for a transitional period. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-14-007128, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Additional closing-time agreements included an Employee Matters Agreement, a Tax Sharing
  Agreement, intellectual-property arrangements, and an Area of Mutual Interest Agreement
  giving Occidental a right to acquire an interest in certain oil and gas properties CRC
  acquires in the United States outside California. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-14-007128, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction detached an oil and gas exploration-and-production asset, its in-house
  drilling/production processes, and its Occidental-sourced management team into a
  standalone public company, while the parent's centrally located shared corporate
  functions (IT, tax, treasury, legal, accounting, HR, and related services) did not move
  intact and were bridged at closing by a Transition Services Agreement and related
  Separation and Distribution, Employee Matters, Tax Sharing, and Area of Mutual Interest
  agreements. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-14-007128, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
