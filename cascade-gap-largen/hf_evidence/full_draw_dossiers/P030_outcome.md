## Public-reporting status and outcome window
- The registrant continued to file annual reports on Form 10-K after the separation; the Form 10-K for the year ended December 31, 2012 was filed 2013-02-22 and reports the fiscal 2010, 2011 and 2012 record used here as the fixed outcome window. [SRC: SEC accession 0001376812-13-000006, Form 10-K (FY2012)]
- The 10-K confirms the entity became a separate publicly-traded company effective October 31, 2007, and that on July 23, 2008 it completed the acquisition of Magnum Coal Company, one of the largest Appalachian coal producers, adding eight mining complexes and controlling more than 600 million tons of reserves. [SRC: SEC accession 0001376812-13-000006, Form 10-K (FY2012)]

## Best-documented realized scalar: Chapter 11 and net loss
- On July 9, 2012 (the Petition Date), Patriot Coal Corporation and substantially all of its wholly-owned subsidiaries filed voluntary petitions for reorganization under Chapter 11 of the U.S. Bankruptcy Code in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York (jointly administered as In re: Patriot Coal Corporation, et al., Case No. 12-51502), and operated as debtors-in-possession. [SRC: SEC accession 0001376812-13-000006, Form 10-K (FY2012)]
- Net loss widened across the outcome window: net loss of $97.7 million (FY2010), $139.1 million (FY2011), and $730.6 million (FY2012); the FY2012 result included reorganization items, net of $135.9 million. [SRC: SEC accession 0001376812-13-000006, Form 10-K (FY2012)]
- Following the Chapter 11 petitions, the company's pre-petition unsecured long-term debt of $458.5 million was reclassified to "Liabilities subject to compromise" on the December 31, 2012 consolidated balance sheet; the company obtained debtor-in-possession (DIP) financing facilities. [SRC: SEC accession 0001376812-13-000006, Form 10-K (FY2012)]
- The 10-K identified the company's ability to continue as a going concern among the factors that could cause actual results to differ materially. [SRC: SEC accession 0001376812-13-000006, Form 10-K (FY2012)]

## Documented pressure at the operations / labor-and-legacy interface
- The 10-K attributed rising liabilities to the costs associated with labor-related legacy liabilities, sharply rising regulatory-compliance costs, and the increasing burden of selenium water treatment, against a backdrop of declining coal share of the U.S. energy market and falling thermal-coal prices amid expanded natural-gas supply. [SRC: SEC accession 0001376812-13-000006, Form 10-K (FY2012)]
- The reorganization was stated to depend on implementing changes to wage and benefit programs and postretirement (retiree healthcare) benefit obligations pursuant to Sections 1113 and 1114 of the Bankruptcy Code, including negotiations with the UMWA, which could result in labor disruptions, work stoppages or slowdowns. [SRC: SEC accession 0001376812-13-000006, Form 10-K (FY2012)]
- The workforce and its representation shifted over the window: as of December 31, 2012 the company had approximately 4,100 employees, approximately 40% of whom were represented by the UMWA (versus approximately 2,500 employees, ~61% UMWA-represented, at the time of separation). [SRC: SEC accession 0001376812-13-000006, Form 10-K (FY2012)]

## Outcome summary (realized record only)
- Within the fixed 2010–2012 window the registrant's net loss deepened from $97.7 million (FY2010) to $730.6 million (FY2012), it filed voluntary Chapter 11 petitions on July 9, 2012 and operated as a debtor-in-possession with $458.5 million of pre-petition debt reclassified to liabilities subject to compromise, and its stated path to emergence turned on renegotiating collective-bargaining and retiree-healthcare/postretirement obligations (Bankruptcy Code Sections 1113/1114) with the UMWA amid labor-related legacy liabilities and selenium-treatment costs — a realized record centered on the labor-and-legacy substrate and adverse coal-market conditions. [SRC: SEC accession 0001376812-13-000006, Form 10-K (FY2012)]
