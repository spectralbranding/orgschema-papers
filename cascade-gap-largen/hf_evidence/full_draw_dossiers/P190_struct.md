# Structural sub-dossier — combination of Baker Hughes Incorporated and GE Oil & Gas (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Transaction and parties at closing
- The transaction was structured under a Transaction Agreement and Plan of Merger dated
  October 30, 2016 (as amended March 27, 2017) among General Electric Company ("GE"),
  Baker Hughes Incorporated ("Baker Hughes"), Bear Newco, Inc. ("New Baker Hughes"), Bear
  MergerSub, Inc., BHI Newco, Inc. and Bear MergerSub 2, Inc., combining GE's Oil & Gas
  business ("GE O&G") with Baker Hughes through a series of mergers, a conversion, and a
  contribution. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-187240, DEFM14A Notice of Special Meeting / summary]
- The combined business of GE O&G and Baker Hughes is to be held by a newly formed
  Delaware limited liability company, Newco LLC (the surviving, converted form of the
  merged Baker Hughes entity), which will be managed by EHHC (a Baker Hughes-affiliated
  entity) as managing member; New Baker Hughes, in turn, will be the top public holding
  company. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-187240, DEFM14A "The Transactions" summary]
- Effective from and following Closing the parties agreed that Newco LLC and its
  subsidiaries would operate under the name "Baker Hughes, a GE Company." [SRC: SEC
  accession 0001193125-17-187240, DEFM14A Transaction Agreement §7.20]
- New Baker Hughes' principal executive offices are dual-located at 17021 Aldine
  Westfield Road, Houston, Texas and at The Ark, 201 Talgarth Road, Hammersmith, London,
  UK following Closing. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-187240, DEFM14A "The Companies" summary]

## Consideration and resulting ownership split
- GE transferred to Newco LLC all of the equity interests of the GE O&G holding
  companies (holding all GE O&G assets/liabilities/operating subsidiaries) plus $7.4
  billion in cash, in exchange for approximately 62.5% of the Common Units of Newco LLC;
  Baker Hughes stockholders held, through New Baker Hughes, the remaining approximately
  37.5% of the Common Units. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-187240, DEFM14A "The Transactions" summary, "The Contribution"]
- Each share of Baker Hughes common stock was converted into the right to receive one
  share of New Baker Hughes Class A Common Stock; New Baker Hughes distributed a Special
  Dividend of $17.50 per share to holders of record of Class A Common Stock, funded
  substantially from the $7.4 billion cash GE contributed. [SRC: SEC accession
  0001193125-17-187240, DEFM14A "The Transactions" summary, "Consideration"]
- Immediately following Closing, GE held 100% of Class B Common Stock (~62.5% of the
  voting power of New Baker Hughes on a fully diluted basis), and former Baker Hughes
  stockholders held 100% of Class A Common Stock (~37.5% of the voting power and 100% of
  the economic rights in New Baker Hughes itself). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-187240,
  DEFM14A "Former Baker Hughes Stockholders Will Hold Shares Representing Approximately
  37.5% of the Voting Power..."]
- The parties anticipated the Class A Common Stock would be listed on the NYSE under the
  symbol "BHGE"; Baker Hughes common stock itself would no longer be listed on NYSE and
  would be removed from Exchange Act registration if the transactions completed. [SRC: SEC
  accession 0001193125-17-187240, DEFM14A "Class A Common Stock Anticipated to be Listed
  on NYSE" summary section]

## Governance at closing
- The New Baker Hughes Board was structured to consist of eleven directors: six
  designated by GE (including the Chairman) and five designated by (or acceptable to)
  Baker Hughes, including Martin Craighead, Baker Hughes' then-Chairman and CEO. GE's
  right to maintain proportional board representation was tied to it continuing to hold
  at least 50% of voting power or being required to consolidate New Baker Hughes'
  financials under GAAP. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-187240, DEFM14A "Post-Transactions
  Governance and Management" summary]
- Jeffrey R. Immelt, then Chairman and CEO of GE, was slated to serve as Chairman of the
  New Baker Hughes Board; Martin Craighead was slated to serve as Vice Chairman. [SRC: SEC
  accession 0001193125-17-187240, DEFM14A "Post-Transactions Governance and Management"
  summary]
- Lorenzo Simonelli, then President and CEO of GE O&G, was slated to become President and
  CEO of New Baker Hughes; Brian Worrell, then VP and CFO of GE O&G, was slated to become
  CFO of New Baker Hughes. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-187240, DEFM14A "Post-Transactions
  Governance and Management" summary]
- At Closing, GE and New Baker Hughes were to enter into a Stockholders Agreement setting
  out board composition, director-designation, and committee-membership terms, including
  that GE could acquire additional shares up to an aggregate of 65% of Common Stock
  without approval of a Conflicts Committee. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-187240,
  DEFM14A "Stockholders Agreement" summary + "Certain Relationships" body discussion]

## What combined at the tier level and how the shared substrate was bridged
- The combination was framed as complementary rather than substitutive at the
  product/technology tier: Baker Hughes was described as a leader in oilfield services,
  advanced drilling, logging/evaluation, completion systems and production optimization,
  while GE O&G was described as a top-four provider in turbomachinery/downstream
  technology, surface/subsea/drilling, and digital solutions — the stated intent being a
  combined "fullstream" portfolio across upstream, midstream and downstream segments.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-187240, DEFM14A "Reasons for the Transactions" /
  Complete fullstream portfolio discussion]
- The Transaction Agreement required the parties to negotiate and enter into, effective
  as of Closing, a defined package of ancillary agreements bridging the shared
  substrate: a Tax Matters Agreement, a Transition Services Agreement, supply agreements
  running in both directions (GE to Newco, and Newco to GE), an Intellectual Property
  Cross-License Agreement, a Trademark License Agreement, a GE Digital Agreement, an
  Intercompany Services Agreement, and a Non-Competition Agreement. [SRC: SEC accession
  0001193125-17-187240, DEFM14A Transaction Agreement §7.18]
- As part of the closing structure, the Transaction Agreement was subsequently amended to
  substitute an Intercompany Services Agreement for the services originally contemplated
  under the Transition Services Agreement. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-187240,
  DEFM14A Amendment to Transaction Agreement]
- New Baker Hughes was disclosed to have access to "the GE Store" — GE's cross-business
  technology, talent and expertise network — and to GE's Predix industrial-Internet
  operating system, framed as capability the combined business would draw on as "a GE
  company." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-187240, DEFM14A "Reasons for the
  Transactions" / GE Store and Predix discussion]
- The stated pro forma combination premise assumed total annual run-rate synergies of
  approximately $1.6 billion (approximately $1.2 billion cost synergies and $0.4 billion
  additional EBITDA from revenue synergies) to be realized over three years from Closing.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-187240, DEFM14A "Reasons for the Transactions" /
  Robust synergy opportunities discussion]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- At closing, GE's Oil & Gas business and Baker Hughes were combined under a new public
  holding company (New Baker Hughes, operating the business as "Baker Hughes, a GE
  Company") via an Up-C-style structure (Newco LLC) with GE holding ~62.5% economic/
  voting interest and former Baker Hughes stockholders ~37.5%, a special dividend to
  Baker Hughes stockholders, an 11-member board split 6 GE/5 Baker Hughes-designated
  directors with GE holding the Chairman seat and a GE O&G executive installed as CEO of
  the combined company, and the shared operational substrate (tax, transition/
  intercompany services, supply, IP/trademark licensing, GE Digital access,
  non-competition) bridged through a defined package of ancillary agreements executed
  effective at Closing rather than fully integrated at that moment. [SRC: SEC accession
  0001193125-17-187240, DEFM14A "The Transactions" / "Reasons for the Transactions"
  summary sections, Transaction Agreement §7.18, §7.20]
