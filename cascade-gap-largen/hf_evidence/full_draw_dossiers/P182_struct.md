# Structural sub-dossier — Enbridge Energy Partners, L.P. merger into Enbridge Inc. (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Transaction and parties at closing
- On September 17, 2018, Enbridge Energy Partners, L.P. ("EEP"), a publicly-traded
  Delaware limited partnership, entered into an Agreement and Plan of Merger with its
  general partner Enbridge Energy Company, Inc. (the "General Partner"), the General
  Partner's delegate Enbridge Energy Management, L.L.C. ("EEQ"), Enbridge Inc.
  ("Enbridge"), Enbridge (U.S.) Inc., and Winter Acquisition Sub II, LLC ("Merger Sub"),
  an indirect wholly owned Delaware subsidiary of Enbridge formed solely to complete the
  transaction. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-323821, Form DEFM14A]
- The merger agreement provided that Merger Sub would merge with and into EEP, with EEP
  surviving as an indirect wholly owned subsidiary of Enbridge; each outstanding Class A
  common unit not already owned by Enbridge or its subsidiaries would convert into the
  right to receive 0.335 of an Enbridge common share (the "Exchange Ratio"/"Merger
  Consideration"). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-323821, Form DEFM14A]
- The transaction was announced alongside separate definitive agreements under which
  Enbridge would acquire, in parallel combination transactions, the publicly held equity
  of EEQ, Spectra Energy Partners, LP, and Enbridge Income Fund Holdings Inc. that
  Enbridge did not already own. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-275827, Form 8-K dated 2018-09-18]
- The transaction closed on December 20, 2018: Merger Sub merged with and into EEP, EEP
  continued as the surviving entity and an indirect wholly owned subsidiary of Enbridge,
  each converted Class A common unit was exchanged for the Merger Consideration, and
  EEP's units ceased trading prior to that day's market opening and were no longer to be
  listed on the NYSE. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-354624, Form 8-K dated 2018-12-20]
- EEP stated its intent to file a Form 15 to terminate its Exchange Act reporting
  obligations, and subsequently filed a Form 15-12B on 2018-12-31. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-354624, Form 8-K dated 2018-12-20] [SRC: SEC EDGAR filing index for CIK 0000880285, Form 15-12B accession 0001193125-18-361861]

## Pre-existing ownership and control position at closing
- Before the merger, Enbridge indirectly owned all of the common stock of the General
  Partner, which held an effective 2% general-partner interest in EEP and its operating
  partnerships; Enbridge also indirectly owned approximately 32.9% of EEP's outstanding
  limited-partner units, so that the General Partner and limited-partner interests
  together represented approximately 34.2% of EEP's total effective ownership and
  economic interest. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-323821, Form DEFM14A]
- Enbridge is the ultimate parent of the General Partner, which through its ownership of
  the GP Delegate's voting shares elects all of the GP Delegate's directors; six of the
  ten directors of the General Partner and the GP Delegate also held positions at
  Enbridge or its subsidiaries as of the proxy date. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-323821, Form DEFM14A]
- EEP does not have any employees and relies on the GP Delegate to manage the conduct of
  its business; under a pre-existing delegation of control agreement, the General
  Partner delegated substantially all of its power and authority to manage and control
  EEP's business and affairs to the GP Delegate. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-323821, Form DEFM14A]

## What crossed the ownership boundary at closing
- The customer-facing asset — EEP's crude oil and liquid petroleum transportation and
  storage business, principally the Lakehead System (the U.S. portion of a crude oil and
  liquid petroleum pipeline system extending from western Canada through the Great Lakes
  region to eastern Canada) and related natural gas gathering, treating, processing,
  transportation and marketing assets — remained with the surviving entity EEP, which
  became a wholly owned subsidiary of Enbridge; the proxy statement/prospectus does not
  describe any carve-out or disposition of this asset base as part of the Merger. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-323821, Form DEFM14A]
- The legal vehicle for the transaction was a merger of Merger Sub with and into EEP
  (EEP as surviving entity), effected under Delaware law by filing a certificate of
  merger with the Delaware Secretary of State. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-323821, Form DEFM14A]
- The operating/management substrate (the GP Delegate's delegation of control over EEP's
  business and affairs) was already in place before the merger and is described in the
  proxy statement/prospectus as continuing; the document does not describe a
  post-closing operational restructuring of how EEP's business is run. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-323821, Form DEFM14A]
- At the effective time of the merger, EECI (the General Partner), on behalf of EEP's
  limited partners, executed Amendment No. 2 to EEP's Eighth Amended and Restated
  Agreement of Limited Partnership, waiving a provision that otherwise would have
  prohibited a merger of EEP with Enbridge while I-Units remained outstanding unless
  I-Unit holders received cash, common units, or a substantially equivalent security.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-354624, Form 8-K dated 2018-12-20]
- At the effective time of the merger, seven named individuals (Jeffrey A. Connelly, Dan
  A. Westbrook, William S. Waldheim, J. Richard Bird, J. Herbert England, Mark A. Maki,
  and John K. Whelen) resigned from the boards of directors of EECI (the General
  Partner) and EEQ (the GP Delegate), and Laura J. Buss Sayavedra also resigned from the
  EECI board. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-354624, Form 8-K dated 2018-12-20]

## Stated rationale for the transaction structure
- The transaction was presented to unitholders as advancing a "simplified corporate
  structure," stated to eliminate potential conflicts of interest between Enbridge and
  EEP, alongside reduced SEC filing requirements from maintaining one public company
  rather than two, and a step-up in tax basis; from Enbridge's perspective it was
  presented as increasing Enbridge's ownership in "core businesses" and improving
  Enbridge's credit profile through elimination of EEP's public distributions. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-323821, Form DEFM14A]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction converted a publicly traded limited partnership — already indirectly
  controlled by its acquirer through a general-partner interest, a delegation of control
  agreement, and roughly one-third of the economic interest, and already without its own
  employees — into a wholly owned subsidiary of that same acquirer via a
  reverse-triangular merger, with public unitholders receiving a fixed exchange ratio of
  Enbridge common shares; the asset base and the pre-existing management-delegation
  arrangement are described in the closing-era filings as carrying forward, while
  several independent directors of the general-partner entities resigned at closing.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-323821, Form DEFM14A] [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-354624, Form 8-K dated 2018-12-20]
