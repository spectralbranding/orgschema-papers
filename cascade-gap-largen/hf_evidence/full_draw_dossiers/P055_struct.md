# Structural sub-dossier — Owens Corning / Saint-Gobain glass-reinforcements joint venture (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Note on the drawn filing
- The drawn source is Owens Corning's Form 8-K reporting an event dated February 20, 2007
  (filed February 21, 2007). Its Item 1.01 (Entry into a Material Definitive Agreement)
  IS the deal-terms report for the transaction in scope, and it attaches the underlying
  agreements as exhibits: the Master Contribution Agreement (EX-10.1), the Joint Venture
  Agreement (EX-10.2), and the Option Agreement (EX-10.3), each dated February 20, 2007,
  plus a press release (EX-99.1). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-07-035988, Form 8-K Item 1.01 and Exhibit Index]
- The same 8-K also carries two unrelated matters — Item 2.02 (a Q4/FY2006 earnings
  release, furnished not filed) and Item 8.01 (Board approval of a buy-back of up to 5% of
  outstanding common stock); these are not part of the transaction in scope. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-07-035988, Form 8-K Items 2.02 and 8.01]

## Transaction and parties at closing
- Owens Corning and Saint-Gobain "entered into agreements to form a global joint venture
  to develop, manufacture, market, sell and distribute glass reinforcements products";
  the new entity is named OCV Reinforcements S.P.R.L. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-07-035988, Form 8-K Item 1.01]
- Background: on July 27, 2006 the two companies announced they were in discussions to
  merge their respective reinforcements and composites businesses, "thereby creating a
  global company in reinforcements and composite fabrics products with worldwide revenues
  of approximately $1.8 billion (€1.5 billion) and 10,000 employees"; the Saint-Gobain
  business contributed is its Vetrotex reinforcements business. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-07-035988, Form 8-K EX-99.1 press release]

## What crossed the ownership boundary at closing (tier level)
- Both parties contributed operating businesses, not fractional passive interests:
  "each of Owens Corning and Saint-Gobain are contributing to the joint venture their
  worldwide glass reinforcements businesses, including plant, property and equipment,
  working capital, contractual arrangements, customers and intellectual property." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-07-035988, Form 8-K Item 1.01]
- Ownership split: in exchange for their contributions, Owens Corning receives 60% and
  Saint-Gobain 40% of the voting and economic interests; the joint venture is consolidated
  on the financial statements of Owens Corning. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-07-035988, Form 8-K Item 1.01]
- People and pension obligations move with the businesses: the Master Contribution
  Agreement defines "Company Employees" and provides for "Transferred DB Plan" and
  "Transferred DC Plan" pension plans (each relating solely to the relevant Company
  Employees) to "be assumed by the Company and its Subsidiaries" on completion. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-07-035988, Form 8-K EX-10.1 Master Contribution Agreement]

## Legal vehicle and governance at closing
- Per the Joint Venture Agreement, the structure "consists of two entities, a management
  company and an operating company"; the management company serves as manager of the JV,
  with its board of directors directing the operating company's operations. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-07-035988, Form 8-K Item 1.01]
- Board composition: Owens Corning appoints three members of the management company's
  board and Saint-Gobain appoints two. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-07-035988, Form 8-K Item 1.01]

## Controlling intent / management fusion at closing
- Charles E. Dana, the President of Owens Corning's Composite Solutions business, "will be
  the President and CEO of the joint venture and will remain President of the Composite
  Solutions business" — i.e., the acquirer's incumbent segment head simultaneously runs the
  new venture. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-07-035988, Form 8-K Item 1.01]

## Bridging / operating arrangements at closing
- The Master Contribution Agreement's "Transaction Documents" definition lists the
  bridging instruments entered alongside the contribution: a Master Transition Services
  Agreement (as defined in the Joint Venture Agreement), a Form of Product Supply
  Agreements, OC and SG Intellectual Property License Agreements, SG Leased Real Property
  Assignment Agreements, an Asbestos Indemnity Agreement, and an Asbestos Guaranty, in
  addition to the Option Agreement and the Joint Venture Agreement. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-07-035988, Form 8-K EX-10.1 Master Contribution Agreement]

## Exit mechanism embedded at closing (Option Agreement)
- An indirect, wholly owned subsidiary of Owens Corning "has the right to acquire
  Saint-Gobain's interest in the joint venture at any time after the 48 month anniversary
  of the closing," and Saint-Gobain has the reciprocal right to sell its interest to that
  subsidiary after the same 48-month anniversary. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-07-035988, Form 8-K Item 1.01]
- The purchase price for Saint-Gobain's interest is set by a formula representing "a
  multiple of the average earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization of
  the joint venture during the up to 5 year period preceding" the purchase and sale. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-07-035988, Form 8-K Item 1.01]

## Closing conditions
- Closing was "subject to satisfaction of certain conditions, including the receipt of
  regulatory approvals and consents," and was expected to close in mid-2007. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-07-035988, Form 8-K Item 1.01]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- Two producers merged their worldwide glass-reinforcements businesses — including plant,
  equipment, working capital, contracts, customers, intellectual property, and the
  employees and pension plans running them — into a jointly held, two-tier (management +
  operating) vehicle owned 60% by Owens Corning and 40% by Saint-Gobain and consolidated
  by Owens Corning, governed by a five-seat board (3 OC / 2 SG) and led by Owens Corning's
  incumbent Composite Solutions president; a Master Transition Services Agreement, product
  supply agreements, and IP license agreements bridged the shared substrate at closing, and
  an Option Agreement gave Owens Corning a call (and Saint-Gobain a put) on the 40% interest
  after 48 months at an EBITDA-multiple price. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-07-035988, Form 8-K Item 1.01, EX-10.1, and EX-99.1]
