## Transaction and parties at closing
- Patriot Coal Corporation ("Patriot") was a wholly-owned subsidiary of Peabody Energy Corporation ("Peabody") that Peabody's Board approved to separate into an independent, publicly-traded company; following the separation Patriot's assets and business would consist of coal operations and reserves in Central Appalachia, Northern Appalachia and the Illinois Basin. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950137-07-012653, Form 10-12B EX-99.1 information statement]
- The separation was effected by a pro rata distribution of Patriot common shares to Peabody stockholders — one Patriot share for every ten Peabody shares held on the record date — with no stockholder vote required; Peabody would distribute 100% of Patriot's shares. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950137-07-012653, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The distribution was structured to be tax-free to stockholders under Section 355 of the Internal Revenue Code, conditioned on a favorable IRS ruling and an Ernst & Young LLP opinion; Peabody common stock continued on the NYSE under "BTU" and Patriot common stock was to list on the NYSE under "PCX." [SRC: SEC accession 0000950137-07-012653, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## What crossed the ownership boundary at the tier level
- The customer-facing product and the operating assets that produce it crossed together: at separation Patriot held coal operations comprising eight company-operated mines, two joint-venture mines and numerous contractor-operated mines serviced by eight coal preparation facilities in northern and southern West Virginia and western Kentucky, plus Patriot-controlled loading facilities and rail/river transportation access. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950137-07-012653, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Patriot controlled approximately 1.2 billion tons of proven and probable coal reserves (premium coking coal and steam coal), and in 2006 sold 24.3 million tons of coal (77% to domestic electric utilities, 23% to steel producers). [SRC: SEC accession 0000950137-07-012653, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The people and organization that run the operations crossed with the business: as of December 31, 2006 Patriot had approximately 2,500 employees, approximately 61% of whom at company operations were represented by the United Mine Workers of America (UMWA) under a labor contract effective January 1, 2007 expiring December 31, 2011. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950137-07-012653, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The senior management team transferred from within Peabody: Richard M. Whiting (President & CEO) was Peabody's Executive Vice President & Chief Marketing Officer and had joined Peabody's predecessor in 1976; Jiri Nemec (SVP & COO) was a 20-year Peabody veteran running Peabody's U.S. Eastern Operations; Mark N. Schroeder (SVP & CFO) had held management positions at Peabody since 2000; Charles A. Ebetino, Jr. (SVP — Corporate Development) had been a Peabody SVP since 2006. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950137-07-012653, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Allocation of the shared substrate and legacy obligations at closing
- Before the separation Patriot and Peabody were to enter a Separation Agreement, Plan of Reorganization and Distribution allocating between them the assets, liabilities and obligations currently owned by Peabody — including retiree healthcare, pension obligations, workers' compensation and tax-related assets and liabilities attributable to periods prior to, at and after separation. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950137-07-012653, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Patriot would NOT assume Peabody's defined benefit pension plan applicable to Patriot employees; Peabody would assume certain retiree healthcare liabilities related to Patriot's business pursuant to liability assumption agreements, and Patriot would administer those Peabody-assumed retiree healthcare benefits under an administrative services agreement. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950137-07-012653, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Contractual bridging of the shared substrate (closing-time)
- Peabody would continue to provide certain administrative services to Patriot for an agreed number of months following the separation to ease Patriot's transition to a stand-alone public company. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950137-07-012653, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Ancillary agreements at closing provided for: the ongoing sale of Patriot coal to Peabody and its affiliates on a contract basis (so Peabody could meet pre-existing customer commitments sourced from Patriot operations); a sublease from Peabody of certain mining equipment currently used in Patriot's operations; a software license granted to Patriot; and a throughput agreement giving Patriot access to a Peabody coal transloading facility. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950137-07-012653, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction detached a full standalone operating business — coal reserves, mines, preparation and loading assets, the workforce (~2,500 employees, ~61% UMWA), and a management team drawn from Peabody — into a newly independent public company, while the shared corporate/legacy substrate (administrative services, equipment, software, transloading access, and retiree-healthcare/pension/workers'-compensation obligations) was allocated by a Separation Agreement and bridged at closing through an administrative-services agreement, a coal supply agreement, an equipment sublease, a software license, a throughput agreement, and retiree-healthcare liability-assumption/administration agreements rather than transferred whole. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950137-07-012653, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
