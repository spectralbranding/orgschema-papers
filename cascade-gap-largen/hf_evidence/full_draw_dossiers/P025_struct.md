## Transaction and parties at closing
- The Timken Company's board approved a plan to separate its steel business from The Timken Company through a spinoff; the steel business was to be transferred to TimkenSteel Corporation, a newly created Ohio corporation, and its shares distributed as a dividend to Timken shareholders on June 30, 2014. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-201428, Form 10-12B EX-99.1 information statement]
- TimkenSteel was, immediately before the distribution, a wholly owned subsidiary of Timken; after the distribution it would be an independent, publicly traded company listed on the New York Stock Exchange under the symbol "TMST". [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-201428, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Each Timken shareholder would receive one TimkenSteel common share for every two Timken common shares held on the record date; approximately 45.4 million TimkenSteel common shares were to be distributed, constituting all outstanding shares immediately after the spinoff. Shareholder approval was not required. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-201428, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- After the spinoff, The Timken Company would continue to engineer, manufacture and market bearings and related mechanical power transmission components and services, trading on the NYSE under "TKR". [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-201428, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## What crossed the ownership boundary at the tier level
- Customer-facing product/asset: TimkenSteel was described as a company that creates tailored alloy steel products and services; its portfolio included special bar quality (SBQ) steel bars, seamless mechanical steel tubing, and precision steel components, and it manufactured more than 450 grades of high-performance carbon, micro-alloy and alloy steel sold as ingots, bars and tubes. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-201428, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Operational processes: the steel business's manufacturing facilities were located in Akron, Canton (2) and Eaton, Ohio; Columbus, North Carolina; and Houston, Texas, plus owned/leased warehouses and distribution facilities (aggregate approximately 3.7 million square feet); it was headquartered in Canton, Ohio, at an owned facility, and retained its raw-material management process, research/engineering function, and R&D expenditure ($9.4 million for the year ended December 31, 2013). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-201428, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- People/organization: at December 31, 2013 the business had approximately 2,700 employees, approximately 61% covered under collective bargaining agreements; the executive officers listed for TimkenSteel were expected to resign from their positions with Timken upon completion of the spinoff. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-201428, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Controlling intent / management: TimkenSteel was to be led by Ward J. Timken, Jr. as Chairman, Chief Executive Officer and President; he had previously served as President of Timken's steel business (2004–2005) and as Chairman of Timken's board since 2005 and would remain a Timken director after the distribution. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-201428, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Historically shared corporate functions did NOT transfer as an intact substrate: Timken performed many corporate functions for the steel business (treasury, tax administration, accounting, financial reporting, human-resource services, marketing/communications, information technology, incentive compensation, legal and other services), for which the business paid Timken on a cost-allocation basis. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-201428, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Contractual arrangements governing the separated shared substrate (closing-time)
- Legal vehicle: before the distribution date, Timken and TimkenSteel would enter into a separation and distribution agreement identifying the assets to be transferred, liabilities to be assumed and contracts to be assigned to TimkenSteel, and describing when and how those transfers would occur. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-201428, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Several agreements would govern the ongoing relationship: a tax sharing agreement, an employee matters agreement, and a transition services agreement, together with other ancillary agreements. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-201428, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Transition services agreement: following the spinoff Timken would continue to provide some of the above corporate functions to TimkenSteel on a transitional basis; the filing noted it could not assure that all such functions would be successfully executed by Timken during the transition period. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-201428, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Trademark license agreement: Timken would license certain trademarks to TimkenSteel in connection with the operations of its business; the license would be perpetual, irrevocable, fully paid-up, royalty-free, and worldwide, with limited rights to sublicense and assign. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-201428, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Commercial supply agreement: TimkenSteel would be a strategic supplier of choice to Timken for certain specific categories of steel products, with an initial 18-month term; intercompany sales of these products to Timken historically accounted for approximately 5% of net sales (based on 2013 full-year net sales), and the agreement was not a "take or pay" arrangement. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-201428, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Technology license agreement: Timken would assign to TimkenSteel certain technology (material modeling/characterization) and TimkenSteel would license to Timken certain technology (assessing cleanliness of alloys for bearing production), including a license letting TimkenSteel manufacture certain bearing-related steel products for Timken. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-201428, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- R&D collaboration agreement: the parties would cooperate from time to time on development of material fundamentals, characterization methods and modeling technology after the spinoff, under a general framework with no specific projects finalized. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-201428, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Office lease/sublease and an information-technology data-center arrangement: existing IT data-center and disaster-recovery assets owned by Timken, located at TimkenSteel's facilities in Canton, Ohio, would be operated by Timken on behalf of both companies under one or more long-term agreements, with each company retaining responsibility for operating its own IT systems. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-201428, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction detached an entire operating steel business — its customer-facing product line (SBQ bars, seamless tubing, billets, precision components), the manufacturing facilities and processes that produce it, its approximately 2,700-person workforce, and a management team led by a principal who had previously run that same steel business inside Timken — into a standalone company, while the parent's historically shared corporate service substrate (treasury, tax, accounting, IT/data-center, HR, legal) was bridged at closing by a transition services agreement plus tax sharing, employee matters, trademark license, commercial supply, technology license, R&D collaboration, and IT-data-center agreements rather than transferred whole. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-201428, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
