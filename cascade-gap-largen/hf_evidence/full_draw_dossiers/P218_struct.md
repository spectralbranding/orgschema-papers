# Structural sub-dossier — Andeavor merger with Marathon Petroleum Corporation (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Transaction and parties at closing
- Marathon Petroleum Corporation (MPC), Andeavor, Mahi Inc. (Merger Sub 1) and Mahi LLC
  (Merger Sub 2) entered into an Agreement and Plan of Merger dated April 29, 2018, later
  amended by Amendment No. 1 (July 3, 2018) and Amendment No. 2 (September 18, 2018).
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-238276, Form DEFM14A]
- The transaction was structured as a two-step merger: Merger Sub 1 (a direct, wholly
  owned Delaware corporation subsidiary of MPC formed April 27, 2018 solely to effect
  the merger) merged with and into Andeavor, with Andeavor surviving as a wholly owned
  subsidiary of MPC (the "first merger"); immediately thereafter, the surviving
  corporation merged with and into Merger Sub 2 (a direct, wholly owned Delaware LLC
  subsidiary of MPC, also formed April 27, 2018), with Merger Sub 2 surviving as a
  wholly owned subsidiary of MPC. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-238276, Form DEFM14A]
- Andeavor stockholders could elect, per share, either 1.87 shares of MPC common stock
  ("stock consideration") or $152.27 in cash ("cash consideration"), subject to
  proration procedures ensuring approximately 22,885,359 shares converted into cash
  consideration. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-238276, Form DEFM14A]
- The merger closed October 1, 2018. Merger Sub 1 merged with and into Andeavor (first
  merger); Andeavor then merged with and into Merger Sub 2, with Merger Sub 2 (n/k/a
  Andeavor LLC) surviving as a wholly owned subsidiary of MPC. Based on preliminary
  prorationing, MPC paid approximately $3.5 billion in cash and issued approximately
  240 million shares of MPC common stock to former Andeavor holders. [SRC: SEC
  accession 0001193125-18-289750, Form 8-K dated October 1, 2018]
- Prior to the DEFM14A, on August 1, 2017 the entity operated under the legal name
  Andeavor, having changed its name from Tesoro Corporation; Andeavor was incorporated
  in Delaware in 1968. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-238276, Form DEFM14A]

## What Andeavor comprised at closing (the acquired asset/product tier)
- Andeavor was headquartered in San Antonio, Texas and described as "a highly
  integrated marketing, logistics and refining company operating primarily in the
  western and mid-continent United States." Its marketing segment sold gasoline and
  diesel fuel in the western and mid-continent United States through retail, branded
  and unbranded channels (ARCO, Shell, Mobil and SUPERAMERICA fuel brands; ampm,
  SUPERAMERICA and Giant convenience-store brands). Its logistics segment comprised the
  operations of the publicly traded Andeavor Logistics LP (of which Andeavor owned
  approximately 59% of outstanding common units as of March 31, 2018). Its refining
  segment bought and refined crude oil and other feedstocks into transportation fuels.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-238276, Form DEFM14A]
- MPC was, at the time, the nation's second-largest refiner (approximately 1.9 million
  barrels-per-calendar-day crude refining capacity across a six-refinery system) and
  separately owned Speedway LLC (a convenience-store chain) and, through subsidiaries,
  the general partner of MPLX LP (a midstream master limited partnership). [SRC: SEC
  accession 0001193125-18-238276, Form DEFM14A]
- The parties' stated rationale was to create "a leading integrated and geographically
  diverse refining, marketing and midstream company," citing shared approaches to an
  integrated business model for marketing, refining and logistics, and MPC
  management's expectation of incrementally generating in excess of $5 billion of free
  cash flow over the following five years including expected synergies. [SRC: SEC
  accession 0001193125-18-238276, Form DEFM14A]

## The legal vehicle and listing/registration changes at closing
- At the effective time, Andeavor's Certificate of Incorporation was amended and
  restated, and the surviving entity's bylaws were replaced by Merger Sub 1's bylaws;
  upon completion of the second merger, the Certificate of Formation and Limited
  Liability Company Agreement of Andeavor LLC became those of the surviving company —
  i.e., the legal vehicle converted from a Delaware corporation into a Delaware LLC,
  wholly owned by MPC. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-289750, Form 8-K]
- In connection with closing, the company requested that the New York Stock Exchange
  process a notification of removal from listing (Form 25) with respect to its common
  stock; the common stock ceased trading prior to the market opening on October 1,
  2018 and was no longer listed on the NYSE; the surviving company also intended to
  file a Form 15 to suspend its SEC reporting obligations under the Exchange Act. [SRC:
  SEC accession 0001193125-18-289750, Form 8-K]
- The issuance of MPC common stock in the merger was registered under a Form S-4
  (Registration No. 333-225244), declared effective by the SEC on August 3, 2018 — the
  same date on which the joint proxy statement/prospectus (the drawn DEFM14A) was
  filed. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-238276, Form DEFM14A]

## What crossed at the people/management/organization tier
- All twelve directors comprising the Andeavor board immediately prior to the
  effective time (Gregory J. Goff, Rodney F. Chase, Paul L. Foster, Edward G. Galante,
  David Lilley, Mary Pat McCarthy, J.W. Nokes, William H. Schumann III, Jeff A.
  Stevens, Susan Tomasky, Michael E. Wiley and Patrick Y. Yang) resigned as directors
  effective at the effective time; the filing states none of the resignations resulted
  from any disagreement with Andeavor, its management or its board. [SRC: SEC
  accession 0001193125-18-289750, Form 8-K]
- All of Andeavor's directors, principal executive officer, president, principal
  financial officer, principal accounting officer, principal operating officer and
  other named executive officers ceased their respective service as directors or
  officers upon completion of the second merger; the sole member of the surviving
  company then appointed Donald C. Templin (an existing MPC executive) as President,
  Timothy T. Griffith (an existing MPC executive) as Vice President/principal
  financial officer, and John J. Quaid (an existing MPC executive) as Vice
  President/Controller of the surviving company — i.e., incumbent Andeavor leadership
  was replaced entirely by incumbent MPC officers. [SRC: SEC accession
  0001193125-18-289750, Form 8-K]
- Separately, per the merger agreement, MPC agreed to cause four then-current members
  of the Andeavor board (to be identified by Andeavor before closing) to be appointed
  to the MPC (parent) board of directors on the closing date, and MPC stockholders were
  asked to approve increasing the maximum MPC board size from 12 to 14 to accommodate
  this. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-238276, Form DEFM14A]
- Andeavor employees held options/RSUs/PSAs/MSUs/restricted shares that were
  automatically converted at the effective time into MPC-denominated equivalent
  awards (using the 1.87 exchange ratio), and continuing employees were covered by an
  Executive Severance and Change in Control Plan providing benefits on a qualifying
  termination within two years following the change in control. [SRC: SEC accession
  0001193125-18-289750, Form 8-K; SEC accession 0001193125-18-238276, Form DEFM14A]
- The joint proxy statement/prospectus disclosed that "[e]mployee retention at
  Andeavor may be particularly challenging during the pendency of the merger," and
  identified transaction costs including employee retention costs, severance and
  benefit costs among the anticipated expenses of the merger. [SRC: SEC accession
  0001193125-18-238276, Form DEFM14A]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction combined the whole of Andeavor (a highly integrated marketing,
  logistics and refining company) into MPC through a two-step forward merger, with
  consideration split between cash and MPC stock at stockholders' election; the
  acquired legal vehicle converted from a Delaware corporation into a wholly owned
  Delaware LLC of MPC, its common stock was removed from NYSE trading and its SEC
  reporting obligations were slated for suspension at closing. [SRC: SEC accession
  0001193125-18-289750, Form 8-K]
- At the people/organization tier, the acquired company's entire incumbent board and
  senior executive officer slate departed at the effective time and was replaced by
  existing MPC officers running the surviving legal entity, while a subset of former
  Andeavor directors was carried forward onto the parent's own board rather than
  retained inside the acquired entity's management structure; the merger agreement and
  proxy materials separately flagged employee-retention risk and provided
  change-in-control severance protection for continuing Andeavor employees. [SRC: SEC
  accession 0001193125-18-289750, Form 8-K; SEC accession 0001193125-18-238276, Form
  DEFM14A]
