# Structural sub-dossier — Brighthouse Financial, Inc. separation from MetLife, Inc. (closing structure)

## Transaction and parties at closing
- Brighthouse Financial, Inc. is a newly formed company being separated from MetLife, Inc.; to effect the separation MetLife will distribute at least 80.1% of Brighthouse's common stock on a pro rata basis to holders of MetLife common stock, and MetLife currently owns all outstanding Brighthouse shares. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-126443, Form 10-12B EX-99.1 information statement]
- Following the restructuring, MetLife will distribute at least 80.1% of Brighthouse's common stock and retain no more than a 19.9% ownership interest, after which Brighthouse will be a separate, publicly traded company. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-126443, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The distribution is expected to be tax-free to MetLife's U.S. shareholders (except for cash in lieu of fractional shares); shares are distributed in book-entry form; Brighthouse applied to list on NASDAQ under the symbol "BHF." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-126443, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Customer-facing product and business model that crossed
- Brighthouse is described as a "major provider of life insurance and annuity products in the United States with $222 billion of total assets... and approximately $653 billion of life insurance face amount in-force, as of December 31, 2016," with an in-force book of "approximately 2.8 million insurance policies and annuity contracts." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-126443, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Products carried into the standalone company include variable, fixed, index-linked and income annuities, plus universal, term, variable and whole life insurance policies. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-126443, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Three new reporting segments were established for the standalone company: (i) Annuities, (ii) Life, and (iii) Run-off (products not actively being sold and separately managed). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-126443, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The company describes itself as a "focused product manufacturer with an emphasis on independent distribution," offering products solely in the United States through independent distribution channels. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-126443, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Legal vehicle and the restructuring
- Business is conducted principally through MetLife life-insurance company subsidiaries — Metropolitan Life Insurance Company (MLIC), General American Life Insurance Company (GALIC) and Metropolitan Tower Life Insurance Company (MTL) — as well as New England Life Insurance Company (NELICO) and Brighthouse Life Insurance Company of NY (formerly First MetLife Investors Insurance Company). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-126443, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- In the third quarter of 2016 MetLife reorganized its businesses; under the restructuring, specified assets and liabilities are allocated between MetLife and Brighthouse, and three affiliated life insurers plus a former offshore reinsurance affiliate (which mainly reinsured variable-annuity guarantees issued by MetLife affiliates) are merged into a single reinsurance subsidiary with its own financing. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-126443, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## People / management
- The management team responsible for MetLife's retail business prior to the distribution, based at the Charlotte, North Carolina campus, "will continue to manage our business as a separate company." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-126443, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- "Many of the Brighthouse associates who conduct our business, including management, will be former employees of MetLife," and the company states it "will need to provide training to all the Brighthouse associates who conduct our business regarding our new strategy." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-126443, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Distribution and salesforce that did NOT cross
- MetLife sold its former Retail segment's proprietary (captive) distribution channel, MetLife Premier Client Group (MPCG), to Massachusetts Mutual Life Insurance Company (MassMutual), reducing the former Retail segment employee base by "approximately 3,900 advisors and over 2,000 support employees," completing a transition to an independent, third-party distribution model. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-126443, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Brand / trademark
- Brighthouse "will have a license to use certain trademarks and the 'MetLife' name in certain limited circumstances... for a transition period," and had "begun operational and legal work to rebrand to 'Brighthouse'"; it filed trademark applications to protect the Brighthouse Financial name and logo. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-126443, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Operational processes bridged by contract (shared substrate at closing)
- The parties will execute a Master Separation Agreement, Registration Rights Agreement, Investment Management Agreements, Transition Services Agreements, Intellectual Property License Agreement, Tax Receivables Agreement and Tax Separation Agreement. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-126443, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- On January 1, 2017, Brighthouse entered into Investment Management Agreements under which a MetLife affiliate, MetLife Investment Advisors, LLC (MLIA), will manage Brighthouse's and its insurance subsidiaries' assets, together with Investment Finance Services Agreements for investment finance and reporting services. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-126443, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Under the Transition Services Agreement, MetLife and Brighthouse will provide each other specified services on a transitional basis; for the duration of that agreement Brighthouse relies on MetLife's computer systems to process claims and transactions and for disaster recovery. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-126443, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Brighthouse's senior management agreed to outsource the administration of certain in-force policies "housed on up to 20 systems," of which "at least 13 of such systems will be consolidated down to one" (a Third-Party Administrative Services Agreement). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-126443, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The customer-facing product (an in-force book of life-insurance and annuity policies) is separated into a new standalone company, while the processes that produce and service it — asset management (retained by MetLife affiliate MLIA), information-technology systems (MetLife's, during the transition), and certain policy administration (outsourced to a third-party administrator) — do not transfer as an intact operating substrate but are bridged by transitional and services agreements. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-126443, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The retail management team transfers with the business, while the former captive distribution salesforce (MPCG) was sold to a third party rather than moving with the product. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-126443, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
