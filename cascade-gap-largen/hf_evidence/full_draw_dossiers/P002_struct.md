# Structural sub-dossier — CSAB Inc. separation from Cadbury Schweppes (closing structure)

> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what

## Transaction and parties at closing
- CSAB Inc. was furnished an information statement to the shareholders of Cadbury Schweppes plc in connection with the distribution of all outstanding shares of CSAB common stock to those shareholders; after the distribution CSAB would be a separate company owning and operating Cadbury Schweppes' beverage business in the United States, Canada, Mexico and the Caribbean (the "Americas Beverages business"). [SRC: SEC accession 0000950134-07-023765, Form 10-12B EX-99.1 preliminary information statement]
- Cadbury Schweppes' global confectionery business would be owned and operated by Cadbury plc, a U.K. company, which would be the new publicly-traded parent company of Cadbury Schweppes; Cadbury Schweppes would not retain any ownership interest in CSAB. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950134-07-023765, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The separation was effected as a spin-off/distribution registered on Form 10; a shareholder vote in the United Kingdom and court approval of a scheme of arrangement, under which Cadbury Schweppes would become a subsidiary of Cadbury plc, were conditions to the distribution, which was expected to complete in the second quarter of 2008. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950134-07-023765, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- CSAB and Cadbury plc would each be independent, publicly-traded companies with their own management teams and boards of directors; CSAB intended to list its common stock on the New York Stock Exchange. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950134-07-023765, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## What crossed the ownership boundary at the tier level
- Business model: the separated business was described as "a leading integrated brand owner, bottler and distributor of non-alcoholic beverages," and management stated its brand ownership, bottling and distribution were "more integrated than the U.S. operations of our principal competitors"; recently acquired bottling businesses gave it "more control over the bottling, distribution and route-to-market" for its products. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950134-07-023765, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Customer-facing product: the beverage brand portfolio (flavored carbonated soft drinks and non-carbonated soft drinks including ready-to-drink teas, juices, juice drinks and mixers; brands including Dr Pepper, Snapple, Mott's, and the Sunkist soft drink licensed from Sunkist Growers) crossed into CSAB. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950134-07-023765, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Operational processes: manufacturing (hot-fill, cold-fill and aseptic bottling; approximately 3,500 full-time manufacturing employees; 87% of manufactured volumes in 2006 were the company's own brands) and the bottling/distribution route-to-market crossed together with the brands as an integrated operation. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950134-07-023765, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- People: at September 30, 2007 the business employed approximately 20,000 full-time employees (approximately 17,000 in the United States, approximately 3,000 in Mexico and the Caribbean), with union collective bargaining agreements covering approximately 5,000 full-time employees. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950134-07-023765, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Shared substrate that did NOT transfer whole
- The business had historically relied on Cadbury Schweppes' purchasing power and shared functions — "information technology, media purchasing, insurance, treasury services, property support and, to a lesser extent, the procurement of goods" — and disclosed it "may be more difficult" to obtain these on comparable terms as a stand-alone company. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950134-07-023765, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The business had "relied on certain financial, administrative and other support functions of Cadbury Schweppes" and stated it "will need to enhance our own financial, administrative and other support systems" and "rapidly establish our own accounting and auditing policies," moving from IFRS to U.S. GAAP reporting. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950134-07-023765, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Contractual arrangements bridging the shared substrate at closing
- A separation and distribution agreement would identify assets transferred, liabilities assumed and contracts assigned, transferring all assets related to CSAB's business to CSAB on an "as is," "where is" basis, with cross-indemnities placing financial responsibility for each business with its respective owner. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950134-07-023765, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- A transition services agreement would have each party provide specified services to the other "on an interim basis for terms ranging generally from one month to one year," covering human resources, finance and accounting, intellectual property, information technology and other services "consistent with past practices," paid at the providing party's cost. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950134-07-023765, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- A tax-sharing and indemnification agreement would apportion tax liabilities between the parties and impose covenants preserving the tax-free nature of the separation; an employee matters agreement (to be negotiated) would govern employee matters. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950134-07-023765, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Intellectual-property licenses would let each party use the other's marks royalty-free "for up to one year" (the "Cadbury Marks" and "CSAB Marks"), with additional standing licenses: Rose's trademark and certain technology licensed from Cadbury to CSAB, and Canada Dry (with Cadbury's Halls product) and Snapple, Mott's, Clamato and Holland House licensed from CSAB to Cadbury for its Australia/Asia beverage business. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950134-07-023765, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction separated a vertically integrated beverage operation — brands together with their own manufacturing, bottling and distribution processes and approximately 20,000 employees — from Cadbury Schweppes, while the shared corporate service substrate (IT, treasury, tax, insurance, media purchasing, procurement, financial/administrative support) did NOT transfer as an intact operating whole and was instead bridged at closing by a separation agreement, a transition services agreement (one month to one year), a tax-sharing agreement, an employee matters agreement, and time-limited trademark licenses. [SRC: SEC accession 0000950134-07-023765, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
