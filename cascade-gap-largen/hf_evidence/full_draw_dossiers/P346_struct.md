# Structural sub-dossier — Chicago Bridge & Iron Company N.V. combination with McDermott International, Inc. (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Transaction and parties

- On December 18, 2017, McDermott International, Inc. ("McDermott"), a Panamanian
  corporation, and Chicago Bridge & Iron Company N.V. ("CB&I"), a Netherlands public
  company, entered into a Business Combination Agreement providing for the companies to
  combine their businesses through a series of transactions referred to as the "Core
  Transactions," preceded by an Exchange Offer (together, the "Combination"). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-102136, DEFM14A cover page]
- The Combination was structured in defined steps: (1) a "CB&I Technology Acquisition,"
  in which McDermott subsidiaries would acquire for cash the equity of certain CB&I
  subsidiaries that own CB&I's technology business (primarily its former Technology
  reportable segment and the "Engineered Products Operations" portion of its Fabrication
  Services segment); (2) an "Exchange Offer" for CB&I common stock; (3) a "Merger" of
  CB&I into a newly formed subsidiary ("Comet II B.V."); (4) a "Share Sale" of that
  surviving entity to a McDermott subsidiary in exchange for an exchangeable note; and
  (5) a "Liquidation" distributing McDermott common stock to remaining CB&I
  shareholders. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-102136, DEFM14A "The Combination"]
- Per share of CB&I common stock, holders were entitled to 2.47221 shares of McDermott
  common stock (or 0.82407 shares if a contemplated McDermott reverse stock split
  occurred first), plus cash in lieu of fractional shares — an all-stock exchange ratio,
  with the technology-business carve-out separately funded in cash. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-102136, DEFM14A cover page]
- Based on estimated share counts, McDermott stockholders were expected to hold
  approximately 53% and CB&I shareholders approximately 47% of McDermott common stock
  outstanding upon closing. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-102136, DEFM14A cover page]
- The Business Combination Agreement provided for a mutual $60.0 million cash
  termination fee payable by either party under specified circumstances. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-102136, DEFM14A "Termination Fees"]
- The Combination closed on May 10, 2018: McDermott Bidco's exchange offer was completed
  (approximately 65% of outstanding CB&I shares tendered, aggregate stock consideration
  approximately $1.1 billion based on the May 9, 2018 McDermott closing price); the CB&I
  Technology Acquisition was completed for cash consideration of approximately $2.65
  billion, used to repay existing CB&I debt; and the Merger, Share Sale and Liquidation
  followed substantially concurrently, with CB&I ceasing to exist and its successor
  becoming an indirect, wholly owned subsidiary of McDermott. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-159577, Form 8-K Items 1.02/2.01/5.01]

## What crossed at closing, by tier

- Legal vehicle: the entire CB&I corporate entity was the subject of the transaction —
  CB&I merged into Comet II B.V. and ceased to exist, with the surviving entity becoming
  an indirect, wholly owned subsidiary of McDermott; this was not a carve-out of a single
  business unit into an independent entity. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-159577, Form 8-K Item 5.01]
- Product/asset (technology business): CB&I's technology-business subsidiaries were
  purchased for cash ahead of, and as a condition to, the exchange-offer/merger sequence,
  rather than being folded into the stock-for-stock exchange. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-102136, DEFM14A "Step 1 — CB&I Technology Acquisition"]
- People/organization: at closing, the directors of CB&I's supervisory and management
  boards resigned, and a McDermott subsidiary became the sole director of the surviving
  entity. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-159577, Form 8-K Item 5.02]
- Executive leadership: McDermott's pre-existing President/CEO (David Dickson) and
  Executive Vice President/CFO (Stuart Spence) continued in those roles for the combined
  company; CB&I's President/CEO (Patrick Mullen) ceased to hold that title at closing and
  instead entered a letter agreement to serve as "Executive Advisor to the Chief
  Executive Officer of McDermott" through an expected date of June 6, 2018; CB&I's EVP/CFO,
  EVP of Global Operations Service, and EVP of Engineering and Construction each ceased
  their roles at or shortly after closing (one, Daniel M. McCarthy, continued as EVP,
  Lummus Technology). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-159577, Form 8-K Item 5.02]
- Governance: the McDermott board of directors was set to have 11 members at closing —
  six current McDermott directors (including Chairman Gary Luquette and CEO David
  Dickson) and five directors designated from the current CB&I Supervisory Board. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-102136, DEFM14A "Post-Combination Governance and Management"]
- Employees: for the period from the Merger Effective Time through December 31, 2018,
  McDermott contractually committed to provide each CB&I employee base compensation,
  target bonus opportunity and severance benefits at least as favorable, on an
  item-by-item basis, as immediately before closing, plus service-credit continuity and
  comparable benefit-plan eligibility without new waiting periods or pre-existing-
  condition exclusions. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-102136, DEFM14A "Certain Employee Matters"]
- Labor consultation: as a condition applicable to two Dutch technology-business
  entities (Lummus Technology Heat Transfer B.V. and Novolen Technology Holdings C.V.), a
  works-council consultation procedure in the Netherlands was completed on February 28,
  2018, ahead of closing. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-102136, DEFM14A "CB&I Works Council Consultation Procedure"]

## Financing structure and contractual bridging

- The technology-acquisition cash payment and related financings were backed by debt
  commitment letters, with McDermott contractually obligated to use reasonable best
  efforts to obtain, maintain and (if needed) replace the committed financing. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-102136, DEFM14A "Financing"]
- At closing, CB&I's pre-existing credit facilities (a $1.15 billion revolving credit
  facility, an $800 million revolving credit facility and a $500 million term facility)
  and its outstanding senior notes under two note purchase agreements were prepaid in
  full and terminated, with related liens released. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-159577, Form 8-K Item 1.02]
- A one-month Dutch statutory creditor-opposition period with respect to the Merger,
  commencing after the February 2, 2018 announcement, expired March 2, 2018 without any
  notice of opposition. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-102136, DEFM14A "Additional Agreements — Creditor Opposition"]

## Public-market status at closing

- The joint proxy statement/prospectus stated that if the Combination were completed,
  CB&I would cease to exist, would no longer be a publicly held company, and that
  following the Combination CB&I intended to remove CB&I common stock from trading on
  the New York Stock Exchange and to deregister it under the Exchange Act. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-102136, DEFM14A "Questions and Answers"]
- In connection with closing on May 10, 2018, the surviving entity notified the NYSE and
  requested that trading of the common stock be suspended before the market open on May
  11, 2018 and that the NYSE file an application with the SEC to remove the stock from
  listing and registration under Section 12(b) of the Exchange Act, with a further
  certification to terminate registration under Section 12(g) to follow. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-159577, Form 8-K Item 3.01]

## Structural summary (closing only)

- The Combination was structured as a two-track process: a cash purchase that carved out
  CB&I's technology-business subsidiaries ahead of the main transaction (funded by debt
  financing, proceeds used to retire CB&I's existing indebtedness), paired with an
  all-stock exchange offer/merger/liquidation sequence that folded CB&I's remaining
  corporate entity, in its entirety, into McDermott as an indirect wholly owned
  subsidiary. McDermott's own CEO, CFO and board leadership continued running the
  combined company; CB&I's own directors and most named executive officers exited at or
  near closing (with one transition-advisor exception), while rank-and-file CB&I
  employees received a contractual, time-bounded continuity commitment on pay and
  benefits rather than an organizational-continuity guarantee. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-102136, DEFM14A] [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-159577, Form 8-K]
