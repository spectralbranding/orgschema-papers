# Structural sub-dossier — WABCO Holdings Inc. separation from American Standard Companies Inc. (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Transaction and parties at closing
- WABCO Holdings Inc. is a newly formed holding company that, prior to distribution, holds
  all of the assets and liabilities of American Standard Companies Inc.'s Vehicle Control
  Systems business as a result of an internal reorganization implemented by American
  Standard. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-07-121554, Form 10-12B EX-99.1 information statement]
- The separation was effected as a spin-off: American Standard distributed all of the shares
  of WABCO common stock to American Standard shareholders on a pro rata basis (a pro rata
  dividend), after which American Standard shareholders would own 100% of WABCO. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-07-121554, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The distribution ratio was one share of WABCO common stock (plus a related preferred stock
  purchase right) for every three shares of American Standard common stock held on the record
  date. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-07-121554, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The separation was one leg of a three-way plan announced February 1, 2007 to spin off the
  Vehicle Control Systems business, sell the Bath and Kitchen business, and retain the Air
  Conditioning Systems and Services business with American Standard. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-07-121554, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## The customer-facing product and the operations that produce it
- The separated business is a provider of braking, stability, suspension and transmission
  control systems for commercial vehicles; its largest-selling products are pneumatic
  anti-lock braking systems (ABS), electronic braking systems (EBS), automated manual
  transmission systems, and air disk brakes. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-07-121554, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The business traces to a company founded in 1869 (Westinghouse Air Brake Company, "WABCO")
  and was acquired by American Standard in 1968; it operated as an integrated unit within
  American Standard for the intervening period. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-07-121554, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The product line separated together with the operations and people that produce it: the
  business carried an integrated global sourcing and manufacturing strategy (over 40% of its
  manufacturing workforce in low-cost countries) and its own engineering organization, with
  approximately 800 employees in engineering/product development. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-07-121554, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## What did NOT cross whole (the shared corporate substrate)
- The financials were prepared as combined ("carve-out") statements comprising the assets and
  liabilities used in managing the Vehicle Control Systems business; corporate/administrative
  costs had historically been allocated to the business ($21.8 million, $18.9 million and
  $19.7 million for the years presented) rather than incurred by it as a stand-alone entity. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-07-121554, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- After the separation American Standard would be contractually obligated to provide only
  those services specified in the Transition Services Agreement and related agreements; on
  expiration many such services would have to be provided internally or by third parties. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-07-121554, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Contractual arrangements bridging / allocating the shared substrate (closing-time)
- At separation the company and American Standard entered into a Separation and Distribution
  Agreement, a Tax Sharing Agreement, a Transition Services Agreement, an Employee Matters
  Agreement, an Indemnification and Cooperation Agreement, and other agreements; these were
  negotiated while the business was still part of American Standard. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-07-121554, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The Tax Sharing Agreement allocated tax responsibilities (including for a failed tax-free
  distribution) and imposed covenants restricting WABCO's post-separation actions; the
  Indemnification and Cooperation Agreement allocated certain liabilities between the parties. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-07-121554, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## People / culture at the boundary
- A special equity award (a "founders' grant" of stock options and restricted stock units,
  including a $6 million grant for certain employees) was made to approximately 200 employees
  and the Chairman of the Board effective at the distribution date, stated to foster an
  ownership culture. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-07-121554, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- A long-integrated, whole operating business (commercial-vehicle control-systems products
  together with its manufacturing processes and engineering organization) was separated from
  its parent by pro rata spin-off; what did not transfer as an intact operating substrate was
  the parent's shared corporate/administrative functions, bridged at closing by a Transition
  Services Agreement and a set of separation, tax, employee, and indemnification agreements. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-07-121554, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
