# Structural sub-dossier — Investment Technology Group, Inc. acquisition by Virtu Financial, Inc. (closing structure)

## Transaction and parties at closing
- On November 6, 2018, Investment Technology Group, Inc. ("the Company" / "ITG") and
  Virtu Financial, Inc. ("Virtu") entered into an Agreement and Plan of Merger under
  which Impala Merger Sub, Inc., an indirect wholly owned subsidiary of Virtu, would
  merge with and into the Company, with the Company surviving the merger as an indirect
  wholly owned subsidiary of Virtu. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-007677, Form DEFM14A]
- The transaction was structured as an all-cash acquisition: each outstanding share of
  Company common stock (other than specified excluded shares) would be converted into
  the right to receive $30.30 in cash without interest (the "merger consideration"),
  representing a premium of approximately 41% over the Company's average closing share
  price on the New York Stock Exchange. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-007677, Form DEFM14A]
- The Company is described as a global financial technology company that helps leading
  brokers and asset managers improve returns for investors, empowering traders to reduce
  the end-to-end cost of implementing investments via liquidity, execution, analytics
  and workflow technology solutions, with offices in Asia Pacific, Europe and North
  America and execution services offered in more than 50 countries; its shares traded
  on the NYSE under the symbol "ITG." [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-007677, Form DEFM14A]
- Virtu is described as a leading financial firm that leverages technology to deliver
  liquidity to global markets, acting as a market maker providing competitive bids and
  offers in over 25,000 securities at over 235 venues in 36 countries; its Class A
  common stock traded on NASDAQ under the symbol "VIRT." [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-007677, Form DEFM14A]
- Virtu entered into a debt financing commitment on November 6, 2018 to fund a portion
  of the aggregate merger consideration, and the merger agreement obligated Virtu to use
  reasonable best efforts to consummate that debt financing (or obtain alternative debt
  financing if any portion became unavailable), with the Company agreeing to provide
  reasonable cooperation in connection with the financing's arrangement. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-007677, Form DEFM14A]

## What crossed the ownership boundary at closing and what did not
- Legal vehicle: the Company's separate corporate existence continued post-merger as an
  indirect wholly owned subsidiary of Virtu (a "Surviving Corporation"), rather than
  being dissolved into Virtu's own legal entity at closing. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-007677, Form DEFM14A]
- Customer-facing product/asset: the Company's execution, liquidity, analytics and
  workflow technology business and its brokerage/technology client relationships passed
  to Virtu's ownership as the acquired operating business; the merger agreement
  contains representations by the Company covering, among other things, material
  contracts, intellectual property matters, and privacy and data security as part of
  what was being acquired. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-007677, Form DEFM14A]
- Listing status: upon completion of the merger, Company common stock would be removed
  from listing on the NYSE and deregistered under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934,
  and the Company would no longer file periodic reports with the SEC on account of its
  common stock. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-007677, Form DEFM14A]
- Employee stock purchase plan: the Company was required to suspend and then terminate
  its Employee Stock Purchase Plan effective no later than immediately prior to closing,
  returning unused contributed amounts to participants. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-007677, Form DEFM14A]
- Post-closing reorganization: the Company agreed, upon Parent's reasonable request, to
  assist Parent's preparations for reorganizing Parent's and the Surviving Corporation's
  corporate structure, capital structure, business, operations or assets following
  closing, indicating the acquired legal/operating structure was contemplated to be
  reshaped after the merger rather than preserved intact. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-007677, Form DEFM14A]

## Processes, people/organization, and contractual bridging at closing
- Integration/process combination: the merger agreement's Risk Controls covenant
  required the Company and its representatives to "assist and cooperate with Parent in
  integration planning, including with respect to implementing risk controls across the
  businesses of the Company and Parent to be combined following the Merger," following
  expiration of the HSR Act waiting period — i.e., the operational combining of the two
  firms' businesses was scheduled to occur after closing, not accomplished at signing. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-007677, Form DEFM14A]
- People/organization — executive retention risk: the Company's Chief Executive Officer
  and President had an employment agreement, and named executive officers had change-in
  -control agreements, providing for severance benefits upon a qualifying termination of
  employment following the merger; the Company estimated the aggregate value of
  severance-related cash compensation payable to certain executives, if severance
  -qualifying terminations occurred immediately after closing, at $3,031,788 (one set of
  estimates) and $4,479,103 (a further aggregate estimate covering additional named
  executives). [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-007677, Form DEFM14A]
- People/organization — broader workforce protection: under an Employee Matters
  covenant, Parent agreed to cause the Surviving Corporation to provide "Company
  Employees" whose employment terminates during the one-year period following the
  effective time with severance benefits at specified levels, and to provide, during
  that one-year period, base salary/wages and annual incentive opportunities no less
  favorable than those in effect immediately before closing, together with service
  credit under Parent's benefit plans for the Company Employees' prior service — a
  contractual bridge intended to preserve workforce continuity through the immediate
  post-closing period rather than transferring the operating business without workforce
  protections. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-007677, Form DEFM14A]
- Governance fusion note: the Company's Chief Executive Officer, President and other
  named executive officers were disclosed as having interests in the merger (equity
  award treatment, severance, change-in-control arrangements) that the proxy states
  stockholders should be aware of when considering the Company board's recommendation —
  i.e., the controlling executives who ran the pre-merger business also held
  financial interests tied to the transaction's completion. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-007677, Form DEFM14A]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction was structured as a whole-company, all-cash acquisition in which the
  target's legal vehicle survived as an indirect wholly owned subsidiary of the
  acquirer, its stock was removed from exchange listing and deregistered, and its
  customer-facing technology/execution business passed to the acquirer's ownership.
  Operational combination of the two businesses ("integration planning," "businesses...
  to be combined") was contractually deferred to the post-closing period, while
  workforce continuity (compensation, benefits, service credit) and certain executives'
  severance/change-in-control interests were addressed through closing-time contractual
  covenants rather than an immediate operational merger of people and processes. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-007677, Form DEFM14A]
