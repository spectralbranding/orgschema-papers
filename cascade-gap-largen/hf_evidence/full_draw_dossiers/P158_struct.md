# Structural sub-dossier — Caesars Entertainment Operating Company reorganization (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## The drawn filing (what it establishes)
- The drawn filing is a Current Report on Form 8-K furnished/filed by Caesars Entertainment
  Corporation ("CEC") reporting events of January 13–15, 2015; it is not itself a
  deal-terms document but records the commencement of the reorganization. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-011354, Form 8-K dated January 15, 2015]
- It reports that on January 15, 2015 (the "Petition Date"), Caesars Entertainment Operating
  Company, Inc. ("CEOC"), a majority-owned subsidiary of CEC, and certain wholly owned
  subsidiaries filed voluntary petitions for reorganization under Chapter 11 in the U.S.
  Bankruptcy Court for the Northern District of Illinois, pursuant to a Restructuring Support
  and Forbearance Agreement among CEC, CEOC and holders of CEOC first-lien-notes claims. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-011354, Form 8-K, Item 8.01]
- The same 8-K states the petitions accelerated CEOC debt instruments, itemized as including a
  Credit Agreement (~$5,354 million outstanding) and multiple secured- and unsecured-note
  indentures, and reports a related trustee resignation/appointment for CEOC's 12.75%
  Second-Priority Senior Secured Notes due 2018. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-011354, Form 8-K, Items 7.01 and 8.01]
- CEC stated it expected CEOC to be deconsolidated from CEC's financial statements beginning on
  the Petition Date. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-011354, Form 8-K, Item 8.01]

## The transaction as structured at consummation (plan effective October 6, 2017)
- On October 6, 2017 (the "Effective Date"), CEOC and the other Debtors emerged from bankruptcy
  and consummated their third amended joint plan of reorganization. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-309911, Form 8-K dated October 13, 2017 (event date October 6, 2017)]
- The reorganization divided CEOC into an operating company ("OpCo") and a property company
  ("PropCo"): OpCo (which merged with and into CEOC, LLC, a wholly owned CEC subsidiary, with
  CEOC LLC surviving) operates certain properties formerly held by CEOC; PropCo holds certain
  real property and related fixtures formerly held by CEOC and leases them to OpCo. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-309911, Form 8-K]
- PropCo became a separate entity, VICI Properties Inc., that is not consolidated by CEC and is
  owned by certain of CEOC's former creditors. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-309911, Form 8-K]
- CEC purchased 100% of OpCo's common equity for total consideration of approximately
  $2.3 billion (a $700 million cash component plus approximately $1.6 billion in stock); the
  plan issued approximately 562 million shares of CEC common stock (valued at $12.80 per share)
  to certain creditors, representing 59.1% of outstanding CEC common stock. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-309911, Form 8-K]
- Also on the Effective Date, Caesars Acquisition Company ("CAC") merged with and into CEC (CEC
  surviving), and Caesars Growth Properties Holdings, LLC ("CGPH") and Caesars Entertainment
  Resort Properties, LLC ("CERP") merged to form Caesars Resort Collection, LLC ("CRC"), a wholly
  owned CEC subsidiary. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-310774, Form 8-K dated October 16, 2017]

## What crossed the boundary vs. what did not, by tier
- The physical real-property assets (the land and improvements of the casino/hotel facilities)
  crossed to PropCo / VICI Properties, owned by former creditors and separate from CEC; the
  customer-facing operating businesses (the casinos and hotels) did NOT cross — they remained
  operated by OpCo/CEOC LLC as a wholly owned CEC subsidiary. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-309911, Form 8-K]
- The operating processes/management were retained on the CEC side rather than moving with the
  real estate: OpCo continued to operate the facilities, and material facility decisions
  (renovations, significant projects) were to be made by CEC in collaboration with the PropCo
  landlord's board and management. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-309911, Form 8-K]

## Contractual arrangements bridging the split substrate (closing-time)
- On the Effective Date, OpCo tenants leased the facilities back from PropCo landlords under three
  triple-net Lease Agreements (the CPLV Lease for Caesars Palace Las Vegas; the Non-CPLV Lease
  covering an initial portfolio of 17 gaming facilities in eight states plus a Kentucky racetrack
  and other properties; and the Joliet Lease), each with a 15-year initial term and four five-year
  renewal options, under which the tenant bears all operating costs, taxes, insurance and repairs. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-309911, Form 8-K, Item 1.01]
- Each OpCo tenant and PropCo landlord entered into a Management and Lease Support Agreement
  (MLSA) with CEC and a wholly owned CEC management entity ("CEC Manager"), under which CEC Manager
  manages the leased facilities (with operating expenses reimbursed by the OpCo tenant) and CEC
  guarantees the OpCo tenant's monetary obligations under the corresponding Lease Agreement. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-309911, Form 8-K, Item 1.01]
- Centralized enterprise functions were allocated through a Second Amended and Restated Omnibus
  License and Enterprise Services Agreement among CEOC LLC, CGPH, CERP, and Caesars Enterprise
  Services, LLC ("CES"); CES (established/funded in 2014) holds system-wide intellectual property
  and provides centralized services, and certain executives and employees of the members had been
  transitioned to CES with their services made available to the members. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-309911, Form 8-K]
- The lease terms provided that on a permitted change of control the quality and experience of
  facility management must remain consistent with that existing prior to the change, and included
  a Golf Course Use Agreement and a CPLV Trademark License bridging shared assets. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-309911, Form 8-K, Item 1.01]

## Controlling-interest change at closing
- Prior to the merger, Hamlet Holdings LLC (whose members were three individuals affiliated with
  Apollo Global Management, LLC and two affiliated with TPG Global, LLC) controlled CEC; on the
  Effective Date Hamlet Holdings contributed 87,605,299 CEC shares to CEC for cancellation and the
  prior sponsor services/monitoring arrangement was terminated. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-309911, Form 8-K]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction split a single operating enterprise into a creditor-owned real-property vehicle
  (PropCo / VICI Properties) and a CEC-owned operating company (OpCo / CEOC LLC), with the
  facilities' physical assets crossing to former creditors while operations, management and staff
  stayed on the CEC side — the two sides bridged at closing by long-term triple-net leases, CEC
  management-and-lease-support agreements with a CEC guaranty, and a centralized enterprise-services
  and IP-licensing agreement (CES). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-309911, Form 8-K] [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-310774, Form 8-K]
