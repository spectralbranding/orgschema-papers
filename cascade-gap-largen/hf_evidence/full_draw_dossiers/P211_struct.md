# Structural sub-dossier — WildHorse Resource Development Corporation merger with Chesapeake Energy Corporation (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Transaction and parties at closing
- Chesapeake Energy Corporation ("Chesapeake") and WildHorse Resource Development
  Corporation ("WildHorse") entered into an Agreement and Plan of Merger dated October
  29, 2018 (as amended December 12, 2018), among Chesapeake, Coleburn Inc. (a wholly
  owned Chesapeake subsidiary, "Merger Sub"), and WildHorse. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-357922, Form DEFM14A]
- The transaction was structured as a two-step merger: Merger Sub merged with and into
  WildHorse (the "First Merger"), with WildHorse surviving as a direct, wholly owned
  subsidiary of Chesapeake; immediately afterward the surviving corporation merged with
  and into Brazos Valley Longhorn, L.L.C. ("BVL"), a wholly owned Chesapeake limited
  liability company subsidiary, with BVL continuing as the surviving entity. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-357922, Form DEFM14A]
- Holders of WildHorse common stock could elect, per share, either (a) 5.336 shares of
  Chesapeake common stock plus $3.00 cash ("Mixed Consideration") or (b) 5.989 shares of
  Chesapeake common stock only ("Share Consideration"), with cash in lieu of fractional
  shares. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-357922, Form DEFM14A]
- WildHorse's CEO Jay C. Graham, entities affiliated with NGP Energy Capital Management,
  and an affiliate of Carlyle Group Management LLC (together holding approximately 69%
  of WildHorse voting shares) entered into Voting and Support Agreements committing to
  vote in favor of the merger. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-357922, Form DEFM14A]
- The merger closed February 1, 2019: Coleburn Inc. merged with and into WildHorse
  (First Merger), then WildHorse merged with and into BVL (Second Merger), with BVL
  continuing as a wholly owned Chesapeake subsidiary. [SRC: SEC accession 0001681714-19-000010, Form 8-K]

## What crossed at the product/asset tier
- WildHorse was described as an independent oil and natural gas company focused on
  acquisition, exploitation, development and production of oil, natural gas and NGL
  properties primarily in the Eagle Ford Shale and Austin Chalk in East Texas. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-357922, Form DEFM14A]
- At the effective time of the Second Merger, BVL (as successor to WildHorse) assumed
  the rights and obligations of WildHorse as issuer of $700 million aggregate principal
  amount of 6.875% Senior Notes due 2025, and became the borrower under the WildHorse
  credit facility (maximum credit amount $2.0 billion; then-current aggregate elected
  commitments and borrowing base of $1.3 billion). [SRC: SEC accession 0001681714-19-000010, Form 8-K]

## Legal-vehicle change at closing
- The certificate of formation and limited liability company agreement of BVL, as in
  effect immediately before the Second Merger, became the governing organizational
  documents of the surviving company; the acquired business's legal form changed from a
  Delaware corporation (WildHorse) to a Delaware limited liability company subsidiary of
  Chesapeake (BVL). [SRC: SEC accession 0001681714-19-000010, Form 8-K]
- In connection with the completion of the merger, WildHorse's common stock ceased being
  traded prior to the opening of the market on February 1, 2019, and WildHorse notified
  the NYSE, which filed a notification of removal from listing on Form 25. [SRC: SEC accession 0001681714-19-000010, Form 8-K]

## What did NOT cross at the people/management tier
- In accordance with the merger agreement, all ten members of WildHorse's board of
  directors (Jay C. Graham, Anthony Bahr, Brian A. Bernasek, Jonathan M. Clarkson, Scott
  A. Gieselman, David W. Hayes, Stephanie C. Hildebrandt, Grant E. Sims, Martin W.
  Sumner, and Tony R. Weber) resigned effective at the First Merger's effective time; the
  filing states none of these resignations resulted from any disagreement with
  WildHorse, its management, or its board. [SRC: SEC accession 0001681714-19-000010, Form 8-K]
- Also effective at the First Merger's effective time, each of WildHorse's officers
  ceased serving as an officer of WildHorse. [SRC: SEC accession 0001681714-19-000010, Form 8-K]
- Effective at the Second Merger, the sole member of the surviving company appointed
  Robert D. Lawler (Chesapeake's own President and CEO) as President and CEO, Domenic J.
  Dell'Osso, Jr. as EVP and CFO, and William M. Buergler as SVP and Chief Accounting
  Officer of the surviving company — each an existing Chesapeake officer, not a
  continuing WildHorse executive. [SRC: SEC accession 0001681714-19-000010, Form 8-K]
- Separately, as part of the deal negotiation, WildHorse secured the right to designate
  two WildHorse nominees to the board of the combined (Chesapeake) company at closing,
  subject to a charter amendment and nomination process — a governance-level accommodation
  distinct from operating-management continuity. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-357922, Form DEFM14A]
- As of the joint proxy statement/prospectus, WildHorse and two of its named executive
  officers (Messrs. Graham and Bahr) had held discussions toward possible new agreements
  effective after closing, but the filing states there was "no assurance" any such
  agreements would result, or what their terms would be. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-357922, Form DEFM14A]
- WildHorse's named executive officers were entitled to "golden parachute" severance
  compensation (subject to a non-binding advisory stockholder vote) upon a qualifying
  termination in connection with the merger. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-18-357922, Form DEFM14A]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction combined a whole-company stock-and-cash merger — in which WildHorse's
  oil and gas producing assets, wells, and outstanding public debt were assumed intact by
  a newly organized Chesapeake LLC subsidiary — with a complete turnover of the
  operating-management tier: every WildHorse director and officer left office at closing
  and was replaced (at the top three executive positions) by incumbent Chesapeake
  executives, while only a non-binding possibility of retaining two individual WildHorse
  executives, and a contractual right for two WildHorse board designees at the parent
  level, remained open at the time of the closing disclosures. [SRC: SEC accession 0001681714-19-000010, Form 8-K]
