# Structural sub-dossier — Adient plc separation from Johnson Controls (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Transaction and parties at closing
- On July 24, 2015 Johnson Controls announced plans to separate its Automotive Seating
  and Interiors businesses from the rest of Johnson Controls "by means of a spin-off of a
  newly formed company named Adient plc"; Johnson Controls (the existing publicly traded
  company) would continue to manage its building efficiency and power solutions
  businesses. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-16-015575, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1 information statement]
- To implement the separation, "Johnson Controls will transfer its automotive seating and
  interiors businesses to Adient, and in return, Adient will issue ordinary shares to
  Johnson Controls shareholders, pro rata to their respective holdings." [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-16-015575, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]
- Distribution mechanics: each Johnson Controls shareholder would receive one Adient
  ordinary share on October 31, 2016 for every ten Johnson Controls shares held as of the
  close of business on October 19, 2016 (the record date); no shareholder vote was
  required, and the distribution would generally be taxable to Johnson Controls
  shareholders for U.S. federal income tax purposes. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-16-015575, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]
- The separation was registered on Form 10-12B and amended on Form 10-12B/A; this
  information statement is the EX-99.1 to the amendment filed 2016-09-20. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-16-015575, Form 10-12B/A]

## What separated from the parent at the tier level
- The customer-facing product line that crossed the boundary was the automotive seating
  and interiors business; Adient described itself as "the global leader in automotive
  seating and interiors" and "an established and trusted partner for all of the world's
  major automakers." [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-16-015575, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]
- The operating substrate that crossed: approximately 74,000 employees, ~230 wholly- and
  majority-owned manufacturing or assembly facilities in 33 countries, 60 manufacturing
  locations in 32 cities, and 17 joint ventures, delivering 25 million seating systems used
  on more than 360 nameplates. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-16-015575, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]
- Shared corporate functions historically performed by the Parent did NOT transfer as an
  intact operating substrate: "Johnson Controls or one of its affiliates performed various
  corporate functions for Adient, such as accounting, information technology, and
  treasury," and Adient's historical/pro forma results "reflect allocations of corporate
  expenses from Johnson Controls for such functions." [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-16-015575, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]
- Adient's "working capital requirements and capital for its general corporate purposes …
  have historically been satisfied as part of the corporate-wide cash management policies
  of Johnson Controls," and following separation Adient "may need to obtain additional
  financing" independently. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-16-015575, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]
- The combined financial statements "have been prepared on a stand-alone basis and are
  derived from Johnson Controls' consolidated financial statements," including "allocations
  of expenses arising from shared services and infrastructure provided by Johnson Controls
  … such as information technology, accounting, legal, real estate and facilities,
  corporate advertising, risk and insurance services, treasury, shareholder services and
  other corporate and infrastructure services." [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-16-015575, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]

## Contractual arrangements governing the separated shared substrate (closing-time)
- The parties entered into a separation agreement (also called the separation and
  distribution agreement) plus "various other agreements to effect the separation and
  provide a framework for its relationship with Johnson Controls after the separation, such
  as a transition services agreement, a tax matters agreement, an employee matters
  agreement and a transitional trademark license agreement." [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-16-015575, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]
- Following the separation, Johnson Controls "will provide some of these [corporate]
  functions to Adient" under the transition services arrangement, bridging the shared
  substrate for a transitional period rather than transferring it whole. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-16-015575, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction detached a customer-facing product operation (automotive seating and
  interiors, with ~74,000 employees and its manufacturing footprint) from the Parent's
  shared corporate service substrate (accounting, IT, treasury, cash management, tax,
  trademark), with that substrate bridged at closing by a transition services agreement and
  related transitional agreements rather than transferred as an intact unit. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-16-015575, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]
