# Structural sub-dossier — The Babcock & Wilcox Company spin-off from McDermott International (closing structure)

## Transaction and parties at closing
- The information statement was furnished in connection with the distribution by McDermott International, Inc. ("McDermott") to its stockholders of all the outstanding shares of common stock of The Babcock & Wilcox Company ("B&W"); as of the statement date McDermott owned all of B&W's outstanding common stock, and McDermott's board approved the distribution of 100% of its interest in B&W. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-055423, Form 10-12B EX-99.1 information statement]
- The transaction was structured as a spin-off: each McDermott stockholder would receive one share of B&W common stock for a stated number of McDermott shares held on the record date, without paying cash or other consideration and without surrendering or exchanging McDermott shares; the distribution would not affect the number of McDermott shares held and no McDermott stockholder approval was required or sought. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-055423, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- B&W applied to list its common stock on the New York Stock Exchange under the symbol "BWC"; McDermott would not retain any ownership interest in B&W after the spin-off. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-055423, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## What crossed the ownership boundary at the tier level
- Customer-facing product/asset: B&W's assets and businesses primarily consisted of those McDermott reported as its Power Generation Systems and Government Operations segments; B&W operated in two business segments — Power Generation Systems (design, engineering, manufacture, supply, construction and maintenance of power generation and environmental control systems for utility and industrial customers, plus aftermarket parts and services) and Government Operations (manufacture of nuclear components and services to the U.S. Government, including uranium processing, environmental site restoration, and management/operation of U.S. Government-owned facilities for the DOE and NNSA). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-055423, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- People / organization: at December 31, 2009 B&W employed approximately 13,000 persons worldwide, not including approximately 10,000 joint-venture employees; the Government Operations segment managed and operated complex nuclear and national-security operations for the DOE and NNSA largely through joint ventures. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-055423, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Management continuity: Brandon C. Bethards had served as B&W's President and Chief Executive Officer since November 2008 (Interim CEO from September 2008), and Michael S. Taff would serve as Senior Vice President and Chief Financial Officer upon effectiveness of the spin-off — the operating management team was drawn from the businesses being distributed. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-055423, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Business scale at separation (historical): for the year ended December 31, 2009 the business generated revenues of approximately $2.9 billion and operating income of approximately $270 million (versus approximately $3.4 billion and $444.5 million for 2008). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-055423, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Shared substrate NOT transferred whole (closing-time)
- B&W had historically used McDermott's infrastructure to support its business functions, specifically: accounting and financial reporting; information technology and communications; legal; human resources and employee benefits; tax administration; and treasury and corporate finance. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-055423, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Following the spin-off B&W would no longer have access to McDermott's infrastructure and would need to establish its own; B&W had paid McDermott for these services on a cost-allocation basis and expected to incur costs in 2010 to stand up the necessary functions itself or procure them from third parties. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-055423, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Contractual arrangements bridging the shared substrate at closing
- Master separation agreement: to be entered into before the distribution date, containing the key provisions relating to the separation and identifying the assets to be transferred, liabilities to be assumed and contracts to be assigned between McDermott and B&W, and describing when and how those transfers, assumptions and assignments would occur; B&W would also indemnify McDermott against various claims and liabilities relating to the past operation of B&W's business. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-055423, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Transition services agreement: following the spin-off McDermott would continue to provide some of the shared services to B&W on a transitional basis for a period of up to one year; at the end of the transition period B&W would need to perform the functions itself or hire third parties. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-055423, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Tax sharing agreement: B&W and a subsidiary of McDermott would enter into an agreement providing for the sharing of taxes, under which B&W would generally be responsible for taxes imposed on it or on McDermott in the event that the spin-off and certain related preparatory transactions failed to qualify for tax-free treatment because of B&W's actions. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-055423, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Employee matters agreement: to be entered into in connection with the spin-off, under which the parties would, among other things, indemnify each other against certain liabilities arising from their respective businesses. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-055423, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Stated reasons for the spin-off
- McDermott's board and management stated the separation would provide: improved positioning for each company to accelerate growth based on its distinct strategy, markets, free cash flow and customer relationships; more efficient capital allocation without a holding-company/conglomerate structure; distinct publicly traded stock usable as currency for future acquisitions; elimination of the risk posed by recent U.S. Federal Acquisition Regulation changes limiting the U.S. Government's ability to contract with "inverted" companies and their subsidiaries; and sharpened management focus with closer alignment of management incentives to stockholder value. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-055423, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction separated the Power Generation Systems and Government Operations businesses (two reported segments; approximately 13,000 employees plus approximately 10,000 joint-venture employees) from McDermott by distributing 100% of the newly independent registrant's shares pro rata to McDermott stockholders in a tax-free spin-off (NYSE "BWC"), with the corporate service substrate historically provided by McDermott — accounting/financial reporting, IT and communications, legal, HR and employee benefits, tax administration, and treasury and corporate finance — NOT transferred whole but to be re-established by the registrant, bridged at closing by a master separation agreement, a transition services agreement (up to one year), a tax sharing agreement, and an employee matters agreement. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-055423, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
