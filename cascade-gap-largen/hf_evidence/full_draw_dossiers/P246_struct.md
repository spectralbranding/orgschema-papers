# Structural sub-dossier — Avista Corporation merger with Hydro One Limited (as-signed closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured as signed — what was agreed to cross the ownership
> boundary and what was not. It contains no information about whether or how the
> transaction was ultimately consummated.

## Transaction and parties as signed
- On July 19, 2017, Avista Corporation ("Avista" or the "Company"), a Washington
  corporation, entered into an Agreement and Plan of Merger with Hydro One Limited
  ("Hydro One"), a corporation organized under the laws of the Province of Ontario;
  Olympus Holding Corp. ("US Parent"), a newly formed Delaware corporation and an
  indirect, wholly owned subsidiary of Hydro One; and Olympus Corp. ("Merger Sub"), a
  newly formed Washington corporation and an indirect, wholly owned subsidiary of Hydro
  One. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-301020, Form DEFM14A]
- The agreement provided for Merger Sub to merge with and into Avista, with Avista
  surviving the merger as an indirect, wholly owned subsidiary of Hydro One. [SRC: SEC
  accession 0001193125-17-301020, Form DEFM14A]
- Each outstanding share of Avista common stock was to be converted into the right to
  receive $53.00 in cash, without interest (subject to dissenters'-rights and
  intercompany-share exceptions) — a 24% premium over the last trading price before the
  public announcement of the merger. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-301020, Form
  DEFM14A]
- Consummation was conditioned on, among other things, expiration/termination of the
  HSR Act waiting period; clearance by the Committee on Foreign Investment in the
  United States (CFIUS); and approvals from the Washington Utilities and Transportation
  Commission (WUTC), the Idaho Public Utilities Commission (IPUC), the Montana Public
  Service Commission (MPSC), the Oregon Public Utility Commission (OPUC), the Regulatory
  Commission of Alaska (RCA), the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) under FPA
  Section 203, and the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) under Communications Act
  Section 310. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-301020, Form DEFM14A]
- Approval of the merger agreement required the affirmative vote of holders of a
  majority of all outstanding shares of Company common stock entitled to vote, at a
  special meeting called for November 21, 2017. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-301020,
  Form DEFM14A]

## Business/asset tier as structured to cross whole
- As of June 30, 2017, Avista had two reportable segments: Avista Utilities, an
  operating division (not a subsidiary) generating, transmitting and distributing
  electricity and distributing natural gas to customers in eastern Washington, northern
  Idaho and parts of Oregon; and AEL&P, a wholly owned subsidiary providing electric
  utility service in Juneau, Alaska. Avista also held non-segment businesses including
  sheet-metal fabrication (Advanced Manufacturing and Development, d/b/a METALfx),
  venture-fund and real-estate investments, and an LNG-market venture conducted through
  Avista Capital and other subsidiaries. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-301020, Form
  DEFM14A]
- The transaction was structured as a whole-company cash acquisition: all of Avista's
  operating segments and other-business subsidiaries would become part of the surviving
  corporation as an indirect, wholly owned subsidiary of Hydro One, with no carve-out or
  spin-off of any tier. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-301020, Form DEFM14A]
- Between signing and closing, Avista agreed to conduct its business (and cause its
  subsidiaries to conduct their businesses) in the ordinary course consistent with past
  practice, preserve its present lines of business, and preserve satisfactory
  relationships with governmental authorities, employees, customers and suppliers,
  subject to specified negative covenants (limits on dividends, indebtedness, asset
  dispositions, capital expenditures and acquisitions). [SRC: SEC accession
  0001193125-17-301020, Form DEFM14A]

## Governance and management-continuity commitments (people/organization tier)
- The merger agreement committed that, within one business day after closing, the
  surviving corporation's board would be reconstituted to nine members: two designated
  by Hydro One who are Hydro One executives; three Pacific Northwest resident directors
  who are not Hydro One officers/employees/directors; three directors who were sitting
  Avista board members immediately before closing (including the Avista board chairman,
  if different from the surviving corporation's CEO); and the surviving corporation's
  chief executive officer. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-301020, Form DEFM14A]
- Officers of Avista immediately before the effective time were to become the officers
  of the surviving corporation. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-301020, Form DEFM14A]
- For a three-year "continuation period" after closing, Hydro One committed to provide
  each Company employee base salary/wages, annual cash bonus and long-term-incentive
  opportunities, and benefits no less favorable in the aggregate than immediately before
  closing, with prior service credited for most purposes and pre-existing-condition
  exclusions waived under replacement benefit plans; postretirement medical commitments
  were likewise made no less favorable during the continuation period. [SRC: SEC
  accession 0001193125-17-301020, Form DEFM14A]
- Hydro One committed that the surviving corporation would assume, honor and continue
  all Company benefit, compensation and severance arrangements (including Change of
  Control Agreements) as in effect immediately before closing, with the merger deemed a
  qualifying "change in control" under those arrangements. [SRC: SEC accession
  0001193125-17-301020, Form DEFM14A]

## Ring-fencing / community and operating commitments bridging the acquired substrate
- The merger agreement committed that, immediately following closing, the surviving
  corporation would (i) maintain its corporate headquarters in Spokane, Washington and
  its office locations in each other service territory, at no less a presence than
  immediately before closing; (ii) maintain at least its existing levels of community
  involvement and support initiatives in existing service territories; (iii) fund a $4
  million annual charitable-contribution budget, a $7 million initial contribution to
  its charitable foundation, and a $2 million annual contribution thereafter; (iv)
  maintain existing levels of economic development and capital investment; and (v)
  maintain safety, reliability and service-quality standards substantially comparable to
  or better than those Avista maintained as of closing. [SRC: SEC accession
  0001193125-17-301020, Form DEFM14A]
- At the effective time, Avista's articles of incorporation and bylaws were to be
  amended and restated to those of Merger Sub as in effect immediately before closing
  (subject to conforming changes needed to effectuate the governance commitments
  above). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-301020, Form DEFM14A]

## Legal-vehicle and listing terms at closing (as-signed)
- Upon consummation, Avista common stock was to no longer be traded on the New York
  Stock Exchange or any other public market, with Avista continuing to exist as an
  indirect, wholly owned subsidiary of Hydro One. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-301020,
  Form DEFM14A]
- If the merger were not completed, Avista would remain an independent public company
  and its shareholders would continue to own their shares; the agreement provided for a
  termination fee payable by one party to the other under specified circumstances. [SRC:
  SEC accession 0001193125-17-301020, Form DEFM14A]

## Structural summary (as-signed only)
- As structured at signing, the transaction was a whole-company, all-cash reverse
  triangular merger in which Avista's entire business (regulated utility operations,
  the Alaska subsidiary, and its other non-utility ventures) would cross into Hydro
  One's ownership together, with the product/asset, process and people/management tiers
  bridged rather than separated: management and officer continuity, a fixed board
  composition drawing on incumbent directors, a three-year employee-terms-parity
  commitment, and headquarters/community/safety ring-fencing commitments were built into
  the merger agreement rather than a transition-services carve-out structure. [SRC: SEC
  accession 0001193125-17-301020, Form DEFM14A]
