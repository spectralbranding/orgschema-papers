## Transaction and parties at closing
- Baxalta Incorporated was a wholly owned subsidiary of Baxter International Inc. formed to hold, directly or indirectly, the assets and liabilities associated with Baxter's biopharmaceuticals business; Baxter separated that business by distributing Baxalta common stock to Baxter shareholders. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-204641, Form 10-12B EX-99.1 information statement]
- At the distribution Baxter distributed approximately 80.5% of the outstanding shares of Baxalta common stock on a pro rata basis to Baxter shareholders, in a manner intended to be tax-free for U.S. federal income tax purposes, applying a distribution ratio of 1 share of Baxalta common stock for each share of Baxter common stock. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-204641, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Baxter retained approximately 19.5% of Baxalta's outstanding shares (approximately 131,837,976 shares) for a limited period of time and stated it planned to dispose of all retained Baxalta common stock after the distribution, potentially through one or more subsequent exchanges. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-204641, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The separation was registered on Form 10 (a Registration Statement on Form 10 relating to the securities was filed with the SEC under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-204641, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## What crossed the ownership boundary at the tier level
- Controlling intent: the board determined that separating the biopharmaceuticals business "is in the best interests of Baxter and its shareholders," including that "the separation will allow greater management focus on the distinct businesses of biopharmaceuticals and medical products by Baxalta and Baxter, respectively." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-204641, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Business model / value capture: Baxalta was described as a biopharmaceuticals business developing, manufacturing and commercializing therapies in hematology, immunology and oncology; Baxter's retained portfolio was to include intravenous solutions, nutritional therapies, and drug delivery and administration products. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-204641, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Customer-facing product: the biopharmaceutical product portfolio crossed into Baxalta, including hemophilia therapy ADVATE, inhibitor therapy FEIBA, and immunoglobulin therapies GAMMAGARD LIQUID and HYQVIA, alongside albumin products, spanning the Hemophilia, BioTherapeutics, Immunoglobulin and Oncology franchises. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-204641, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Operational processes: Baxalta was described as retaining an established manufacturing heritage with "world-class manufacturing operations" and globally managed quality control and quality assurance programs at its manufacturing sites, including oversight of contract manufacturers. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-204641, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- People / management: the management team was drawn largely from Baxter, led by Dr. Ludwig N. Hantson (Baxter's Corporate Vice President and President, BioScience, expected to become Baxalta CEO), with Robert J. Hombach (Baxter's then-CFO) expected to become Baxalta's CFO, David D. Meek as EVP and President, Oncology, and Anne-Marie Law as EVP and Head of Human Resources. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-204641, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Shared substrate that did NOT transfer whole
- Baxalta stated it "has not historically operated as a standalone business" and had "various relationships with Baxter whereby Baxter provides services to the Company," including "executive oversight, treasury, finance, legal, human resources" and other corporate services allocated as corporate overhead. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-204641, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Baxalta's financial statements were "prepared on a 'carve-out' basis," as Baxalta "did not operate as a standalone entity in the past" / "was not operated as a separate, independent company for the periods presented," reflecting an allocation of corporate costs for administrative services (tax, treasury, finance, audit, risk management, among others). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-204641, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Contractual arrangements bridging the shared substrate at closing
- Baxalta and Baxter were to enter into a separation and distribution agreement to effect the separation and provide a framework for their post-distribution relationship, together with a suite of other agreements: a transition services agreement, a long term services agreement, a tax matters agreement, a manufacturing and supply agreement, an employee matters agreement, a trademark license agreement, a Galaxy license agreement, an international commercial operations agreement, a shareholder's and registration rights agreement (with respect to Baxter's continuing ownership of Baxalta common stock), and certain other commercial agreements. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-204641, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Under the manufacturing and supply agreement, "Baxalta or Baxter, as the case may be, will manufacture, label, and package products for the other party," with initial terms ranging from five to ten years; because products had historically been transferred at cost between Baxter and the businesses comprising Baxalta, these arrangements would result in changes to both sales and cost of goods sold. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-204641, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction separated a biopharmaceutical product operation (hematology, immunology and oncology therapies, e.g. ADVATE, FEIBA, GAMMAGARD LIQUID) from Baxter by distributing approximately 80.5% of a newly formed subsidiary's shares to Baxter shareholders (Baxter retaining approximately 19.5%); the entity had never operated as a standalone company and its financials were prepared on a "carve-out" basis, so the shared corporate service substrate (treasury, finance, legal, HR, IT, tax, executive oversight) and cross-entity product manufacturing were not transferred as an intact operating whole but were bridged at closing by a separation and distribution agreement, a transition services agreement, a long term services agreement, and a five-to-ten-year reciprocal manufacturing and supply agreement, among related agreements. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-15-204641, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
