# Structural sub-dossier — State National Companies, Inc. acquisition by Markel Corporation (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Transaction and parties at closing
- On July 26, 2017, State National Companies, Inc. ("State National" or the "Company"),
  a Delaware corporation, entered into an Agreement and Plan of Merger with Markel
  Corporation ("Parent" or "Markel"), a Virginia corporation, and Markelverick
  Corporation, a Delaware corporation and direct, wholly-owned subsidiary of Parent
  ("Merger Sub"). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-281924, DEFM14A]
- Under the merger agreement, Merger Sub was to merge with and into the Company, with the
  Company continuing in existence as the surviving corporation and a direct,
  wholly-owned subsidiary of Parent; as a result of the merger under Delaware law, the
  surviving corporation retains "all the properties, rights, privileges, powers,
  interests and franchises" of the Company and Merger Sub, and remains subject to their
  restrictions, disabilities, duties and liabilities. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-281924, DEFM14A]
- The Company's principal executive offices were located at 1900 L. Don Dodson Drive,
  Bedford, Texas, and its common stock traded on the NASDAQ Global Select Market under
  the symbol "SNC." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-281924, DEFM14A]

## Business and value-capture model at closing
- State National was described as a specialty provider of property and casualty
  insurance services operating in two niche markets: a lender services segment
  (collateral protection insurance, insuring vehicles held as collateral for loans made
  by credit unions, banks and specialty finance companies) and a program services
  segment (leveraging the Company's "A" (Excellent) A.M. Best rating, licenses and
  reputation to provide market access to third parties in exchange for ceding fees).
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-281924, DEFM14A]
- The financial advisor's analysis characterized the program services division, together
  with comparable insurance broker companies, as having a fee-for-service model "rather
  than a risk-retention model." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-281924, DEFM14A]

## Consideration and what crossed the boundary
- At the effective time, each outstanding share of Company common stock (other than
  treasury shares, shares held by subsidiaries, dissenting shares, and restricted stock)
  was to be canceled and converted into the right to receive $21.00 in cash, without
  interest, the "merger consideration." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-281924, DEFM14A]
- The $21.00 per-share merger consideration represented a 38% premium to the Company's
  30-day volume-weighted average stock price as of May 18, 2017, the last trading day
  before published market speculation about a potential sale. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-281924, DEFM14A]
- As a whole-company stock acquisition, the transaction structure carried the Company's
  full operating entity — its insurance licenses, A.M. Best rating, and its underwriting
  subsidiaries — into Parent's ownership as a surviving, intact subsidiary, rather than
  separating a product line from the processes or people that run it. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-281924, DEFM14A]
- The merger required change-of-control regulatory approvals (Form A / Form E filings)
  from multiple state insurance departments covering City National Insurance Company,
  Independent Specialty Insurance Company, National Specialty Insurance Company, State
  National Insurance Company, Inc., and United Specialty Insurance Company. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-281924, DEFM14A]

## Management and employee continuity at closing
- The merger agreement provided that Parent would maintain, until December 31, 2018,
  certain levels of compensation and benefits for Company employees, including executive
  officers, whose employment was not terminated. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-281924, DEFM14A]
- Named executive officers held severance agreements providing payments if their service
  was separated other than for cause, death, or disability, or if they resigned for good
  reason (e.g., Terry Ledbetter at 3 times base salary plus target bonus; Matthew Freeman
  at 30 months of base salary; David Hale, John Pearson and David Cleff at 2.5 times base
  salary; Trace Ledbetter and Luke Ledbetter at 1.75 times base salary). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-281924, DEFM14A]
- Each executive officer agreed not to engage in noncompetition or nonsolicitation of
  employees and customers for two years following the merger. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-281924, DEFM14A]
- The merger agreement provided indemnification arrangements and continuation of
  insurance arrangements for the Company's then-current officers and directors for six
  years after completion of the transaction. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-281924, DEFM14A]

## Mechanics of separation from public-market status
- Parent and the Company agreed to use reasonable best efforts to cause the Company's
  securities to be removed from listing on NASDAQ and deregistered under the Securities
  Exchange Act of 1934 as soon as reasonably practicable following the effective time.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-281924, DEFM14A]
- The merger agreement provided for a termination fee of $27,564,000 or $13,782,000,
  payable by the Company to Parent under certain specified circumstances if the merger
  agreement were terminated. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-281924, DEFM14A]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction was structured as a whole-company stock-for-cash acquisition in which
  Merger Sub merged into State National, leaving State National as an intact, wholly-owned
  operating subsidiary of Markel; the Company's insurance licenses, underwriting
  subsidiaries, and workforce crossed together rather than being separated by product,
  process, or people, with continuity bridged at closing through compensation-and-benefit
  continuation commitments, executive severance and noncompetition terms, and continued
  D&O indemnification, while the Company's status as an independent public registrant was
  to end via removal from NASDAQ listing and Exchange Act deregistration. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-281924, DEFM14A]
