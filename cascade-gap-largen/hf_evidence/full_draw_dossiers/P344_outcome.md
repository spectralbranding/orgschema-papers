## Realized business record under private ownership (press-sourced)

- On May 15, 2023, Envision Healthcare — described in press coverage as "KKR-owned" —
  filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection, citing approximately $7.7 billion in debt
  obligations, declining patient volumes, implementation of the federal No Surprises
  Act, and disputes with health insurers as contributing factors. [SRC: https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/envision-chapter-11-bankruptcy/650277/]
- Under the reorganization plan disclosed at the filing, all of the company's debt
  except a revolving credit facility for working capital was to be equitized or
  cancelled, reducing debt by about $5.6 billion; the company's ambulatory-surgery unit
  was to purchase Envision's surgery centers for $300 million plus a waiver of
  intercompany loans, separating that unit's ownership from the physician-services
  business going forward. [SRC: https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/envision-chapter-11-bankruptcy/650277/]
- Press coverage characterized the bankruptcy as wiping out KKR's 2018 equity
  investment, reported as "over $5 billion," in a transaction "valued at $9.9 billion
  including debt." [SRC: https://pestakeholder.org/news/it-should-come-as-no-surprise-that-kkr-owned-envision-healthcare-has-finally-declared-bankruptcy/]
- Reporting in early November 2023 described Envision Healthcare as having emerged from
  Chapter 11 restructuring, with reported debt reduced by more than 70%; Henry Howe
  (the company's then-CFO) was named interim CEO effective December 1, 2023, and Steve
  Nelson (president of ChenMed) was named to head the restructured company's board; the
  restructuring split Envision from its Amsurg ambulatory-surgery unit, which was to
  operate under new, separate ownership. [SRC: https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/envision-healthcare-emerges-bankruptcy-restructuring-names-interim-ceo/699006/]

## Outcome summary (realized record only)

- Because the company was taken private and deregistered its common stock in October
  2018, there is no public 10-K outcome record for the ~2021-2023 window; the traceable
  realized record shows that, roughly four-and-a-half to five years after the 2018
  going-private transaction, the company (still described in contemporaneous press as
  "KKR-owned") filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on May 15, 2023 citing about
  $7.7 billion in debt obligations, equitized or cancelled roughly $5.6 billion of that
  debt through the reorganization, separated its ambulatory-surgery unit into
  separate ownership via an intercompany sale, and emerged from bankruptcy in
  November 2023 with new interim executive leadership — a trajectory press coverage
  described as eliminating KKR's original equity investment in the 2018 transaction.
