## Registrant, business model, and legal vehicle at the time
- Inland Real Estate Corporation is a Maryland corporation formed May 12, 1994; it is described as a self-managed, publicly traded real estate investment trust (REIT) that owns and operates neighborhood, community, power, and single-tenant retail centers located primarily in Midwest markets. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-12-012808, Form 10-K (FY2011) Item 1]
- Its stated external growth model consists of three types of unconsolidated joint-venture activities: a venture with Inland Private Capital Corporation (IPCC) through which it sources, acquires, and manages properties for 1031 tenant-in-common / Delaware Statutory Trust buyers, and asset-based ventures with New York State Teachers' Retirement System (NYSTRS) and PGGM, through which it sources, acquires, and manages Midwest retail properties for fee income. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-12-012808, Form 10-K (FY2011) Item 1]
- As of December 31, 2011 it owned interests in 146 investment properties including those owned through unconsolidated joint ventures, and fee simple interests in 108 investment properties excluding those joint ventures. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-12-012808, Form 10-K (FY2011) Item 1 / Item 2]
- The "Inland" name and logo are used under license from The Inland Group, Inc., for the business of acquiring, owning, operating, managing, and disposing of retail centers. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-12-012808, Form 10-K (FY2011) Item 1]
- All shopping centers are aggregated and reported as a single operating segment because the properties exhibit highly similar economic characteristics. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-12-012808, Form 10-K (FY2011) Item 1]

## The transaction as structured (unit of analysis)
- During the year ended December 31, 2011, the Company took control of Orchard Crossing (Ft. Wayne, Indiana), a property previously held through its joint venture with Pine Tree Institutional Realty, LLC ("Pine Tree"); prior to this change-in-control transaction the property was unconsolidated and accounted for under the equity method. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-12-012808, Form 10-K (FY2011) Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements]
- The change-in-control transaction was accounted for as a business combination, requiring the Company to record the property's assets and liabilities at fair value using level-three inputs. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-12-012808, Form 10-K (FY2011) Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements]
- Estimated fair values recorded at the date of acquisition for Orchard Crossing: investment properties $19,800; other assets $299; total assets acquired $20,099; mortgages payable $14,800; other liabilities $294; net assets acquired $5,005 (amounts in thousands). [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-12-012808, Form 10-K (FY2011) Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements]
- The Company's investment in and advances to the unconsolidated joint venture prior to the change-in-control transaction was $6,597 (thousands). [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-12-012808, Form 10-K (FY2011) Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements]

## Tier-level detail — what crossed the ownership boundary and what did not
- What crossed: the customer-facing asset — the Orchard Crossing retail center — moved from unconsolidated (equity-method) status onto the Company's consolidated balance sheet at fair value, together with its associated mortgage debt of $14,800 (thousands). [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-12-012808, Form 10-K (FY2011) Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements]
- Processes and people that operate the asset did NOT newly transfer at consolidation: the Company was already the operator of its joint-venture properties. It describes itself as the managing partner responsible for the day-to-day activities of its ventures and states it earns fees for asset management, property management, leasing, and acquisition activities provided to those ventures. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-12-012808, Form 10-K (FY2011) Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements]
- Thus the product/asset's ownership and control crossed the boundary while the producing/delivering processes (property management, leasing) and the people running them were already in-house and continued unchanged; the transaction re-characterized ownership accounting rather than moving an operating organization. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-12-012808, Form 10-K (FY2011) Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements]

## Shared-substrate / bridging arrangements at the time
- The Company is a member of a limited-liability captive insurance association owned in equal proportions with three other REITs sponsored by an affiliate of The Inland Group, Inc., serviced by an affiliated management entity — a shared operating substrate treated as a variable interest entity in which the Company is not the primary beneficiary. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-12-012808, Form 10-K (FY2011) Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements]
- No separate transition-services or merger agreement governs this change-in-control consolidation; the property's management was already performed in-house under the pre-existing joint-venture and management-fee arrangements. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-12-012808, Form 10-K (FY2011) Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- Within the FY2011 annual report, the transaction of record is the Company's assumption of control over a retail center (Orchard Crossing) that it already managed but held through a joint venture with Pine Tree; the asset and its mortgage were consolidated at fair value as a business combination, while the operating processes and personnel that run the property were pre-existing and did not separately transfer. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-12-012808, Form 10-K (FY2011) Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements]
