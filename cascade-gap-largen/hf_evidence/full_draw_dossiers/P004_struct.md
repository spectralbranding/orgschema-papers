# Structural sub-dossier — AOL Inc. separation from Time Warner (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Transaction and parties at closing
- The transaction is a spin-off: Time Warner Inc. separated its wholly-owned subsidiary
  AOL Inc. by distributing all of the shares of AOL common stock on a pro rata basis to
  the holders of Time Warner common stock, after which AOL was to be an independent,
  publicly-traded company. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-192456, Form 10-12B EX-99.1 information statement]
- The distribution was structured to be tax-free to Time Warner shareholders for U.S.
  Federal income tax purposes, except to the extent of cash received in lieu of
  fractional shares; no Time Warner shareholder vote or consideration was required, and
  the shares were to be delivered in book-entry form. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-192456, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- AOL common stock (par value $0.01) was to be listed on the New York Stock Exchange
  under the symbol "AOL." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-192456, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Internal transactions preceding the distribution: prior to the spin-off Time Warner
  would convert AOL Holdings LLC into a Delaware corporation to be named AOL Inc., then
  cause substantially all of the assets and liabilities of AOL LLC (the wholly-owned
  subsidiary that then held the AOL business) to be transferred to and assumed by AOL
  Inc.; ownership of AOL LLC itself would then be transferred to and retained by Time
  Warner. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-192456, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- On July 8, 2009, Time Warner completed the purchase of Google's 5% interest in AOL,
  after which AOL became a 100%-owned subsidiary of Time Warner. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-192456, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## What crossed the ownership boundary at the tier level
- Customer-facing product/asset: AOL was described as a leading global web services
  company whose business spanned online content, products and services offered to
  consumers, publishers and advertisers, with revenues from advertising on its owned-and-
  operated properties ("AOL Media"), advertising on third-party websites (the "Third
  Party Network," branded "Advertising.com" / "AOL Advertising"), and a subscription
  access service. This product operation was what separated into the standalone company.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-192456, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Controlling intent / management: in April 2009, before the spin-off, Tim Armstrong was
  appointed AOL's Chairman and Chief Executive Officer and commenced a review of AOL's
  strategy, developing a strategy focused on attracting/engaging Internet consumers and
  generating advertising revenues, with the subscription access service managed as a
  distribution channel. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-192456, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Shared corporate substrate that did NOT transfer intact: AOL stated it had
  "historically operated as part of Time Warner's corporate organization, and Time Warner
  has assisted us by providing certain corporate functions," and that following the
  spin-off Time Warner would have "no obligation to provide assistance to us other than
  the interim services" described in the related-party agreements. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-192456, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Contractual arrangements bridging the shared substrate (closing-time)
- The parties intended to enter into a Separation and Distribution Agreement, a
  Transition Services Agreement, a Tax Matters Agreement, an Employee Matters Agreement,
  and an Intellectual Property Cross-License Agreement in connection with the separation.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-192456, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Transition Services Agreement: each of AOL and Time Warner would provide the other with
  certain services for a limited time "to help ensure an orderly transition"; the final
  schedule of services had not yet been determined at the time of the information
  statement. Given the short-term nature of the agreement, AOL stated it was developing a
  plan to build internal capabilities to eliminate reliance on Time Warner for the
  services it would provide immediately following the distribution. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-192456, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- IT Applications and Database Agreement: each party would provide the other with
  internally developed software applications; Time Warner would transfer to AOL all
  AOL-data then residing on Time Warner's databases and, to the extent necessary for
  business continuity, continue to provide AOL access to certain network and/or hosted
  applications. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-192456, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Tax Matters Agreement: AOL had been a member of Time Warner's consolidated tax group;
  the agreement would allocate tax liabilities between the parties after the spin-off and
  included covenants restricting AOL's ability to pursue certain strategic transactions
  that could jeopardize the tax-free status of the distribution. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-192456, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction detached AOL's customer-facing web-services product operation
  (advertising on AOL Media, the Third Party Network, and a subscription access service)
  from Time Warner's shared corporate-function substrate, with that substrate — corporate
  services, data hosting, IT applications, and tax administration — bridged at closing by
  a Transition Services Agreement, an IT Applications and Database Agreement, a Tax
  Matters Agreement, and related agreements rather than transferred whole. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-09-192456, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
