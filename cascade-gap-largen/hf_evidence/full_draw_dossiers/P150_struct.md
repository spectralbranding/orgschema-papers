# Structural sub-dossier — Penn National Gaming proposed acquisition of the unfinished Fontainebleau Las Vegas (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## The drawn filing — what it establishes
- The drawn Form 8-K reports that on November 16, 2009, Nevada Gaming Ventures, Inc. ("NGVI"), an indirect subsidiary of Penn National Gaming, Inc. ("Penn"), entered into (a) a "stalking horse" Asset Purchase Agreement and (b) a Debtor-In-Possession (DIP) Credit Agreement with the Fontainebleau Las Vegas seller entities. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-09-065443, Form 8-K Item 8.01]
- The 8-K is a proposal/agreement-announcement filing, not a closing document: the effectiveness of both the Stalking Horse Agreement and the DIP Credit Agreement was expressly stated to be "subject to the approval of the Bankruptcy Court." [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-09-065443, Form 8-K Item 8.01]
- The full Asset Purchase Agreement (dated November 16, 2009) was filed as Exhibit 99.1 and the DIP Credit Agreement as Exhibit 99.2 to the same 8-K. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-09-065443, Form 8-K Item 9.01]

## Parties and transaction type as structured
- The buyer vehicle was NGVI, an indirect subsidiary of Penn. The sellers were Fontainebleau Las Vegas Holdings, LLC; Fontainebleau Las Vegas, LLC; Fontainebleau Las Vegas Capital Corp. (the "Resort Sellers") plus three "Retail Sellers." [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-09-065443, Form 8-K Item 8.01]
- Each of the Resort Sellers was a debtor in a chapter 11 case before the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Florida; the Retail Sellers had agreed to commence their own chapter 11 cases. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-09-065443, Form 8-K Item 8.01]
- The transaction was structured as a court-supervised asset purchase: if the agreements were approved, NGVI would be designated the "stalking horse" bidder in an auction of the Project under Section 363 of the U.S. Bankruptcy Code, and its offer would be the standard against which any other qualifying bids were evaluated. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-09-065443, Form 8-K Item 8.01]
- The sellers agreed to request the Bankruptcy Court set January 15, 2010 as the deadline for other bids and schedule the auction for January 21, 2010. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-09-065443, Form 8-K Item 8.01]

## What would have crossed the ownership boundary (asset, not going concern)
- The asset to be purchased was defined as the assets comprising "the unfinished hotel, resort and casino known as the Fontainebleau Las Vegas (the 'Project')" — a partially-constructed real-estate project, not an operating business. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-09-065443, Form 8-K Item 8.01]
- Consideration: absent any higher or better bid, NGVI would acquire the Project for (i) $50 million in cash (subject to reduction for certain remediation costs and other adjustments) and (ii) release of the sellers, at closing, from any outstanding DIP obligations. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-09-065443, Form 8-K Item 8.01]
- Because the Project was unfinished and not operating, the drawn filing describes no customer-facing operating product, no going-concern operating processes, and no transferring workforce/management; the DIP funds were to be spent "to stabilize the unfinished buildings at the Project and on other budgeted expenses." [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-09-065443, Form 8-K Item 8.01]
- The filing discloses no transitional-services, employee-transfer, or shared-services arrangement — consistent with an unfinished-asset purchase rather than an operating-business transfer. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-09-065443, Form 8-K Item 8.01]

## Interim funding bridge at signing
- Pursuant to the DIP Credit Agreement, if approved, NGVI would provide the resort seller a revolving credit facility of approximately $51.5 million through February 9, 2010, subject to earlier termination on certain events. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-09-065443, Form 8-K Item 8.01]
- A termination fee equal to 3% of ($50 million plus the sellers' outstanding DIP obligations) would be payable to NGVI if, after Bankruptcy Court approval, the agreement terminated in specified circumstances including the court's approval of a competing transaction. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-09-065443, Form 8-K Item 8.01]

## Whether the transaction closed (consummation)
- The transaction did NOT close: it was a stalking-horse proposal that was outbid at the Section 363 auction. Penn's own subsequent disclosure states that, after Penn "in November submitted a proposal to become the stalking horse bidder in the auction process, another bidder emerged who was willing to pay, on a relative basis, more than what we believe the unfinished asset is worth given the cost to complete the project." [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-10-005666, Form 8-K Exhibit 99.1 (press release dated February 4, 2010)]
- No ownership boundary was crossed by Penn: NGVI/Penn did not acquire the Fontainebleau Las Vegas Project. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-10-005666, Form 8-K Exhibit 99.1 (press release dated February 4, 2010)]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The drawn filing documents a proposed court-supervised (Section 363) purchase of an unfinished real-estate asset — the never-opened Fontainebleau Las Vegas hotel/resort/casino — by an indirect Penn subsidiary (NGVI) for $50 million cash plus DIP release, backed by a ~$51.5 million interim DIP revolver, with the buyer positioned only as the stalking-horse bidder subject to Bankruptcy Court approval and a competitive auction. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-09-065443, Form 8-K Item 8.01]
- The transaction was NON-CONSUMMATED: a competing bidder outbid Penn at the auction and Penn did not acquire the asset; because the asset was an unfinished, non-operating project, no operating product, processes, or workforce were ever positioned to cross the ownership boundary. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-10-005666, Form 8-K Exhibit 99.1 (press release dated February 4, 2010)]
