# Structural sub-dossier — Crosstex Energy, L.P. / Apache Permian Basin processing arrangement (closing structure)

> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the

## The drawn filing (what it establishes)
- The drawn Current Report on Form 8-K (date of earliest event July 11, 2011) is filed by
  Crosstex Energy, L.P. ("the Partnership," NASDAQ: XTEX), a Delaware limited partnership
  headquartered in Dallas, Texas. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-11-038694, Form 8-K]
- Under Item 1.01 the filing establishes a Second Amendment to the Partnership's Amended
  and Restated Credit Agreement (Bank of America, N.A., as Administrative Agent) and, under
  a separate Item 1.01 heading, a Supplemental Indenture (Wells Fargo Bank, N.A., trustee)
  adding two subsidiaries as guarantors of the 8.875% senior unsecured notes due 2018;
  it is therefore principally a financing-amendment filing that references the underlying
  business arrangement rather than a stand-alone deal-terms document. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-11-038694, Form 8-K]
- The 8-K discloses that the Partnership's subsidiary, Crosstex Permian, LLC, entered into
  a joint interest arrangement with Apache Midstream LLC, "as outlined in more detail in
  Item 7.01," whose terms are set out in the press release furnished as Exhibit 99.1.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-11-038694, Form 8-K, Items 1.01 and 7.01]

## Parties and transaction type (from the contemporaneous closing-era primary)
- The transaction is a jointly funded, jointly owned new-build asset: Crosstex Energy, L.P.
  and Apache Corporation "will jointly invest $85 million in a new-build natural gas
  processing facility in the Permian Basin in West Texas," funding the project equally with
  "each hold[ing] a 50 percent working interest." [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-11-038694, Form 8-K EX-99.1 press release]
- The facility serves Apache's Deadwood development in Glasscock County; the initial phase
  provides interim and long-term processing, compression and residue gas takeaway.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-11-038694, Form 8-K EX-99.1 press release]

## What crossed the boundary at closing, and what did not
- Customer-facing asset (product): the newly built processing capacity is the co-owned
  asset — an interim refrigeration plant of 20 MMcf/day (expected operational Q4 2011) and
  a cryogenic gas processing facility of 50 MMcf/day (expected operational Q2 2012). Both
  parties take a 50% working interest in this asset. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-11-038694, Form 8-K EX-99.1 press release]
- Operating processes: the processes that build and run the asset did NOT cross to a shared
  operator — "Crosstex will manage construction and operate the facilities," so operational
  control remained solely with the Partnership while ownership was split 50/50.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-11-038694, Form 8-K EX-99.1 press release]
- People / organization: no transfer of management or workforce is described; Apache's role
  is stated as an upstream co-investor and producer ("premier independent energy company
  ... one of the largest producers in the region") supplying gas to the co-owned plant,
  while Crosstex supplies the midstream operating organization. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-11-038694, Form 8-K EX-99.1 press release]
- Legal vehicle: the arrangement is structured as split working interests in the asset (a
  joint interest arrangement), not as a transfer into a pre-existing operating company; a
  contemporaneous later filing characterizes the same holding as a "50-percent undivided
  ownership interest" in the facility. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-11-038694, Form 8-K EX-99.1 press release] [SRC: SEC accession 0001179060-16-000036, Form 10-K (FY2015)]

## Contractual arrangements bridging the shared substrate (closing-time)
- The Credit Agreement Amendment was executed to accommodate the arrangement: it permits
  Apache to hold a first-priority lien on certain of the co-owned assets (the new-build
  processing facility and related Permian Basin assets) to secure obligations Crosstex
  Permian owes Apache, and it increases the Partnership's ability to make investments in
  joint ventures and subsidiaries without those subsidiaries becoming guarantors under the
  Credit Agreement. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-11-038694, Form 8-K, Item 1.01]
- Concurrently the Partnership caused Crosstex Permian, LLC and Crosstex Permian II, LLC to
  be added as guarantors under the Credit Agreement and as guarantors of the senior notes
  via the Supplemental Indenture. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-11-038694, Form 8-K, Items 1.01]

## Related but separate (wholly-owned, not co-owned)
- Separately from the co-owned facility, Crosstex alone agreed to purchase and upgrade the
  abandoned Patriot Fractionator (a rail terminal) in Midland County for a $12 million
  investment, to move Apache raw-make NGL by rail to Crosstex's Eunice fractionation
  facility in Louisiana; this asset is wholly Crosstex-owned and operated. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-11-038694, Form 8-K EX-99.1 press release]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- At closing a customer-facing productive asset (new-build Permian gas-processing capacity)
  was placed under split 50/50 ownership between the Partnership and an upstream producer,
  while the processes to construct and operate that asset, and the operating organization
  behind them, remained solely with the Partnership; the shared-asset substrate was
  contractually bridged by a lien accommodation and investment-capacity changes in the
  Partnership's credit facility rather than by a transfer of operations or people. [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-11-038694, Form 8-K and Form 8-K EX-99.1 press release]
