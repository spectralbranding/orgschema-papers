# Structural sub-dossier — Cigna Corporation / Express Scripts Holding Company combination (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Transaction and parties
- Cigna Corporation ("Cigna") and Express Scripts Holding Company ("Express Scripts")
  entered into an Agreement and Plan of Merger dated March 8, 2018 (amended June 27,
  2018) with Halfmoon Parent, Inc. ("New Cigna," a direct wholly owned subsidiary of
  Cigna incorporated March 6, 2018 solely to effect the transaction), Halfmoon I, Inc.
  ("Cigna Merger Sub") and Halfmoon II, Inc. ("Express Scripts Merger Sub"), each a
  direct wholly owned subsidiary of New Cigna. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-032613, DEFM14A cover letter]
- Structure: Cigna Merger Sub merged with and into Cigna, and Express Scripts Merger Sub
  merged with and into Express Scripts, with each of Cigna and Express Scripts surviving
  as a direct wholly owned subsidiary of New Cigna — a double reverse-triangular merger
  under a newly formed top holding company, rather than either company acquiring the
  other directly. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-032613, DEFM14A "The Mergers"]
- Consideration: each share of Cigna common stock converted into one share of New Cigna
  common stock; each share of Express Scripts common stock converted into 0.2434 of a
  share of New Cigna common stock plus $48.75 in cash. Based on estimated pre-closing
  shares outstanding, former Cigna stockholders were expected to hold approximately 64%
  and former Express Scripts stockholders approximately 36% of New Cigna common stock
  immediately after closing. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-032613, DEFM14A cover letter]
- At the effective time (December 20, 2018), Cigna's corporate name was changed from
  "Cigna Corporation" to "Cigna Holding Company," and New Cigna's name was changed from
  "Halfmoon Parent, Inc." to "Cigna Corporation"; New Cigna common stock began trading on
  the NYSE, while the pre-merger Cigna and Express Scripts common stock ceased trading.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-045478, Form 8-K Item 2.01]

## Financing structure (what would fund the cash consideration)
- Cigna and New Cigna entered into a commitment letter (March 8, 2018, later joined by
  additional banks) for a $26.7 billion 364-day senior unsecured bridge term loan
  facility; a $3.0 billion term loan credit agreement executed April 6, 2018 reduced the
  bridge facility commitment to $23.7 billion, and a $1.5 billion revolving credit
  agreement was also executed that date. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-032613, DEFM14A "Description of Financing"]
- Consummation of the mergers was expressly not conditioned on Cigna's or New Cigna's
  ability to obtain financing. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-032613, DEFM14A "Description of Financing"]
- Assuming completion by year-end 2018 funded with $22.6 billion of new indebtedness
  backed by the bridge facility, the combined company's pro forma indebtedness at closing
  was estimated at approximately $40.9 billion, against approximately $5.3 billion of
  existing Cigna debt and $15.0 billion of existing Express Scripts debt as of May 14,
  2018. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-032613, DEFM14A "Description of Financing"]

## What crossed at the tier level
- Legal vehicle: both pre-merger public companies became wholly owned operating
  subsidiaries of a newly formed top holding company (New Cigna, subsequently renamed
  Cigna Corporation); neither predecessor entity was dissolved or merged into the other
  directly. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-045478, Form 8-K Item 2.01]
- Controlling intent / management: the merger agreement provided that "the officers of
  Cigna immediately before the effective time of the Cigna merger will be the officers of
  New Cigna," while Timothy Wentworth, then Chief Executive Officer of Express Scripts,
  would become "President of the Express Scripts division of New Cigna, reporting to the
  Chief Executive Officer of New Cigna." [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-032613, DEFM14A "The Mergers -- New Cigna's Board of Directors and Management after the Mergers"]
- Board composition: the New Cigna board of directors was set at 14 members comprising
  eight independent Cigna directors plus Cigna's Chief Executive Officer (nine Cigna-side
  seats), four independent Express Scripts directors selected by Cigna from a list
  provided by Express Scripts, and (subject to willingness to serve) one additional
  designee. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-032613, DEFM14A "The Mergers -- New Cigna's Board of Directors and Management after the Mergers"]
- Product/operational location: under the merger agreement, New Cigna's "corporate
  headquarters, principal executive offices and related corporate and operational
  functions" were to be located in Bloomfield, Connecticut (Cigna's existing headquarters
  location), while "the principal executive offices and related corporate and operational
  functions for Express Scripts' business" were to remain in St. Louis, Missouri. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-032613, DEFM14A "The Mergers -- Management"]
- People/employees: the joint proxy statement/prospectus discusses employee- and
  compensation-related merger-agreement provisions and notes each Cigna named executive
  officer signed a waiver of "change of control" rights under Cigna's compensation and
  benefit plans in connection with the mergers. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-032613, DEFM14A "Interests of Cigna's Directors and Executive Officers in the Mergers"]

## Termination fees and regulatory conditions
- A mutual $1.6 billion "fiduciary" termination fee was payable by either party to the
  other under specified circumstances (e.g., termination to accept a superior proposal);
  a separate $2.1 billion reverse termination fee was payable by Cigna to Express Scripts
  if the mergers failed to close for reasons tied to the failure to obtain required
  regulatory approvals; the two fees could not both be triggered. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-032613, DEFM14A "The Merger Agreement -- Termination Fees; Expenses"]
- Consummation was conditioned on expiration/termination of the HSR Act waiting period.
  Cigna and Express Scripts filed HSR notifications on March 22, 2018, and on April 23,
  2018 each received a "second request" for additional information from the DOJ
  Antitrust Division, extending the statutory waiting period; closing was also
  conditioned on approvals from state insurance departments, state health departments,
  and (for certain Express Scripts subsidiaries) state pharmacy boards. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-032613, DEFM14A "Governmental and Regulatory Approvals"]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction combined Cigna and Express Scripts as co-equal wholly owned
  subsidiaries under a newly formed holding company that took the Cigna name and traded
  in Cigna's place on the NYSE; ownership was split roughly 64%/36% in favor of former
  Cigna stockholders. The holding company's corporate seat, executive-officer roster, and
  board majority (nine of fourteen seats) were drawn from Cigna, while Express Scripts'
  own operational headquarters and its former CEO (now reporting to Cigna's CEO) remained
  in place under the "Express Scripts division." The deal was financed by a large
  committed bridge facility (not a closing condition) and carried a mutual $1.6 billion
  fiduciary termination fee plus a $2.1 billion reverse fee tied specifically to
  regulatory-approval failure. [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-032613, DEFM14A] [SRC: SEC accession 0001140361-18-045478, Form 8-K]
