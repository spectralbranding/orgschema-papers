# Structural sub-dossier — Berry Plastics Group, Inc. roll-up transactions (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transactions were structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Entity, ownership, and transaction type at closing
- Berry Plastics Group, Inc. ("Berry" or "the Company"), a Delaware corporation, is a
  holding company that relies on dividends and other payments from its subsidiaries to
  meet its obligations; its Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended September 29, 2012 was
  filed December 17, 2012. [SRC: SEC accession 0000919465-12-000024, Form 10-K]
- Funds affiliated with Apollo Global Management, LLC ("Apollo") indirectly beneficially
  owned approximately 54% of the Company's common stock, giving Apollo the power to elect
  all directors and to strongly influence business decisions including mergers,
  acquisitions, and the incurrence of debt; the Company qualified as a "controlled
  company" under NYSE governance standards. [SRC: SEC accession 0000919465-12-000024, Form 10-K]
- The Company was majority owned by affiliates of Apollo Management, L.P. and Graham
  Partners; those sponsors charged the Company a management fee (the greater of $3
  million or 1.25% of adjusted EBITDA), which terminated upon completion of the
  Company's October 2012 initial public offering. [SRC: SEC accession 0000919465-12-000024, Form 10-K]
- The Company describes itself as having "a long history of acquiring and integrating
  companies, having completed eleven transactions in the last six years," pursuing "an
  opportunistic acquisition strategy" focused on obtaining businesses at attractive
  post-synergy multiples and leveraging acquired products across its existing customer
  base. [SRC: SEC accession 0000919465-12-000024, Form 10-K]

## Individual acquisitions documented at or before closing
- Pliant Corporation: acquisition-related debt of $620 million was issued in November
  2009 to fund the Pliant acquisition; the acquired business's products (barrier and
  personal-care films, bags, sheeting) were placed within the Engineered Materials and
  Flexible Packaging segments. [SRC: SEC accession 0000919465-12-000024, Form 10-K]
- Superfos Packaging, Inc.: acquired prior to fiscal 2011 and placed within the Rigid
  Open Top business. [SRC: SEC accession 0000919465-12-000024, Form 10-K]
- Rexam Specialty and Beverage Closures ("Rexam SBC"): in September 2011 the Company
  acquired 100% of the capital stock of Rexam SBC for an aggregate purchase price of $351
  million ($340 million net of cash acquired), financed with cash on hand and existing
  credit facilities and accounted for under the purchase method; the acquired business
  (plastic closures, fitments, dispensing closure systems, and jars) was placed in the
  Company's Rigid Packaging business. [SRC: SEC accession 0000919465-12-000024, Form 10-K]
- Stopaq: in June 2012 the Company acquired 100% of the shares of Frans Nooren Beheer
  B.V. and its operating companies ("Stopaq") for $65 million ($62 million net of cash
  acquired), financed with cash on hand and existing credit facilities and accounted for
  under the purchase method; the newly added business (visco-elastic corrosion-prevention
  technologies) was placed in the Company's Engineered Materials segment. [SRC: SEC accession 0000919465-12-000024, Form 10-K]
- In October 2012 the Company completed an initial public offering of 29,411,764 shares
  at $16.00 per share; the net proceeds and cash on hand were used to repurchase all $455
  million of the Company's 11% Senior Subordinated Notes due 2016. [SRC: SEC accession 0000919465-12-000024, Form 10-K]

## What crossed at closing for each acquired business, and what did not move as an intact unit
- For each acquisition the purchase price was allocated to identifiable assets and
  liabilities at estimated fair values under purchase-method accounting; the Company
  states it typically required selling stockholders to indemnify it against certain
  undisclosed liabilities. [SRC: SEC accession 0000919465-12-000024, Form 10-K]
- The Company discloses that during fiscal 2012 it announced the shutdown of three
  facilities across its Rigid Closed Top, Engineered Materials, and Flexible Packaging
  divisions, "with the majority of the operations transferred to other facilities" — i.e.,
  production processes from acquired/existing lines were consolidated into a smaller set
  of operating facilities rather than left in place. [SRC: SEC accession 0000919465-12-000024, Form 10-K]
- The Company states that expected synergies from acquisitions are realized "by
  eliminating duplicative costs and rationalizing facilities and integrating the
  production into the most efficient operating facility," and that it has historically
  been unable to accurately separate realized synergies from what was initially
  identified during due diligence, owing to "systems integrations and movement of
  activities to multiple facilities." [SRC: SEC accession 0000919465-12-000024, Form 10-K]
- Listed acquisition risks disclosed by the Company include: diversion of management
  attention to assimilating acquired companies and their employees; "problems associated
  with maintaining relationships with employees and customers of acquired businesses";
  difficulty integrating disclosure controls and internal controls for financial
  reporting within Sarbanes-Oxley timeframes; and "the loss of key employees and the
  difficulty of presenting a unified corporate image." [SRC: SEC accession 0000919465-12-000024, Form 10-K]
- The Company further discloses that it "may not be able to successfully integrate
  future acquisitions without substantial costs, delays or other problems," citing
  "difficulties in integrating shared services with our business, higher than expected
  employee severance or retention costs, higher than expected overhead expenses" as
  specific risks to realizing expected cost savings and synergies. [SRC: SEC accession 0000919465-12-000024, Form 10-K]
- The Rexam SBC and Stopaq acquisitions are each described as purchases of 100% of the
  target's stock, financed from cash on hand and existing credit facilities; the filing
  does not describe any transitional-services agreement, shared-services agreement, or
  ongoing supply arrangement bridging a shared substrate with either seller. [SRC: SEC accession 0000919465-12-000024, Form 10-K]

## Product portfolio and organizational structure at closing
- At closing the Company organized its business into four operating divisions: Rigid
  Open Top, Rigid Closed Top (together "Rigid Packaging"), Engineered Materials, and
  Flexible Packaging, selling more than 13,000 customers products including thermoform
  drink cups, thin-wall containers, blow-molded bottles, specialty closures, prescription
  vials, specialty plastic films, tapes, and corrosion-protection materials. [SRC: SEC accession 0000919465-12-000024, Form 10-K]
- At the end of fiscal 2012 the Company employed over 15,000 employees, of whom
  approximately 11% were covered by collective bargaining agreements (12 agreements
  covering the represented workforce). [SRC: SEC accession 0000919465-12-000024, Form 10-K]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- As structured through fiscal 2012, Berry Plastics Group is a sponsor-controlled
  (Apollo/Graham) holding company that grew its four-division plastics-packaging product
  portfolio through a series of discrete 100%-stock or asset purchases (Pliant, Superfos,
  Rexam SBC, Stopaq, among "eleven transactions in the last six years"), financed from
  cash, credit facilities, and debt issuance rather than stock-for-stock combinations; the
  Company's own risk disclosures identify integration of acquired employees, customer
  relationships, and operating/financial-reporting systems — and facility/process
  consolidation ("integrating the production into the most efficient operating
  facility") — as recurring, not-yet-resolved tasks rather than automatic consequences of
  closing. [SRC: SEC accession 0000919465-12-000024, Form 10-K]
