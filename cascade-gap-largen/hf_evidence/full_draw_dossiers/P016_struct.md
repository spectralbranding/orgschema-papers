# Structural sub-dossier — Alcoa Corporation separation from Alcoa Inc. (closing structure)

> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what

## Transaction and parties at closing
- In September 2015, Alcoa Inc. announced a plan to separate into two independent, publicly traded companies: a "globally cost-competitive upstream company" and an "innovation and technology-driven value-add company." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-16-708643, Form 10-12B EX-99.1 information statement]
- The separation was structured to occur by means of a pro rata distribution by Alcoa Inc. ("ParentCo") of at least 80.1% of the outstanding shares of a newly formed upstream company named Alcoa Upstream Corporation ("Alcoa Corporation"), which would own the Upstream Businesses. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-16-708643, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- In conjunction with the separation, ParentCo would change its name to "Arconic Inc." and its stock symbol from "AA" to "ARNC," retain the Value-Add Businesses, and become the value-add company; "Alcoa Upstream Corporation" would change its name to "Alcoa Corporation" and apply to list its common stock on the New York Stock Exchange under the symbol "AA." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-16-708643, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Immediately after the distribution became effective, ParentCo (Arconic) would own no more than 19.9% of the outstanding shares of Alcoa Corporation common stock (a retained minority stake). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-16-708643, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## What crossed the ownership boundary at the tier level
- Business/value-capture model and customer-facing assets: the upstream company was formed to hold the five business units that made up ParentCo's Global Primary Products — Bauxite, Alumina, Aluminum, Cast Products and Energy — plus a Rolled Products business (ParentCo's rolling mill operations in Warrick, Indiana, and ParentCo's 25.1% interest in the Ma'aden Rolling Company in Saudi Arabia). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-16-708643, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Following its internal reorganization, Alcoa Corporation's operations consisted of six reportable segments: Bauxite, Alumina, Aluminum, Cast Products, Rolled Products and Energy. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-16-708643, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Vertically integrated operational processes moved together: bauxite is mined and sold primarily to internal customers within the Alumina segment, which processes it into alumina, mainly sold to internal and external smelter customers; bauxite is transferred to the Alumina segment at negotiated terms intended to approximate market prices, with a portion sold to third parties. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-16-708643, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- People / organization: Alcoa Corporation's total worldwide employment at the end of 2015 was approximately 16,000 employees in 15 countries; approximately 2,500 employees were represented by various labor unions (including a USW master agreement covering approximately 1,950 employees at six U.S. locations). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-16-708643, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Management continuity from the parent's operating line: Roy C. Harvey, who had served as President of ParentCo's Global Primary Products business, would be Alcoa Corporation's Chief Executive Officer, and Tómas Már Sigurdsson, the Chief Operating Officer of ParentCo's Global Primary Products business, would become Alcoa Corporation's Chief Operating Officer. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-16-708643, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Controlling intent / imprint: the registrant traced its lineage to ParentCo, "previously known as the Aluminum Company of America," which "pioneered the aluminum industry 128 years ago with the discovery of the first commercial process for the affordable production of aluminum." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-16-708643, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Capital structure allocated at separation: Alcoa Corporation would incur approximately $1,250 million of indebtedness prior to or concurrent with the separation (the pro forma financial statements were prepared to reflect this incurrence). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-16-708643, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Contractual arrangements bridging the shared substrate at closing
- Separation and distribution agreement: Alcoa Corporation would enter into a separation and distribution agreement with ParentCo (the "separation agreement") to effect the separation and allocate assets, liabilities and previously divested operating locations between the two companies. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-16-708643, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Transition services agreement: an interim/transitional services arrangement between the two companies; the registrant disclosed indemnity and liability-cap provisions and warned its business could be negatively impacted if it failed to perform certain services under the transition services agreement. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-16-708643, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Toll processing and services agreement ("North American packaging business agreement"): pursuant to which Arconic would continue producing the wide can body sheet at Tennessee and provide it to Alcoa Corporation "to permit Alcoa Corporation to continue supplying its customers without interruption during this period," and Arconic would also process used beverage containers (UBCs) owned by Alcoa Corporation. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-16-708643, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Additional agreements at separation: a tax matters agreement (under which Alcoa Corporation could be required to indemnify ParentCo for material taxes), an employee matters agreement, and a stockholder and registration rights agreement (governing the retained-stake relationship). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-16-708643, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Product/process and process/organization interfaces at closing (neutral)
- The customer-facing product for the North American packaging business (can body sheet) would, for a transitional period, continue to be physically produced by the counterparty (Arconic) at Tennessee rather than by the registrant, and provided to the registrant under the toll processing agreement — i.e., a customer-facing product supplied without its producing process residing inside the registrant at closing. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-16-708643, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The registrant's core vertically integrated processes (bauxite → alumina → aluminum → cast/rolled products, plus captive Energy) and the incumbent Global Primary Products management line (CEO and COO drawn from that business) moved together into the registrant. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-16-708643, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction split Alcoa Inc. into two independent public companies by pro rata distribution of at least 80.1% of the newly formed upstream registrant's shares (Arconic retaining no more than 19.9%): the registrant received the upstream bauxite/alumina/aluminum/cast/energy business plus a rolled-products unit (Warrick mill and a 25.1% Ma'aden Rolling Company interest), organized into six reportable segments, with approximately 16,000 employees and continuity of the incumbent Global Primary Products CEO (Roy C. Harvey) and COO (Tómas Már Sigurdsson); it incurred approximately $1,250 million of separation indebtedness; and the shared substrate was bridged at closing by a separation and distribution agreement, a transition services agreement, a toll processing and services agreement (Arconic to keep producing can body sheet for the registrant), and tax, employee, and stockholder/registration-rights agreements. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-16-708643, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
