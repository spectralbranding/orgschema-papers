## Transaction and parties at closing
- On February 24, 2017, DigitalGlobe, Inc., a Delaware corporation, entered into an Agreement and Plan of Merger with MacDonald, Dettwiler and Associates Ltd. ("MDA"), a corporation organized under the laws of British Columbia, SSL MDA Holdings, Inc. ("Holdings"), a Delaware corporation and wholly owned subsidiary of MDA, and Merlin Merger Sub, Inc. ("Merger Sub"), a Delaware corporation and wholly owned subsidiary of Holdings. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-210383, Form DEFM14A]
- Under the merger agreement, Merger Sub was merged with and into DigitalGlobe, with DigitalGlobe surviving the merger as an indirect wholly owned subsidiary of MDA. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-210383, Form DEFM14A]
- Each share of DigitalGlobe common stock outstanding immediately prior to the effective time was converted into the right to receive (a) cash of US $17.50 and (b) 0.3132 of a validly issued MDA common share; based on MDA's TSX closing price shortly before the deal's public disclosure, the implied per-share value of the consideration was $35.00. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-210383, Form DEFM14A]
- DigitalGlobe common stock traded on the NYSE under "DGI"; MDA common shares traded on the TSX under "MDA." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-210383, Form DEFM14A]
- The merger closed on October 5, 2017, with Merger Sub merging into DigitalGlobe and DigitalGlobe continuing as an indirect wholly owned subsidiary of MDA (by then renamed Maxar Technologies Ltd., formerly MacDonald, Dettwiler and Associates Ltd.). [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-17-061357, Form 8-K Item 2.01]

## Business descriptions established at signing
- DigitalGlobe was described as a global provider of high-resolution Earth imagery, data, services and analytics, sourced from its own satellite constellation and third-party providers, serving mission-planning, mapping, environmental monitoring, oil-and-gas exploration and infrastructure-management customers; it was originally incorporated as EarthWatch in 1993, renamed DigitalGlobe in 2002, and had acquired GeoEye, Inc. in 2013. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-210383, Form DEFM14A]
- MDA was described as a global communications and information company providing operational solutions to commercial and government organizations, with its business focused on the communications sector and the surveillance-and-intelligence sector. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-210383, Form DEFM14A]

## What crossed the ownership boundary at closing, and what did not
- Controlling intent and legal vehicle: at the effective time all members of DigitalGlobe's board of directors and all of its officers were removed; new directors and officers were appointed, drawn partly from DigitalGlobe's own prior leadership (Dan Jablonsky, previously on DigitalGlobe's Executive Leadership Team, was appointed President; Walter S. Scott was appointed Executive Vice President and Technical Officer) and partly from the MDA/Holdings side (William McCombe, from MDA's management, was appointed Chief Financial Officer; Michelle Kley, already senior vice president and general counsel of Maxar and Holdings, was appointed Secretary). [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-17-061357, Form 8-K Item 5.02]
- Legal vehicle: DigitalGlobe continued to exist as the surviving Delaware corporation, with its certificate of incorporation amended and restated per the merger agreement, rather than being dissolved into the parent. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-210383, Form DEFM14A]
- Customer-facing product/asset: the merger agreement did not itself alter DigitalGlobe's Earth-imagery/geospatial-analytics product line or MDA's separate communications and surveillance/intelligence product lines as described to shareholders; the two companies' products were described as distinct businesses being combined under common ownership. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-210383, Form DEFM14A]
- Operational continuity: DigitalGlobe's satellite-imagery operations, including its Enhanced View Imagery Acquisition Contract No. HM0210-10-C-0002 with the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency ("NGA contract," dated August 6, 2010), were carried forward as an operating asset of the surviving subsidiary. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-210383, Form DEFM14A]
- People/organization (employees, as distinct from officers/directors): during the period from closing to the first anniversary of the closing date, MDA agreed to provide "Continuing Employees" (DigitalGlobe employees at closing) a base salary/wage no less than pre-closing levels and benefits substantially comparable in the aggregate, with prior service credited for eligibility, vesting and severance purposes, and to honor listed employment, severance, retention and change-in-control agreements. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-210383, Form DEFM14A]

## Contractual arrangements bridging the shared substrate at closing
- Because MDA is a Canadian entity while certain of its U.S. subsidiaries (including Space Systems/Loral, LLC) and, upon closing, DigitalGlobe itself require U.S. facility security clearances to perform classified U.S. government work, MDA entered into a Security Control Agreement dated January 26, 2017 with Holdings and the U.S. Department of Defense as a foreign-ownership-control-or-influence mitigation arrangement under the National Industrial Security Program. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-210383, Form DEFM14A]
- The merger agreement's definition of an "Extraordinary Condition" that MDA would not be required to accept as a condition of regulatory approval specifically excluded being forced into a proxy agreement or voting trust arrangement with respect to the services DigitalGlobe provides under the NGA contract — i.e., the parties structured the deal to keep that specific government contract's governance outside any FOCI-mitigation carve-out. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-210383, Form DEFM14A]
- MDA agreed to pay DigitalGlobe a reverse termination fee of $150 million if the NGA contract were terminated, not renewed, or materially reduced in scope and MDA elected to terminate the merger agreement on that basis — allocating deal risk specifically around the customer-facing government contract. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-210383, Form DEFM14A]
- The merger agreement provided that, promptly after the effective time and in any event within 10 days, DigitalGlobe common stock would cease to be listed on the NYSE and would be deregistered under the U.S. Exchange Act. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-210383, Form DEFM14A]
- MDA disclosed at signing an intention to reincorporate in the United States following closing; the merger agreement barred MDA from accepting any regulatory condition that would materially change the timing of that reincorporation plan. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-210383, Form DEFM14A]
- The merger agreement provided a six-year tail period following the effective time during which DigitalGlobe's certificate of incorporation and bylaws would retain indemnification provisions no less favorable than those in effect at signing, and during which MDA/Holdings would maintain (or purchase a run-off endorsement for) DigitalGlobe's existing directors' and officers' liability insurance. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-210383, Form DEFM14A]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction was a whole-company acquisition structured as a reverse triangular merger, with cash-plus-stock consideration, DigitalGlobe surviving as an indirect wholly owned subsidiary of a Canadian acquirer; governance (board and officer slate) was replaced with a mixed roster drawn from both companies' prior leadership, ordinary employees' compensation and benefits were contractually protected for the year following closing, and a specific national-security-sensitive government contract (the NGA contract) was carved out for special risk allocation and kept outside any foreign-ownership mitigation/proxy-board arrangement. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-210383, Form DEFM14A] [SRC: SEC accession 0001104659-17-061357, Form 8-K]
