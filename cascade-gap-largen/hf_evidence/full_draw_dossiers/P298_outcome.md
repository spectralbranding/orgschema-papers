## Residual acquisition-accounting items tagged to the acquired entity in the acquirer's later filings
- The acquirer's fiscal-year-2020 Form 10-K reports merger-and-acquisition expense of
  $11.8 million recognized in 2018 in connection with the WashingtonFirst acquisition
  (compared with $1.3 million of merger-and-acquisition expense from a separate,
  subsequent acquisition in 2019). [SRC: SEC accession 0000824410-21-000017, Form 10-K]
- The same filing reports that, in connection with the WashingtonFirst acquisition, the
  acquirer assumed $25.0 million in subordinated debt (with a $2.2 million associated
  purchase premium); as of December 31, 2020, $20.0 million of that assumed debt
  remained classified as Tier 2 regulatory capital. [SRC: SEC accession
  0000824410-21-000017, Form 10-K]
- The same filing reports that, also in connection with the WashingtonFirst acquisition,
  the acquirer assumed $10.3 million in callable junior subordinated debt securities
  (with a $0.1 million associated purchase premium), and that during the first quarter
  of 2020 the acquirer redeemed the entire $10.3 million outstanding principal balance of
  that assumed debt. [SRC: SEC accession 0000824410-21-000017, Form 10-K]
- The same filing reports a $1.8 million tax benefit recognized in 2020 from carrying
  back, under CARES Act provisions, net operating losses that had been acquired as part
  of the 2018 WashingtonFirst acquisition (carried back to tax years 2013-2015). [SRC:
  SEC accession 0000824410-21-000017, Form 10-K]
- These are acquisition-accounting and tax/capital line items identified in the notes to
  the acquirer's consolidated financial statements; the filing does not present a
  business-performance trajectory (e.g., revenue, deposits, net income, or customer
  counts) specifically attributable to the acquired business on a standalone basis for
  this or any later period reviewed. [SRC: SEC accession 0000824410-21-000017, Form 10-K]

## Outcome summary (realized record only)
- Because WashingtonFirst Bankshares, Inc. terminated its SEC registration within
  roughly ten days of closing and was not carried forward as a separately reported
  business by the acquirer, there is no public, audited 3-5-year outcome record for the
  entity as a standalone business. The only traceable post-closing facts in the
  acquirer's later filings are residual acquisition-accounting and tax/capital items
  (merger-and-acquisition expense, assumed subordinated debt and its subsequent partial
  redemption, and a net-operating-loss tax benefit) tied to the 2018 transaction, not a
  disclosed business-performance trajectory. [SRC: SEC accession 0001144204-18-001834,
  Form 15-12B; SEC accession 0000824410-21-000017, Form 10-K]
