# Structural sub-dossier — HCBF Holding Company, Inc. combination with CenterState Bank Corporation (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Transaction and parties at closing
- HCBF Holding Company, Inc. ("HCBF"), a Florida bank holding company headquartered in
  Fort Pierce, Florida, and CenterState Bank Corporation ("CenterState") entered into an
  Agreement and Plan of Merger dated August 12, 2017 under which HCBF would merge with
  and into CenterState (a direct statutory merger; no separate merger-subsidiary vehicle
  is described), with CenterState as the surviving company; immediately thereafter
  HCBF's wholly-owned subsidiary bank, Harbor Community Bank, would merge with and into
  CenterState's subsidiary bank, CenterState Bank, N.A., with CenterState Bank
  surviving. Each share of HCBF common stock converted into the right to receive 0.675
  shares of CenterState common stock plus $1.925 in cash (plus certain dividend amounts
  and cash in lieu of fractional shares). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-327431, DEFM14A joint proxy statement/prospectus]
- The merger closed and became effective on January 1, 2018; at the effective time each
  outstanding HCBF stock option was converted into a CenterState stock option. [SRC: SEC accession 0001144204-18-000005, Form 8-K (HCBF), Item 2.01]
- Concurrently, CenterState had also entered into a separate August 12, 2017 merger
  agreement to acquire Sunshine Bancorp, Inc. on the same direct-merger structure,
  expected to close in Q1 2018 — HCBF's combination was one of two contemporaneous
  whole-company combinations into the same acquirer. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-327431, DEFM14A]
- HCBF common stock was not traded on any established public trading market prior to the
  merger (approximately 425 registered holders of record); CenterState common stock
  continued to trade on Nasdaq under "CSFL" after the merger. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-327431, DEFM14A]

## What crossed / did not cross at the tier level
- Legal vehicle / product: the customer-facing banking business (deposit, commercial and
  residential lending, cash management, wealth-management services) crossed via a direct
  whole-entity statutory merger of the bank subsidiaries (Harbor Community Bank into
  CenterState Bank, N.A.), so the product and its licensed legal vehicle (the bank
  charter) crossed together at the same effective time. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-327431, DEFM14A]
- Operational processes: the data-processing and related electronic informational
  systems used to deliver that product had NOT converted as of closing. The merger
  agreement's Section 5.13 provides only that CenterState and HCBF "will use their
  commercially reasonable efforts to facilitate the integration of HCBF with the
  business of CenterState following consummation" and will "meet on a regular basis to
  discuss and plan for the conversion of the data processing and related electronic
  informational systems of HCBF ... to those used by CenterState," with conversion
  actions to occur "as soon as practicable following the Effective Time" — the
  product/legal-entity crossing at closing was not accompanied by an already-converted
  processing substrate. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-327431, DEFM14A, Merger Agreement Section 5.13]
- Controlling intent: CenterState's own pre-merger executive officers were to "continue
  as the officers of the surviving company," and the post-merger board would consist of
  CenterState's pre-merger directors plus only one HCBF-selected director. HCBF's
  founder/Chairman and CEO, Michael J. Brown, Sr. (who had led HCBF since its 2010
  inception and led Harbor Community Bank since 2011), was designated to join the
  CenterState board as that single director rather than to hold an executive or
  chairman role in the combined company. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-327431, DEFM14A]
- People / organization: HCBF's President and COO, J. Hal Roberts, Jr., entered a new
  employment agreement to serve as "Regional President of the Treasure Coast of
  Florida" at an annual base salary of $240,000 following the merger — a regionalized
  post rather than a corporate-level executive role. At the effective time, HCBF's other
  directors and executive officers ceased serving in those capacities. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-327431, DEFM14A]; [SRC: SEC accession 0001144204-18-000005, Form 8-K (HCBF), Item 5.02]
- CenterState agreed to maintain, for HCBF's continuing ("covered") employees,
  benefits and compensation "substantially comparable" to CenterState's own employees,
  with credit for prior service, but also contracted a severance schedule (two weeks'
  base salary per 12 months of prior service, floor of four weeks / cap of 26 weeks) for
  any covered employee terminated without cause within six months after the merger —
  i.e., workforce attrition following the merger was anticipated and contractually
  priced in at closing. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-327431, DEFM14A]
- Organizational scale at closing (informational, not financial-performance): HCBF/
  Harbor Community Bank reported 449 full-time-equivalent employees operating through a
  main office and 45 branches in 18 Florida counties; CenterState reported 1,180
  full-time-equivalent employees operating 78 full-service locations in 28 Florida
  counties plus a Georgia loan-production office. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-327431, DEFM14A]

## Contractual arrangements bridging the shared substrate at closing
- The Informational Systems Conversion covenant (Section 5.13, described above) is the
  instrument bridging the two companies' data-processing systems, explicitly scheduled
  to occur after the effective time rather than at it. Section 5.14 ("No Control of
  Other Party's Business") separately provided that neither party could direct or
  control the other's operations before the effective time, each retaining control and
  supervision of its own operations until closing. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-327431, DEFM14A, Merger Agreement Sections 5.13-5.14]
- CenterState agreed to indemnify HCBF's and Harbor Community Bank's present and former
  directors and officers for six years post-merger and to maintain directors'-and-
  officers' liability insurance of comparable scope for the same period. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-327431, DEFM14A]
- Prior to the effective time, HCBF was required to terminate all salary-continuation
  and supplemental executive retirement arrangements and pay out change-in-control
  amounts due its officers, directors and employees under HCBF benefit plans — a
  closing-time severing of HCBF's own legacy executive-retention instruments. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-327431, DEFM14A]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction combined the customer-facing product and its licensed legal vehicle
  (the bank charter) in a single direct statutory merger effective January 1, 2018, but
  the underlying operational/informational-processing systems and the controlling
  leadership were not carried across intact: systems conversion was deferred to a
  post-closing period under a dedicated merger-agreement covenant, and the acquired
  company's founder-CEO was reduced at closing to a single board seat while its COO
  moved into a regionalized (not corporate) role, with contractual severance terms
  already in place for anticipated post-merger workforce attrition. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-17-327431, DEFM14A]
