# Structural sub-dossier — Phillips 66 separation from ConocoPhillips (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Transaction and parties at closing
- The transaction was a repositioning of ConocoPhillips accomplished by separating its
  Phillips 66 subsidiary from ConocoPhillips' remaining businesses; on the effective date
  Phillips 66, a Delaware corporation, would become an independent public company holding,
  through its subsidiaries, the assets and liabilities associated with ConocoPhillips'
  Downstream business. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-12-091773, Form 10-12B EX-99.1 information statement]
- The separation was completed by way of a pro rata distribution of all outstanding
  Phillips 66 common stock to ConocoPhillips stockholders of record; each ConocoPhillips
  stockholder received one share of Phillips 66 common stock for every two shares of
  ConocoPhillips common stock held on the record date. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-12-091773, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Under that distribution ratio ConocoPhillips would distribute approximately 640 million
  shares of Phillips 66 common stock; the distribution did not require stockholder approval.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-12-091773, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- ConocoPhillips common stock continued to trade on the NYSE under "COP"; Phillips 66
  applied to list its common stock on the NYSE under "PSX." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-12-091773, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- ConocoPhillips sought a private-letter ruling from the IRS that the distribution, together
  with certain related transactions, would qualify as generally tax-free for U.S. federal
  income-tax purposes; receipt of that ruling was a condition to completing the separation.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-12-091773, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## What crossed the ownership boundary at the tier level
- The customer-facing asset base that crossed was ConocoPhillips' Downstream business,
  organized at separation into three operating segments: R&M (purchases, refines, markets
  and transports crude oil and petroleum products, mainly in the U.S., Europe and Asia, and
  engages in power generation); Midstream (gathers, processes, transports and markets natural
  gas and fractionates/markets NGL, predominantly in the U.S.); and Chemicals (manufactures
  and markets petrochemicals and plastics). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-12-091773, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The physical operating substrate that crossed included 15 refineries with gross crude oil
  capacity of 2.8 million barrels per day, approximately 10,000 branded marketing outlets,
  nearly 80,000 miles of pipeline, and 7.2 billion cubic feet per day of gross natural gas
  processing capacity. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-12-091773, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Petroleum products were sold at approximately 10,000 outlets in the U.S. and Europe,
  primarily under the Phillips 66, Conoco and 76 brands in the U.S. and the JET brand in
  Europe — brands crossing with the marketing operations. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-12-091773, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The Midstream and Chemicals segments crossed largely as equity investments rather than
  wholly owned operations: the Midstream segment primarily consisted of a 50 percent equity
  investment in DCP Midstream (a joint venture with Spectra Energy Corp), and Chemicals
  consisted of a 50 percent equity investment in Chevron Phillips Chemical Company LLC.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-12-091773, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The Downstream operating workforce that crossed comprised approximately 12,400 employees
  at December 31, 2011, excluding employees in corporate and other support functions.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-12-091773, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Shared corporate substrate that did NOT cross intact
- The business had no separate corporate infrastructure of its own: its working-capital
  requirements had historically been satisfied as part of ConocoPhillips' corporate-wide
  cash management programs, and its historical results reflected allocations of expenses for
  services historically provided by ConocoPhillips that "may be significantly lower than the
  comparable expenses we would have incurred as an independent company." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-12-091773, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- After the Transition Services Agreement expired, the company stated it would "no longer
  have access to ConocoPhillips' infrastructure, and we will need to establish our own,"
  expecting to incur costs beginning in 2012 to establish the necessary infrastructure.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-12-091773, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Contractual arrangements bridging the shared substrate (closing-time)
- The parties entered into a Separation and Distribution Agreement plus several other
  agreements to effect the separation and allocate ConocoPhillips' assets, liabilities and
  obligations between Phillips 66 and ConocoPhillips: a Tax Sharing Agreement, an Employee
  Matters Agreement, an Indemnification and Release Agreement, and a Transition Services
  Agreement. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-12-091773, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The Transition Services Agreement, together with several commercial agreements, provided
  for the provision of transitional services, utility cost allocation, delivery-system
  maintenance for certain premises, and ongoing commodity supply arrangements. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-12-091773, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The Tax Sharing Agreement allocated responsibility for prior-period taxes of the
  ConocoPhillips consolidated tax reporting group between the two companies; the Employee
  Matters Agreement governed employee-related allocations. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-12-091773, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The information statement noted these agreements were negotiated in the context of the
  separation while Phillips 66 was still a wholly owned subsidiary of ConocoPhillips.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-12-091773, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction detached a customer-facing downstream operating base (refineries, branded
  marketing outlets, pipelines, and two 50-percent chemicals/midstream joint-venture
  interests) together with approximately 12,400 operating employees, while the parent-level
  corporate service substrate (cash management, tax, treasury, and shared infrastructure) did
  not transfer as an intact operating whole and was instead bridged at closing by a
  Transition Services Agreement and related transitional/commercial agreements the new company
  would have to replace with its own infrastructure over time. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-12-091773, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
