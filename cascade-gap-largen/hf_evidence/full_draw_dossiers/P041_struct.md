# Structural sub-dossier — ONE Gas, Inc. separation from ONEOK, Inc. (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Transaction and parties at closing
- The board of directors of ONEOK, Inc. ("ONEOK"), an Oklahoma corporation, approved the
  distribution of all of the shares of common stock of ONE Gas, Inc. ("ONE Gas") to ONEOK
  shareholders, pursuant to a plan initially approved on July 24, 2013 to separate ONEOK's
  natural gas distribution business from ONEOK's other businesses. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-13-448833, Form 10-12B EX-99.1 information statement]
- ONE Gas was a wholly owned subsidiary of ONEOK formed to hold ONEOK's natural gas
  distribution business; prior to the distribution ONEOK would transfer that business to
  ONE Gas, and upon distribution ONEOK shareholders would own 100 percent of ONE Gas
  common stock on a pro rata basis, with no consideration paid and no shareholder vote
  required. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-13-448833, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Immediately after the distribution, ONE Gas would be a separate, publicly-traded
  company, intending to list on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker "OGS"; ONEOK
  common stock would continue trading under "OKE." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-13-448833, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## What crossed the ownership boundary at the tier level
- The customer-facing business transferred was the natural gas distribution business,
  providing regulated distribution service to more than 2 million residential, commercial,
  industrial and transportation customers in Oklahoma, Kansas and Texas through three
  divisions — Oklahoma Natural Gas, Kansas Gas Service and Texas Gas Service. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-13-448833, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The operating divisions crossed intact and continued to operate as divisions of ONE Gas;
  e.g., Oklahoma Natural Gas' post-separation rate filings were framed on a test year of
  "the first four quarters of Oklahoma Natural Gas' operations as a division of ONE Gas." [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-13-448833, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The management and workforce crossed with the business: immediately following the
  separation approximately 3,100 employees were expected to be working for ONE Gas
  (of whom approximately 700 were represented by collective-bargaining units), and the
  information statement described an experienced management team with deep natural gas
  utility and midstream experience. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-13-448833, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Certain corporate functions did NOT transfer as an intact operating substrate: cash
  needs had historically been satisfied under ONEOK's corporate-wide cash management, and
  functions such as information technology, accounting and internal-controls compliance,
  treasury and risk management, environmental management, tax compliance, and
  telecommunications had been performed by ONEOK; ONE Gas stated it was "developing a plan"
  to replace such services. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-13-448833, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Contractual arrangements governing the separated shared substrate (closing-time)
- In connection with the separation, ONE Gas and ONEOK would enter into a Separation and
  Distribution Agreement, a Transition Services Agreement, a Tax Matters Agreement, an
  Employee Matters Agreement and, if needed, one or more Management Agreements to effect
  the separation and provide the framework for the parties' relationship afterward. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-13-448833, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The Transition Services Agreement was to provide for an orderly transition to being an
  independent company: ONEOK would provide ONE Gas (and ONE Gas would provide ONEOK) with
  various services — including treasury and risk management, environmental management, tax
  compliance, telecommunications and information technology services — with fees set to
  recover the provider's direct and indirect costs without profit. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-13-448833, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The Employee Matters Agreement would govern allocation of employee and employee-benefit
  liabilities, provide that ONEOK stock-based incentive awards be adjusted equitably, end
  ONE Gas participation in ONEOK benefit plans while generally crediting prior ONEOK
  service, and provide that each party would retain liability for employees historically
  associated with its respective business. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-13-448833, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- A Management Agreement was contemplated to address municipal franchise agreements
  granted to ONEOK that required municipal consent for transfer of natural gas distribution
  service rights. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-13-448833, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Capital structure at separation
- In connection with the separation ONE Gas expected to receive approximately $1.2 billion
  of cash from the issuance of debt securities and to make a cash distribution of
  approximately $1.2 billion to ONEOK; the capital structure was designed to obtain
  investment-grade credit ratings. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-13-448833, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction detached an intact, going-concern regulated natural-gas distribution
  business — its three operating divisions, customers, management and workforce — from
  ONEOK into a separate public company, while the shared corporate-service substrate (IT,
  treasury/risk, tax, environmental, telecommunications) was bridged at closing by a
  Transition Services Agreement and related Separation/Distribution, Tax Matters,
  Employee Matters and Management agreements rather than transferred whole. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-13-448833, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
