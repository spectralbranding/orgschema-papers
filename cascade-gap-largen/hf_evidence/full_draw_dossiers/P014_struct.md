## Transaction and parties at closing
- The separation was structured as a spin-off: ServiceMaster Global Holdings, Inc. ("ServiceMaster") separated its American Home Shield business into a newly formed company, frontdoor, inc. ("Frontdoor"), which would own the assets and liabilities associated with that business; ServiceMaster continued to own and operate its Terminix and Franchise Services Group businesses. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-005362, Form 10-12B EX-99.1 information statement]
- The separation was effected by a pro rata distribution of at least 80.1 percent of Frontdoor's outstanding common stock to ServiceMaster stockholders, intended to qualify as generally tax-free for U.S. federal income tax purposes; no ServiceMaster stockholder vote or share surrender was required. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-005362, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Immediately after the distribution, ServiceMaster would own no more than 19.9 percent of Frontdoor's outstanding common stock; Frontdoor, previously an indirect, wholly owned subsidiary of ServiceMaster, would become a separate public company listed on Nasdaq under the symbol "FTDR." [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-005362, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## What crossed the ownership boundary at the tier level
- Customer-facing product: Frontdoor owned multiple home-service-plan brands including HSA, OneGuard, Landmark and American Home Shield (described as the largest provider of home service plans in the U.S.); its customizable home service plans protect homeowners against breakdowns of home systems and appliances, and the platform responded to over four million service requests annually. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-005362, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Business/value-capture model: a recurring home-service-plan subscription business ("large recurring customer base"), delivered through a nationwide network of over 15,000 pre-qualified professional contractor firms that employ more than 45,000 technicians — the technicians and contractor firms being independent, not Frontdoor employees. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-005362, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- People / organization: Frontdoor expected to employ approximately 2,700 persons as of the distribution date, none represented by labor unions; it operated five customer care centers (Carroll, Iowa; LaGrange, Georgia; Memphis, Tennessee; Phoenix, Arizona; Salt Lake City, Utah) and was headquartered in Memphis, Tennessee. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-005362, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Management: a management team was assembled led by Rexford J. Tibbens (President and Chief Executive Officer), Brian K. Turcotte (Senior Vice President and Chief Financial Officer) and Jeffrey A. Fiarman (Senior Vice President, General Counsel and Corporate Secretary). [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-005362, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Shared substrate that did NOT transfer whole
- The business had historically been operated as one of ServiceMaster's business segments, with ServiceMaster performing substantially all corporate functions for it — financial and human resources systems, internal auditing, investor relations, treasury services, accounting, finance and tax administration, benefits administration, legal, regulatory, and corporate branding. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-005362, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Working-capital and general corporate-purpose funding had in the past been satisfied as part of ServiceMaster's corporate-wide cash management policies; after the distribution Frontdoor would need to obtain its own financing, and would need to replicate facilities, systems, infrastructure and personnel to which it would no longer have access, likely incurring material capital and other costs. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-005362, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- As a home-service-plan company, Frontdoor had historically relied on ServiceMaster's net worth when calculating state reserve requirements and might be required to hold more reserves as an independent company after the distribution. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-005362, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Contractual arrangements bridging the shared substrate at closing
- Frontdoor and ServiceMaster would enter into a separation and distribution agreement to effect the separation and provide a framework for their post-separation relationship, including transfers of assets and liabilities and reciprocal, uncapped indemnification for specified liabilities. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-005362, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Additional agreements would include a transition services agreement (under which ServiceMaster would perform certain services for Frontdoor on a transitional basis), a tax matters agreement, an employee matters agreement, and a stockholder and registration rights agreement covering ServiceMaster's continuing ownership. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-005362, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- The information statement cautioned that these transitional arrangements "may not retain or fully capture the benefits" the business had enjoyed while integrated with ServiceMaster and could result in higher charges than in the past. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-005362, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction separated a recurring home-service-plan product operation (American Home Shield and related brands), its customer-care and contractor-network delivery processes, and its approximately 2,700 employees and newly assembled management into a standalone public company via a partial spin-off (at least 80.1 percent distributed, up to 19.9 percent retained by the parent), while the parent's shared corporate service substrate (treasury/cash management, tax, HR, IT, legal, accounting, branding) did NOT transfer as an intact whole and was instead bridged at closing by a separation and distribution agreement, a transition services agreement, a tax matters agreement, and an employee matters agreement. [SRC: SEC accession 0001047469-18-005362, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
