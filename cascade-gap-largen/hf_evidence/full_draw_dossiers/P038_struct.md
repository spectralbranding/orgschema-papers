# Structural sub-dossier — Motorola Mobility Holdings, Inc. separation from Motorola, Inc. (closing structure)

> EVIDENTIARY STANDARD: every factual line ends with a `[SRC: <SEC accession / URL>]`
> tag pointing to a public primary source. A fact that cannot be so sourced is written
> `[UNVERIFIED]` and is never guessed. This sub-dossier describes ONLY how the
> transaction was structured at closing — what crossed the ownership boundary and what
> did not. It contains no post-transaction outcome information.

## Transaction and parties at closing
- Motorola Mobility Holdings, Inc. was a newly formed company (organized in 2010) created
  to hold, through its subsidiaries, the assets and liabilities of Motorola, Inc.'s Mobile
  Devices and Home businesses; the main U.S. operating subsidiary was Motorola Mobility,
  Inc. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-226923, Form 10-12B EX-99.1 information statement]
- The separation was effected as a pro-rata distribution (spin-off): Motorola, Inc. would
  distribute 100% of the Motorola Mobility common stock it owned to Motorola, Inc.
  stockholders on a pro rata basis; no stockholder vote and no payment of consideration
  were required, and the distribution was expected to be tax-free for U.S. federal income
  tax purposes. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-226923, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- On the distribution date, Motorola, Inc. intended to change its name to Motorola
  Solutions, Inc.; Motorola Mobility intended to list on the NYSE under the ticker "MMI."
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-226923, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## What crossed the boundary at the tier level
- Customer-facing product: the separated business's portfolio comprised mobile devices,
  wireless accessories, set-top boxes and video distribution systems, and wireline
  broadband infrastructure products with associated customer premises equipment, organized
  into a Mobile Devices segment and a Home segment; Mobile Devices represented 65% and 66%,
  and Home 35% and 34%, of combined net revenues in 2009 and the first half of 2010,
  respectively. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-226923, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- People/organization: approximately 20,000 employees were in the Mobile Devices and Home
  businesses at December 31, 2009; the business also shared employees with Motorola, Inc.'s
  other businesses, and at the distribution date Motorola Mobility expected approximately
  21,000 employees, reflecting the addition of a portion of those historically shared
  corporate employees. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-226923, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Management continuity: Dr. Sanjay K. Jha, who joined Motorola, Inc. in August 2008 as
  Co-CEO of Motorola, Inc. and CEO of the Mobile Devices business (and became CEO of the
  Home business in February 2010), served as CEO of Motorola Mobility Holdings, Inc. and
  Motorola Mobility, Inc. from June 2010 — i.e., the management running the business
  pre-separation moved with it. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-226923, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Intellectual property: Motorola Mobility received approximately 16,500 granted patents
  and 8,000 pending patent applications worldwide (those more relevant to its business);
  patents not allocated to it were retained by Motorola, Inc.; the two companies entered a
  royalty-free, perpetual cross-license so each could use the other's transferred/retained
  patents, via an Intellectual Property Assignment Agreement and an Intellectual Property
  License Agreement. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-226923, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Trademarks: Motorola Trademark Holdings, LLC (a wholly owned subsidiary of Motorola
  Mobility) would own the "MOTOROLA" mark and Stylized M logo; Motorola, Inc. retained an
  exclusive royalty-free license to use the Motorola Marks within its licensed field of use
  under a ten-year Trademark License Agreement. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-226923, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Shared substrate that did NOT cross intact (bridged contractually)
- The business was historically integrated with Motorola, Inc.'s other businesses, sharing
  "economies of scope and scale in costs, employees, vendor relationships and customer
  relationships"; working-capital and general corporate cash needs had historically been
  satisfied as part of Motorola, Inc.'s corporate-wide liability-management processes, and
  cash generated by Motorola Mobility had been managed and retained by Motorola, Inc.
  [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-226923, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- After separation Motorola, Inc. would no longer provide financing; Motorola Mobility
  expected to be provided a significant amount of cash at separation and thereafter would
  have to obtain its own financing. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-226923, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Contractual arrangements governing the separated shared substrate (closing-time)
- The parties entered a Master Separation and Distribution Agreement plus a Tax Sharing
  Agreement, an Employee Matters Agreement, Intellectual Property (Assignment and License)
  Agreements, and a Trademark License Agreement to allocate assets, liabilities and
  obligations and frame the ongoing relationship. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-226923, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Transition Services Agreements provided for interim services (generally up to 12 months),
  charged at actual cost without profit; total payments to Motorola, Inc. were estimated at
  approximately $12 million and payments received from Motorola, Inc. at approximately
  $1 million over the period. Services covered included financial audit support, financial
  transaction support, data processing, information-technology systems, legal support, and
  other general administrative functions. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-226923, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
- Several commercial agreements provided for continued cooperation with respect to
  integrated digital enhanced network ("iDEN") mobile devices and infrastructure products
  and the ongoing sale and support of various other products. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-226923, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction distributed a going product operation (Mobile Devices and Home segments),
  its patents/trademarks, and the management and employees that ran it out of Motorola,
  Inc. into a newly formed standalone company, while the shared corporate substrate the
  business had historically relied on (cash/liability management, IT systems, audit/financial
  transaction support, legal and administrative functions) was not transferred whole but
  bridged by a set of separation, tax, employee, IP, trademark, transition-services, and
  commercial agreements. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-10-226923, Form 10-12B EX-99.1]
