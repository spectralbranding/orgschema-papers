# Structural sub-dossier — Chesapeake Oilfield Operating, L.L.C. / Seventy Seven Energy Inc. separation from Chesapeake Energy (closing structure)

## Transaction and parties at closing
- Chesapeake Oilfield Operating, L.L.C. ("COO"), an Oklahoma limited liability company and an indirect wholly-owned subsidiary of Chesapeake Energy Corporation ("CHK"), was registered on Form 10 to be separated from CHK and to become an independent, publicly traded company operating under the name Seventy Seven Energy Inc. ("SSE"). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-229415, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1 information statement]
- The separation was structured as a pro rata spin-off: CHK would distribute 100% of its interest in SSE to CHK shareholders, who would receive one share of SSE common stock for every fourteen shares of CHK common stock held on the record date, with no cash or other consideration paid and no CHK shares surrendered or exchanged. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-229415, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]
- SSE's assets and businesses generally consisted of those that CHK reported as its oilfield services operating segment. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-229415, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]
- The registration (of common stock to be listed on the New York Stock Exchange) was made on Form 10 and amended; the amendment was signed by Jerry Winchester as Chief Executive Officer. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-236450, Form 10-12B/A]
- The separation from CHK was completed on June 30, 2014. [SRC: SEC accession 0001532930-17-000006, Form 10-K (states "On June 30, 2014, we separated from CHK")]

## What crossed the ownership boundary and what did not
- The customer-facing operating business that crossed was a diversified oilfield services business providing land-based drilling, hydraulic fracturing, oilfield rentals, rig relocation, and water transport and disposal to its customers; the business employed approximately 5,100 people in the United States. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-229415, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]
- As part of the Transactions, COO transferred all of its existing assets, operations and liabilities (including its 6.625% senior notes due 2019) to the operating entity ("SSO"); COO then converted into SSE, with all of its assets, operations and liabilities vested in SSE. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-229415, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]
- Certain businesses were distributed back to CHK and did NOT cross into the standalone company: the compression unit manufacturing business, the geosteering business, and the crude oil hauling business (or the proceeds from its sale). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-229415, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]
- Buildings and real estate used in the business were transferred to the company by CHK, though the business had historically leased most of that property from CHK. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-229415, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]
- The senior management team continued with the business: Jerry L. Winchester (President and Chief Executive Officer since September 2011) and Cary D. Baetz (Chief Financial Officer and Treasurer since January 2012) remained in their roles. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-229415, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]

## Shared corporate substrate that did not transfer as an intact operating base
- The business had historically used CHK's infrastructure to support its business functions, specifically: accounting; information technology and systems; legal; tax; internal audit; human resources; provision of office and yard space; risk management; and treasury — paid for on a cost-allocation basis. Following the spin-off the company would no longer have access to CHK's support services and would need to establish its own. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-229415, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]
- The company had no history of operating as an independent public company. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-229415, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]

## Contractual arrangements bridging the shared substrate (closing-time)
- The parties entered a Master Separation Agreement containing the key provisions relating to the separation and the share distribution, under which the company would also indemnify CHK against various claims and liabilities relating to past operation of the business. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-229415, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1; listed as Exhibit 2.1 in SEC accession 0001193125-14-236450, Form 10-12B/A]
- A Transition Services Agreement, under which CHK would continue to provide certain of the administrative support services (that it had historically supplied) to the separated business on an interim/transitional basis. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-229415, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1; Exhibit 10.1 in SEC accession 0001193125-14-236450, Form 10-12B/A]
- A Tax Sharing Agreement allocating taxes incurred before, on and after the separation (Exhibit 10.5), and an Employee Matters Agreement (Exhibit 10.6). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-236450, Form 10-12B/A; SEC accession 0001193125-14-229415, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]

## Continuing customer relationship structured at closing
- CHK would remain a customer after the spin-off: a Master Services Agreement, under which the company provides drilling and other services and supplies materials and equipment to CHK, continued. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-229415, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]
- The pre-existing Services Agreement (under which CHK had guaranteed utilization of a portion of the drilling-rig and hydraulic-fracturing fleets) was to be terminated in connection with the spin-off; for hydraulic fracturing services, CHK would for a term of three years guarantee certain minimum utilization rates. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-229415, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]
- The company disclosed that it was dependent on CHK for a significant portion of its revenues and had no control over CHK's business decisions and operations. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-229415, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]

## New capital structure established at separation
- As part of the Transactions the operating entity entered an approximate $275.0 million senior secured New Credit Facility and a $400.0 million secured Term Loan (proceeds used to repay in full and terminate the Existing Credit Facility), and COO issued $500.0 million of New Senior Notes, using approximately $391.0 million of the net proceeds to make a cash distribution to its immediate parent, COSH. [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-229415, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]

## Structural summary (closing only)
- The transaction detached CHK's oilfield-services operating segment (drilling, hydraulic fracturing, rentals, trucking) — together with its ~5,100 employees and its incumbent management — into a newly independent public company via pro rata share distribution, while several adjacent businesses were pulled back to CHK, the shared corporate service base (accounting, IT, legal, tax, HR, treasury, office/yard space) did not transfer intact and had to be rebuilt, and the parent remained both the largest customer (Master Services Agreement + a three-year fracturing-utilization guarantee) and the transitional provider of those corporate services (Transition Services Agreement). [SRC: SEC accession 0001193125-14-229415, Form 10-12B/A EX-99.1]
