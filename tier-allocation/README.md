## What this paper does

Two consumer-goods firms with identical revenues, margins, and aggregate investment exit at 2x versus 9x revenue, with the acquirer of the first writing down goodwill within eighteen months. Capital allocation theory, dynamic capabilities, and marketing-finance research cannot explain this divergence because all three literatures treat the structural direction of investment within a business as given. This paper formalizes the cross-tier allocation problem: organizational tiers differ in asset durability, and the long-run value generated by a dollar of investment depends on which tier receives it. Each tier t accumulates stock according to dS_t/dt = w_t I - delta_t S_t with empirically calibrated decay rates ranging from .50/year at the organizational surface (Tier 6: advertising, paid media) to .05-.10/year at foundational layers (Tiers 2-3: business-model architecture, legal position). Long-run value is a discounted Cobb-Douglas aggregator with Jorgensonian user costs (delta_t + r); optimizing subject to the per-tier rental-rate budget constraint yields the closed-form rule w_t*(r) = alpha_t / (delta_t + r) and the comparative static d(w_6*)/dr > 0 -- higher discount rates shift optimal allocation toward the high-decay surface tier at the expense of durable substrate.

## Four contributions

1. **Architectural decomposition of resource allocation.** Introduces organizational tier as the unit of analysis for within-business investment direction, extending Dierickx and Cool (1989) from single-asset to multi-tier settings and grounding Belo, Lin, and Vitorino's (2014) empirical depreciation estimates in organizational theory.

2. **Four falsifiable propositions.** P1: pre-deal surface-tier intensity predicts post-acquisition goodwill impairment hazard. P2: long-horizon governance (founder-CEOs, patient-capital LPs) lowers equilibrium surface-tier share. P3: cost-of-capital shocks shift allocation predictably in the direction d(w_6*)/dr > 0 under Cobb-Douglas and gross-substitutes regimes. P4: capability-rotation stage moderates returns to Tier-4 codification investment.

3. **Portfolio formalism extending Stein (1997), Mizik and Jacobson (2003, 2009), and Teece (2007).** The decay-rate differential delta_4 << delta_6 makes brand-capital stock and advertising flow structurally non-fungible -- the organizational-layer mechanism that Mizik and Jacobson's empirical signature requires but does not provide. The frictionless-planner optimum supplies the benchmark against which agency distortions (P2) are measured.

4. **Agency extension closing the model-empirical gap.** Short-tenure professional managers face effective r_A > r_P relative to patient principals; the comparative static directly predicts over-allocation to Tier 6 as an agency equilibrium outcome, not merely as a behavioral bias, generating the governance-structure predictions in P2 and strengthening the directional propositions for empirical identification.
