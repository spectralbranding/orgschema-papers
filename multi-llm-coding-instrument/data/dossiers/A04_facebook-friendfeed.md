## 1. Deal identification
- Acquirer / parties: Facebook, Inc. agreed to acquire FriendFeed. `[SRC: https://about.fb.com/news/2009/08/facebook-agrees-to-acquire-sharing-service-friendfeed/]`
- Target: FriendFeed, a real-time sharing service founded in October 2007 by four former Google engineers — Bret Taylor (previously the group product manager who launched Google Maps), Paul Buchheit (Google engineer behind Gmail), Jim Norris, and Sanjeev Singh. `[SRC: https://about.fb.com/news/2009/08/facebook-agrees-to-acquire-sharing-service-friendfeed/]`
- Announced / closed dates: Announced August 10, 2009. `[SRC: https://about.fb.com/news/2009/08/facebook-agrees-to-acquire-sharing-service-friendfeed/]`
- Disclosed deal value: Not disclosed by Facebook (the announcement stated no financial terms). Press reports at the time estimated roughly $50 million in cash and stock, but this figure was not confirmed on a primary source fetched this session. `[UNVERIFIED: deal value ~$50M is a press estimate, not company-confirmed]`
- Legal form (share deal / asset deal / merger / etc.): Acquisition of the company and its team (acqui-hire): "all FriendFeed employees will join Facebook" and the four founders "hold senior roles on Facebook's engineering and product teams." `[SRC: https://about.fb.com/news/2009/08/facebook-agrees-to-acquire-sharing-service-friendfeed/]`

## 2. Tier-level transfer content (evidence for the bundle signature)
For each tier, what crossed the ownership boundary and under what direction.
- **T1 Owner Intent** — FriendFeed's founder-principal intent exited with the acquisition; the founders became senior operators inside Facebook and the controlling strategic intent became Facebook's (Mark Zuckerberg framing the deal around Facebook's engineering culture, "a place where the best engineers come to build things quickly that lots of people will use"). `[SRC: https://about.fb.com/news/2009/08/facebook-agrees-to-acquire-sharing-service-friendfeed/]`
- **T2 Business Model** — FriendFeed's standalone consumer-service model was not carried forward as an independent business; the transaction was a talent/technology acquisition rather than a going-concern purchase, and FriendFeed.com was only kept running "for the time being as the teams determine the longer term plans for the product." `[SRC: https://about.fb.com/news/2009/08/facebook-agrees-to-acquire-sharing-service-friendfeed/]`
- **T3 Business Entity** — FriendFeed's operations and staff were absorbed into Facebook, Inc.; the FriendFeed brand/service continued to run under Facebook ownership until it was shut down on April 9, 2015 (the site had been acquired in 2009 and operated with minimal changes for nearly six years). `[SRC: https://about.fb.com/news/2009/08/facebook-agrees-to-acquire-sharing-service-friendfeed/]` `[SRC: https://www.pcworld.com/article/432331/friendfeed-will-finally-shut-down-on-april-9.html]`
- **T4 Product** — The FriendFeed.com product was maintained largely untouched (read-only in practice, with declining use) and then discarded, shutting down April 9, 2015; the acquisition value was in the team/technology rather than continuation of the product. `[SRC: https://www.pcworld.com/article/432331/friendfeed-will-finally-shut-down-on-april-9.html]`
- **T5 Process** — FriendFeed's engineering capability transferred into Facebook: all employees joined and the founders took senior roles on Facebook's engineering and product teams. `[SRC: https://about.fb.com/news/2009/08/facebook-agrees-to-acquire-sharing-service-friendfeed/]`
- **T6 Organization** — People transferred whole: "all FriendFeed employees will join Facebook," with the four founders in senior engineering/product roles. `[SRC: https://about.fb.com/news/2009/08/facebook-agrees-to-acquire-sharing-service-friendfeed/]`
