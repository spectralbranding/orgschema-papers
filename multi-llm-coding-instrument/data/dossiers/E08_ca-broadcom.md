## 1. Deal identification
- Acquirer / parties: Broadcom Inc., a Delaware corporation headquartered in San Jose, California (Nasdaq: AVGO), a leading designer, developer and global supplier of digital and analog semiconductor connectivity solutions, acting through its wholly owned Delaware subsidiary Collie Acquisition Corp. ("Merger Sub"). `[SRC: DEFM14A accession 0001193125-18-246044, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/356028/000119312518246044/d512980ddefm14a.htm]`
- Target: CA, Inc. (CA Technologies), a Delaware corporation headquartered in New York City (Nasdaq: CA), a global provider of enterprise and mainframe software solutions enabling customers to plan, develop, automate, manage and secure applications across mobile, cloud, distributed and mainframe platforms. `[SRC: DEFM14A accession 0001193125-18-246044, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/356028/000119312518246044/d512980ddefm14a.htm]`
- Announced / closed dates: Agreement and Plan of Merger dated July 11, 2018 (announced July 11, 2018); acquisition completed November 5, 2018. `[SRC: DEFM14A accession 0001193125-18-246044, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/356028/000119312518246044/d512980ddefm14a.htm; Broadcom Form 8-K (Item 2.01), accession 0001193125-18-317917, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1730168/000119312518317917/d648705d8k.htm]`
- Disclosed deal value: $44.50 in cash per share of CA common stock (an approximately 20% premium to CA's $37.21 closing price on July 11, 2018); transaction valued at approximately $18.9 billion. `[SRC: DEFM14A accession 0001193125-18-246044, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/356028/000119312518246044/d512980ddefm14a.htm; Broadcom investor news release, https://investors.broadcom.com/news-releases/news-release-details/broadcom-inc-completes-acquisition-ca-technologies]`
- Legal form (share deal / asset deal / merger / etc.): Reverse triangular cash merger — Merger Sub (Collie Acquisition Corp.) merged with and into CA, with CA surviving and becoming a wholly owned subsidiary of Broadcom; CA ceased to be an independent publicly traded company (delisted from Nasdaq). The merger was not conditioned on financing; Broadcom funded it via cash on hand plus ~$18 billion of committed senior unsecured term facilities. `[SRC: DEFM14A accession 0001193125-18-246044, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/356028/000119312518246044/d512980ddefm14a.htm]`

## 2. Tier-level transfer content (evidence for the bundle signature)
For each tier, what crossed the ownership boundary and under what direction.
- **T1 Owner Intent** — CA's prior public-shareholder principal exited; the asset came under Broadcom's strategic intent — Broadcom (a semiconductor company) entering the infrastructure-software market, folding CA into a new Infrastructure Software segment focused on the largest enterprise customers. `[SRC: DEFM14A accession 0001193125-18-246044, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/356028/000119312518246044/d512980ddefm14a.htm; Market Realist, https://marketrealist.com/2018/12/broadcom-restructures-ca-technologies-business-model/]`
- **T2 Business Model** — The enterprise/mainframe-software value model transferred but was subsequently restructured by Broadcom: reorientation toward CA's largest accounts (CA earned a reported >70% of revenue from its top ~500 accounts), a shift from perpetual licensing toward a ratable subscription model, and higher targeted margins (a reported long-term adjusted EBITDA margin target of ~55%). `[SRC: Market Realist, https://marketrealist.com/2018/12/broadcom-restructures-ca-technologies-business-model/]`
- **T3 Business Entity** — Legal entity survived as a subsidiary: CA continued as the surviving corporation and became a wholly owned subsidiary of Broadcom via the reverse triangular merger; delisted from Nasdaq. `[SRC: DEFM14A accession 0001193125-18-246044, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/356028/000119312518246044/d512980ddefm14a.htm; Broadcom Form 8-K, accession 0001193125-18-317917, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1730168/000119312518317917/d648705d8k.htm]`
- **T4 Product** — Customer-facing product franchise transferred, principally CA's mainframe and enterprise software portfolio; Broadcom concurrently divested one product line — CA's Veracode application-security unit — to Thoma Bravo for ~$950 million (announced at/around completion). `[SRC: Broadcom investor news release, https://investors.broadcom.com/news-releases/news-release-details/broadcom-inc-completes-acquisition-ca-technologies; ChannelE2E, https://www.channele2e.com/news/broadcom-completes-ca-technologies-buyout-sells-veracode]`
- **T5 Process** — Operating processes transferred with the going concern but were re-engineered under Broadcom's cost-focused operating model (concentration on top accounts, reduced go-to-market spend); Broadcom is reported to have applied significant renewal price increases (a reported 30-80% range at CA mainframe renewal) reflecting the reshaped commercial process. `[SRC: Market Realist, https://marketrealist.com/2018/12/broadcom-restructures-ca-technologies-business-model/; Redress Compliance, https://redresscompliance.com/broadcom-software-ca-mainframe-licensing-cio-playbook]`
- **T6 Organization** — People transferred as part of the going-concern acquisition into Broadcom's Infrastructure Software segment; Broadcom's model is widely reported to have involved substantial cost/headcount rationalization in acquired software units. Specific transferred/retained-headcount figure not located in the primary filings fetched this session. `[SRC: DEFM14A accession 0001193125-18-246044, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/356028/000119312518246044/d512980ddefm14a.htm; Market Realist, https://marketrealist.com/2018/12/broadcom-restructures-ca-technologies-business-model/]` `[UNVERIFIED: exact transferred/retained employee count]`

## 3. Post-deal performance at the 3-5 year horizon
- CAR / share-price reaction, OR write-down magnitude as fraction of deal value, OR divestiture timing (months from close): One immediate carve-out divestiture — Veracode sold to Thoma Bravo for ~$950 million at/near close (~0 months). The retained CA assets were absorbed into Broadcom's Infrastructure Software segment and, over the 3-5yr horizon (2019-2023), managed under Broadcom's high-margin operating model (top-account focus, subscription conversion, renewal price increases). No public goodwill impairment on the CA assets was identified this session; CA's success established the template Broadcom repeated with Symantec's enterprise business (2019) and VMware (2023). `[SRC: ChannelE2E, https://www.channele2e.com/news/broadcom-completes-ca-technologies-buyout-sells-veracode; Market Realist, https://marketrealist.com/2019/03/broadcom-taps-infrastructure-software-market-with-ca-technologies/]` `[UNVERIFIED: any goodwill impairment; segment-level 3-5yr financials]`
- Narrative outcome (integration succeeded / failed / mixed): Financially successful for the acquirer — CA became the anchor of a profitable Infrastructure Software franchise and validated Broadcom's software M&A strategy — but accompanied by documented customer friction (large renewal price increases and licensing-model changes) that generated CIO pushback, a pattern later echoed with VMware. `[SRC: Market Realist, https://marketrealist.com/2018/12/broadcom-restructures-ca-technologies-business-model/; Redress Compliance, https://redresscompliance.com/broadcom-software-ca-mainframe-licensing-cio-playbook]`
