## 1. Deal identification
- Acquirer / parties: Daimler-Benz AG (Stuttgart) and Chrysler Corporation (Auburn Hills, Michigan) combined into a new German company, DaimlerChrysler AG. `[SRC: https://dieselnet.com/news/1998/05daimler.php]`
- Target: Chrysler Corporation; its shareholders received 0.547 DaimlerChrysler shares per Chrysler share and ended up holding roughly 43% of the combined company. `[SRC: https://dieselnet.com/news/1998/05daimler.php]`
- Announced / closed dates: Announced May 7, 1998; deal consummated November 1998. `[SRC: https://imaa-institute.org/blog/lessons-from-the-daimler-chrysler-merger/]`
- Disclosed deal value: Approximately $36 billion equity value announced (largest industrial merger of its kind to date); dieselnet reports Chrysler was valued at about $39 billion at the time of the 1998 merger. `[SRC: https://dieselnet.com/news/2007/05daimler.php]`
- Legal form (share deal / asset deal / merger / etc.): Stock-swap merger into a newly incorporated German entity (DaimlerChrysler AG), via exchange-offer and merger transactions; the new company was incorporated in Germany. `[SRC: https://www.wardsauto.com/chrysler/merger-what-merger-clearly-now-daimlerchrysler-is-a-german-company]`

## 2. Tier-level transfer content (evidence for the bundle signature)
For each tier, what crossed the ownership boundary and under what direction.
- **T1 Owner Intent** — Chrysler's controlling principal (its US board/management) exited; the combined company's strategic intent was set from Stuttgart. Schrempp later said the "merger of equals" label was used for "psychological reasons" and that he always intended to make Chrysler a "division," which he described as a takeover of Chrysler by Daimler. `[SRC: https://www.wardsauto.com/chrysler/merger-what-merger-clearly-now-daimlerchrysler-is-a-german-company]`
- **T2 Business Model** — Chrysler's US mass-market volume-automaker model was retained initially but subordinated to the combined group; anticipated synergies failed to materialize. `[SRC: https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/has-daimlerchrysler-hit-the-breakdown-lane-or-just-stopped-to-fill-up/]`
- **T3 Business Entity** — New legal vehicle created: DaimlerChrysler AG, organized/incorporated as a German company (May 1998); Chrysler Corporation's separate legal existence ended in the combination. `[SRC: https://www.wardsauto.com/chrysler/merger-what-merger-clearly-now-daimlerchrysler-is-a-german-company]`
- **T4 Product** — Chrysler's product lines (Chrysler, Dodge, Jeep, Plymouth) transferred whole and continued to be produced and sold under the combined company. `[SRC: https://imaa-institute.org/blog/lessons-from-the-daimler-chrysler-merger/]`
- **T5 Process** — Operations were not deeply integrated: Germans resisted platform-sharing initiatives designed to leverage Chrysler's manufacturing to protect Mercedes-Benz brand exclusivity, so processes largely stayed separate. `[SRC: https://www.wardsauto.com/chrysler/merger-what-merger-clearly-now-daimlerchrysler-is-a-german-company]`
- **T6 Organization** — Partial transfer with US management purge: Chrysler president of North American operations Tom Stallkamp was forced out for publicly opposing Schrempp; senior German executives were promoted; a widely reported cultural clash followed. `[SRC: https://www.wardsauto.com/chrysler/merger-what-merger-clearly-now-daimlerchrysler-is-a-german-company]`

## 3. Post-deal performance at the 3-5 year horizon
- CAR / share-price reaction, OR write-down magnitude as fraction of deal value, OR divestiture timing (months from close): Divested — DaimlerChrysler sold an 80.1% stake in Chrysler to Cerberus Capital Management for $7.4 billion, announced May 14, 2007, roughly 9 years (~108 months) after the November 1998 close; the sale price was a fraction of the ~$36 billion 1998 deal value, and Daimler transferred Chrysler's industrial business free of debt while retaining only a 19.9% stake. `[SRC: https://dieselnet.com/news/2007/05daimler.php]`
- Narrative outcome (integration succeeded / failed / mixed): Failed — widely characterized as a cautionary tale in which the promised "merger of equals" became a German-controlled takeover and the envisioned synergies never materialized. `[SRC: https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/has-daimlerchrysler-hit-the-breakdown-lane-or-just-stopped-to-fill-up/]`

## 4. Failure-pathway evidence (parent-paper P1-P7)
Cite documentation for any pathway that manifested at the 3-5yr horizon; mark the non-cascade channels (T1-archetype, T2-model) separately per Amendment 4.
- P4 Product-Process disruption (T4<->T5): Evidence — planned platform-sharing between Chrysler product lines and Daimler manufacturing was resisted and not implemented. `[SRC: https://www.wardsauto.com/chrysler/merger-what-merger-clearly-now-daimlerchrysler-is-a-german-company]`
- P5 Process-Organization fracture (T5<->T6): Evidence — management purge (Stallkamp ousted) and reported culture clash accompanied the failure to integrate operations. `[SRC: https://www.wardsauto.com/chrysler/merger-what-merger-clearly-now-daimlerchrysler-is-a-german-company]`
- T1-archetype incoherence (non-cascade channel): Evidence — "merger of equals" intent conflicted with actual controlling intent (Chrysler as a subordinate division), acknowledged by Schrempp. `[SRC: https://www.wardsauto.com/chrysler/merger-what-merger-clearly-now-daimlerchrysler-is-a-german-company]`
- T2-model incompatibility (non-cascade channel): Evidence — mass-market volume model (Chrysler) vs premium/luxury model (Mercedes) were kept separate to protect Mercedes brand. `[SRC: https://www.wardsauto.com/chrysler/merger-what-merger-clearly-now-daimlerchrysler-is-a-german-company]`
- Other Pn manifested: Cultural mismatch is the dominant documented failure narrative. `[SRC: https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/has-daimlerchrysler-hit-the-breakdown-lane-or-just-stopped-to-fill-up/]`
