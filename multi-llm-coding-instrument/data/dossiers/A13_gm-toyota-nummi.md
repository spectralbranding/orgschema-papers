## 1. Deal identification
- Parties: New United Motor Manufacturing, Inc. (NUMMI), an automobile manufacturer in Fremont, California, jointly owned in equal (50/50) shares by General Motors and Toyota. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NUMMI]`
- Contributions: GM contributed its troubled Fremont Assembly plant (operated 1962, closed 1982 for severe labor/quality problems); Toyota managed operations and implemented the Toyota Production System, sending ~30 managers/coordinators from Japan including CEO Tatsuro Toyoda. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NUMMI]`
- Founded / closed dates: opened for production December 1984; defunct / final vehicle April 1, 2010. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NUMMI]`
- Disclosed deal value: not a purchase-price transaction; a jointly capitalized manufacturing JV (specific capital split not quantified in sources reviewed). `[UNVERIFIED]`
- Legal form: joint venture — a newly created, separately named legal entity jointly owned by the two parents, not an acquisition of one by the other. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NUMMI]`

## 2. Tier-level transfer content (evidence for the bundle signature)
- **T1 Owner Intent** — New shared/dual imprint. A new controlling intent formed jointly by two principals with distinct goals: GM sought to learn the Toyota Production System; Toyota sought a U.S. manufacturing foothold and a test of TPS with an American UAW workforce. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NUMMI]`
- **T2 Business Model** — New. NUMMI was established as an independent 50-50 joint venture "to produce cars on contract for both companies" — a shared contract-manufacturing vehicle producing for BOTH parents' brands rather than for a single owner's account. `[SRC: https://pressroom.toyota.com/toyota-commits-250-million-to-support-nummi-transition/]` `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NUMMI]`
- **T3 Business Entity** — New vehicle. A fresh jointly held legal entity (NUMMI) was incorporated to hold the venture; the predecessor GM Fremont Assembly entity had been shut down. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NUMMI]`
- **T4 Product** — Dual-branded output. The plant built product specified by both parents: GM-badged Chevrolet Nova (1985–88), Geo/Chevrolet Prizm (1989–2002) and Pontiac Vibe (2003–10); Toyota-badged Corolla (1987–2010), Tacoma (1995–2010), Hilux/pickup and Toyota Matrix. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NUMMI]`
- **T5 Process** — Transferred/taught (the core exchange). Toyota installed the Toyota Production System; workers were trained at Toyota's Takaoka plant in Japan before production began — the process knowledge GM sought to acquire and diffuse. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NUMMI]`
- **T6 Organization** — GM plant workforce rehired under new culture. The venture rehired much of the former (UAW-represented) Fremont workforce — previously described as among the worst in the U.S. auto industry — and, under TPS management, transformed workplace culture into one of GM's best-performing factories. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NUMMI]`

## 3. Post-deal performance at the 3-5 year horizon
- Operating outcome: within about five years the plant operated as efficiently as Toyota's Japanese facilities and became one of GM's best-performing factories, validating the TPS transfer. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NUMMI]`
- Long-run outcome (unwind): in May 2009 GM abruptly ended its Pontiac Vibe production contract (production ceased August 17, 2009); in June 2009, as part of GM's bankruptcy, GM's 50% NUMMI share was left in Motors Liquidation Company ("old GM"); Toyota then decided in August 2009 to end its own production contract effective April 1, 2010. `[SRC: https://pressroom.toyota.com/toyota-commits-250-million-to-support-nummi-transition/]`
- Closure detail: the final vehicle (a red Toyota Corolla) rolled off April 1, 2010; ~4,700 employees received severance totaling ~USD 281 million (avg ~USD 54,000/worker), negotiated via the UAW. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NUMMI]`
- Narrative outcome: operationally successful joint venture; the venture ended not from integration failure but because GM "abandoned" NUMMI amid its 2009 bankruptcy, which Toyota stated "severely undermined the economic viability of the plant," making continued contract manufacturing "not economically feasible." `[SRC: https://pressroom.toyota.com/toyota-commits-250-million-to-support-nummi-transition/]`

## 4. Failure-pathway evidence (parent-paper P1-P7)
- P4 Product-Process disruption (T4<->T5): none — TPS process and dual-branded product co-functioned productively for ~25 years. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NUMMI]`
- P5 Process-Organization fracture (T5<->T6): none — the TPS process and rehired workforce integrated into a high-performing culture. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NUMMI]`
- T1-archetype incoherence (non-cascade channel): the two owners' divergent intents (learning vehicle vs. manufacturing foothold) coexisted for the venture's life; the terminal cause was a change in one owner's circumstances (GM bankruptcy/abandonment), not intent incompatibility per se. `[SRC: https://pressroom.toyota.com/toyota-commits-250-million-to-support-nummi-transition/]` `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NUMMI]`
- T2-model incompatibility (non-cascade channel): none while both parents participated; the shared-vehicle model depended on both parents sourcing production, so GM's withdrawal removed a structural leg. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NUMMI]`
- Other Pn manifested: exogenous shock — GM's 2009 bankruptcy restructuring (its 50% stake dropped into Motors Liquidation Company) — dissolved the partnership rather than any tier-transfer defect. `[SRC: https://pressroom.toyota.com/toyota-commits-250-million-to-support-nummi-transition/]`

## 5. Cascade-gap-mitigation evidence (Amendment 3)
- At closure, Toyota committed transition support (reported ~USD 250 million toward the NUMMI transition) — a documented post-exit absorption of the unwind's cost, distinct from an at-formation cascade-gap instrument. `[SRC: https://pressroom.toyota.com/toyota-commits-250-million-to-support-nummi-transition/]`

## 6. Collapse-state evidence
- No Tier-1=Tier-4 founder-identity fusion (a corporate JV, no embodied founder). Identity/brand sat with the two parents whose badges the plant applied; the plant itself carried no separable founder-identity. `[SRC: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NUMMI]`
