## 1. Deal identification
- Acquirer / party: Service Corporation International (SCI), Houston, Texas; founded 1962 by Robert L. Waltrip; world's largest owner/operator of funeral homes and cemeteries. `[SRC: https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/service-corporation-international-history/]`
- Targets: hundreds of formerly independent, mostly family-owned funeral homes and cemeteries acquired serially over three decades (e.g., Kinney Services' 28 NYC/Miami homes 1970; IFS Industries 91 homes + 22 cemeteries 1981; Pierce Brothers, Sentinel Group, Arlington Corporation 1990s). `[SRC: https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/service-corporation-international-history/]`
- Announced / closed dates: continuous roll-up 1962–late 1990s; growth from 3 homes (1962) → 106 (1972) → 276 (1982) → 1,500+ (early 1990s) → peak >4,500 funeral homes and cemeteries in 20 countries. `[SRC: https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/service-corporation-international-history/]`
- Disclosed deal value: individual-deal-specific; SCI accumulated ~USD 3.3 billion of debt from acquisitions by ~2001; ~USD 1 billion invested in overseas expansion in the early 1990s. `[SRC: https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/service-corporation-international-history/]`
- Legal form: serial asset/share acquisitions; sellers received tailored combinations of common or preferred stock, cash, and secured long-term debt; some deals tax-free stock swaps (e.g., IFS 1981). `[SRC: https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/service-corporation-international-history/]`

## 2. Tier-level transfer content (evidence for the bundle signature)
- **T1 Owner Intent** — Replaced. Founding families' controlling intent exited; SCI imposed Waltrip's standardized-chain "McDonald's of funeral service" consolidation imprint over each acquired home. `[SRC: https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/service-corporation-international-history/]`
- **T2 Business Model** — Changed. Independent single-home economics replaced by SCI's "cluster" model: multiple homes in one market share overhead; SCI added pre-need trust-fund sales as an internal capital pool funding further acquisitions. `[SRC: https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/service-corporation-international-history/]`
- **T3 Business Entity** — Absorbed. Acquired homes became SCI subsidiaries; legal ownership transferred to SCI while SCI itself remained the continuing NYSE-listed parent (ticker SRV). `[SRC: https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/service-corporation-international-history/]`
- **T4 Product** — Transferred whole, deliberately preserved. SCI "kept the original family names on the homes" — the customer-facing brand/acceptance layer was retained intact even as ownership changed, to preserve local goodwill. `[SRC: https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/service-corporation-international-history/]`
- **T5 Process** — Re-engineered. Cluster operations pooled hearses (central fleet vs. one-per-home), shared embalming operations and personnel across the local cluster, eliminating between-funeral idle payroll. `[SRC: https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/service-corporation-international-history/]`
- **T6 Organization** — Partial / pooled. Staff were shared across whichever SCI home in a cluster needed them rather than retained one-per-home, a coordination the prior family businesses could not replicate. `[SRC: https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/service-corporation-international-history/]`

## 3. Post-deal performance at the 3-5 year horizon
- Share-price reaction: after the Q4 1998 profit warning (announced January 26, 1999), SCI's stock dropped by more than 40 percent; an investment of USD 1,000 in SCI stock at the start of 1999 was worth only USD 187 by year-end; SCI was named the *Wall Street Journal*'s worst one-year performer of 1999. `[SRC: https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/service-corporation-international-history/]`
- Precise one-day tick reported as ~USD 34-7/16 → ~USD 19-1/8 (~44%, ~USD 3.5 billion market-cap loss) on Jan 26, 1999; figure surfaced in search summaries but the underlying primary source (court complaint / SEC 8-K, accession 0000950129-99-000276) was not directly opened this session. `[UNVERIFIED]`
- Divestiture / forced unwind: from 1999 SCI reversed the roll-up — discontinued dividends (1999) to service debt, cut U.S. operating clusters from ~200 to 87, and in January 2001 announced plans to sell over 400 funeral homes plus more than 100 cemeteries; homes fell nearly by half (to ~2,500) and footprint from 20 countries to 8. `[SRC: https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/service-corporation-international-history/]`
- Narrative outcome: failed/mixed — the acquisition-driven growth model broke when consolidation drove acquisition prices too high AND the death rate began declining (shrinking market) by 1998; SCI shifted to internal growth and debt paydown. President L. William Heiligbrodt resigned after the >40% drop; a string of executives departed. `[SRC: https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/service-corporation-international-history/]`
