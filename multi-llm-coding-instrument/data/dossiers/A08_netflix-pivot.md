## 1. Deal identification
- Acquirer / parties: Netflix, Inc. (single firm; CEO Reed Hastings). No counterparty — this is an internal restructuring, not an acquisition or divestiture. `[SRC: https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2011/10/10/141209082/netflix-kills-qwikster-price-hike-lives-on]`
- Target: Netflix's own DVD-by-mail business, which the company planned to split off into a separately branded service called "Qwikster," leaving streaming under the Netflix brand. `[SRC: https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2011/10/10/141209082/netflix-kills-qwikster-price-hike-lives-on]`
- Announced / closed dates: In July 2011 Netflix announced the separation of its streaming and DVD plans plus a price increase; the "Qwikster" DVD-service rebrand followed in mid-September 2011; the plan to split was reversed ("killed") on October 10, 2011. `[SRC: https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2011/10/10/141209082/netflix-kills-qwikster-price-hike-lives-on]`
- Disclosed deal value: Not applicable (no transaction). The associated pricing change raised the combined streaming-plus-DVD base price effectively from $10 to $16 (a rate hike of as much as 60%). `[SRC: https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2011/10/10/141209082/netflix-kills-qwikster-price-hike-lives-on]`
- Legal form (share deal / asset deal / merger / etc.): None — an intra-firm strategic pivot; the planned Qwikster separate service was cancelled before launch. `[SRC: https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2011/10/10/141209082/netflix-kills-qwikster-price-hike-lives-on]`

## 2. Tier-level transfer content (evidence for the bundle signature)
For each tier, the tier-level change OVER TIME within Netflix (no ownership boundary crossed).
- **T1 Owner Intent** — The controlling principal was unchanged (Reed Hastings / Netflix board throughout); strategic intent shifted from an integrated DVD+streaming offering toward separating the two, then reversed back to keeping them unified: "we are going to keep Netflix as one place to go for streaming and DVDs." `[SRC: https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2011/10/10/141209082/netflix-kills-qwikster-price-hike-lives-on]`
- **T2 Business Model** — Netflix attempted to unbundle its subscription into two separately-priced services (streaming vs. DVD-by-mail) with a price increase ($10 → $16 combined); the business-model split was abandoned within about a month, though the price increase remained in place. `[SRC: https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2011/10/10/141209082/netflix-kills-qwikster-price-hike-lives-on]`
- **T3 Business Entity** — No separate legal vehicle ultimately materialized: the planned Qwikster DVD service was cancelled before it launched — "in other words, no Qwikster." `[SRC: https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2011/10/10/141209082/netflix-kills-qwikster-price-hike-lives-on]`
- **T4 Product** — The customer-facing offering was to be split into two separate products/experiences (streaming catalog vs. newer-release DVDs) accessed via two sites/accounts; this was reversed to a single product surface: "one website, one account, one password ... under the Netflix brand." `[SRC: https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2011/10/10/141209082/netflix-kills-qwikster-price-hike-lives-on]`
- **T5 Process** — Separate operations/websites for DVD fulfillment vs. streaming were planned under the split; the operational separation was abandoned when the plan was reversed. `[SRC: https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2011/10/10/141209082/netflix-kills-qwikster-price-hike-lives-on]`
- **T6 Organization** — People/culture stayed within one firm; the split (and its reversal) did not transfer staff across any ownership boundary. (Press reports named Andy Rendich as the intended head of Qwikster, but that detail was not confirmed on a primary source fetched this session.) `[SRC: https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2011/10/10/141209082/netflix-kills-qwikster-price-hike-lives-on]` `[UNVERIFIED: Andy Rendich named to run Qwikster]`

## 3. Post-deal performance at the 3-5 year horizon
- CAR / share-price reaction, OR write-down magnitude as fraction of deal value, OR divestiture timing (months from close): No ownership transaction, so no CAR/write-down/divestiture metric. The episode's measured impact: the split was reversed ~23 days after the Qwikster announcement (announced mid-September, killed October 10, 2011); Netflix lost an estimated 1 million subscribers by mid-September 2011; and its shares lost more than half their value since July 2011. The price increases announced in July stayed in effect. `[SRC: https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2011/10/10/141209082/netflix-kills-qwikster-price-hike-lives-on]`
- Narrative outcome (integration succeeded / failed / mixed): The attempted structural split (Qwikster) failed and was reversed amid customer backlash; Netflix continued as a single integrated brand ("one website, one account, one password"), while retaining the price increase. Widely characterized as a major strategic misstep. `[SRC: https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2011/10/10/141209082/netflix-kills-qwikster-price-hike-lives-on]`

## 4. Failure-pathway evidence (parent-paper P1-P7)
Cite documentation for any pathway that manifested at the 3-5yr horizon; mark the
non-cascade channels (T1-archetype, T2-model) separately per Amendment 4.
- P4 Product-Process disruption (T4<->T5): The planned split would have fractured a single customer product (T4) across two separate websites/operations (T5); customer confusion over the two-site design was the stated reason for reversal ("for many of our members two websites would make things more difficult"). `[SRC: https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2011/10/10/141209082/netflix-kills-qwikster-price-hike-lives-on]`
- P5 Process-Organization fracture (T5<->T6): No ownership-crossing fracture; the reorganization stayed within one firm and was undone before launch. `[SRC: https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2011/10/10/141209082/netflix-kills-qwikster-price-hike-lives-on]`
- T1-archetype incoherence (non-cascade channel): The reversal reflects a leadership strategic-intent error (Hastings' own acknowledgment that Netflix had failed to communicate the changes well), corrected by returning to the unified model. `[SRC: https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2011/10/10/141209082/netflix-kills-qwikster-price-hike-lives-on]`
- T2-model incompatibility (non-cascade channel): The unbundled two-service model was rejected by customers, who valued the bundle; Netflix reversed the model split (keeping the price change). `[SRC: https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2011/10/10/141209082/netflix-kills-qwikster-price-hike-lives-on]`
- Other Pn manifested: Subscriber loss (~1 million) and a >50% share-price decline over July-October 2011 accompanied the misstep. `[SRC: https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2011/10/10/141209082/netflix-kills-qwikster-price-hike-lives-on]`
