## 1. Deal identification
- Acquirer / parties: 3Com Corporation (parent) separating its handheld-computing business into a new company, Palm, Inc. This is a divestiture (equity carve-out IPO followed by a pro-rata spin-off to 3Com shareholders), not an inbound acquisition. `[SRC: SEC Palm Inc Form 10-Q FY2000, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001100389/000091205701001273/a2035054z10-q.txt]`
- Target: Palm, Inc. — 3Com's handheld computing (PDA / Palm Pilot) business, carved out into an independent publicly-traded company announced September 13, 1999. `[SRC: SEC Palm Inc Form 10-Q FY2000, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001100389/000091205701001273/a2035054z10-q.txt]`
- Announced / closed dates: Announced September 13, 1999; Master Separation and Distribution Agreement December 1999; IPO March 2, 2000; spin-off distribution to 3Com shareholders July 27, 2000. `[SRC: SEC Palm Inc Form 10-Q FY2000, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001100389/000091205701001273/a2035054z10-q.txt]`
- Disclosed deal value: No acquisition price (divestiture). In the March 2000 IPO 3Com sold ~5% of Palm to the public; after the IPO 3Com owned 532,000,000 Palm shares (~94% of Palm). Palm's first-day close was $95.06, briefly giving Palm a market value larger than parent 3Com. The IPO reportedly raised ~$874 million. `[SRC: SEC Palm Inc Form 10-Q FY2000, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001100389/000091205701001273/a2035054z10-q.txt]` `[SRC: https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/can-market-add-and-subtract]`
- Legal form (share deal / asset deal / merger / etc.): Two-step separation — equity carve-out IPO of a minority stake, then a tax-motivated pro-rata distribution of the remaining shares; on July 27, 2000, 3Com distributed its Palm shares to 3Com stockholders (1.483 Palm shares per 3Com share). `[SRC: SEC Palm Inc Form 10-Q FY2000, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001100389/000091205701001273/a2035054z10-q.txt]`

## 2. Tier-level transfer content (evidence for the bundle signature)
For each tier, what crossed the ownership boundary and under what direction.
- **T1 Owner Intent** — 3Com's controlling intent over Palm was withdrawn; on the July 27, 2000 distribution Palm became an independent public company with its own board and strategic intent (the controlling principal shifted from 3Com to Palm's public shareholders/board). `[SRC: SEC Palm Inc Form 10-Q FY2000, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001100389/000091205701001273/a2035054z10-q.txt]`
- **T2 Business Model** — Palm's handheld-computing product-sales business model separated intact and carried forward as a standalone company. `[SRC: SEC Palm Inc Form 10-Q FY2000, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001100389/000091205701001273/a2035054z10-q.txt]`
- **T3 Business Entity** — New legal vehicle created: Palm, Inc. was incorporated, IPO'd, and then fully separated from 3Com via the Master Separation and Distribution Agreement; 3Com transferred to Palm the 3Com-owned assets and liabilities related to Palm (except most Palm accounts receivable/payable, retained by 3Com). `[SRC: SEC Palm Inc Form 10-Q FY2000, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001100389/000091205701001273/a2035054z10-q.txt]`
- **T4 Product** — Palm's product line (Palm handheld organizers / PDAs) carried forward whole into the independent company. `[SRC: SEC Palm Inc Form 10-Q FY2000, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001100389/000091205701001273/a2035054z10-q.txt]`
- **T5 Process** — Operations were carved out of 3Com; initially 3Com's consolidated statements included allocations of centralized 3Com services (legal, accounting, treasury, real estate, IT, distribution, customer service, sales, marketing, engineering), which had to be stood up independently post-separation. `[SRC: SEC Palm Inc Form 10-Q FY2000, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001100389/000091205701001273/a2035054z10-q.txt]`
- **T6 Organization** — Palm's people transferred with the business; 3Com stock options held by Palm employees were converted into options to purchase Palm common stock at the July 27, 2000 separation. `[SRC: SEC Palm Inc Form 10-Q FY2000, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001100389/000091205701001273/a2035054z10-q.txt]`

## 3. Post-deal performance at the 3-5 year horizon
- CAR / share-price reaction, OR write-down magnitude as fraction of deal value, OR divestiture timing (months from close): Divestiture completed on schedule (IPO March 2, 2000 → full spin-off July 27, 2000, ~5 months). The IPO produced the famous "negative stub" anomaly (implied non-Palm value of 3Com estimated at roughly -$22 billion after Palm's first trading day). As a standalone, Palm's stock collapsed — trading around $6.50 by June 2001, the worst-performing PDA maker on the NASDAQ at the time. `[SRC: https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/can-market-add-and-subtract]`
- Narrative outcome (integration succeeded / failed / mixed): The separation itself was executed cleanly, but the standalone Palm declined and repeatedly restructured — spinning out its software arm as PalmSource and merging hardware with Handspring to form palmOne (shareholder-approved October 28, 2003). `[SRC: https://www.deseret.com/2003/12/28/19803377/palmsource-cites-losses-due-to-palmone-spinoff/]`

## 4. Failure-pathway evidence (parent-paper P1-P7)
Cite documentation for any pathway that manifested at the 3-5yr horizon; mark the non-cascade channels (T1-archetype, T2-model) separately per Amendment 4.
- P4 Product-Process disruption (T4<->T5): The standalone company had to replace 3Com's centralized shared services (distribution, IT, sales, marketing, engineering), a documented process-side dependency at separation; the subsequent PalmSource/palmOne split further separated software (product) from hardware (process/product). `[SRC: SEC Palm Inc Form 10-Q FY2000, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001100389/000091205701001273/a2035054z10-q.txt]`
- P5 Process-Organization fracture (T5<->T6): No direct evidence of an organization/process fracture at separation; employees and their equity moved with the business. `[SRC: SEC Palm Inc Form 10-Q FY2000, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001100389/000091205701001273/a2035054z10-q.txt]`
- T1-archetype incoherence (non-cascade channel): None documented at separation; 3Com's stated intent was a clean, tax-efficient full separation of Palm. `[SRC: SEC Palm Inc Form 10-Q FY2000, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001100389/000091205701001273/a2035054z10-q.txt]`
- T2-model incompatibility (non-cascade channel): Not an incompatibility at separation (single-business standalone); the later strategic difficulty was competitive (PDA market decline / smartphone transition), reflected in subsequent restructurings. `[SRC: https://www.deseret.com/2003/12/28/19803377/palmsource-cites-losses-due-to-palmone-spinoff/]`
- Other Pn manifested: Post-separation the standalone Palm underwent repeated corporate splits (PalmSource, palmOne/Handspring), evidencing structural instability of the divested business. `[SRC: https://www.deseret.com/2003/12/28/19803377/palmsource-cites-losses-due-to-palmone-spinoff/]`

## 5. Cascade-gap-mitigation evidence (Amendment 3)
- Was a documented cascade gap contractually absorbed at closing (transitional-services / operating agreement)? Yes — 3Com and Palm entered a Master Separation and Distribution Agreement (December 1999), and pre-separation financials reflect 3Com providing centralized shared services (legal, accounting, treasury, IT, distribution, customer service, sales, marketing, engineering) as a transitional bridge for the carved-out business. `[SRC: SEC Palm Inc Form 10-Q FY2000, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001100389/000091205701001273/a2035054z10-q.txt]`
