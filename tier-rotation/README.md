## 3 | What this paper does

Three founder-led brands — Yeezy/Adidas, Casamigos/Diageo, Tom Ford/Estée Lauder — reached M&A resolution within the same five-year window, with outcomes ranging from zero transfer value to a $2.8 billion multiple. Standard human-capital and succession-discount theories cannot explain this three-way divergence: all three principals were globally recognized, all drove substantial brand revenues, and quality differences alone do not generate outcomes of this magnitude. The puzzle requires a structural account.

This paper supplies that account. The Tier-Rotation Curve is a continuous, logistic-form model of how brand signal migrates from a founder-bound substrate (Tier 1 in the six-tier organizational architecture of Zharnikov 2026ag) to an institutionally separable product-brand substrate (Tier 4) through deliberate knowledge-externalization effort over time. M&A value at exit is derived as a piecewise function of Tier-4 share at deal time, with a separability threshold above which brand assets become acquirable independently of the originating principal and below which founder-flight risk produces discounts and earnout structures. The model's four-stage valuation rationale — pure-personal, partial-projection, substantial-projection, institutional — emerges as four regions of a single continuous curve rather than four discrete types.

### Three contributions

1. **Formal geometry of non-monotonic M&A value.** Derives V(s) as a piecewise function of Tier-4 share with a structural discontinuity at the separability kink. Explains within-founder, longitudinal variation in acquirer willingness-to-pay — a prediction unavailable to cross-sectional succession-discount theories — and constitutes a falsifiable empirical wedge against human-capital accounts.

2. **Knowledge-externalization as the generative mechanism.** Grounds the logistic-accumulation equation in Nonaka and Takeuchi's (1995) SECI externalization phase and derives four observable proxies for rotation progress: declining founder-language salience in 10-K filings, shifting advertising allocation from founder-attribute to product-attribute, declining founder-media share of brand impressions, and trademark-portfolio migration from founder-name to product-attribute marks.

3. **Endogenization of resource-mobility assumptions in the RBV.** Supplies a theory of why brand resources shift along the mobility spectrum from sticky to separable. The mechanism is knowledge externalization; the threshold is the separability kink; the time path is the logistic curve. Extends the dynamic capabilities tradition (Teece, Pisano, and Shuen 1997; Eisenhardt and Martin 2000) by specifying the precise capability — brand-substrate codification — that governs brand-resource mobility.
