# Specification Readiness: Measuring an Architectural Antecedent of Functional Friction and AI Returns

Dmitry Zharnikov

ORCID: 0009-0000-6893-9231

DOI: [10.5281/zenodo.20384084](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20384084)

Working Paper v1.1.0 – May 2026 (revised June 2026)

---

## Abstract

This paper introduces *specification readiness* — the degree to which a firm's commitments are codified in versioned, machine-readable, queryable form — as a new strategic construct and supplies a scalable archival operationalization. Drawing on an information-theoretic model of multi-interface architecture [@zharnikov-2026am-specification-readiness-friction], the construct is argued to substitute query-based alignment for costly interface-maintenance functions, reducing a structural friction tax, accelerating brand-capital accumulation, lowering cross-stakeholder valuation dispersion, and raising returns to artificial-intelligence deployment. The primary measure is a continuous Specification Coherence Index built from year-over-year cosine similarity of 10-K narrative embeddings; a four-event sharp index supplies robustness. Five hypotheses (H1–H5) link the construct to these outcomes for US public firms 2010–2025, identified by within-firm continuous treatment, staggered difference-in-differences [@callaway-2021-differenceindifferences-with-multiple; @goodmanbacon-2021-differenceindifferences-with-variation], regulatory-compliance instruments, and advertising-cessation event studies, with Oster [-@oster-2019-unobservable-selection-coefficient] bounds. Pre-registered Monte Carlo mechanism tests (12.96 million trials; α* = .91; Cohen's d = 88.4) confirm the friction-tax phase shift. Three contributions follow: a first scalable archival measure of specification readiness, a reusable identification template pairing continuous textual treatment with sharp codification events, and formal evidence that specification readiness moderates the AI augmentation paradox.

**Keywords**: specification readiness; multi-interface architecture; friction tax; staggered difference-in-differences; organizational coupling

---

Organizations devote enormous resources to aligning what they *are* with what heterogeneous audiences perceive them to be. In the United States, selling, general, and administrative expenses routinely absorb fifteen to thirty percent of revenue [@anderson-2003-are-selling-general]; a substantial share of that line item funds operations whose purpose is interface coherence — advertising, investor relations, recruiting, legal compliance, public relations. Traditional strategy research treats these functional outlays as production inputs or governance costs. The architectural reading developed in Zharnikov [-@zharnikov-2026am-specification-readiness-friction] treats them instead as endogenous consequences of a more fundamental choice: the extent to which the firm's core specification is codified into a versioned, machine-readable, queryable substrate.

When commitments exist only in tacit, fragmented, or human-interpreted form, every interface must be actively pushed into alignment. Marketing campaigns correct misperceptions, investor-relations teams manage narrative drift, human-resources policies compensate for cultural ambiguity, and legal teams police regulatory boundaries. The friction tax is structural. Conversely, when commitments are codified at the substrate level, interfaces can pull coherent renderings on demand; the function layer shrinks from force-applier to monitor. The limiting case is an organization whose specification is sufficiently coherent that artificial-intelligence agents can query it directly, rendering human interface-maintenance functions partially obsolete.

This paper introduces *specification readiness* — the degree to which the firm's substrate is codified, versioned, machine-readable, and queryable at rendering time — as a measurable firm-year strategic construct, and develops the empirical apparatus required to test the theory's predictions at scale. The construct has not previously entered a regression as an independent variable in the strategic-management literature. The closest archival analogs measure related but distinct objects: organization capital from selling, general, and administrative residuals [@eisfeldt-2013-organization-capital-crosssection]; brand capital from advertising flows under perpetual inventory [@belo-2014-brand-capital-firm]; disclosure readability from Fog indices and Loughran-McDonald dictionaries [@li-2008-annual-report-readability; @loughran-2011-when-is-liability; @loughran-2014-measuring-readability-financial]; linguistic complexity in disclosures [@bushee-2018-linguistic-complexity-firm]. None operationalizes the upstream architectural variable that the multi-interface specification model identifies as sequentially prior to all of these.

This paper makes three contributions. First, it introduces and validates a continuous, firm-year measure of specification readiness — the Specification Coherence Index (SCI) — that is conceptually prior to existing constructs of organization capital, brand capital, and disclosure readability, and is observable at scale across the Compustat universe of US public firms 2010–2025. Second, it develops and pre-registers a multi-arm identification template that pairs within-firm linguistic-coherence shifts with staggered codification events (handbook publication, ISO 9001 certification, design-system release, investor-day specification overhaul), regulatory instruments (Sarbanes-Oxley Section 404 phase-in; EEOC reporting mandates), and event studies around advertising-spend cessation. The template is designed for reuse across upstream architectural variables that the friction-tax mechanism predicts should govern functional intensity. Third, it supplies formal mechanism-test evidence — Monte Carlo simulation across a pre-specified parameter grid; regression-identification power simulation under both H₀ and H₁ — that the construct's predicted phase-shift behavior is internally consistent and that the design has the statistical power to detect the pre-registered effect sizes. Together these contributions translate an architectural theory into an empirically testable research program.

These contributions speak to three literatures. Organizational-design scholars have long recognized coupling and the codification of commitments as central [@nonaka-1994-dynamic-theory-organizational; @orton-1990-loosely-coupled-systems; @puranam-2014-whats-new-about] yet have lacked a scalable archival measure of substrate coherence. Text-as-data researchers have advanced political-risk and complexity metrics [@hassan-2019-firmlevel-political-risk; @bushee-2018-linguistic-complexity-firm] but have not isolated year-over-year substrate stability from disclosure-strategy heterogeneity. The rapidly expanding AI-management literature documents heterogeneous returns to adoption [@babina-2024-artificial-intelligence-firm; @felten-2021-occupational-industry-geographic] but under-specifies the organizational preconditions that determine whether AI automates or augments [@raisch-2021-artificial-intelligence-management]. The architectural moderator developed here bridges these streams: it is a substrate-level firm characteristic, measurable from public filings, that predicts both the functional friction tax and the differential return on AI deployment.

## **Theoretical Framework**

***Specification readiness as an empirical construct***

The multi-interface specification model [@zharnikov-2026am-specification-readiness-friction] decomposes organizational output architecture into three layers. A specification substrate **S** ∈ ℝ^d encodes commitments across six ontological tiers: owner intent, business-model logic, legal-entity structure, product and brand definition, operational process codification, and organizational capability and cultural commitment. An interface layer comprises N structurally distinct renderings of **S** for N recipient classes, each characterized by a perception-weight vector **w**_i ∈ ℝ^d that determines which dimensions of **S** are visible to recipient class i. A function layer comprises the interface-maintaining operational capabilities — marketing, investor relations, human resources, legal and compliance, procurement, communications — whose role is to maintain alignment between **S** and the rendered interfaces. The rendering operator R_i(**S**, **w**_i) = ⟨**S**, **w**_i⟩ + ε_i produces the scalar evaluation recipient class i applies to the firm. The coherence condition ‖⟨**S**, **w**_i⟩/‖**w**_i‖ − ⟨**S**, **w**_j⟩/‖**w**_j‖‖ ≤ κ constrains the maximum admissible inter-interface divergence consistent with a single coherent specification.

Specification readiness, in this framework, denotes the degree to which **S** is codified — versioned, machine-readable, queryable by an operator at rendering time — and consistent across the six tiers. The theoretical construct is multidimensional and partially latent. An empirical operationalization must compress this construct into a measurable firm-year scalar without losing the structural property that matters for the predictions: the substitutability of force-application by query-based access to a coherent substrate. Three operationalizations are pursued in this paper. The primary measure is continuous: year-over-year linguistic coherence of the firm's 10-K filing, computed as cosine similarity between bag-of-words or contextual-embedding representations of the Business (Item 1) and Management Discussion and Analysis (Item 7) sections at t and t−1. The secondary measure is sharp and event-based: a four-event Specification Coherence Index (SCI) constructed as the mean of four binary components — employee handbook publicly published; ISO 9001 (or equivalent quality-management) certification active; design-system publicly released; investor-day specification-overhaul event dated within the past three years — yielding SCI ∈ {0, .25, .50, .75, 1.00} at firm-year. The tertiary measure is an exposure-based instrument: SEC comment-letter volume in [t−2, t], which serves as a robustness exposure variable but is explicitly not used as a first-stage instrumental variable for textual coherence (see the Identification Strategy section).

The choice to center the design on the continuous 10-K coherence measure follows from three constraints. The continuous measure exists for the entire Compustat universe of US public firms with non-missing 10-K filings, generating a balanced panel of approximately 25,000–35,000 firm-year observations after the standard analysis-window restriction. The four-event SCI, by contrast, identifies cleanly only ~20–50 firms with publicly verifiable handbook publication events and a similar order of design-system releases; sample-size considerations make this index unsuitable as the primary identification arm but ideal as a robustness check on the continuous measure. The exposure-based instrument is included for the same reason the four-event SCI is included: to demonstrate that the continuous-treatment estimate is not an artifact of any single measurement choice.

Specification readiness is not equivalent to disclosure clarity. Loughran and McDonald [-@loughran-2011-when-is-liability; -@loughran-2014-measuring-readability-financial] develop the canonical financial-text dictionary and the Fog-index readability measures that capture the cognitive burden of parsing a document; Li [-@li-2008-annual-report-readability] anchors the empirical readability literature in 10-K filings; Bushee, Gow, and Taylor [-@bushee-2018-linguistic-complexity-firm] extend the linguistic-complexity construct as a determinant of analyst forecast dispersion and information-environment quality. All three measure properties of the disclosure as text — properties of the rendering, in the language of the present model — not properties of the underlying substrate. Two firms can produce identically readable disclosures from radically different specification substrates; equivalently, a firm with a coherent codified substrate can produce a difficult-to-parse disclosure if its compliance counsel prefers density. The empirical claim defended in this paper is that year-over-year linguistic coherence of the same firm's filings tracks an underlying property of the specification substrate that disclosure-clarity measures do not capture: the stability of the firm's substrate across the year of disclosure. A coherent substrate produces year-over-year similarity above the firm-class baseline; an incoherent or actively-restructuring substrate produces below-baseline similarity even when each individual filing is independently readable.

The textual-treatment empirical lineage anchors on Hassan, Hollander, van Lent, and Tahoun [-@hassan-2019-firmlevel-political-risk], whose firm-level political risk measure derives a continuous, firm-year text-based exposure variable from earnings-call transcripts and demonstrates that the measure predicts firm investment, hiring, and stock-return outcomes after firm-and-year fixed-effects absorption. The text-as-treatment template is now established in the empirical finance and accounting literatures as a workable identification strategy when the textual measure is constructed transparently and validated against external benchmarks. The continuous-SCI construction in this paper follows the same general design pattern: a firm-level text-derived continuous variable, constructed at firm-year frequency from machine-readable archival filings, entering panel regressions as the principal independent variable of interest with firm-and-year fixed-effects absorption. Bushee, Gow, and Taylor [-@bushee-2018-linguistic-complexity-firm] supply the additional organization-relevant textual-identity anchor: their linguistic-complexity measure operates as an organizational variable rather than merely as an information-environment variable, supporting the interpretation of text-derived firm-level measures as proxies for the underlying organizational construct rather than as artifacts of disclosure-strategy choice. The methodological lineage on text-derived organizational measurement is reviewed at length in Hannigan et al. [-@hannigan-2019-topic-modeling-management], whose synthesis of topic-modeling and embedding strategies justifies the choice of contextual transformer embeddings (BERT-base) over simpler dictionary approaches for measuring substrate stability — context-sensitive embeddings recover semantic similarity across paraphrase rewording that dictionary cosine misses. The continuous SCI defended in this paper extends this lineage in one structurally specific direction: the measure is constructed from within-firm year-over-year variation rather than between-firm cross-sectional variation, isolating the substrate-stability signal from the cross-sectional confounds of industry composition, firm size, and disclosure-strategy heterogeneity.

***Multi-interface rendering and the friction tax***

The function-as-friction-tax mechanism predicts that interface-maintaining functional capacity is a structural consequence of specification gaps rather than a flexibly-chosen production input. Anderson, Banker, and Janakiraman [-@anderson-2003-are-selling-general] document empirically that selling, general, and administrative costs are quasi-fixed and asymmetrically sticky — they rise faster with sales increases than they fall with equivalent sales decreases. The multi-interface specification model interprets this asymmetry as a structural consequence of the function layer existing to compensate for specification gaps: when the gap widens, force must be applied to maintain interface coherence; when the gap narrows, the previously-applied force does not retract symmetrically because the organizational structure that generated it remains in place. The theory predicts that specification codification investment that reduces the gap will, with a lag, produce a measurable decline in functional intensity — not merely a slowing of growth but an absolute reduction in interface-maintaining headcount and spend as a share of revenue.

The function-as-friction-tax mechanism inherits its formal lineage from Radner [-@radner-1993-organization-decentralized-information], whose treatment of decentralized information processing establishes the foundation for treating organizational layers as endogenous information-processing costs rather than as exogenous production inputs. Arrow [-@arrow-1974-limits-organization-new] supplies the prior foundation: authority and communication channels are organizational costs that cap feasible organizational complexity. Milgrom and Roberts [-@milgrom-1992-economics] establish complementarities in organizational design — coordination, information, and incentive costs are jointly determined, meaning that specification gaps propagate simultaneously across multiple function layers rather than remaining confined to one. Csaszar and Eggers [-@csaszar-2013-organizational-decision-making] and Csaszar [-@csaszar-2020-certum-quod-factum-formal-models] model organizational decision-making using Shannon-adjacent language. Eisfeldt and Papanikolaou [-@eisfeldt-2013-organization-capital-crosssection] supply the empirical translation: their organization-capital measure constructed from SG&A residuals captures a related but distinct upstream variable — the firm's accumulated investment in organizational structure — which the multi-interface specification model interprets as one observable consequence of specification codification but not as a measure of the substrate itself. The empirical contribution of this paper is to introduce the substrate-level measure that the function-as-friction-tax mechanism predicts should drive both functional intensity and organization-capital accumulation as downstream consequences.

Three interface domains supply the principal empirical traction. The consumer interface is observable through Compustat XAD (advertising expenditure) and the perpetual-inventory brand capital construction of Belo, Lin, and Vitorino [-@belo-2014-brand-capital-firm], with the canonical depreciation rate δ₆ = .50/year. The investor interface is observable through SEC EDGAR filings — 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, proxy statements — and the comment-letter exchange record. The employer-brand interface is observable through Glassdoor employer-perception panels [@green-2019-crowdsourced-employer-reviews] and Burning Glass / Lightcast occupational postings panels [@hershbein-2018-do-recessions-accelerate; @deming-2018-skill-requirements-across], with Burning Glass also supplying the artificial-intelligence-hiring proxy [@babina-2024-artificial-intelligence-firm]. The remaining three canonical interfaces — regulatory-positioning, supplier-partner, and peer-and-media — are observable with greater measurement noise; the design developed in this paper privileges the three best-measured interfaces and treats the remaining three as robustness extensions.

***Boundary conditions and scope***

The theory and its empirical operationalization both have well-defined scope. The empirical design developed here is targeted at the population for which the theory makes determinate predictions and is explicit about the populations for which the predictions are silent or reverse. Table 1 specifies six boundary conditions, each with the operational restriction it places on the empirical sample.

Table 1: Boundary Conditions for the Multi-Interface Specification Model.

| Boundary condition | Operational restriction | Implication for sample | Falsification status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coherence parameter | Theory holds when κ < .15 in the coherence condition across at least three observable interfaces | Firms with single-interface dominance excluded | Excluded firms do not falsify; they are out of scope |
| Firm scale | Theory holds when total full-time-equivalent employee count exceeds approximately 500 | Below-threshold firms excluded from primary sample; included in robustness only | Below-threshold predictions are silent, not falsified |
| Specification codifiability | Theory holds when the firm's core specification is amenable to documentation [@nonaka-1994-dynamic-theory-organizational] | Artisan and bespoke-service firms excluded; tacit-specification industries flagged | Out of scope; not falsified by exclusion |
| Recipient-class non-adversariality | Theory holds when the principal recipient classes are non-adversarial; pull architecture reverses for adversarial classes | Firms with material activist-investor or hostile-regulator exposure flagged separately | Predictions reverse, not falsified |
| Multi-recipient architecture | Theory requires at least two structurally distinct recipient classes | Pure platform firms whose interfaces collapse to API-only excluded from primary sample | Theory falsified in pure-API firms is genuinely falsified |
| AI consumption-layer penetration | Theory's phase-shift predictions apply when consumption-layer artificial-intelligence penetration α ≥ α* ≈ .60 | Pre-AI subwindow tests baseline predictions only; P5/H5 restricted to 2018–2025 | Out-of-window predictions silent, not falsified |

*Notes*: Boundary conditions follow the scope-condition specification in Zharnikov [-@zharnikov-2026am-specification-readiness-friction]. The first three restrictions are exclusionary; the last three are stratifying. The primary analysis sample applies all six restrictions; robustness analyses progressively relax the restrictions and report the effect-size sensitivity. The pure-platform-firm exclusion is the only condition under which the theory is genuinely falsified rather than silent; this exclusion identifies the empirical setting where the model's failure can be diagnosed cleanly.

The boundary table makes explicit a feature of the empirical design that deserves emphasis. The pure-API firm is the location where the multi-interface architecture collapses to a degenerate single-interface case; in this population, the theory predicts that specification codification investment will have no detectable effect on functional friction because there is no function layer to convert. If the empirical design finds the predicted negative association between SCI and functional intensity in the boundary-respecting sample but does not find it in the pure-API subsample, this is consistent with the theory rather than against it. If the predicted association fails to appear in the boundary-respecting sample, the theory is falsified at the primary specification. The boundary conditions thus partition the empirical universe into populations where the theory makes determinate predictions, populations where the theory is silent, and a single population where the theory's failure is interpretable as a falsification.

## **Hypotheses**

The five hypotheses below operationalize propositions P1–P5 from Zharnikov [-@zharnikov-2026am-specification-readiness-friction] as directional empirical predictions. Each hypothesis is stated as a falsifiable claim with a primary proxy, a predicted economic magnitude, and the pre-registered effect size in standardized units. Full operationalization, identification, and threats are developed in the Data and Measurement and Identification Strategy sections.

***H1: Specification readiness reduces functional friction tax***

Within firms, after firm and year fixed-effects absorption, an increase in continuous SCI predicts a decrease in aggregate function-layer spending as a share of revenue. Function-layer spending sums marketing, investor relations, human resources, legal, procurement, and communications spending decomposed from Compustat SG&A where firm-level segment disclosures permit; an SG&A-aggregate variant serves as robustness. The specification-readiness mechanism differs from the sticky-cost mechanism of Anderson, Banker, and Janakiraman [-@anderson-2003-are-selling-general] in identifying the upstream architectural variable — specification substrate codification — as the structural driver of the asymmetry; prior cost-stickiness research treats the asymmetry as an inherent property of SG&A without specifying the organizational cause. *Predicted economic magnitude*: a one-standard-deviation increase in continuous SCI is associated with a decrease of approximately one percentage point in function-layer spending share, equivalent to β ≈ −.08 in standardized regression units. *Pre-registered Cohen's d*: .5. *Directional prediction*: negative. *Falsification rule*: H1 is falsified if SCI is uncorrelated with functional spend share after firm-and-year fixed-effects absorption, after a one-year lag of SCI to address reverse causality, and after the Sarbanes-Oxley and EEOC instrument supplements.

***H2: Specification readiness accelerates brand-capital accumulation***

Conditional on revenue cohort and industry × year cell, an increase in continuous SCI predicts an increase in brand-capital growth net of depreciation. Brand capital is constructed via Belo, Lin, and Vitorino's [-@belo-2014-brand-capital-firm] perpetual-inventory model with canonical depreciation rate δ₆ = .50/year and reported under δ₆ ∈ {.30, .50, .70} for robustness following Peters and Taylor [-@peters-2017-intangible-capital-investmentq]. The theoretical mechanism inverts the H1 claim: as specification codification rises, less of the firm's marketing budget is consumed by interface-coherence-maintenance and more contributes to net brand-capital accumulation. *Predicted economic magnitude*: a one-standard-deviation increase in continuous SCI is associated with an approximately twelve percent acceleration in brand-capital net growth, equivalent to β ≈ −.12 on the inverted (functional-headcount-to-brand-capital) outcome. *Pre-registered Cohen's d*: .3. *Directional prediction*: positive on brand-capital growth; negative on functional headcount per unit brand capital. *Falsification rule*: H2 is falsified if functional-headcount growth predicts brand-capital accumulation positively after IV correction using regulatory-compliance shocks as exogenous functional-expansion instruments.

***H3: Multi-interface incoherence raises cross-stakeholder valuation dispersion***

Firms with documented cross-interface contradictions exhibit higher dispersion in cross-stakeholder valuation metrics than coherent-specification firms, after firm and year fixed effects. The cross-interface contradiction index is the mean pairwise NLP cosine distance across three interface pairs: 10-K Item 1 (Business) versus 10-K Item 7 (MD&A); Glassdoor employer profile versus 10-K MD&A; SEC comment-letter language versus 10-K Item 1. Cross-stakeholder dispersion is the geometric mean of three normalized components: consumer NPS within-firm-year variance from YouGov BrandIndex (or equivalent), Glassdoor rating standard deviation, and I/B/E/S analyst price-target dispersion. *Predicted economic magnitude*: a one-standard-deviation increase in the contradiction index predicts a ten percent increase in normalized cross-stakeholder dispersion, equivalent to β ≈ +.10. *Pre-registered Cohen's d*: .4. *Directional prediction*: positive. *Falsification rule*: H3 is falsified if the contradiction index does not predict cross-stakeholder dispersion after register-calibration validation that distinguishes substantive contradictions from linguistic-register differences. *Sample restriction*: H3 is estimated on single-brand-dominant firms (firms in which one brand accounts for ≥ 70 percent of revenue per Compustat segment disclosures) to address the brand-portfolio confound in firm-level YouGov BrandIndex dispersion, and supplemented with brand-fixed effects in subsamples where the YouGov brand-to-firm crosswalk permits cleaner identification.

***H4: Substrate readiness conditions AI deployment ROI***

Firms with high continuous SCI measured in the year before first major AI deployment exhibit higher AI-ROI realization than firms with low SCI at deployment, controlling for AI spend, industry, and firm size. AI deployment is dated using the Babina, Fedyk, He, and Hodson [-@babina-2024-artificial-intelligence-firm] Burning Glass AI-hiring panel: a firm's first AI-deployment year is the first year in which AI-related job postings exceed two percent of total postings. AI-ROI is operationalized as the change in interface-maintaining functional headcount in [t+1, t+2] divided by AI-related spending in the same window. The theoretical prediction is that Substrate-Operator realization should produce flat or declining interface-maintenance headcount alongside rising AI spend; Surface-Operator deployment should produce rising AI spend without corresponding headcount reduction. *Predicted economic magnitude*: a one-standard-deviation increase in pre-deployment SCI is associated with a fifteen percent increase in the AI-ROI proxy, equivalent to β ≈ +.15. *Pre-registered Cohen's d*: .5. *Directional prediction*: positive. *Falsification rule*: H4 is falsified if pre-deployment SCI does not predict the functional-headcount-to-AI-spend ratio in [t+1, t+2] after IV correction using CEO transitions in [t−3, t−1] as instrument for pre-deployment codification, with the reduced-form CEO-transition effect on AI-ROI reported as placebo. *Sample restriction*: as with H3, H4 restricts to single-brand-dominant firms in the primary specification.

***H5: Pull architecture shrinks function-as-friction-tax in spend cessation***

High-push-intensity firms (top quintile of three-year-average XAD/SALE within Fama-French industry × year cells) exhibit larger negative cumulative abnormal returns in the [−1, +3 years] window following advertising-spend-cessation events than low-push-intensity firms (bottom quintile), controlling for category, firm size, and macroeconomic conditions. Spend cessation is identified as a year in which XAD falls by more than thirty percent from prior year without a corresponding total-asset decline exceeding ten percent. The Fama-French three-factor model is estimated on the [−5, −2 years] pre-event window. *Predicted economic magnitude*: the cumulative abnormal return differential between high-push and low-push firms is approximately fifteen percentage points in the [0, +1 year] window post-cessation, equivalent to Cohen's d ≈ .7. *Pre-registered effect size*: CAR differential = −15 pp; Cohen's d = .7. *Directional prediction*: negative for high-push relative to low-push. *Falsification rule*: H5 is falsified if high-push-intensity firms do not show larger negative CARs than low-push-intensity firms following spend cessation after distress-event exclusion (firms with > 10% total asset decline in the cessation year removed) and propensity-score reweighting on pre-event fundamentals.

The five hypotheses jointly operationalize the theory's central architectural claim. H1 tests whether codification reduces interface-maintenance intensity. H2 tests whether codification redirects marketing spend from interface-coherence-maintenance to brand-capital accumulation. H3 tests whether incoherence in the substrate propagates to cross-stakeholder valuation. H4 tests whether substrate readiness moderates the return on AI augmentation. H5 tests whether push-dependent firms collapse faster than pull-capable firms when force is withdrawn. The joint failure of all five would falsify the theory at the primary specification; the failure of any one identifies a scope or measurement boundary requiring theoretical refinement.

## **Data and Measurement**

***Sample construction***

The analysis window 2010–2025 is chosen to span both the pre-AI baseline and the AI-adoption diffusion period. The 2010 lower bound aligns with three operational requirements. Machine-readable 10-K filings via SEC EDGAR XBRL are reliably available from 2009 forward; the one-year lag for year-over-year coherence computation moves the effective sample start to 2010. Glassdoor employee-perception coverage stabilizes around 2010 for the firms with sufficient review density to support firm-year panel use [@green-2019-crowdsourced-employer-reviews]. LinkedIn Economic Graph and Burning Glass / Lightcast occupational-postings coverage stabilizes around 2010 for the panel of firms of interest. The 2018–2025 sub-window carries the AI-deployment analyses (H4) where the Babina, Fedyk, He, and Hodson [-@babina-2024-artificial-intelligence-firm] Burning Glass AI-hiring panel provides the AI-deployment treatment timing; H1, H2, H3, and H5 use the full 2010–2025 window.

The analysis universe is US public firms with at least five consecutive years of non-missing Compustat XAD, SG&A, and SALE between 2010 and 2025. The five-year minimum supports within-firm variation in the firm-fixed-effects specification; the 2010 lower bound aligns with the diffusion of machine-readable 10-K filings sufficient for NLP-based coherence measurement; the 2025 upper bound is the most recent fiscal year with audited Compustat data. The expected sample is approximately 2,500–3,500 unique firms × 10–15 years = 25,000–35,000 firm-year observations before exclusions. Application of the boundary conditions in Table 1 reduces the primary-analysis sample by approximately twenty percent: pure-platform firms excluded; below-500-employee firms excluded; firms in artisan and bespoke-service NAICS codes flagged for separate robustness. The expected primary-analysis sample is therefore approximately 20,000–28,000 firm-year observations — comfortably above the regression-identification simulation's N = 1,000 firms × 10 years = 10,000 firm-year benchmark at which all five hypotheses achieve simulation power ≥ .80.

***Specification Coherence Index (SCI)***

The continuous-SCI primary measure is constructed in three steps. First, each firm's 10-K filing for year t is parsed via SEC EDGAR XBRL into its Item 1 (Business) and Item 7 (MD&A) sections; non-narrative content (financial tables, exhibits, references) is stripped. Second, contextual embeddings of each section are computed using a pretrained transformer (the BERT-base-uncased model is the primary; the Loughran and McDonald [-@loughran-2014-measuring-readability-financial] dictionary cosine is the secondary). Third, cosine similarity between the firm's t-section embedding and its t−1-section embedding produces the year-over-year continuous SCI: SCI_continuous_{i,t} = cos(emb(Item7_{i,t}), emb(Item7_{i,t−1})) ∈ [−1, 1], rescaled to [0, 1]. The within-firm-year-over-year similarity captures the stability of the firm's substrate across the year; the between-firm-year-over-year similarity captures the firm's position relative to its industry peers. Both variants are computed; the primary specification uses the within-firm-year-over-year similarity to align with the firm-fixed-effects identification.

The sharp four-event SCI is constructed from manual coding of four binary components, each dated to firm-year from public corporate communications and Wayback Machine archival evidence: (a) employee handbook publicly published as of year t (GitLab handbook 2014; Basecamp/37signals handbook public-publication date; Stripe API specification public release dates; analogous events); (b) ISO 9001 (or equivalent quality-management system) certification active as of year t (verifiable from registrar databases); (c) design-system publicly released as of year t (Material Design 2014; Carbon 2017; Polaris 2017; Lightning; analogous events); (d) investor-day specification-overhaul event dated within [t−2, t] (verifiable from SEC 8-K and investor-day transcripts). SCI_sharp_{i,t} = (a + b + c + d) / 4 ∈ {0, .25, .50, .75, 1.00}. The sharp index is the robustness arm; the continuous measure is primary.

The SEC comment-letter exposure variable is constructed as the count of SEC comment letters received in [t−2, t], normalized by firm-size quintile within industry-year. This variable is included as an exposure-based robustness regressor, not as a first-stage instrumental variable for SCI. The exclusion restriction required for IV use of comment-letter volume — that comment-letter receipt affect outcomes only through the substrate-coherence channel — is contestable: SEC review plausibly affects firms through remediation costs, managerial time, internal-control changes, market perceptions, and governance actions independent of any textual-coherence channel. The variable is therefore used only as an exposure regressor in robustness specifications.

***Functional friction tax proxies***

H1's dependent variable is aggregate function-layer spending as a share of revenue. The primary construction sums marketing (Compustat XAD), legal (Compustat XSGA-derived where firm segment disclosure permits, otherwise imputed from industry-mean legal-spend ratio), human-resources spending (imputed from Burning Glass / Lightcast headcount fractions multiplied by industry-mean fully-loaded labor cost), investor-relations spending (estimated from IR-team headcount per LinkedIn data multiplied by industry mean), procurement spending (from Capital IQ segment disclosures where available), and communications/PR spending (from Capital IQ where available, otherwise the analogous Burning Glass imputation). The aggregate is normalized by SALE. Because firm-level decomposition of SG&A into these six function categories is imprecise, an SG&A-aggregate variant — total SG&A net of R&D, divided by SALE — is reported as the primary robustness check.

***Brand-capital stock***

H2's dependent variable is brand-capital stock constructed via Belo, Lin, and Vitorino's [-@belo-2014-brand-capital-firm] perpetual-inventory model: Brand_Capital_{i,t} = (1 − δ₆) × Brand_Capital_{i,t−1} + XAD_{i,t}, with initial-year value set at the industry-median XAD/(δ₆ + g_industry) following standard practice [@peters-2017-intangible-capital-investmentq]. The canonical depreciation rate δ₆ = .50/year is the primary; δ₆ ∈ {.30, .50, .70} robustness is reported. Brand-capital net growth is the year-over-year percent change after subtracting the depreciation flow: Δ_net_{i,t} = (Brand_Capital_{i,t} − (1 − δ₆) × Brand_Capital_{i,t−1}) / Brand_Capital_{i,t−1}. The post-Belo brand-capital literature [@daniel-2020-another-look-at; @eisfeldt-2020-intangible-value; @belo-2021-external-equity-financing] confirms the perpetual-inventory construction as the canonical archival measure of brand capital. Mizik and Jacobson's [-@mizik-2008-financial-value-relevance] analysis of the financial-value impact of Y&R BAV perceptual brand attributes supports the construct validity of advertising-flow brand capital as a proxy for the perceptual brand-stock construct that the model's interface layer identifies.

The continuous-SCI primary measure differs structurally from the closest text-based disclosure construct in the accounting literature. The linguistic-complexity measure of Bushee, Gow, and Taylor [-@bushee-2018-linguistic-complexity-firm] captures analyst-forecast-dispersion effects of disclosure density — a cross-sectional information-environment property of a single filing; the continuous SCI captures within-firm year-over-year substrate stability — a temporal property of the firm orthogonal to any single filing's complexity level. Two firms with identically high linguistic complexity can exhibit divergent year-over-year coherence; equivalently, a firm with stable substrate can produce filings of varying complexity across years depending on counsel preference. The two measures address different theoretical constructs and are expected to load on different downstream outcomes.

***Cross-stakeholder dispersion***

H3's dependent variable is the geometric mean of three normalized within-firm-year dispersion components. The consumer-NPS variance component is the within-firm-year variance of YouGov BrandIndex consumer-impression scores. The Glassdoor rating-dispersion component is the standard deviation of Glassdoor employee reviews within firm-year. The analyst-dispersion component is the standard deviation of I/B/E/S analyst price targets within firm-year, scaled by mean price target. Each component is normalized by industry × year cell to remove industry-level baseline dispersion; the three components are combined via geometric mean to weight each interface dispersion equally. The cross-interface contradiction index is the mean pairwise NLP cosine distance across the three interface pairs specified above; embeddings use the same BERT-base-uncased model used for the continuous-SCI construction, with Loughran-McDonald dictionary cosine as the robustness variant. Hassan, Hollander, van Lent, and Tahoun [-@hassan-2019-firmlevel-political-risk] provide the canonical template for text-derived firm-level panel exposure variables; the contradiction-index construction follows the same general design pattern.

***AI deployment proxies***

H4's treatment is the year of first major AI deployment, dated using two complementary sources. The primary source is Babina, Fedyk, He, and Hodson [-@babina-2024-artificial-intelligence-firm], whose Burning Glass / Lightcast AI-hiring panel identifies firm-year AI-related job-posting counts and intensity. The secondary source is the Alekseeva, Azar, Giné, and Samila [-@alekseeva-2026-artificial-intelligence-adoption] AI-adoption indicator, which provides cross-firm validation. AI deployment is dated as the first year in which AI-related postings exceed two percent of total postings, conditional on at least ten total postings in that year. AI-related spending is constructed from a combination of (a) AI-related job postings × industry-mean fully-loaded AI-engineer compensation, (b) explicit AI-related expenditures disclosed in 10-K Item 7 MD&A sections (extracted via keyword search and manual validation), and (c) hardware-and-cloud expenditures attributable to AI inferred from CapEx growth in cloud-services line items where firm segment disclosures permit. The AI-ROI proxy is the change in interface-maintaining functional headcount in [t+1, t+2] divided by AI-related spending in the same window — operationally, the inverse of the marginal labor displacement per dollar of AI investment.

## **Identification Strategy**

***Continuous 10-K coherence as primary treatment***

The primary identification design treats continuous SCI as a within-firm continuous treatment with firm and year fixed effects absorbing time-invariant firm characteristics and aggregate shocks. The estimating equation for H1 is

> y_{i,t} = β · SCI_continuous_{i,t−1} + γ · X_{i,t} + α_i + δ_t + θ_{c(i),t} + ε_{i,t}

where y_{i,t} is the function-layer spending share, X_{i,t} is a vector of firm-year controls (log revenue, log total assets, log employment, the lagged dependent variable), α_i is a firm fixed effect, δ_t is a year fixed effect, θ_{c(i),t} is a Fama-French 48-industry × year fixed effect, and ε_{i,t} is the residual clustered at the firm level. SCI is lagged one year to address simultaneity. Standard errors are clustered at the firm level following the post-hoc deviation remediation specified in the Results section.

This continuous-treatment specification is the primary identification arm. It exploits within-firm year-over-year variation in linguistic coherence after absorbing all stable firm characteristics and all aggregate-industry shocks. The identifying variation is the firm's substrate-stability shift from t−1 to t, net of industry-wide shifts. The principal threat to this specification is omitted variable bias from time-varying firm quality: a high-quality management team plausibly both increases substrate codification and reduces functional friction through unobserved channels. This threat is addressed by the Oster [-@oster-2019-unobservable-selection-coefficient] bound analysis reported in the threats register and by the IV supplement specified below.

***Staggered DiD with Callaway-Sant'Anna and Goodman-Bacon***

The robustness identification arm exploits staggered adoption of the four sharp SCI components — handbook publication, ISO 9001 certification, design-system release, investor-day specification-overhaul — as quasi-natural experiments. Treatment timing varies across firms because adoption is staggered; the conventional two-way fixed-effects DiD estimator produces biased estimates under heterogeneous treatment effects [@goodmanbacon-2021-differenceindifferences-with-variation; @de-2020-twoway-fixed-effects]. The estimator of choice is the Callaway and Sant'Anna [-@callaway-2021-differenceindifferences-with-multiple] group-time average treatment effect on the treated (ATT(g,t)), aggregated to an event-study profile and a single summary ATT.

Choudhury, Foroughi, and Larson [-@choudhury-2021-work-from-anywhere] provide one of the cleanest strategy-field precedents for the Callaway-Sant'Anna estimator applied to staggered-policy DiD: their work-from-anywhere natural experiment at the US Patent and Trademark Office exploits examiner assignment as the source of exogenous variation, establishing that within-firm quasi-random policy adoption can identify causal productivity effects in the strategy context. The handbook-publication arm of the sharp SCI follows an analogous identification logic — the public release date is verifiable, staggered across firms, and plausibly exogenous to short-run outcome variation. Choudhury, Starr, and Agarwal [-@choudhury-2022-machine-learning-human] extend the strategy-field staggered-DiD repertoire with within-firm worker-mobility shocks; their use of Callaway-Sant'Anna alongside event-study aggregation supplies the secondary strategy-research precedent for the estimator choice here. Christensen, Hail, and Leuz [-@christensen-2021-mandatory-csr-sustainability] supply the broader evidence base for staggered disclosure-mandate DiD in accounting. Cumming, Johan, and Li [-@cumming-2011-exchange-trading-rules], Gao, Lisic, and Zhang [-@gao-2014-commitment-social-good], and Christensen, Hail, and Leuz [-@christensen-2021-mandatory-csr-sustainability] collectively establish the precedent class for formal-policy adoption as staggered DiD treatment, extending naturally to handbook and design-system release events as organizational-codification analogs. The organizational-design microfoundations literature [@puranam-2014-whats-new-about] anchors the substantive interpretation: the four sharp SCI components are observable codifications of the division-of-labor and integration choices that Puranam et al. identify as the microfoundational unit of organizational design.

The platform-and-standards literature supplies analogous precedents for treating publicly-released technical artifacts as staggered organizational shocks. Boudreau [-@boudreau-2012-let-thousand-flowers] uses iOS SDK / platform openings as natural experiments; Leyden [-@leyden-2026-sherlocking-platform-entry] treats platform-owner entry ('Sherlocking') as a quasi-experimental DiD shock, supplying the most direct platform-policy-as-treatment precedent for the design-system-release component. The design-system-release component of the sharp four-event SCI maps cleanly onto this precedent class: Material Design, Carbon, Polaris, and analogous design-system public releases are publicly-dated technical-standard publications that constitute observable codification events at the firm-year level. The open-source-publication literature supplies a closely-related identification template: Boudreau and Lakhani [-@boudreau-2015-open-disclosure-innovations] treat open-disclosure events as appropriation-strategy shocks; Nagle [-@nagle-2018-learning-by-contributing] treats a firm's first open-source-software contribution date as an organizational treatment. The handbook-publication component of the sharp four-event SCI extends this class to internal codification artifacts (GitLab handbook 2014; Basecamp / 37signals handbook publication; Stripe API specification public release) whose publicly-dated release supplies the staggered-treatment timing variation.

Every staggered-DiD specification reported in this paper reports the Goodman-Bacon [-@goodmanbacon-2021-differenceindifferences-with-variation] decomposition alongside the Callaway-Sant'Anna estimate. The Goodman-Bacon decomposition partitions the conventional two-way fixed-effects DiD estimate into weighted components corresponding to (a) treatment-versus-never-treated contrasts, (b) earlier-versus-later-treated contrasts, and (c) later-versus-earlier-treated "forbidden comparisons" that generate the bias. Reporting the decomposition transparently identifies the source of any divergence between the conventional and Callaway-Sant'Anna estimates and supports the interpretation of the heterogeneous treatment effects. Sun and Abraham [-@sun-2021-estimating-dynamic-treatment] estimators are reported as a secondary robustness check. Placebo leads and lags are reported for every DiD specification to test for anticipation effects.

***Instrumental-variable supplement***

The instrumental-variable supplement addresses the principal threat to the continuous-treatment specification: time-varying unobserved firm quality that drives both substrate codification and downstream outcomes through unmodeled channels. Two regulatory-compliance shocks supply candidate instruments for exogenous variation in function-layer headcount that is uncorrelated with specification readiness conditional on industry × year. The Sarbanes-Oxley Section 404 compliance year — staggered across firms by accelerated-filer status and by firm-size threshold — supplies an exogenous expansion in legal-function and internal-audit-function headcount that is mandatory regardless of firm strategy. EEOC reporting mandate years supply analogous exogenous variation in human-resources-function expansion. Both shocks satisfy the relevance condition (substantial first-stage F-statistics on functional headcount in the post-shock window) and a defensible exclusion restriction (regulatory compliance affects functional headcount mandatorily, with no necessary channel through specification codification quality, conditional on industry × year controls). H4 uses CEO transitions in [t−3, t−1] as an instrument for pre-deployment specification codification, exploiting the documented association between CEO transitions and strategic-reset codification initiatives. The CEO-transition exclusion restriction is the most contestable in the design; the reduced-form CEO-transition effect on AI-ROI is reported as placebo to support the exclusion-restriction defense, and the H4 estimates are reported with appropriate caveats. Both regulatory-shock instruments and the CEO-transition instrument are reported with first-stage F-statistics, over-identification tests where multiple instruments are available, and Anderson-Rubin confidence intervals robust to weak-instrument concerns.

***Robustness: four-event SCI + comment-letter exposure***

The four-event sharp SCI moves to the robustness arm. Each sharp-SCI component is treated as a staggered-DiD treatment in its own specification, with the Callaway-Sant'Anna estimator and Goodman-Bacon decomposition. The four-event composite is also estimated as a single staggered treatment using the year of first component-event adoption as the cohort assignment. The expected pattern is that the four-event-composite estimate aligns directionally with the continuous-SCI estimate but with larger standard errors due to the smaller treated sample. The SEC comment-letter exposure variable enters the robustness specifications as an additional time-varying control; under the H1 specification, the prediction is that the SCI coefficient is robust to controlling for comment-letter exposure, ruling out the alternative interpretation that the SCI-friction-tax association is mediated entirely by SEC regulatory pressure rather than substrate codification.

Five additional robustness checks are pre-registered. First, the brand-capital depreciation rate is varied across δ₆ ∈ {.30, .50, .70} following Peters and Taylor [-@peters-2017-intangible-capital-investmentq]. Second, alternative NLP embeddings (Loughran-McDonald dictionary cosine versus BERT-base-uncased) are reported in parallel for all coherence measurements. Third, a tech-firm subsample exclusion (NAICS 5112, 5182, 5191, 5415) tests sensitivity to selection on technical sophistication. Fourth, the analysis is restricted to single-brand-dominant firms for H3 and H4 to address the brand-FE confound. Fifth, a placebo treatment using a randomly-assigned coherence shift date is reported for the staggered-DiD specifications to confirm the absence of mechanical event-time clustering effects.

## **Mechanism Tests**

This section reports two formal mechanism tests of the theoretical model: a Monte Carlo simulation that operationalizes the friction-tax phase-shift prediction across a pre-specified parameter grid, and a regression-identification simulation that establishes the design's statistical power at the pre-registered effect sizes under H₁ and nominal Type I error coverage under H₀. Both tests were generated by the companion code repository at fixed seed 20260525 under the pre-registration anchored in the Methods Appendix. The Monte Carlo confirms that the theoretical mechanism is internally consistent across the relevant parameter range; the regression-identification simulation confirms that the multi-arm identification template developed in the preceding section can detect the predicted effects at the analysis-window sample size. Archival panel estimates implementing the design are in execution as a companion empirical paper.

***Monte Carlo phase shift confirmation***

The Monte Carlo simulation operationalizes the friction-tax mechanism over a parameter grid spanning misalignment variance σ ∈ {.1, .2, .3, .5, .7, 1.0}, recipient population size N ∈ {100, 500, 1000, 5000}, AI-mediation factor α ∈ {0, .2, .4, .6, .8, 1.0}, and three friction functional forms (quadratic, L1, log-quadratic). The simulation executes 10,000 trials per parameter cell across 432 cells, producing 4.32 million friction-magnitude samples; combined with 30,000 phase-shift sensitivity trials at six α values, the total trial count is 12.96 million. At the baseline parameter cell (σ = .3, N = 1,000, α = 0, quadratic norm), the push friction-tax μ_push = 1.4396 tracks the analytic expectation 2σ²d = 1.4400 to four significant figures. The pull friction-tax μ_pull = .0000720 corresponds to targeted queries with σ_query = .003 (one percent of push noise). The ratio μ_push / μ_pull = 19,993; Cohen's d for the contrast is 88.4, exceeding the pre-registered floor of 1.0 by approximately two orders of magnitude.

The phase-shift threshold α* — the AI-mediation parameter value at which the pull regime becomes dominant — equals .91 at σ = .3, within the pre-registered interval [.85, .95] and within .01 of the analytic prediction .90. The phase-shift threshold is stable across the four tested σ values: α* = .91 at σ = .1, σ = .3, σ = .5, and σ = .7. All six pre-registered falsification checks pass: the qualitative ordering μ_push >> μ_pull holds across all parameter cells; the directional prediction ∂(C_push − C_pull)/∂c > 0 holds at every interior point; the dimensionality robustness check confirms the result is not artifactual at d = 2, 8, or 16; the pull-friction floor confirms the result is not driven by an artificially-zero σ_query; the functional-form robustness confirms the result across L1 and log-quadratic norms; the N-scaling check confirms the result is not driven by small-sample variance. The full PASS/FAIL evaluation against the pre-registered criteria is logged in [`POST_EXPERIMENT_REPORT.md`](https://github.com/spectralbranding/orgschema-papers/blob/main/specification-readiness/code/POST_EXPERIMENT_REPORT.md).

These results confirm the formalism's internal consistency across the specified parameter grid. They do not constitute empirical evidence in real firms; real-firm confirmation requires the archival panel study whose design is specified in the preceding sections.

***Regression-identification simulation power***

The regression-identification simulation generates 1,000 simulated datasets per hypothesis under both H₀ (no effect) and H₁ (pre-registered effect). Each dataset contains N = 1,000 firms × 10 years = 10,000 firm-year observations with a panel data-generating process incorporating firm random effects, year shocks, industry × year shocks, the pre-registered treatment effect, and residual noise calibrated to match observed within-firm variation in the Compustat-XAD/SALE distribution. The pre-registered effect sizes are β = −.08 (H1), β = −.12 (H2), CAR differential = −15 pp (H3 mapped to H5), β = +.10 (H3 contradiction index → dispersion), β = +.15 (H4). All estimators use firm-clustered standard errors as the primary specification per the post-hoc deviation remediation discussed below.

Power under H₁ at the pre-registered effect sizes is reported in Table 3.

***Post-hoc deviation transparency***

One post-hoc deviation from the pre-registration is documented. The H₀ simulation — designed to confirm nominal Type I error at α = .05 — surfaced Type I error inflation in the range .053–.091 across H2 through H5 specifications. The H1 primary specification passed nominal Type I (rate = .042). Diagnostic analysis traced the inflation to a panel-DGP / classical-OLS interaction: the data-generating process incorporates firm-level random effects (consistent with the within-firm correlation structure of real Compustat panels), but the fast NumPy OLS implementation used in the H₀ simulation omitted firm-clustered standard errors, generating mechanically-anti-conservative variance estimates. The pre-registered remediation is the use of firm-clustered cluster-robust standard errors as the primary estimator in the empirical study, which resolves the inflation at the cost of approximately fifteen percent power reduction at the pre-registered effect sizes. The remediated H1–H5 power estimates with cluster-robust SEs all remain above .80 at the pre-registered effects. This deviation is logged transparently as a simulation-implementation artifact, not a defect in the empirical design. The H₀ simulation under the remediated estimator returns Type I error in [.042, .051] across H1–H5, recovering nominal coverage. The full diagnostic record and remediation log are in [`POST_EXPERIMENT_REPORT.md`](https://github.com/spectralbranding/orgschema-papers/blob/main/specification-readiness/code/POST_EXPERIMENT_REPORT.md).

A note on interpretation. The simulation results reported above confirm that the design has sufficient statistical power to detect the pre-registered effects at the analysis-window sample size, under the panel data-generating process calibrated to observed Compustat within-firm variation. They do not constitute archival evidence about the truth of the hypotheses in real firms. The mechanism tests establish a necessary condition for credible inference — the design can detect the pre-registered effects if they are present — that pre-registration of point estimates alone does not enforce; the companion empirical paper implementing the design will establish whether the effects are present at all. The separation of these two questions — can the design detect the effects, and are the effects there — is a discipline that pre-registered mechanism testing makes routine.

## **Discussion**

***Theoretical contributions***

The paper advances organizational architecture theory in three structurally distinct ways. First, it introduces specification readiness as a continuous, firm-year measurable construct that prior literature has not had — within-firm year-over-year textual coherence in 10-K filings as a scalable signal of substrate stability. This measurement move is a necessary condition for any empirical engagement with theories that treat the codification of organizational commitments as a strategic variable rather than as an administrative outcome of governance. The construct is conceptually prior to existing strategic-measurement constructs: organization capital [@eisfeldt-2013-organization-capital-crosssection] measures the accumulated investment in the structure that maintains the substrate, brand capital [@belo-2014-brand-capital-firm] measures one downstream consequence of substrate coherence in the consumer-interface rendering, and disclosure readability [@li-2008-annual-report-readability; @loughran-2014-measuring-readability-financial] measures a property of the rendering rather than of the substrate. Specification readiness is the upstream architectural variable that this paper makes observable at scale across the Compustat universe and in retrospect from existing public-filing archives back to 1994.

Second, the paper formalizes the substitutability of specification capital for functional force as a testable architectural claim. The function-as-friction-tax mechanism has been theorized for at least three decades — Radner [-@radner-1993-organization-decentralized-information] supplies the original information-processing formalization; Anderson, Banker, and Janakiraman [-@anderson-2003-are-selling-general] supply the empirical demonstration that SG&A is quasi-fixed and asymmetrically sticky; Eisfeldt and Papanikolaou [-@eisfeldt-2013-organization-capital-crosssection] supply the organization-capital interpretation of the SG&A residual. What has been missing is identification of the upstream architectural variable that the mechanism predicts should drive functional intensity. By naming specification readiness as that variable and operationalizing it at firm-year frequency, the design makes the substitutability claim testable in panel form: the continuous-SCI primary arm tests the within-firm year-over-year substitution; the four-event sharp arm tests the discrete codification shock; the joint pattern of estimates across the two arms supports causal inference that neither arm alone could establish.

Third, the paper identifies the pure-API platform firm as the critical falsification case for the theory. Boundary Table 1 makes the distinction explicit: in the pure-API population the interface layer collapses to a single rendering, the function-as-friction-tax mechanism predicts no detectable effect of substrate codification on functional intensity (because there is no function layer to convert), and the failure of the prediction in the boundary-respecting sample would constitute genuine falsification. The clean separation of populations where the theory is silent, where the theory is stratifying, and where the theory's failure would falsify it is a structural feature of the design that the boundary table operationalizes.

***Methodological contribution***

The identification template developed in this paper supplies a reusable toolkit for friction-tax research that the field can adopt across other upstream-architectural variables: governance codification, process codification, knowledge-management codification. The combination of continuous-treatment within-firm variation, staggered-DiD with the Callaway-Sant'Anna estimator and the Goodman-Bacon decomposition, regulatory-compliance IV supplements, and event-study analysis around advertising-spend cessation pairs continuous and sharp identification within a single design [@choudhury-2021-work-from-anywhere; @choudhury-2022-machine-learning-human]. Each arm is exposed to different identification threats — the continuous arm to time-varying unobserved firm quality, the sharp arm to selection-into-codification-events — and the joint pattern across the two arms supports causal inference that neither arm alone could establish. The explicit threats register and the pre-registered Oster [-@oster-2019-unobservable-selection-coefficient] δ-bound diagnostic discipline complement the design: a coefficient that survives at δ = 1 (the convention that unobservables are no more correlated with treatment than the included observables) is robust to plausible omitted-variable bias; a coefficient requiring δ > 1 to be overturned is reported with the corresponding caveat. The discipline of reporting the δ-bound diagnostic for every primary specification, paired with the public code companion, is offered as a methodological complement to the pre-registered design.

***Substrate readiness as moderator of AI returns***

The paper engages the artificial-intelligence-augmentation literature on its own terms by operationalizing substrate readiness as a moderator of realized AI deployment ROI. The closest empirical literature — Babina, Fedyk, He, and Hodson [-@babina-2024-artificial-intelligence-firm]; Alekseeva, Azar, Giné, and Samila [-@alekseeva-2026-artificial-intelligence-adoption]; Felten, Raj, and Seamans [-@felten-2021-occupational-industry-geographic] — measures AI adoption as a treatment variable and tests for effects on firm outcomes. H4 inverts the empirical lens: the firm-side variable that moderates AI ROI is itself identifiable and measurable, and the design tests whether substrate readiness measured in the year before deployment predicts the marginal labor displacement per dollar of AI investment in the two years after. This is the architectural moderator that Raisch and Krakowski [-@raisch-2021-artificial-intelligence-management] identify as the unresolved problem in the automation-augmentation paradox but do not specify at the firm-level construct. The complementarity between management practices and technology adoption documented in Bloom et al. [-@bloom-2019-what-drives-differences] supplies the broader logic: codification investment is a complement to AI deployment, not a substitute, and firms that invest in the substrate before scaling AI capture the labor-displacement gains while firms that do not absorb the spend without the gain.

***Boundary conditions and limitations***

The empirical design has three principal limitations. The first is the partial coverage of the Compustat XAD variable, which is non-missing for approximately 25–50 percent of firm-years and is concentrated in consumer-facing sectors [@grullon-2004-advertising-breadth-ownership; @mcalister-2007-advertising-research-development]. This restricts H2 and H5 to the XAD-observed subsample and requires Heckman-style sample-selection correction in the H1 specifications that condition on functional-spending decomposition. The Heckman correction is pre-registered; sensitivity to the selection equation is reported as part of the robustness battery. The second is the cross-sectional sparsity of the four-event sharp SCI, which limits the sharp-treatment arm to a small subsample of firms with publicly verifiable codification events. The continuous-SCI primary arm addresses this by scaling to the full Compustat universe, but the two arms are estimating somewhat different parameters: the continuous arm estimates the average within-firm year-over-year coherence shift effect; the sharp arm estimates the average treatment effect on the treated of a sharp codification event. Convergence of the two estimates supports causal inference; divergence requires investigation of the differential treatment populations.

The third is the inherent measurement noise in the cross-interface contradiction index (H3) and the AI-deployment-dating (H4). The contradiction index combines three pairwise NLP cosine distances, each of which may capture linguistic-register differences rather than substantive contradictions; the register-calibration validation pre-registered for the threats register addresses this concern but cannot eliminate it. The AI-deployment dating from Burning Glass / Lightcast job-posting data is a proxy for first major deployment; firms may deploy AI internally before exposing the deployment in hiring patterns, and the two-percent posting threshold is conventional but arbitrary. Both measurement-noise threats reduce statistical power in the direction of the null; the mechanism-test evidence above suggests that the pre-registered effect sizes remain detectable at the design's expected sample size, but real-data noise levels may differ from the simulation calibration. The companion empirical paper implementing the design will report measurement-noise sensitivity as part of the archival analysis.

The boundary conditions in Table 1 specify the populations for which the theory makes determinate predictions. The pure-platform firm is the population where the theory's failure would constitute genuine falsification rather than out-of-scope silence; this is the cleanest falsification venue. The artisan and bespoke-service population is the population where the theory predicts permanent push-dependence as a structural consequence of non-codifiable specifications; this is testable through cross-industry comparison of function-layer spend ratios.

The design's external validity is bounded by the US-public-firm setting. The Compustat universe excludes private firms, foreign-listed firms, and the substantial mid-market segment that does not file with the SEC. The continuous-SCI measure relies on 10-K filings, an artifact of US securities regulation; analogous measures for foreign-listed firms would require analogous machine-readable disclosure infrastructures (UK annual reports, EU non-financial reporting directive disclosures, Japan kessan-tanshin filings) and would require independent validation that the year-over-year coherence signal carries the same construct meaning across regulatory regimes. The four-event sharp SCI is similarly US-firm-anchored: handbook publication, ISO 9001 certification, design-system release, and investor-day specification-overhaul events are observable in the US setting but require institutional translation for non-US samples. The design is offered as a US-public-firm empirical research program; cross-country extension is future work that requires institutional knowledge beyond the scope of this paper.

A final limitation worth flagging is the absence of direct measurement of perception-geometry vectors **w**_i. The empirical design treats the perception-weight vectors as latent and identifies the model's predictions through their downstream consequences (functional friction, brand-capital accumulation, cross-stakeholder dispersion). Direct measurement of **w**_i would require experimental methods — conjoint-analysis profiles of recipient classes responding to common specification stimuli — that fall outside the archival empirical framework defended here. Such direct measurement is a complementary empirical contribution that would substantially strengthen the joint test of the multi-interface specification model, and it is flagged as a priority for future empirical work. The archival design's claim is that the theory's downstream predictions can be tested without direct measurement of the latent **w**_i; the experimental complement would test the construct validity of the **w**_i vectors themselves.

***Future-research agenda***

Real archival panel analyses implementing the design specified here are in execution as a companion empirical paper. That paper will report first-stage F-statistics on the regulatory-compliance instruments, Oster δ-bounds on the continuous-treatment specifications, Callaway-Sant'Anna event-study estimates for the four sharp codification events, and the realized effect sizes for H1–H5 on the Compustat / EDGAR / Glassdoor / Burning Glass panel. Cross-country extension to UK, EU, and Japan disclosure regimes will require institutional translation of the sharp four-event SCI components and independent validation of the within-firm year-over-year coherence signal across regulatory contexts. The experimental complement that would directly measure perception-weight vectors **w**_i via conjoint analysis is a parallel research stream rather than a successor.

## **Conclusion**

This paper introduces specification readiness as a measurable, strategically consequential architectural property of the firm. The continuous Specification Coherence Index — within-firm year-over-year cosine similarity of 10-K narrative embeddings — is the first scalable archival measure of substrate codification, observable across the Compustat universe of US public firms and conceptually prior to organization capital, brand capital, and disclosure-readability constructs. The four-event sharp SCI supplies the discrete robustness arm. Five hypotheses link the construct to functional friction, brand-capital accumulation, cross-stakeholder valuation dispersion, AI deployment returns, and the valuation consequence of advertising-spend cessation; each is stated with directional prediction, pre-registered economic magnitude, and falsification rule. The multi-arm identification strategy pairs continuous-treatment within-firm regression with staggered difference-in-differences (Callaway-Sant'Anna; Goodman-Bacon decomposition), regulatory-compliance instruments, and event-study analysis, anchored by an explicit threats register and Oster [-@oster-2019-unobservable-selection-coefficient] bound diagnostics.

Three contributions follow. The first is the SCI construct — a new strategic variable that prior literature has treated as theoretical but not measured. The second is the multi-arm identification template — a reusable toolkit pairing continuous textual treatment with sharp codification events, designed for extension to governance, process, and knowledge-management codification research. The third is the formal mechanism-test evidence — Monte Carlo simulation (12.96 million trials; α* = .91; Cohen's d = 88.4) confirming the friction-tax phase shift, and regression-identification simulation confirming statistical power ≥ .80 for all five hypotheses at the pre-registered effect sizes — that substrate readiness moderates the augmentation paradox in a manner consistent with the multi-interface model. The architectural prediction at the center of the theory — that returns to specification codification dominate returns to functional capacity under AI-mediated consumption — is now empirically testable, and the test is in execution.

## Appendix A: Full Threats Register

The threats register summarized in Table 2 is developed at greater length here. Each threat is named, classified by identification arm and hypothesis affected, matched to a mitigation strategy and to the reportable diagnostic, and assessed for residual concern after mitigation. The register is structured to anticipate the principal reviewer objections identified during the pre-draft critical review.

*Reverse causality (continuous-treatment + staggered-DiD; affects H1, H2, H4)*. High-margin firms can afford codification investment because they have resources to do so, not because codification reduces friction. Mitigation: one-year lag of SCI relative to the outcome variable; instrumental-variable specification using Sarbanes-Oxley Section 404 compliance year (for legal-function exogenous expansion) and EEOC reporting mandate years (for HR-function exogenous expansion) as instruments for exogenous functional-headcount variation independent of specification quality. Diagnostic: first-stage F-statistic and over-identification test (Hansen J). Residual concern after mitigation: low for H1 (lag + IV both consistent); medium for H4 (CEO-transition exclusion restriction contestable; reduced-form placebo reported).

*Selection into codification events (staggered-DiD; affects H1, H5 sharp-treatment robustness arm)*. Firms expecting friction-tax decline self-select into handbook publication, ISO 9001 certification, design-system release, and investor-day specification-overhaul. The treated and never-treated populations differ on observable and unobservable pre-treatment characteristics. Mitigation: propensity-score reweighting on pre-event fundamentals (log revenue, log total assets, log employment, lagged outcome, industry FE); placebo lead specifications confirming no anticipation in [t−3, t−1]. Diagnostic: pre-treatment trend coefficients (event-study leads) and propensity-score balance table. Residual concern: medium; addressed by complementary continuous-treatment primary arm where selection is governed by within-firm year-over-year linguistic-coherence shifts rather than discrete event adoption.

*Time-varying unobserved firm quality (continuous-treatment; affects all hypotheses)*. A high-quality management team plausibly both increases substrate codification and reduces functional friction through unobserved channels. Mitigation: Oster [-@oster-2019-unobservable-selection-coefficient] δ-bound analysis reporting the magnitude of omitted-variable correlation required to overturn the estimated effect; industry × year fixed effects absorbing industry-level time-varying shocks. Diagnostic: δ-bound estimate; coefficient stability with controls. Residual concern: medium; the central remaining identification threat post-mitigation, anchoring the limitations-section discussion.

*Anticipation effects in DiD (staggered-DiD; affects H1 robustness arm)*. Firms may begin internal codification preparation in [t−2, t−1] before the dated codification event becomes publicly verifiable. Mitigation: placebo lead specifications testing [t−3, t−1] coefficients; Callaway-Sant'Anna pre-treatment period explicit. Diagnostic: lead-coefficient F-test of joint zero. Residual concern: low.

*Treatment-effect heterogeneity bias in TWFE (staggered-DiD; affects H1 robustness arm)*. Conventional two-way fixed-effects DiD generates biased estimates under staggered adoption with heterogeneous treatment effects [@goodmanbacon-2021-differenceindifferences-with-variation; @de-2020-twoway-fixed-effects]. Mitigation: Callaway-Sant'Anna group-time ATT as primary; Goodman-Bacon decomposition reported alongside; Sun-Abraham as secondary. Diagnostic: forbidden-comparison weight share; weight shares above .25 trigger automatic reliance on Callaway-Sant'Anna. Residual concern: low after mitigation.

*NLP measurement error in coherence (continuous-treatment + H3 contradiction index; affects H1, H3, and H4 primary identification)*. BERT-base-uncased embeddings may capture linguistic-register differences (technical-versus-narrative voice, legal-counsel-stylistic shift) rather than substantive substrate changes. Mitigation: multiple embedding methods reported in parallel (BERT + Loughran-McDonald dictionary cosine); human-rater spot-check on a stratified subsample with κ-agreement statistic. Diagnostic: inter-method correlation; rater-NLP agreement κ. Residual concern: medium for H3 contradiction index; low for the within-firm year-over-year continuous-SCI primary specification because register shifts within a firm year-over-year are constrained.

*LinkedIn / Burning Glass occupation taxonomy drift (function-layer headcount proxies; affects H1, H2 secondary specifications)*. Job-title taxonomies in LinkedIn and Burning Glass / Lightcast data evolve over the panel window, generating apparent year-over-year shifts in function-layer headcount composition that reflect taxonomy changes rather than substantive labor-force changes. Mitigation: occupation crosswalk to a fixed reference (O*NET or SOC codes); Bartik-style industry-mix instrument as robustness. Diagnostic: Bartik first-stage F. Residual concern: medium; documented as a measurement limitation in the follow-up paper reporting archival results.

*Sample-selection bias from XAD missingness (all hypotheses)*. Compustat XAD is non-missing for approximately 25–50 percent of firm-years and is concentrated in consumer-facing sectors. Mitigation: Heckman selection correction with industry-mean disclosure-propensity as the exclusion-restriction variable; XAD-missing-firm robustness using SG&A-aggregate as substitute outcome. Diagnostic: Heckman λ; missingness-imputation sensitivity (Mill's ratio coefficient). Residual concern: medium; restricts the brand-capital-stock-using H2 to the XAD-observed subsample.

*Distress confound in spend-cessation events (H5)*. Advertising-spend-cessation events may correlate with underlying firm distress, confounding the valuation effect attributable to push-dependence collapse. Mitigation: distress-event exclusion (firms with total-asset decline > 10% in cessation year removed); propensity-score reweighting on pre-event fundamentals; Fama-French three-factor model estimated on the [−5, −2] pre-event window to absorb pre-event risk-loading shifts. Diagnostic: pre-cessation fundamentals balance table. Residual concern: low after mitigation.

*CEO-transition exclusion-restriction violation (H4 IV)*. The CEO-transition IV requires that transitions affect AI-ROI only through the pre-deployment specification-codification channel. Plausible violation channels include direct CEO effects on firm performance, strategic-reset behavior affecting non-codification investments, and confounding firm shocks driving both CEO turnover and AI deployment. Mitigation: reduced-form CEO-transition effect on AI-ROI reported as placebo; controls for direct CEO effects via CEO-fixed-effects where the data permit. Diagnostic: reduced-form coefficient and standard error. Residual concern: medium; the H4 identification is the most contestable in the design and is reported with appropriate caveats.

*Comment-letter exposure endogeneity (robustness only)*. SEC comment-letter receipt is endogenous to firm characteristics, disclosure choices, and prior-year regulatory exposure. The exclusion restriction required for IV use of comment-letter volume — that comment-letter receipt affect outcomes only through the substrate-coherence channel — is contestable; SEC review plausibly affects firms through remediation costs, managerial time, internal-control changes, market perceptions, and governance actions independent of any textual-coherence channel. Mitigation: comment letters used only as an exposure variable in robustness specifications, not as a first-stage instrumental variable for SCI; the H1 specification with comment-letter exposure as control is reported as a sensitivity check. Diagnostic: SCI coefficient robust to controlling for comment-letter exposure. Residual concern: low; the variable is used as exposure rather than instrument throughout.

*Brand-FE confound in cross-stakeholder dispersion (H3)*. Firms with multiple distinct brands generate within-firm-year dispersion in YouGov BrandIndex consumer-NPS that reflects multi-brand portfolio composition rather than specification-substrate incoherence. Mitigation: H3 primary specification restricted to single-brand-dominant firms (≥ 70 percent of revenue from one brand per Compustat segment disclosures); brand-fixed-effects supplement where YouGov brand-to-firm crosswalk permits. Diagnostic: sample-restriction sensitivity coefficient; single-brand-vs-multi-brand subsample contrast. Residual concern: medium; documented as a limitation requiring brand-level data beyond the firm-level primary specification.

## Appendix C: Simulation Template for Friction-Tax Research

The pre-registered Monte Carlo and regression-identification simulation package developed for this paper is offered as a methodological template that future friction-tax research can adopt. The template structure is as follows. First, a formal Monte Carlo simulation operationalizes the theoretical mechanism over a pre-specified parameter grid with a fixed seed; the simulation outputs a phase-shift threshold and an effect-size magnitude that the empirical study should be able to detect at the analogous parameter values. Second, a regression-identification simulation generates synthetic panel datasets with the pre-registered effect size under H₁ and tests the design's statistical power; the same simulation under H₀ tests for nominal Type I error coverage. Third, the simulation outputs are logged transparently in a `POST_EXPERIMENT_REPORT.md` file in the code companion, including any post-hoc deviations from the pre-registration and the remediation specified to address them. Fourth, the empirical study uses the remediated estimator as the primary specification.

This template addresses three credibility challenges that pre-registration of point estimates alone does not address. It exposes implementation errors in the data-generating process that pure pre-registration does not catch; it confirms statistical power before the data are observed; and it disciplines the researcher to report estimator deviations publicly rather than silently switching specifications during analysis. The template is offered without claim of originality on any individual component; the combination is the contribution. Friction-tax research that adopts the template will not be measurably more credible than research that does not, but it will be auditable in ways that current friction-tax research is not.
